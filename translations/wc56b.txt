978=With Tosho Boy and Green Grass he formed the "Big Three" of 1976-77, known as "TTG" from their initials. His beautiful chestnut coat and blaze earned him the name "the Shooting Star Prince." He emerged early, winning the Hanshin Sansai S. (old age system), and his clashes with Tosho Boy in the classics were the talk of racing. He was 2nd to Tosho Boy in the Satsuki Sho, 7th in the Derby, and 2nd to late bloomer Green Grass in the Kikuka Sho - no classic. But older, he showed his true worth: winning 3 straight to the Tenno Sho (Spring), then, after a 2nd to Tosho Boy in the Takarazuka Kinen, 3 more in autumn to the Arima Kinen, avenging himself on Tosho Boy. That year of battles among the three, above all that Arima Kinen, is told as one of racing's greatest duels. Then at 6 came tragedy: in the Nihon Keizai Shinshun Hai, a prep for a trip abroad, he broke a leg and had to be put down. Many fans laid flowers at his grave at Yoshida Farm, his birthplace. In 1990, 12 years after his death, he was added to the JRA Hall of Fame for contributions beyond his record.
982=Winner of 4 graded races incl. the 1973 Yasuda Kinen and Sapporo Kinen, he was from the last crop of Hindostan, 7-time champion sire - "his final masterpiece." Best at 1200-2000m, at 4 (old age system) he won the Kabutoyama Kinen and was 2nd in the NHK Hai, Sprinters S. and Kumohata Kinen. At 5 he won the Yasuda Kinen, Sapporo Kinen and All Comers. A fracture in that year's Tenno Sho (Autumn) ended his career. At stud he sired local graded winners, and Hakuho Queen, holder of local racing's record 161-race losing streak.
983=The 1974 Derby winner, with 4 wins from 11, always in the top 3. At 3 (old age system) he won 3 allowance races; at 4 he was 2nd in the Keisei Hai, 3rd in the Yayoi Sho and 2nd to Kitano Kachidoki in the Satsuki Sho. In the Derby he beat Kitano Kachidoki in a record 2:27.4. Injury ended his career after it. At stud he sired local graded winner Seven Lancer and others, and in 1984 was presented to the President of South Korea.
984=Winner of the 2009 Tenno Sho (Spring). A late bloomer, he reached open class at 5 and grew stronger in G2s and G3s. At 6, after a 2nd in the Nikkei Sho, he won the Tenno Sho (Spring) at 46.5 to 1, a huge upset: favorite Asakusa Kings and 2nd choice Screen Hero were swallowed by the pack, and he won a tight finish by a neck. The next year he won the Nikkei Sho and was 2nd in the Tenno Sho (Spring), and the year after won the Stayers S., a long-distance force for years until retiring in 2013 at 10.
985=She won 4 straight allowance races from late 4 into spring at 5, then was 5th in the Victoria Mile. She finally won the Hakodate Sprint S. in summer at 6. She retired to breed after a 10th in the '08 Hankyu Hai. All 8 wins came at 1500m or less - a pure sprinter.
990=She finished behind Mejiro Ramonu 6 times: Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West), Oka Sho, Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East), Oaks, Rose S. and Queen Elizabeth Cup. After that she won the Hanshin and Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu, and at 4 the Swan S., holding her own against colts.
991=Based at local Oi, she won 6 at 3 and 3 at 4. At 5 she won the Queen Sho, Kanto Hai and Tokyo Hai in a row, then moved to the JRA, winning only the Kokura Nikkei Sho at 6. Often a narrow 2nd.
995=Son of Tomei, hailed as the greatest mare, he made the first mother-son Tenno Sho double. Late at 3 he showed his stamina with 2nds in the Kikuka Sho and Hanshin Daishoten. In autumn at 4, as 5th choice, he won the Tenno Sho (Autumn), the race his dam had won. He won the Kyoto Daishoten at 5, then raced in Iwate until 8.
996=The first Japanese horse to go toe-to-toe with foreign horses in the Japan Cup. At 5 he won the Diamond S. and was 4th in the Takarazuka Kinen. In autumn at 6 he finally won a big race, the Tenno Sho (Autumn). With Triple Crown winner Mr. C.B. absent, he was Japan's main hope in that year's Japan Cup, and ran a superb 2nd, beaten a head - the best by a Japanese horse. He retired after the race, the first to give Japan real hope of winning the Japan Cup.
997=The most-watched horse of the 1981 classics. He won all 3 trials - the Spring S., NHK Hai and Kyoto Shimbun Hai - as favorite, yet was scratched from the Satsuki Sho and 2nd in both the Derby and Kikuka Sho - the "Trial Triple Crown winner." But all agreed on his ability: older, he won the Sankei Osaka Hai and was 2nd in the Takamatsunomiya Hai and Mainichi Okan. At stud he sired local stars like Tokyo Daishoten winner Bold Face and Takasaki Derby winner San Ei Speed.
999=The 1980 Satsuki Sho winner. Strong on dirt and heavy ground, he was at his best in the Satsuki Sho, run in a downpour: 4th choice, he sat handy and held off Opec Horse in the straight. 14th in the Derby, he then won Fukushima's Radio Tampa Sho, Fukushima Kinen and the Local Racing Invitational, and raced until 7.
987=As 12th choice she won the Rose S., beating favorite Elps. After a 13th in the Queen Elizabeth Cup and an 18-month break, she ran a few times and retired to breed. Her foals include Shingun Opera (98: Opera House).
