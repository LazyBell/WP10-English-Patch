2959=The 1979 Kikuka Sho winner. At 4 (old age system) he won the Mainichi Hai but was 9th in the Spring S. and 11th in the Satsuki Sho. After a 6th and 8th in autumn, he was a head 2nd to Tenmei in the Kyoto Daishoten, then won the Kikuka Sho easily over favorite Bingo Garoo and others. Injured for over 2 years, he moved to local racing, winning at Obihiro and Hakodate - a Kikuka winner's pride. He left only 1 crop at stud before becoming a pensioner.
2960=Full sister of Kaburaya O; winner of the 1979 QEII Cup. Debuting at 4 (old age system), she won her debut and the 4yo Himba S., rested until autumn, was 2nd in the UHB Hai and Sapphire S., 6th in the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu - her only unplaced run so far - then as 2nd choice ran down favorite Agnes Lady to win the QEII Cup, last leg of the fillies' Triple Crown. 2nd in the Kimpai (East) at 5, unplaced after, she retired that spring. Only 6 foals, none notable.
2961=Much noticed from his debut, he won it and the Kaede Sho but flopped otherwise, 6th as 2nd choice in the Asahi Hai 3yo S. (old age system). At 4 he was transformed: an open race, the Tokyo 4yo S. (now Kyodo News Hai), Yayoi Sho and Spring S. - 4 straight - then Satsuki Sho favorite, 4th to Bingo Garoo, and 8th to Katsurano Haiseiko in the Derby. At stud for 15 years, with some success.
2962=Winner of the 1979 Asahi Hai 3yo S. (old age system), champion 3yo colt - in his 9th start, after 2nds in the Hakodate 3yo S., Keisei Hai 3yo S. and Fuchu 3yo S. At 4 he won the Tokyo 4yo S. (now Kyodo News Hai), then rested long; back in autumn, 4th in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai, 14th in the Kikuka Sho. Winless after, he retired late at 5.
2963=A home-bred sire who held his own in the import-dominated 1960s, siring Big Eight winners like Yamano O and Sea Ace. He won the 1953 Kikuka Sho and 1954 Tenno Sho (Spring). 2nd and 3rd to Bostonian in the Satsuki Sho and Derby, he bloomed in autumn and denied Bostonian the Triple Crown in the Kikuka Sho, winning by 3.5 lengths. In 1954 he won all 5 starts incl. the Tenno Sho (Spring), named the first-ever Horse of the Year. His first crop gave Takarazuka Kinen winner Caesar, his 3rd Satsuki winner Yamano O; later Oka Sho winner Sea Ace and many graded winners - a leading sire. His blood lives on through dams too: Mistarumae, 3rd dam of Yamanin Zephyr, and Fushimi Ace, 5th dam of Water Navillera.
2964=The 1963 Oaks winner, named for owner Ito's initials, read as letters. Winning late at 3 (old age system) and once at 4, she was 6th of 25 in the Oka Sho and 3rd in the 4yo Himba Tokubetsu, then won the Oaks as 4th choice, surging clear as favorite Milford sank. In Hokkaido she won an open race and was 2nd in the Hakodate Kinen, then 2nd to later Kikuka winner Great Yoruka in the St. Lite Kinen, retiring after 1 race at 5. Of 9 foals, her 6th, Itei Shiro, won the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu and was 4th in the Oaks; via her 2nd, Itei Grace, came Arima Kinen winner Hikari Duel and later Kokura Kinen winner Hokusei Amber.
2965=The 1961 Oka Sho winner. 2nd on debut and then 2 wins at 3 (old age system), last in 2 races early at 4, she won the Kisaragi Sho, then 2nd in an open race, the Oka Sho as 2nd choice of 28 by 5 lengths. 16th as 2nd choice in the Oaks, she won an open race and the Kobe Hai (now Kobe Shimbun Hai), then the Kinko Sho (before graded status) and Osaka Hai at 5 - 4 graded wins - and retired. Breeding at once, she had 9 foals in 12 years, incl. Kyoto Kinen winner Nihon Pillow Homare, Sapporo Kinen winner Erimo Marchis, and Ace Cosmo, 2nd in the Kyoto Daishoten and Victoria C.
2966=Unbeaten in 7 with 2 graded wins - the 3rd unbeaten graded winner after Kurifuji (11 from 11, 1943-44) and Tokino Minoru (10 from 10, 1950-51). He won his debut by 8 lengths in record time, the Hakodate 3yo S. (old age system) by 9 in record time, and the Hokkaido 3yo S. (now Sapporo 2yo S.) easily from last after a slow start, without the whip - "Tokino Minoru reborn," the top classic hope. A fracture in training cost nearly 2 years; back in summer at 5 he won 3 easily, aiming at the Tenno Sho and Arima Kinen, then periostitis struck. He won on return at 6, but it recurred and he retired. With partbred blood on his dam's side, he was classed partbred and neglected at stud, yet sired graded winners like Aichi Hai winner Hard Lark.
2967=The 1959 Derby winner, beating fancied Meitai and Will Deal on heavy ground. Debuting in November at 3 (old age system), winless that year, he won early at 4, then an allowance and an open race; too late for the Satsuki Sho, he was 3rd in the NHK Hai. 3rd choice behind Satsuki 1-2 Will Deal and Meitai, he relished the rain-soaked going and won easily - the race of his life. Winless that year after (6th Kikuka Sho, 5th Arima Kinen), older he won the Tokyo Hai (now Tokyo Shimbun Hai) and an open race, retiring in January at 6. He sired Kyoto 4yo Tokubetsu winner Meisei Hikari.
2968=Winner of the 1967 Satsuki Sho and 1968 Arima Kinen. At 3 (old age system) he won 5 of 6 incl. the Hokkaido 3yo S. (now Sapporo 2yo S.). 6th in the Spring S. at 4, he won the Satsuki Sho, breaking clear early from a handy spot by 2.5 lengths. 5th as Derby favorite, he won the Hakodate Kinen, was 2nd in the Kyoto Hai (now Kyoto Shimbun Hai) and Kikuka Sho - the real thing. At 5 he won the Hakodate Kinen again; 9th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), he won the Arima Kinen dominantly - a 2nd Big Eight win. At 6, 2 open wins, 2nd in the British Fair Commemorative (now Sprinters S.), then 6th and 15th; after 4 defeats at 7 he retired. He sired Kabutoyama Kinen winner Ryu Fubuki.
2969=The 1966 Satsuki Sho winner. Unbeaten in 3 incl. the Hanshin 3yo S. (old age system), champion 3yo colt, he won an open race at 4 - 4 straight - then 2nd in the Yayoi Sho and 4th in the Spring S. 2nd choice in the Satsuki Sho, he beat favorite and Spring S. winner Shogun by half a length. Then he faded: 5th in the NHK Hai, 20th as 2nd choice in the Derby, 5th and 17th in autumn. At 5 he won 3 open races and the Hankyu Hai, and after a 4th in the Tenno Sho (Spring) at 6 retired - beaten badly at long trips, but never out of the top five at 2000m or less. He sired Sekiya Kinen winner Suzu Hope.
2970=In 1974 she became the 3rd winner of the US fillies' Triple Tiara. Her owner sponsored tennis star Chris Evert, hence the name. At 2 she won 4 of 5, 2nd only in the G1 Frizette S. At 3 she won the Acorn S. (G1), the Mother Goose S. narrowly, and the CCA Oaks by 3.5 lengths - the Triple Tiara. She then beat Hollywood Oaks winner Miss Musket in a match race, Eclipse champion 3yo filly. At 4 she won the La Canada S., then flopped as favorite in the Santa Margarita H. and retired. Her daughter Six Crowns produced successful sire Chief's Crown, and her line flourished.
2971=The 2021 2yo queen and a 2022 3yo filly fancy. 3rd to later 6-G1 Equinox on debut, she won a maiden, then the Artemis S. as 7th choice with a fine late run. 3rd choice in the Hanshin JF, she sat ideally in midfield and closed with a superior kick - 3 straight, a G1, 2yo queen. A classic lead player, she was 3rd to Namur in the Tulip Sho and just missed in the Oka Sho, 4th to Stars on Earth. Oaks favorite, liked for the longer trip, she never quickened from the back, 12th. 4th to Stunning Rose in the Shion S., she then had a tendon injury; unwilling to push her, her camp announced her retirement in autumn at 4.
2972=A 2021 dirt name who won the JBC 2yo Yushun. He won his debut under Lemaire, breaking clear from up front, then at Mombetsu was 3rd choice behind local fancies Nudge and Scharfsinn in the JBC 2yo Yushun, closing powerfully from the back to beat Nudge by 1.5 lengths. 9th in the Zen-Nihon 2yo Yushun, a fracture cost 3 months; since, 12th in all 3 races.
2973=Debuting in June at 2, he won from up front by 2 lengths. 5th as favorite in the Clover Sho from midfield, he raced forward again to win the Keio Hai 2yo S. as 8th choice - impressive. A fracture followed; back in the Arlington C. in spring at 3, he was 3rd to Danon Scorpion, then 6th in the NHK Mile C. In Europe he was 11th in the July C. and Prix Maurice de Gheest. Back home, 6th, a length behind, in the Capital S. (L).
2974=Offered at 140 million yen in all (3.5 million x 40 shares), the hope of the Kunieda stable for a first Derby. He won his debut at 1.1, then the Saudi Arabia Royal C. - a fine start. But he was 12th as favorite in the Hopeful S., 8th in the Mainichi Hai at 3, 14th on dirt in the Japan Dirt Derby, and last (18th) in the Capital S. (L).
2975=Unbeaten in 3, he was favorite in the Asahi Hai FS (G1) but 2nd to Do Deuce. Taking the mile route at 3, 4th in the NHK Mile C. and Yasuda Kinen (G1), he grew in autumn, beating NHK winner Danon Scorpion and others in the Fuji S. (G2). In the Mile CS (G1), 6th choice behind Schnell Meister and Sodashi, the first white G1 winner, he charged from the back with a stunning finish - his G1 at last. At 4, 5th in the Dubai Turf, 2nd to Songline in the Yasuda Kinen, then 8th in the Mile CS and 7th in the Hong Kong Mile. At 5, 2nd in the Milers C. (G2), 5th in the Yasuda Kinen, 6th in the Mile CS; he retired that year.
2976=A 2022 3yo miler who won the NHK Mile C. Unbeaten in 2 incl. the Hagi S. (L), he was 3rd to Do Deuce in the Asahi Hai FS. 7th in the Kyodo News Hai at 3, he won the Arlington C. as favorite, closing sharply from the back. In the NHK Mile C., 4th choice behind Asahi Hai runner-up Serifos and Junior C. winner Industria, he led mid-straight from midfield and held off Matenro Orion - 3yo mile king. 3rd in the Fuji S., he was 4th choice as the leading 3yo in the Mile CS but 11th as fellow 3yo Serifos won, then 6th in the Hong Kong Mile.
