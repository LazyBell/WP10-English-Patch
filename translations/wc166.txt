3616=A legendary 19th-century star of New Zealand and Australia, still called the greatest in Oceania's history. Like his sire Musket, he was named after a gun. Debuting in New Zealand in 1887/88 at 2, he won all 5. Racing in Australia from 3, he was Victoria Derby favorite but lost by a head through his jockey's complacency; his owner, who lost heavily, sold him to new Australian owners. He then won at every trip from 8f to 24f, incl. the Melbourne Cup and Sydney Cup, and won 15 straight from 4 into 5. Retiring at the end of 5, his earnings of 29,476 pounds stood as a record for over 20 years. His first crop included Victoria Derby winner Wallace; later moved to England, he sired Derby winner Spearmint, leaving his mark on European racing too.
3617=A speedster of 1948-1949, called America's fastest. At 3 in 1948 he was overshadowed by stablemate and crop-mate Triple Crown winner Citation; 2nd to him in the Kentucky Derby, he focused on sprints, winning 4 incl. the Jerome H. and Phoenix H. - champion sprinter. At 4, with Citation injured, he stepped in at middle distances, winning his first 8 incl. the Gulfstream Park H. in record time - 12 wins from 15. Beaten twice that autumn by dual classic winner Capot, he shared Horse of the Year with him. Declining after, he won 3 more by 6 and retired. Not a successful sire.
3618=Unbeaten in 15 in America in 1907 at 2 and into 3, Horse of the Year both years - the most wins by any unbeaten US champion. Debuting in May at 2, he swept through sprints, winning with ease rather than by margins, 12 of 12 incl. the Futurity S. and Champagne S. Called the greatest 2yo ever, he was Horse of the Year at 2. At 3 leg trouble kept him out about 6 months; aiming at the Belmont, he won the Withers S. a week before, then the Belmont and Tidal S., and retired. At stud in England and America for 23 years, poor fertility left just 81 foals. Not a success, but his son Neddie led to Alsab, and 3 generations on to Ack Ack, keeping his line alive.
3619=The 6th US Triple Crown winner: 16 wins, 4 seconds and a 3rd from 21. He won the Belmont by 25 lengths, the record until Secretariat 30 years later. Often 2nd early from his June debut at 2, he blossomed after summer, winning easily in record times, and took his final 2yo race, the Walden S., by 30 lengths. Unbeaten at 3, he won the Kentucky Derby, the Preakness by 8 lengths at 1.15-1, the Withers S., and the Belmont at 1.05-1 by 25 lengths. He was injured in the Belmont and never raced again. A great sire, with Kentucky Derby winner Count Turf and Belmont winner Counterpoint; his male line has faded, but as broodmare sire of Mr. Prospector's dam, his blood is everywhere.
3620=US champion 2yo colt of 1958. Debuting in April at 2, he won 3 straight to the Juvenile S., an allowance, then the Great American S., Saratoga Special S. and Hopeful S. - 7 straight. 2nd to Intentionally in the Futurity S., he won 3 more by year-end - 10 of 11, champion 2yo. At 3 he faded: inconsistent, 3rd in the Kentucky Derby and 9th in the Preakness, his first heavy loss. Just 3 stakes wins from 18 more to autumn at 4, and he retired. At stud he sired dual classic winner Riva Ridge and many stakes winners; his son First Family, imported to Japan, sired Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Arima Kinen winner Hoyo Boy.
3621=A legendary horse, perhaps France's greatest, and the only French-bred winner of the English Triple Crown. Ordinary at 2 with 1 win from 3, he won the 2000 Guineas as 5th choice in his first race at 3, narrowly, then the Derby as favorite. English racing then reigned supreme, and foreign-breds rarely won its big races; as the first French-bred Derby winner, he sent France into raptures. He won 3 straight in summer incl. the Grand Prix de Paris, then the St Leger easily - the Triple Crown. At 4 he won 5 straight to the Ascot Gold C., waiting last and winning by 40 lengths with an astonishing finish, then the Grand Prix de l'Empereur at home - 6 of 6 that year - and retired. Amid the Franco-Prussian War he failed at stud, but daughter Keepsake's line produced, 4 generations on, Ksar (sire of Tourbillon), so his blood lives on.
3622=A South African star who moved to America and became champion turf horse. Debuting in South Africa in 1966/67, he won 15 of 18 to early 1968/69 at 4, unbeatable at a mile and under; at 3 he won the Guineas triple (Royal Reserve Guineas, Cape Merriwood Guineas, South African Guineas), champion 3yo colt. Moving to the US in 1969, travel and new surroundings delayed his start 6 months, but he won an allowance easily, then the Stars & Stripes H. and Bernard Baruch H., and in autumn the 12f Sunrise H. and Man o' War S. - a stayer too. 2nd to Karabas in the Washington DC International, where Japan's Takeshiba O also ran, he was champion turf horse and retired. At stud he sired Derby winner Henbit and Kentucky Oaks winner Sun and Snow; his male line ended, but he appears in dam lines, e.g. in Ghostzapper's and Transcend's pedigrees.
3623=A 1950 US classic star, winner of the Preakness and Horse of the Year. Winning 6 of 7 at 2, at 3 he won the Wood Memorial, was 2nd to Middleground in the Kentucky Derby, then beat him in the Withers S. and Preakness. 7th to Middleground in the Belmont, he won the American Derby, Jerome H. and Jockey Club Gold C. in autumn, then the Sunset H. - champion 3yo and Horse of the Year with one classic. At 4 his only stakes win was the New York H.; at 5 he added the San Marcos H. and retired. A fair sire with 23 stakes winners; few sons succeeded, so his male line ended, but his blood survives through dams.
3624=A carrier of Man o' War's blood who extended that rare line. His best son, In Reality, led to sires Relaunch, Tiznow and Warning; Warning sired Dikat and, in Japan, G1 winners Carlton Light O and Sunningdale. Through daughter Ta Wee came Holy Bull 2 generations later. A gifted racehorse in the US from 2 to 6, he won 18 of 34, mainly at a mile and under: at 2 the Futurity S. over First Landing; at 3, 5 wins incl. the Withers S. and Jerome H. - US champion sprinter. At 5 and 6 he had great battles with dual classic winner Carry Back, 2 years younger, and won his last race, the Seminole H., by 8 lengths.
3625=US champion 3yo colt of 1962 with 6 wins incl. the Belmont S. His fierce duel with Ridan in the Travers S. is a classic, and the Jaipur S. (then ungraded, now G1) was created in his name in 1984. Winning his debut in July at 2 and the Flash S., then the Hopeful S. and Cowdin S., he won 4 of 7 at 2. Starting in April at 3, he won 2 straight, was 10th in the Preakness, his first heavy loss, then after a win took the Belmont. That summer he beat Ridan by a nose in the Travers, earning champion 3yo over Ridan, winner of the Florida Derby and Arlington Classic. Winless after, he retired after a 4th in the Seminole H. in February at 4. At stud he sired Prix Morny winner Amber Rama, later imported to Japan.
3626=A legend who was US Horse of the Year 5 years running from 1960 at 3. Effective from a mile to 16f, he stayed on top even under heavy weights; 4th in The Blood-Horse's top 100 of the 20th century. Starting after the classics at 3, he won 6 straight stakes, 3 in record time incl. the Jockey Club Gold C. - Horse of the Year without a classic run. At 4 he won the New York Handicap Triple (Metropolitan H., Suburban H., Brooklyn H.), 7 of 9. Unwell until summer at 5, he then won the Stymie H., Woodward S. and Jockey Club Gold C. by 10 lengths in record time - Horse of the Year a 3rd time. At 6 he won 8 straight from the Gulfstream Park H. to the Jockey Club Gold C. At 7 he won a 5th straight Gold C. and, after 3 straight 2nds, the Washington DC International in record time - 5 straight titles. Declining at 8 with 3 wins from 6, he lost the title, ran once at 9 and retired. A gelding, he never bred, but was much loved until his death at 26.
3627=A historic 19th-century Hungarian mare, unbeaten in 54 races across Europe, famous worldwide with countless stories told of her. Debuting in Germany in June at 2, she won all 10, 5 by over 10 lengths. At 3 she won classics incl. the Hungarian 2000 Guineas and Austrian Derby - 17 of 17, already hugely popular. At 4 she won abroad incl. England's Goodwood C. and France's Grand Prix de Deauville; in Germany's Grosser Preis von Baden she dead-heated with Prince Giles, then won the run-off easily - 15 of 15. At 5, her last year, she won all 12 under heavy weights, retiring with a Guinness record 54 unbeaten. A fine broodmare with several classic winners; daughter Budagyongye's line produced, 16 generations later, Derby and Irish Derby winner Camelot.
3628=A French horse who won the Arc in 1921 and 1922. Unusually for Bruleur's slow-maturing stock, he raced well at 2 and won 3 straight to the French Derby in spring at 3. Beaten by England's Lemonora in the Grand Prix de Paris, he won the 2nd-ever Arc in autumn over the English, defending French pride. At 4, hot Arc favorite with no foreign runners, he won easily - the first 2-time winner. 2nd in the Prix Gladiateur, perhaps too far at 6200m, he retired. At stud he sired French Derby winners Tourbillon and Thor, French Oaks winner Ukrania and more. Tourbillon succeeded at stud, siring Clarion and Partholon, whose blood is common in Japan.
3629=An Irish horse who won the 1969 Arc. Debuting in July at 2, he won his 2nd start. At 3 he went to England for the classics, but was 4th in the Derby Trial S. and Queen's Vase. He won the Oxfordshire S. that summer, then lost until the Prix du Cadran at 4, followed by the Ascot Gold C. and Leinster H. - 3 straight staying wins. Dropping back in trip for the Arc, 14th choice, he raced forward on his stamina and held on by 3/4 length, stunning the crowd. He won the November H. and retired. At stud he sired stayers Shafaraz and M-Lolshan; his male line ended, but granddaughter Malt produced Horlicks, spreading his name worldwide.
