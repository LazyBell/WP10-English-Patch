3661=Winner of the 1952 Derby and St Leger. Poor at 2 and little fancied, he improved rapidly at 3: 3 straight wins, then the Derby as favorite, edging Gay Time. Against older horses he won the Eclipse S. by 3 lengths, then the 2nd-ever King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S., again beating Gay Time, and the St Leger easily, a 3rd defeat of Gay Time - 7 of 7 that year. Skipped the 2000 Guineas as too short; many say he would have won the Triple Crown. Retired to stud amid huge expectations, he sired one Irish and one French classic winner, then was sold to America, where he again disappointed.
3662=The 4th US Triple Crown winner, in 1937, known as great rival of hugely popular Seabiscuit. Modest at 2 with 3 wins from 6 incl. one stakes, he blossomed at 3: 2 straight incl. the Chesapeake S. easily, the Kentucky Derby in hand, the Preakness in a tight finish with Derby runner-up Pompoon, and the Belmont in a US record 2:28.6 - an undisputed Triple Crown. On the West Coast, older Seabiscuit was a sensation, and a clash with the East's champion was demanded. It came in autumn at 4; favored after 8 wins from 9 that year, he was beaten 4 lengths - the end of the race of the century. 2 more wins, and he retired in spring at 5. At stud he sired Busher, Blue Peter and many more, North American champion sire in 1945. His sons failed at stud, but as broodmare sire of Buckpasser he influenced the world.
3663=The 1853 English Triple Crown winner - the first ever. 2nd on debut, his only defeat, he won the Glasgow S., then at 3 the 2000 Guineas and Derby as favorite, both in tight finishes. After a rest he won the St Leger easily as hot favorite - the first to sweep all 3. Rivals then avoided him, and he walked over 3 times running. At 4 he won 4 incl. the Ascot Gold C. and retired. At stud he was overshadowed by Stockwell, "the Emperor of Stallions," a year older, but sired Oaks winner Summerside and 2000 Guineas winner The Wizard. His son Australian led, 4 generations on, to Man o' War, and son Solon to Hurry On - later hailed as key to the Matchem line.
3664=Winner of the 1934 Derby and St Leger. Debuting in July at 2, he won his 3rd start, 1 of 3. Aimed at long races at 3, he skipped the 2000 Guineas, won at 12f and 10f, then beat 2000 Guineas winner Colombo, unbeaten in 9 and favorite, in the Derby. 3rd in the Eclipse S., he won the 12f Great Yorkshire S., then the St Leger as hot favorite in a race record 3:01.6. At 4 he won all 4 starts incl. the Coronation C. and Eclipse S., and retired. Dying young of illness, he left few foals; his best was Irish Triple Crown winner Windsor Slipper.
3665=A legendary filly of wartime Japan who won an irregular Triple Crown: the Tokyo Yushun (Derby), Hanshin Yushun Himba (Oaks) and Kyoto 4yo race (Kikuka Sho). Unbeaten in 11 - still the record for an unbeaten top-class horse. Debuting in 1943 at 4 (old age system), she won easily, then by a huge margin from the front, then the Tokyo Yushun by 6 lengths in record time. In autumn she won the Hanshin Yushun Himba by 10, 2 more, and the Kyoto race by a huge margin - no one could live with her. No Kikuka Sho winner before or since has won by such a margin. In 1944, as the war intensified, racing was halted and only betting-free ability trials held without crowds; she won 3 by 5, 10 and 6 lengths and retired, 11 of 11. As a broodmare she produced Kumohata Kinen winner Ichijo and Oaks winner Yamaichi; Ichijo's family later produced 1994 Kisaragi Sho winner Samson Big and 2011 Hyogo star Oe Raijin.
3666=Winner of the 1963 Kentucky Derby and Belmont S., later imported to Japan as a sire of Hokuto Flag, Metro Jumbo and others. His crop had stars Candy Spots and Never Bend, and he was overlooked; even after beating both in the Kentucky Derby he was underrated, 2nd to favorite Candy Spots in the Preakness. Beating Candy Spots in the Belmont, he finally took top billing. He won the Jerome H. that autumn, but no more stakes, and retired at 5. After 7 modest years at stud in the US, in Japan he sired many graded winners incl. Asahi Hai 3yo S. winner Hokuto Flag and autumn Kyoto Kinen winner Metro Jumbo; as broodmare sire of Tamamo Cross and Mery Nice, he spread his blood widely.
3667=A French sprint star of 1965-1966. With brilliant speed he won 5 of 6 sprints at 2, beating older stars in the Abbaye and Prix du Petit Couvert. At 3, beaten in the Poulains and its trial, he won 4 straight incl. middle-distance races like the Prix Jean Prat and Prix d'Ispahan, then the Prix du Moulin, and retired. At stud in Ireland he sired Irish 2000 Guineas winner Sharp Edge and other stakes winners; imported to Japan in his 7th season, he sired graded winners Asaka Silver and Sea Bird Park. Yet he is best known as a broodmare sire: of White Nalby, dam of siblings Oguri Cap and Oguri Roman, and, from his Irish days, Tuna, dam of Arc winner Carroll House.
3668=A sensational US 2yo of 1964, winning the Hopeful S. and Champagne S. easily - 8 wins and 2 seconds from 10. The Jockey Club's Free Handicap rated him 130, above later 2yo Horse of the Year Secretariat's 129. Classic favorite at 3, he was 10th in the Kentucky Derby, anemic, and rested the rest of the year. Back in spring at 4 he won 4 straight incl. the Roseben H. and Metropolitan H.; 6th in the Suburban H. under 135 lb, a fracture was found and he retired. At stud in the US, then France from 11 and Japan from 16, he did better in France and Japan. Note: an Irish-bred by the same sire, 2 years younger, was also named Bold Lad; the owners were sisters, and the name was reportedly reused in his honor.
3669=The 1968 Oka Sho winner. Debuting in September at 3 (old age system), she won a newcomer race and the Daily Hai 3yo S. At 4 she kept missing, 8th in the Hanshin 4yo Himba Tokubetsu, but in the Oka Sho, from the widest draw of 24, she raced boldly forward and outfought Nittou Yayoi by a head - a Big Eight win. Injury ruled out the Oaks. Back in spring at 5, she was injured again; 14th in an allowance that autumn. At 6 she won the Ikaruga Tokubetsu, then 3 more incl. the Uzushio Tokubetsu and HBC Hai, ran well in stakes, and retired that autumn. No notable foals; she died at 22. Her own line ended, but full sister Miss Tokyo and half-sister Shadai Center carried on the family.
3670=The 1968 Oaks winner, from Never Beat's first crop; with crop-mate Satsuki Sho winner Mahchis, she gave her sire classic titles at once. Debuting in July at 3 (old age system), she won her 3rd start, then 3 straight early at 4. 5th in the Oka Sho, she won the Oaks as 8th choice, holding off Suzu Gerbera. In autumn she won an open race, was 4th in the Mainichi Okan and Himba Tokyo Times Hai (forerunner of the Fuchu Himba S.), ran in the Arima Kinen, and was champion 4yo filly. Winless at 5 but 2nd in the Tanabata Sho and Fukushima Daishoten, she raced until February at 6. Of 7 foals, none stood out.
3671=The 1969 Satsuki Sho winner. With stablemates Minoru, Hakuei Ho and Mejiro Asama, he was one of trainer Ogata's "Four Kings" of that classic crop. Winning his August debut at 3 (old age system) by a huge margin, he won 2 allowances early at 4, the Yayoi Sho in record time over rival Minoru, the Spring S. easily on bad ground, and the Satsuki Sho - 5 straight and a classic. A fracture followed; 2nd on his autumn return, he kept missing from 5: 3rd in the Kumohata Kinen late at 5, a win in the UHB Sho at 7, little else. Retired at 7 to stud, his JRA foals rarely rose above allowances, though local runners like Asahi Teru Hikari did well.
3672=Winner of the 1969 Takarazuka Kinen and many more graded races: Nihon Keizai Shinshun Hai, Sankei Osaka Hai, Asahi Challenge C. Debuting in July at 3 (old age system), he won his 2nd start, his only win that year. At 4 he won 2 allowances and the Mainichi Hai; barred from the Satsuki Sho after a disqualification and scratched from the Derby, he missed the big stage, but was 3rd in the Asahi Challenge C. and Kyoto Hai and 2nd in the Kikuka Sho. At 5 he won the Nihon Keizai Shinshun Hai, Chukyo Daishoten (forerunner of the Takamatsunomiya Kinen) and Sankei Osaka Hai; 4th to Takeshiba O in the Tenno Sho (Spring), he won the Takarazuka Kinen in record time, then the Asahi Challenge C. under top weight 61kg, again in record time. A fracture followed; 2nd in the Milers C. at 6, he retired. His best foal was Teio Sho and Haneda Hai winner Konan Ruby.
3673=The 1970 Oka Sho winner, also of the Hanshin 4yo Himba Tokubetsu (old age system) and Sprinters S. - champion sprinter, a speedy filly. Winning her August debut at 3, she struggled in allowances, then surged late at 3 into 4: 3 straight to the Queen C., the Hanshin 4yo Himba Tokubetsu, and the Oka Sho as favorite by 4 lengths - 5 straight. 14th as Oaks favorite and 11th in the Queen S., she won the Sprinters S., proving it no fluke; champion 4yo filly went to 2-time graded winner Harbor Game, but she was champion sprinter. At 5 she won the Keio Hai Spring H., 3rd in the Tokyo Shimbun Hai, and retired that July. She died at 11 without notable foals.
3674=Winner of the 1970 Tenno Sho (Spring). Emerging early, he won his August debut at 3 (old age system), 3 wins from 7, always top 5, and beat later Oka Sho winner Hide Kotobuki in the Hanshin 3yo S. - top of his crop. A classic contender at 4, he was 16th in the Satsuki Sho, then 4th as 17th choice in the Derby. 2nd against older horses in the Sapporo Kinen, 2nd to Akane Tenryu in the Kikuka Sho, he beat Akane Tenryu in the Tenno Sho (Spring) at 5 - a Big Eight win. Racing until 7, he won 4 open races and was 2nd in the Hakodate Kinen and Sapporo Kinen - 13 wins from 47. No stars at stud; he lived as a pensioner to 35.
3675=The 1970 Oaks winner. Only 3 wins from 26, but one was a Big Eight race. Winning her first 2 from August at 3 (old age system), she then stalled and was little fancied at 4. 5th to Tamami in the Oka Sho, she won the Oaks as 12th choice by 5 lengths: on rain-soaked bad ground, as favorite Tamami floundered to 14th, she alone surged up the rail. The final 3f took nearly 42 seconds - a true mudbath. 2nd in the autumn Kobe Hai, she won no more and retired in autumn at 5. Of 8 foals none starred, but the line via daughter Hamana Jubee to granddaughter Isami Sakura continues.
