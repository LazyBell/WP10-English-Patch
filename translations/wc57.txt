1007=A French great who won the 1965 Derby and Arc and was rated 145 lbs by Timeform, then the highest ever - unbeaten for 47 years until Frankel's 147 in 2012. At 2 he won 2 of 3, rated behind Grey Dawn. At 3 he won the Prix Greffulhe and Prix Lupin, then the Epsom Derby in England, the Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud, and the Arc by 6 lengths - European Horse of the Year. He retired after the Arc, was leased to America for 7 crops, and died soon after the start of his first season back in France. His short stud career produced French fillies' Triple Crown and Arc winner Allez France, and his male line continues faintly through Arctic Tern and Bering.
1008=Winner of 11 G1s, then the most by an American mare, she ran 32 times at 3-4, earning the name "Iron Lady." Often beaten until spring at 3, she then won 8 straight, beating Triple Tiara winner Mom's Command in the Test S. and winning the Maskette S. by 5.5 lengths for her first G1. 2nd in the BC Distaff, she lost the champion 3yo filly title to Mom's Command. At 4 she took on males, winning the Whitney H. - the first mare to do so in 38 years - and ran well in the Metropolitan H. and Woodward S. With a record 8 G1s that year, she was Eclipse Horse of the Year. From 5 she won just 2 allowance races and retired. Her foals disappointed, but granddaughter Sound Barrier won the Fillies' Revue, and her foal Sound Chiara won 3 graded races in a row and was 2nd in the Victoria Mile - her family thrives in Japan.
1009=Gelded before his debut, he struggled in low classes, but bloomed in summer at 4. Inconsistent but powerful, he won the '05 Hakodate Kinen and again the next year. After a 5th in the Sapporo Kinen, a tendon injury cost him nearly a year, but in his 2nd race back he won the Hakodate Kinen a 3rd straight time. 3rd in the All Comers and 4th in the Mainichi Okan, he was 4th going for a 4th Hakodate Kinen in '08, showing his age.
1010=Two long injury breaks limited him to 18 starts by 7. Hard to keep sound, but talented, with 6 wins incl. the Tanabata Sho at 6. He retired to stud after a 16th in the '06 Niigata Kinen.
1011=Winning 6 dirt allowance races, he went to the Japan Cup Dirt at 4 (8th). 2nd in the Negishi S., he won the Tokai S. at 6, then the Breeders' Gold C., but broke down in the year-end Tokyo Daishoten and was put down.
1012=A strong mare who won the English fillies' Triple Crown and held her own against older horses and colts. Unbeaten in 5, she won the 1000 Guineas, then the Oaks by 6 lengths from Triptych, later winner of 9 G1s. In summer she was 2nd in the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. and Benson & Hedges Gold C., proving herself against older horses and colts. In autumn she won the St. Leger, the 9th fillies' Triple Crown winner and first in 30 years. She retired after it to breed: 7 wins and two 2nds from 9, favorite every time. Her 2nd foal Shaima won a US G2, her 3rd, Rosefinch, the French G1 Prix Saint-Alary, and Shaima's son Shantou the St. Leger and Gran Premio di Milano - a fine broodmare.
1013=Her first graded race was the Shuka Sho in autumn at 3, where she stayed at the back, 12th behind Daiwa Scarlet. Stuck in the lower classes for 18 months, she finally reached open class in summer at 5, leading all the way in the Minami Hokkaido S. on a light weight. 9th in the Kyoto Daishoten, her first graded race in 2 years, she then set her own pace in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup and held off Buena Vista and the rest - the first Kanto-trained winner since Mejiro Dober in '99. In her last race, the Hong Kong Cup, she led boldly but tired in the straight, last of 10.

On his return he lost his rider at the start of the Antares S. and was scratched from the Tokai S., but as 8th choice he was a fine 5th in the Teio Sho - a veteran's grit.

1016=A tough mare who ran 48 times on turf and dirt. Inconsistent, she settled in open class only in autumn at 6, then the next spring won the Fukushima Himba S. with a powerful late charge - her first and last graded win.
1017=Long in the lower classes, he reached open class at 5 and was 2nd in the Tokai S., then won the Antares S. the next spring. 1018=Little noticed at first, he needed 9 starts to win. 1020=He won 3 straight to the Sapporo 2yo S., then the Kyodo News Hai and Yayoi Sho, a heavy classic favorite - but 4th in the Satsuki Sho and 7th in the Derby. In autumn he beat older horses in the Sapporo Kinen and, putting distance first, chose the Tenno Sho (Autumn) over the Kikuka Sho, a fine 3rd. His last race at 3, the Hong Kong Cup, brought a 2nd. His first crop debuted in 2011: Fine Choice won the Hakodate 2yo S. at once, followed by graded winners like Leo Active and Hakusan Moon, and in 2018 Fine Needle won both sprint G1s. A big hope among sires standing in Hidaka.
1021=Won the Flower C. but retired injured after one more race. Dam of '07 February S. winner Sunrise Bacchus (02: Hennessy).
1022=A speedster who won the Lord Derby CT. Dam of 6-time winner Golden Rodrigo (97: Rodrigo de Triano).
1023=His first 4 races on turf brought 6th at best. Switched to dirt, he emerged at once, much like crop rival Kane Hekili. In the Derby Grand Prix he was 2nd, 2.5 lengths behind Kane Hekili, but next in the Musashino S. he beat the previously unbeaten-on-dirt Kane Hekili for his first graded win. 1024=She won the Flora S. and, with 3 wins from 4, was Oaks favorite in the absence of Vodka and Daiwa Scarlet, but lost by a nose to Robe Decollete. In autumn she was 2nd in the Rose S. and 4th in the Shuka Sho; at 4, 3rd in the Fuchu Himba S. and Queen Elizabeth II Cup. With a powerful kick, always among the fastest finishers yet falling short, she was frustrating. In her last race, the '09 Fuchu Himba S., Mood Indigo outkicked her, 2nd. 1025=He won 3 straight on dirt from a maiden, then the Unicorn S. As the JRA's best 3yo dirt horse he went to the Japan Dirt Derby, but was a well-beaten 3rd to local stars Furioso and Ampersand. 1026=Poor in the Yayoi Sho and Mainichi Hai, he missed the Satsuki Sho, but won the Kyoto Shimbun Hai to make the Derby (11th). 9th in the Kikuka Sho, he returned to middle distances, 3rd in the Chunichi Shimbun Hai, then won the Chukyo Kinen and Sapporo Kinen. In '09 he was a fair 5th in Singapore's Airlines International C., and in '10 was 2nd in the Chukyo Kinen as 12th choice, a popular supporting player. He broke down in the Kinko Sho and was put down.
1027=She won her debut and the Kokura Sansai S. easily, but after an 8th in the Hanshin Sansai Himba S. she ran poorly and retired. Her first foal Alluring Voice (03: French Deputy) also won the Kokura 2yo S., and also fell away after the Hanshin JF.

1029=Slow to rise, he emerged in spring at 5, winning the Niigata Daishoten in a tight finish, then 2nd in the Epsom C. and Asahi Challenge C. After a 10th in his first G1, the Tenno Sho (Autumn), he faded, retiring after a 16th in the '09 Kyoto Kimpai.
1030=Dam of Monte Prince and Monte Fast, the 4th pair of brothers to win the Tenno Sho. Talented herself, she won 9 races, mainly in allowances, and was 2nd in the Nakayama Himba S. 2 years running. Racing until spring at 7 (old age system), she went straight to stud, breeding until 20 and leaving 9 foals. The best were her 3rd and 4th. Monte Prince starred in the classics, 2nd in the Derby and Kikuka Sho, then won the Tenno Sho (Spring) and Takarazuka Kinen at 6. Monte Fast struggled in allowances until 6, scorned as the lesser brother, but won the Tenno Sho (Spring) at 7, silencing critics. Thanks to the two, Monte Okan's place as a great mare was secure. She also produced Monte Japan, 3rd in the Tokyo Shimbun Hai and Nakayama Kinen.
1031=Winning 2 straight at 5, he was 2nd in the Kokura Kinen, then narrowly beat Erimo Harrier and others in the Asahi Challenge C. Twice 3rd in graded races after, he never won again; his last race was the Nakayama Kimpai at 10.
1032=She won the Queen C. and was 3rd in the Oka Sho, but pulled up in the Oaks. Back in the Shuka Sho, she was 17th. 1033=The 1973 Oaks winner. 1036=He needed 3 starts to win and was 2nd in the Tokyo Sports Hai 2yo S. and Kisaragi Sho, slow to win a graded race. When he won the Spring S. and went to the Satsuki Sho it was already his 10th start - heavily raced for a classic horse. But his wins in the Satsuki Sho and Derby, breaking clear from a good position, were dominant, and a Triple Crown seemed possible. Then he slipped to 2nd in the Kobe Shimbun Hai, 4th in the Kikuka Sho and 5th in the Arima Kinen, and his stock fell. At 4 he won the Osaka Hai and Tenno Sho (Spring); 2nd to Admire Moon in the Takarazuka Kinen, he regained, even raised, his reputation. An Arc plan was wiped out by an equine flu epidemic. Venting his frustration, he crushed top domestic rivals like Admire Moon and Daiwa Major in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), leaving many fans wondering "what if he had run in the Arc?" That "if" came true in '08: winless since, but strong in defeat with 2nds in the Tenno Sho (Spring) and Takarazuka Kinen, he went to the Arc, but was 10th in the rough European race. Back home he was 6th in the Japan Cup and 8th in his farewell Arima Kinen, ending 4 years of racing. He went to stud at Shadai Stallion Station from '09. His first crop included Principal S. (OP) winner Samson's Pride, and in 2015 Denko Ange won the Artemis S. as 12th choice, his first graded winner.

1038=The 1979 Oaks winner. Her daughter was Oka Sho winner Agnes Flora, whose sons were Derby winner Agnes Flight and Satsuki Sho winner Agnes Tachyon - classic winners for 3 generations. Bred to British great Mill Reef, she produced a foal injured before racing, but 5 years later, bred to Royal Ski, came Agnes Flora, founding a great family.
