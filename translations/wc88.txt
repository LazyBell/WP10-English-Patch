2070=Debuting at Funabashi with 6 wins at 3, he moved to the JRA when older. After a long slump he reached the 16-million class at 6 and blossomed in JRA-local races: the Cluster C. by 4 lengths, twice 2nd in the Empress Hai, winning from 1200m to 2100m. Tough too: 18 starts at 4, 68 in all.
2071=Winning on dirt at first, his Sadler's Wells blood came out with age on long turf trips. With stamina and a light 51kg he won the Stayers S. late at 5. Trainer Ichizo Iwamoto laughed: "The longer the better. Give me a 5000m race!"
2072=A gelding with a US-record 16 G1s, the only star by minor sire Ole Bob Bowers. Sold for just $1,100, he earned a then-world-record $6.6 million - the American dream. Poor at 2 and 3, he emerged at 4, won 3 graded races in a row at 5, then 4 turf G1s from the San Luis Rey S., Eclipse champion turf horse. At 6 he won 5 G1s on turf and dirt, Horse of the Year. At 7 he came to the 2nd Japan Cup as favorite but was 13th. At 9 he won 4 G1s, Horse of the Year again, retiring at 10 with 39 wins from 83.
2073=The American mare who won the first Japan Cup. Lightly regarded, she easily beat Japan's best like Monte Prince and Hoyo Boy, showing the gap with the world. Winning her 4th start and 3 allowances at 3, she won her first stakes, the Yerba Buena H. (G3), in May at 4. At 5, G1 tries brought a 7th, 2nd and 5th, but she then won 3 graded races in a row from a 2nd Yerba Buena H. and was invited to the Japan Cup. The Very One, the only foreign G1 winner, was favorite, followed by Monte Prince, Hoyo Boy and Gold Spencer; she was 5th choice. Waiting at the back, she swept past in the straight to win, with foreign horses taking the first 4 places - a shock for Japanese fans. She retired to breed; her sons Mairzy Dancer and High Bridge Thru were imported to Japan as sires.
2074=Winner of the 1975 English and Irish Derbies and the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. Imported to Japan as a sire, he disappointed. Unbeaten in 4 incl. the G1 Dewhurst S. at 2, he was the classic favorite. 2nd in the 2000 Guineas, he won the Irish 2000 Guineas, the Derby by 3 lengths and the Irish Derby by 2. In the King George he and older star Bustino broke clear in a fierce duel, and he won by half a length in a record 2:26.98, over 2 seconds faster - the "Race of the Century." Perhaps burnt out, he was 4th next time and retired. Siring 3 G1 winners, he came to Japan as Europe's best, but had no G1 or even graded winners here.
2075=In 1980 she became only the 2nd filly to win the Kentucky Derby, and was 2nd in the Preakness and Belmont - a match for colts. Unbeaten in 4 at 2 incl. the Demoiselle S. (G2), at 3 she was sent down the colts' classic path: 3rd in the Wood Memorial, then 6th choice in the Derby, the first filly in 21 years. Leading at the 4th turn, she held on - the first filly winner since Regret in 1915. Placing in all 3 Triple Crown races was a first for a filly. In autumn she won the Ruffian H., a 2nd G1. After 2 wins at 4 she retired. As a broodmare she had only 2 live foals, at 16 and 19; neither raced, and her line ended.
2076=Fragile, he debuted only after maidens ended at 3, then after 3 races and a year off restarted in Hokkaido. Stronger with age, he raced 12 times at 6 and reached graded level, then won a 4-way photo in the Kokura Kinen at 8.
2077=Known as a front-running sprinter, in the Centaur S. in autumn at 6 he raced from the back and closed in a then-unheard-of 33.4, passing heavy favorite Senryo Yakusha in a flash.
2078=A late-blooming Sunday Silence colt. Peaking in autumn at 6, he won the Newmarket C. (16M) by 7 lengths and the Opal S. (OP), then a wide-open Fukushima Kinen. Racing to 8, 5th in the Kokura Daishoten, he was fatally injured in the Osakajo S. (OP).
2079=By Sakura Shingeki, the "Rising Sun kamikaze" who led boldly in the first Japan Cup. A front-runner too, he loved Fukushima, winning 4 there incl. the Kabutoyama Kinen.
2080=The best Japanese runner by US sire Fappiano. Often odds-on at 2, he was 3rd to Mihono Bourbon in the Asahi Hai 3yo S., then won the Keisei Hai in a blanket finish at 3. Running well at sprints and miles, he faded after a 3rd in the Lord Derby CT at 4, retiring at 7. He often raced with his mouth open. Related to sire Gone West, he went to stud in his native US and was New York's leading sire in 2005. In 2006 he fought a fellow stallion, Gold Token, in a paddock and was badly hurt; his rival hit a tree and died.
2081=A sprinter with a strong finish who won the Chunichi Sports Sho 4yo S. at 3. A steady sprinter, short of G1 class. Full brother of 3-G1 sprinter Durandal, the "famed sword."
2082=A sprint closer at 1200-1600m, swooping from the back. At his best in the Keisei Hai AH at 5: on perfect opening-week turf, Hikoki Gumo set a hot 57.0 for 1000m, and he ran down mile stars like Black Hawk and Speed World.
2083=A front-runner at 1600-1800m on turf. In the Kisaragi Sho, his 3rd start, he dropped to 2nd at the 4th turn but fought back to win. Gritty, and popular for a quirky name inspired by his sire Keen. Later a riding horse at Baji Koen.
2084=Raced only on dirt, 8 wins from 1200m to 1800m, but 6 at 1400m or less - a speed horse. Best in the Tokyo Sprint at 7: from 3rd he burst clear, beating Gavrin and Fujino Wave.
2085=A local specialist with all 7 wins at 1800-2000m on turf, 5 at Chukyo and Kokura. All his first 6 wins came from the front, but in the Kokura Daishoten at 6 he raced wide from the outside draw, made a long run from the 3rd turn and won from behind for the first time - a stunning upset.
2086=In the Hankyu Hai, a tricky handicap where favorites had lost 18 straight, he won as favorite from the outside draw - a new sprint star. But 2 year-long breaks for leg trouble limited him to 1 graded win. Sound, he'd surely have been the "star" of his name.
2087=A classic wide-closing sprinter. In the Hankyu Hai, from gate 1, he dropped back by design, swung wide and exploded in the straight. Still in the 16-million class on a light 53kg, but 2nd in the Kitakyushu Kinen and 3rd in the Centaur S. proved it no fluke.
