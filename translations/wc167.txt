3630=A historic US champion of 1919-1920: 20 wins from 21, one crushing victory after another, once by 100 lengths. 1st in The Blood-Horse's top 100 of the 20th century. Called "Big Red" for his reddish chestnut coat, he is seen as the greatest in American racing history. At 2 he won 9 of 10, losing only the Sanford Memorial S. by half a length; the winner, Upset, is remembered as "the only horse to beat Man o' War." At 3 he won the Preakness, Withers S., Belmont S., Dwyer S. and more, many in record time - the Belmont by 20 lengths, and the Lawrence Realization S. by 100, setting an unbroken 2:40.8 record. 3 more record wins to year-end - 11 of 11 - and he retired. At stud, though his book was limited and he was champion sire only once, he sired Triple Crown winner War Admiral; War Admiral and War Relic founded his sire line. The War Relic - Intent - In Reality - Relaunch branch still holds its place in America, carrying Man o' War's blood today.
3631=Winner of the 1955 English Fillies' Triple Crown. Debuting in September at 2, she won 1 of 2. Straight to the 1000 Guineas at 3, highly rated, she won as favorite; even more fancied in the Oaks, she won by 6 lengths from Ark Royal, so easily she wasn't even blowing in the winner's photos. After the Coronation S. in summer, she rested for the St Leger; below par with a cold, she raced more patiently and won - the 8th Fillies' Triple Crown, the first in 13 years - and retired. A fine broodmare: Derby winner Charlottown and Mellay, twice champion sire in New Zealand.
3632=A US star of 1961-1964, mainly on turf, winner of 2 United Nations H. and the Washington DC International; champion turf male of 1963 at 4. Emerging at 3, he won 5 handicaps incl. the United Nations H., Trenton H. and Lexington H. At 4 he won the United Nations H. again, the Washington DC International - beating champion Kelso at his peak by half a length - the Kelly-Olympic H., Diamond State H. and more, on turf and dirt. At 5 he won 5 dirt handicaps incl. the Monmouth H., John B. Campbell H. and Widener H., and retired. A fair sire with 21 stakes winners incl. Bushongo and Mongo Queen.
3633=A French horse who won the 1948 2000 Guineas and Sussex S. At 2 he won the Champagne S. and New S. in England; at 3 he won the 2000 Guineas in a stunning record 1:35.8 and the Sussex S. - a famed speedster. He won the 7f Victoria C. at 4 and retired. A successful sire of many stakes winners incl. Milesian and Our Babu. Milesian sired Partholon, imported to Japan, where the line developed uniquely, carrying Tourbillon's line on: Partholon sired Symboli Rudolf, Mejiro Asama and more, leading Japanese racing from the 1970s to the 1990s. The male line has nearly faded, but as Mejiro McQueen is broodmare sire of Orfevre and Gold Ship, My Babu's blood lives on through dams.
3634=Raced in England, then in America from 4, where at 5 he beat Citation 4 times, all in record time. Debuting in England in May at 2, he won his 5th start; at 3 he was 9th in the 2000 Guineas, 3rd in the Derby, 8th in the St Leger. After winning the Great Foal S., he was sold to America. After about a year off, his US career began in autumn at 4; struggling at first on dirt, at 5 he hit form: the Santa Anita H. over Citation in record time, then the San Juan Capistrano H., Forty-Niners H. and Golden Gate H., beating Citation each time. Helped at first by the weights, in the Golden Gate he carried more than Citation and still won - America's best. He added the Hollywood Gold C. in record time and retired as champion handicap male. At stud, 13 stakes winners, but among Nasrullah's many fine sire sons he never stood out.
3635=The 4th English Triple Crown winner, in 1886, unbeaten in 16 - a great British horse. Debuting in October at 2, he won 3 straight incl. the Dewhurst Plate. At 3 he beat fancied Minting in the 2000 Guineas, won the Derby easily as favorite, 2 more races, and the St Leger easily - the first unbeaten Triple Crown. A breathing problem (roaring) then set in, and he retired after 3 runs at 4. At stud his fertility was low; his owner, fearing the roaring was hereditary, sold him to Argentina, then he went to America, siring few foals anywhere. Yet among his early English foals was Orme, whose line produced Teddy, founder of the Teddy line, still alive today.
3636=He ruled English sprinting in 1955 at 3, unbeaten in 8 that year, with Timeform's top rating of any age, 139 - one of Britain's best sprinters. At 2, not yet a star, he raced locally and won the Moulton S. at Newmarket, 4 wins from 7. At 3, under big weights, he won the Tetrarch S., Prince of Wales H. and Stewards' S., then the Festival S. and Gosforth Park C. Rivals so feared him that the July C. drew just 2 opponents; he won easily, then a 2-runner Diadem S. in autumn - 8 of 8 - and retired. At stud in America he sired 20 stakes winners, but was no great success, and his male line has died out.
3637=English champion 2yo of 1967 and a successful sire - the 4th straight generation of English champion sires after great-grandsire Fairway, grandsire Fair Trial and sire Petition, part of the then-dominant Fairway line. At 2 he won 3 straight incl. the Gimcrack S. and Middle Park S.; at 3 the Craven S., St. James's Palace S. and Sussex S., and he retired that year. At stud he sired Derby and Irish Derby winner Troy, Irish Derby winner English Prince and sire Pitcairn; champion sire in Britain and Ireland in 1979. Pitcairn sired Ela-Mana-Mou and others, champion sire too, and in Japan graded winners Kane Kuroshio and Soshin Hoju. No further heirs followed, and the male line has faded.
3638=Winner of the 1959 1000 Guineas and Oaks. Skipping the St Leger, she won the Yorkshire Oaks and Champion S. instead - 6 of 6 that year, rated 134 by Timeform, the best of her crop. At 4 she won the Victor Wild S., then met Derby winner Parthia in the Coronation C. as hot favorite and beat him easily - best of her generation, again rated 134, top older horse. At 5 she won 4 of 6 incl. the Coronation S. and Coronation C., and retired. As a broodmare, barren years and stillbirths left just 3 foals, all failures, but daughter Zahra built a family that produced Arc winner Zarkava 4 generations later.
3639=A legendary Australian speedster of 1929-1932, feared on his US trip as "The Red Terror" for his power and deep chestnut coat. Ordinary at 2 in 1928/29, losing often, he was transformed at 3 over middle and long trips: 4 straight from the Rosehill Guineas, then 9 straight in 1930 incl. the AJC Plate. At 4 in 1930/31 he won 14 straight incl. the Melbourne Cup; at 5, 8 more from the Underwood S. Late at 5, in 1932, he went to Mexico and won the Agua Caliente H. on his first dirt start, stunning North America. Two weeks later he suddenly died of colic and high fever; poisoning was whispered, but the truth is still unknown. His impact was such that, with a single North American start, he ranks 22nd in The Blood-Horse's top 100 of the 20th century.
3640=A great mare of 1903-1906 with 22 wins and 2 seconds from 24, winner of the Fillies' Triple Crown and, against males, the Champion S., Coronation C. and more - and an immensely influential broodmare. From her June debut at 2 she won 15 straight through the Triple Crown; 2nd in France's Prix du Conseil Municipal, she won again from her first start at 4, and retired after a 2nd in the Ascot Gold C. at 5. Winning from sprints to the 19f Jockey Club C., she had both speed and stamina. Her 10 foals did little racing, but 4 daughters bred on, and her female line produced Brigadier Gerard, Northern Taste, Luthier and many more - founder of a worldwide family.
3641=Winner of the 1963 Irish Derby, King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. and more; a fine sire, known in Japan as broodmare sire of Mill George. Little noticed until spring at 3, he emerged with a 3rd in the Derby, then won the Irish Derby and 4 straight incl. the King George and St Leger - Timeform 137. At 4 he won 2 of 4 and retired after an unplaced Arc. At stud he sired Derby winner Morston, Irish 2000 Guineas winner Ballymore, Gold Cup winner Ragstone and more. Dying at 13, he left few foals, but as broodmare sire of Japan's champion sire Mill George, he greatly influenced Japanese racing.
3642=An English star of 1966-1967 with 11 wins from 14, rated 132 by Timeform, the best 3yo of 1967. Lowly rated and not entered for the classics, he defied that with 5 straight wins at 2 incl. the Granville S., Rous Memorial S. and Clarence House S. - the best 2yo colt. At 3 he won the St. James's S., St. James's Palace S. and Sussex S., then the QEII S. by 10 lengths, and beat dual classic winner Royal Palace in the Champion S. - top of his crop - and retired. At stud: Oaks winner Polygamy, French Derby winner Roi Lear, Grand Prix de Paris winner Valiant Heart and more.
3643=A French winner of the 1963 Derby. A positive drug test afterward put the result on hold, causing a furor in both countries; a later retest proved negative, and he was confirmed as Derby winner. At 2 he won 2 of 5 in France. At 3 he won the Poulains and Prix de Guiche, then went for the Derby, winning by 6 lengths; cleared over 4 months later. In autumn he won the Prix Royal-Oak, France's final classic - Timeform 136. At 4 he won the Prix Ganay and Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud in France and the Coronation C. in England - 3 of 3 - and retired. At stud: Irish Oaks winner Olwyn, Give Thanks, Benson & Hedges Gold C. winner Relkino and more.
3644=A great English sprinter of the 1950s who won 4 sprints twice each - Cork & Orrery S., July C., King George S., Nunthorpe S. - rated 137 by Timeform. Still developing to 3, his big results were a King's Stand S. win and Nunthorpe 2nd. At 4 he blossomed, winning 6 straight incl. those 4 - top of sprinting. At 5, under heavier weights, he won all 4 again, then was 2nd conceding 32 lb in the Portland H., and retired. Not a great sire, but his few stars included sprinters Reet Lass and Village Boy; in Japan he is the third dam sire of miler Material.
