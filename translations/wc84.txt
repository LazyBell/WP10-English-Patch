1822=A Symboli Kris S dirt horse; won 3 straight, then the Hakusan Daishoten by 6 lengths on heavy going.
1942=From the first crop of Daiwa Major, speedy winner of 5 G1s incl. the Satsuki Sho and Tenno Sho (Autumn). With his sire's speed and power he won his debut and a 5-million race, the New Zealand T. by 2.5 lengths, then the NHK Mile C. by 3.5 lengths over 2yo champion Alfredo - unbeaten in 4, 3yo mile king. After a summer break he beat older horses like Eishin Flash and Real Impact in the Mainichi Okan for 5 straight. But in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), bidding to match his sire, perhaps beyond his trip, he was 5th to Eishin Flash, his first loss. At 4 he tried dirt in the February S. as favorite but was swallowed up, 15th. Perhaps shaken, he lost his 3yo brilliance, yet won the Lord Derby CT in spring at 5, his first win in 1.5 years, and the next year's Kokura Daishoten - tough at 1600-1800m. No more G1s; he retired at the end of 6 and went to stud.
1943=From Deep Impact's first crop, related to Dance in the Dark and Dance Partner. Winning his 3rd start, he led all the way in the Erica Sho, a classic hope, but missed the spring after poor trials. 4th from the front to Orfevre in the Kobe Shimbun Hai, he skipped the Kikuka Sho and beat older horses in the Hiei S. (16M). At 4 he was 5th to To the Glory in the Nikkei Shinshun Hai and 3rd in the Diamond S., won the Metropolitan S., then tracked in 2nd and burst clear to win the Meguro Kinen, his first graded title.
1944=Half-sister of Nagoya Grand Prix winner Wild Soldier and Nambu Hai runner-up Danon Come On. She won a dirt debut by 4 lengths in November at 2, but after 2 poor dirt runs switched to turf. Racing forward and overpowering rivals, she was a close 5th from the front in the Kyoto Himba S., then led all the way in the Hanshin Himba S. as 11th choice, beating Oka Sho winner Marcellina.
1945=By South Vigorous, winner of 8 dirt sprint graded races incl. the JBC Sprint; her dam Haruwaka is related to Ten Point. Based at Funabashi, she was a close 4th to Maniérisme in the Tokyo Princess Sho, 4th to JRA filly Colorful Days in the Kanto Oaks, and steady in Southern Kanto races. In the TCK Jo-o Hai at 4 she charged from wide to beat Colorful Days and others.
1946=Racing until 8, he won 3 middle-distance graded races. Often placed in graded races at 2 and 3, he won the Kyoto Kimpai early at 4. A fracture cost him a year; back at 5 he won the Tanabata Sho as favorite. At 6 he tried Hong Kong's Champions Mile (10th), and that autumn won the All Comers, held at Niigata. Another fracture meant over a year off; back at 8 in the Nakayama Kinen, he kept finishing far back and retired after a 13th in the Kinko Sho.
1947=From the 3rd crop of Dubai World Cup winner Roses in May. Needing 3 starts to win, he won the Habotan Sho on heavy ground. 5th to Gold Ship in the Kyodo News Hai, he was 9th choice in the Yayoi Sho but loved the soft going and beat the classic fancies. Soft ground made him a fancied outsider in the Satsuki Sho, 4th, then 6th in the Derby, 0.3s behind Deep Brillante. Chip fractures followed; he went straight to the Kikuka Sho and tired, 17th, and stayed out of form.
1948=3rd foal of Commercante, a graded winner in Europe and America incl. the G1 E.P. Taylor S. After a debut win he was 4th to Grandezza in a strong Sapporo 2yo S., then won the Hyakunichiso Tokubetsu and Keisei Hai. Hind-leg trouble ruled out the Satsuki Sho and Derby. 11th on his return in the St. Lite Kinen, he stayed out of form. His name means "best deal."
1949=Very big for a Deep Impact colt. Debuting late in spring at 3, he reached open class in his 4th start, then in the Radio Nikkei Sho burst clear from 5th - a late-blooming star. 2nd to Clarente in the Fuji S., he was 3rd choice for the Mile CS but lost heart after trouble in the straight, 12th. He was 3rd to Sun Carlo in the Hanshin C., but never won again.
1950=Unsold until February at 2 and bought cheaply, she was from the first crop of Takamatsunomiya Kinen winner Ore wa Matteruze. With 2 wins from 4, she sensationally ran down Oka Sho favorites like Joie de Vivre and Gentildonna in the Tulip Sho. Mishap after a workout kept her out of the Oka Sho; 7th against colts in the NHK Mile C., she was 7th in the Oaks too. 4th against older horses in the Sapporo Kinen and 5th in the Keihan Hai, she beat Mount Shasta in the Rigel S. (OP). At 1.7 in the Kyoto Himba S., she closed in 34.1 for a 2nd graded win. Then, racing in her owner's native Australia, she charged from last to win the G1 All Aged S. - a horse who truly repaid her owner.
1951=From Daiwa Major's first crop, related to stayers Admire Monarch and Hagino Real King. Winning her 5th start and the Akamatsu Sho, she was 10th to Joie de Vivre in the Hanshin JF, then won a thrilling Fairy S. by a neck.
1952=3rd foal of Whortleberry, a 3-time European graded winner incl. the Prix Jean Romanet, who ran in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. 4th to Gold Ship in the Kyodo News Hai, he was 8th in the Spring S., then switched to dirt, winning a 5-million race by 3 lengths and the Unicorn S. easily. 7th as 2nd choice in the Japan Dirt Derby and winless against older horses, he broke down in the Negishi S. at 4 and was put down.
1953=First in Japan to win 10 G1s - king of the dirt. A top-level runner from 3, winning the Leopard S. and 3rd in the Japan Cup Dirt, he fully bloomed in 2013: from the Saga Kinen (Saga) he won 5 straight to the Kashiwa Kinen and Teio Sho. 2nd in the Nambu Hai and 3rd in the Japan Cup Dirt, he won the JBC Classic and Tokyo Daishoten - 4 G1s that year. In 2014 he won the Kawasaki Kinen, then let Copano Ricky escape in the February S. but beat Belshazzar for 2nd. His camp went for the Dubai World Cup, but on the all-weather he was 16th, with stress colitis after. Back after 6 months, he was 4th in the JBC Classic as Copano Ricky led all the way, then, with Copano Ricky slow away, raced boldly to win the new G1 Champions C., his first JRA dirt G1, and the Tokyo Daishoten easily - 7 G1s. In 2015 he won the Kawasaki Kinen at 1.0, was 5th in the Dubai World Cup, and won the Teio Sho for a 9th G1. After a 3rd, 5th and 2nd, he won the 2016 Kawasaki Kinen for a 10th G1. He was to retire after the Tokyo Daishoten, but injury a month before ended it early.
1954=As his name says, an eye-opening closer. An imported horse by Belmont S. winner Empire Maker, rare in Sunday Silence's heyday. After a turf debut (3rd) he raced only on dirt and soon emerged. Favorite in the Leopard S., he was 3rd after a rough trip, then won a 16-million race with a late rush. In the Musashino S. he missed the break badly yet closed in 35.3 to win.
1955=Debuting at Sonoda in June at 2, she won all 4 there incl. a stakes, moved to Southern Kanto and beat favorite Angel Tweet in the Tokyo Princess Sho. In the JRA-local Kanto Oaks she beat JRA fillies with ease - the first local winner since Charm Asleep in 2006. Beating colts in the Kuroshio Hai and Totsuka Kinen, she won 4 straight Southern Kanto stakes. 2nd to rival Emmy's Paradise in the Rosita Kinen, she beat her in the Queen Sho but was 9th behind older mares. She stayed a steady force among older local mares.
1956=His grand-dam Happy Trails produced his dam, Kyoto Himba S. winner Happy Path, and Mile CS winner Shinko Lovely. Hyped before his debut, he won it and the Sapporo 2yo S., then the Tokyo Sports Hai 2yo S. in record time despite being blocked in the straight. At 1.3 in the Asahi Hai FS, he couldn't catch Logotype, who broke clear early, 2nd. 3rd in the Yayoi Sho and Satsuki Sho, he was 9th to Kizuna in the Derby. 7th in the Mainichi Okan with the fastest finish, he was 5th to Just a Way in the Tenno Sho (Autumn). At 4, 4th in the Tokyo Shimbun Hai and 5th in the Lord Derby CT, he died of colic.
1957=The 2013 Derby winner, half-brother of 2-classic filly Phalaenopsis, a blueblood hyped before his debut. A top classic fancy, he skipped the Satsuki Sho, winning the Mainichi Hai and Kyoto Shimbun Hai and going straight to the Derby. Favorite at 2.9 among 4 fancied horses, he swept past the field from the back to win. He then aimed for the Arc, won France's Prix Niel, and ran alongside Orfevre, 2 years older, carrying Japan's hopes, 4th - promising. At 4 he met Kikuka Sho winner Epiphaneia in the Osaka Hai; 2nd choice after 6 months off, he closed fastest from last to win clearly. Heavy favorite in the Tenno Sho (Spring), he couldn't catch Fenomeno, 4th, and a fracture kept him out for 2014. In 2015 he ran well in the Kyoto Kinen and Osaka Hai without winning, was 7th in the Tenno Sho (Spring), and retired injured to stud. Hailed as Deep Impact's heir, his first crop gave QEII Cup winner Akaito and 2-time Hanshin Daishoten winner Deep Bond, then 2-time Yasuda Kinen winner Songline; in 2024 Justin Milano won the Satsuki Sho, and in 2025 Natural Rise won 2 dirt classics (as of June 2025). He was 2024 leading sire - the "Kizuna" impact goes on.
1958=The 2013 Kikuka Sho winner, out of Cesario, winner of the Japanese and American Oaks, much noticed from his debut. Unbeaten in 3 at 2, he won the Radio Nikkei Hai 2yo S. A classic fancy, his hot temper cost him: 2nd in the Satsuki Sho and Derby. In autumn he won the Kobe Shimbun Hai, then the Kikuka Sho as heavy favorite by 5 lengths over Satono Noblesse. In 2014 the Osaka Hai was a crop summit with Derby winner Kizuna and 3-G1 mare Meisho Mambo; favorite, he was 3rd to Kizuna. Skipping the Tenno Sho (Spring), he was 4th as 2nd choice in Hong Kong's QEII Cup. 6th after a slow start in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), his shine seemed to fade, but in the Japan Cup he raced boldly forward and pulled away to win by 4 lengths for a 2nd G1. 2nd choice in an Arima Kinen with 6-G1 Gold Ship, Just a Way and queen Gentildonna, he led at the 4th turn but was passed by Gentildonna, 4th. At 5 he tried the Dubai World Cup, hated the dirt, last (9th), and retired injured to stud. Fiery and keen in races, he was brilliant in his Kikuka Sho and Japan Cup wins. His first crop gave unbeaten fillies' Triple Crown winner Daring Tact and Kikuka Sho runner-up Aristoteles; the next, Efforia, who won the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Arima Kinen at 3 like his grandsire. In 2024 he sired 4 spring G1 winners: Stellenbosch, Ten Happy Rose, Danon Decile and Blow the Horn. Danon Decile won the Derby, avenging his sire, and the 2025 Dubai Sheema Classic. 3rd on the 2024 sire list, he has shown his class at stud.
1959=Half-brother of Fillies' Revue 3rd Prenotato. He won the Niigata 2yo S. from near last with a finish under 34s, then was beaten badly in the Tokyo Sports Hai 2yo S. He keeps closing from last, without results.
1960=Named "stalk and ray," he swept past the leaders in the Hakodate 2yo S. to be his crop's first graded winner. Then 10th in the Fantasy S. and 15th in the Hanshin JF.
1961=Muscular and superb in work, hailed as the best of the Meiner team, entered for the next year's English Derby before he debuted. He won his debut at 1.4 and the Kokura 2yo S., then was 3rd in the Daily Hai 2yo S., 8th from the front in the Keio Hai 2yo S. and last (16th) in the Asahi Hai FS. Seemingly in a mental slump, he kept losing badly, but won the Baden-Baden C. (OP) from midfield in summer at 3.
1962=A gelding twice named US Horse of the Year on turf in dirt-led America - the "Titan of the Turf." Racing mainly on dirt until 4, he won the G1 Clark H. From summer at 5 he raced only on turf, winning the Woodbine Mile, Shadwell Turf Mile and Breeders' Cup Mile - the first turf miler named Horse of the Year. At 6 he won 4 turf G1s incl. a 2nd BC Mile, Horse of the Year again. At 7 he won 3 G1s from the Maker's 46 Mile, but a fracture just before ended his bid for 3 BC Miles like Goldikova. He never returned, retiring at 8. Rated 129 lbs, top US-trained horse in the 2012 and 2013 World Thoroughbred Rankings.
1963=His name comes from his sire Macho Uno, the song "Macho Man" and his big frame; "Mucho" means "much" - "a very macho man." The best son of Macho Uno, a rare Himyar-line sire. Struggling until 3, he won a G2 but was 3rd in the Kentucky Derby, 6th in the Preakness and 7th in the Belmont. At 4 he won 2 G2s and was 2nd in the Woodward S. and Breeders' Cup Classic. At 5 he won the Awesome Again S., his first G1, then the BC Classic as 2nd choice, holding off Will Take Charge. He missed the Eclipse Award but won "Moment of the Year" and the Secretariat Vox Populi Award, a fan idol across the US. After 2 races at 6 he retired, going to stud in 2015 and siring G1 winner Mucho Gusto.
1964=By Black Tide, full brother of Deep Impact. Winning his 2nd start, he boldly tried the Daily Hai 2yo S. and led all the way as 6th choice under Ikezoe. Since, he has shown a sharp finish from behind.
1965=By Heart's Cry, winner of the Dubai Sheema Classic and Arima Kinen. After a debut win she closed from last for 4th in the Daily Hai 2yo S., then won the new Artemis S. as favorite, its first winner. 2nd was Ayusan - a Deep Impact foal chasing a Heart's Cry foal, like that old Arima Kinen.
1966=As hyped, he won his debut and the Chukyo 2yo S. from the front. 5th choice in the Keio Hai 2yo S., doubted on Tokyo's long straight, he won from 2nd in race-record time. In the Asahi Hai FS, billed as a clash with unbeaten Kodino, he was caught in a hot pace, 8th. At 3 he won the Shinzan Kinen and NZT narrowly, but was 7th in the NHK Mile C. Switching to dirt, he won the Shimotsuki S. (OP), was 5th as heavy favorite in the Hyogo Gold T. and last in the February S. Back on turf at 15th choice in the Takamatsunomiya Kinen, he held 4th from the front on heavy ground.
1967=Winning her debut with the fastest finish, she was 9th choice in the Niigata 2yo S. after gaining 20kg, yet 3rd. After a 7th in the Daily Hai 2yo S., she raced forward to win the Fantasy S. by 3 lengths. 3rd choice in the Hanshin JF, she was 17th, and 9th in the Oka Sho. Switched to dirt in the Tango S. (OP), 4th choice, she surged from the back to win by 4 lengths, then was 2nd as favorite to Best Warrior in the Unicorn S.
1968=The 2013 Satsuki Sho winner, from the 2nd crop of Lohengrin, a miler in Japan, Europe and Hong Kong. After a tight debut win in June at 2, he was 4th, 3rd and 4th, then won the Begonia Sho in record time under Mirco Demuro. At 34.5 in the Asahi Hai FS, he led early in the straight and held off hot favorite Kodino by a neck - the G1 his sire never won - and was champion 2yo colt. At 3 he won the Spring S. as favorite, then the Satsuki Sho, beating Epiphaneia, Kodino and others. Near-joint favorite with Kizuna in the Derby, he was 5th, perhaps beyond his trip. He raced at mile-to-middle trips, 6th in the Dubai Duty Free at 4. Winless for 2 years, he won the Yasuda Kinen at 6 as 8th choice for a 3rd G1, then was 5th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Hong Kong Mile. The next year he led in the Yasuda Kinen but was caught near the line by Satono Aladdin, 2nd, and retired.
1969=By South Vigorous, ex-king of dirt sprints and sire of Namura Titan and Love Me Chan. Based in Hokkaido, she won the Edelweiss Sho as 5th choice, then beat one of 3 JRA fancies, Keiai Leone, for 3rd in the Hyogo Junior GP.
