3065=Winner of the 1958 Tenno Sho (Spring) and Arima Kinen. A half-brother to Derby winner O Kitsu, 2 years older, he was hyped from debut and won a newcomer race as favorite. Poor in spring at 4 (old age system) - 5th NHK Hai, 11th Derby - he won the Nakayama 4yo S. (now Radio Nikkei Sho) and Hakodate Kinen in summer, then was 2nd to Rhapsody in the Kikuka Sho and to Hakuchikara in the Arima Kinen. At 5 he won the Kimpai, the Tenno Sho (Spring) as 2nd choice, and the Arima Kinen by 4 lengths - Horse of the Year. Injured in the US at 6, he lost a year; back at 7 he lost 3 and came home. Getting few mares as a homebred sire, he returned to racing at 10 in Hokkaido as Onward Kei, winning 2 of 5, then went back to stud and sired Asahi Hai 3yo S. winner Onward Guy and Stayers S. winner Ziegar.
3066=Winner of the 1956 Derby and the 1957 Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Arima Kinen. He then went abroad - a big undertaking then - and won a US graded race, a first for a Japan-trained horse. Debuting in autumn at 3 (old age system), he won 5 straight, then was 2nd to Kitano O in the Asahi Hai 3yo S., starting their rivalry. At 4 he beat Kitano O in the Derby but trailed him in the Satsuki Sho, Kikuka Sho and Nakayama Grand Prix (now Arima Kinen). At 5 he beat Kitano O in both Meguro Kinen, then won the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Arima Kinen - Horse of the Year, top in name and fact. At 6 he sought a new challenge in the US; winless at first as he adjusted, he won the Washington's Birthday H. at 7 in his 11th US start - the first overseas graded win for a Japan-trained horse, a landmark for Japanese racing. 1 win from 17 in the US, he retired. Little used at stud in Japan, he was donated to India for his later years.
3067=Winner of the 1955 Kikuka Sho and the 1956 Tenno Sho (Spring) and Nakayama Grand Prix (now Arima Kinen). Hoped to succeed great homebred sire Kumohata, he sired only a few graded winners. Debuting in October at 3 (old age system), he won 4 straight incl. the Asahi Hai 3yo S. Classic favorite, he missed the spring through injury, then returned to win the Kikuka Sho by 10 lengths. At 5 he won the Tenno Sho (Spring), showing he was number one. The all-star Nakayama Grand Prix began that year; 2nd in the fan vote, in a field where 8 of 12 were Big Eight winners, he won by 3.5 lengths. After it he retired with the first-ever retirement ceremony in Japanese racing. Unanimous Horse of the Year, he entered the JRA Hall of Fame in 1990.
3068=One of the best from the final crop of English import Theft. A poor sire, he lives on through dams such as Asama Yuri, Mejiro McQueen's third dam. On the track he won the Satsuki Sho and Derby; a sure Triple Crown winner, the first since St Lite, he was beaten into 2nd in the Kikuka Sho by Hakuryo, whom he had always beaten. Older, he won the Hanshin Kinen in record time, but was 2nd to Hakuryo again in the Tenno Sho (Spring) and retired that year. Hoped to succeed his sire, he had few good foals, far behind Hakuryo, who sired top horses like Caesar and Yamano O. Still, through Asama Yuri's thriving female line, his name is firmly in Japanese racing history.
3069=A mare who ran in 4 straight QEII Cups (G1) from 2022 to 2025, always near the top. At 3 she won the Fairy S. (G3), catching later 2-classic filly Stars on Earth, but was beaten badly in all 3 classics (16th, 11th, 10th). 12th choice in the QEII Cup, she closed sharply from the back to dead-heat for 2nd with Win Marilyn. At 4, 4th in the Nikkei Sho, 3rd in the Fuchu Himba S., 4th in the QEII Cup. At 5 never in the top 5, best 6th in the QEII Cup. At 6, after a close 4th in the Ireland T., she was 3rd as 9th choice in the QEII Cup - the race's true regular.
3070=A great mare who founded one of Japan's leading female lines. With half-sisters Tetsuno Homare and Kamiyamato she forms the Craig Dhu Lochi family; the branch through prolific Star Roch is called the "Star Roch line." Her own foals were poor, but later generations include Hard Barge, Sakura Yutaka O, Sakura Star O and Winning Ticket. Unsold at auction and allotted as a lottery horse, she drew little attention, but defied that: 3rd in the Oka Sho, she won the Oaks as 9th choice with a brilliant late run - the first lottery horse to win a Big Eight race. Without further graded wins, she won open and handicap races and was invited into the Arima Kinen; against 2-classic winner Kodama and other Big Eight winners, she led all the way to win as 9th choice. Perhaps paying for it, she lost 5 straight at 5, then won the Keio Hai Autumn H.; dislocating a joint in the Mainichi Okan, she was pulled up and retired.
3071=Foundation mare of one of Mejiro Farm's main female lines, with stars like Mejiro Gekko, Mejiro Dober and Mejiro Monterey, central to the farm's success. Winning 7 straight from her 2nd start, she beat leading colts in the Asahi Hai 3yo S. (old age system). At 4, 3rd in the Oka Sho and 2nd in the Oaks, no classic, but she won the 4yo Himba Tokubetsu and Hakodate Kinen, and retired in spring at 5. Her first foal was Yayoi Sho and Spring S. winner Mejiro Gekko; her daughters Mejiro Kannon, Mejiro Nagasaki and Mejiro Quincy spread her line. Mejiro Nagasaki's granddaughter Mejiro Dober won 4 G1s, and her descendants Shonan Lagoon and Hoo Ixel carry Mejiro Bosatsu's blood today.
3072=The 1965 Oaks winner, by Solonaway, then fighting for the sire title; with his Oka Sho winner Hatsuyuki and Derby winner Keystone, his crop swept the scene. She broke down fatally in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) at 5 (old age system). Winning 3 straight from her 2nd start, she was 3rd to Eight Crown in the Hanshin 3yo S. At 4, 2nd and 1st in open races, she was 12th in the Oka Sho, her first heavy loss. 4th in the 4yo Himba Tokubetsu, she rallied in the Oaks: with Hatsuyuki out, as 4th choice she won a tight finish. She also won the Nihon Tanpa Sho (now Radio Nikkei Sho). In autumn, 2nd in the Queen S., 12th in the St. Lite Kinen, but champion 4yo filly. At 5 she won only an open race; 4th in the autumn Meguro Kinen, she was pulled up in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and put down.
3073=The 1972 Oka Sho winner, who also won that year's Victoria C. (forerunner of the QEII Cup) - only 4 wins from 32, but 2 big ones. Winless in 6 at 3 (old age system), she won her 2nd start at 4, needing 4 more to pass the next class. Stepping up, 4th in the Hanshin 4yo Himba Tokubetsu, she earned an Oka Sho place; 8th choice on her thin record, she closed from the back to win - a classic. Not entered for the Oaks, she rested; back in autumn she was unplaced in the Sapphire S., Kobe Shimbun Hai and Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu, 5th choice in the Victoria C. She led early and held on by a neck. Then 12 straight losses, retiring at the end of 5. As a broodmare, only 2 open winners.
3074=A 1973 4yo filly star (old age system), winner of the Oka Sho and Victoria C. (forerunner of the QEII Cup), 2nd in the Oaks. After a 4th on debut she was never out of the top 3, with 3 wins by January at 4. She beat classic favorite Kishu Laurel in the Hanshin 4yo Himba Tokubetsu, then, as Oka Sho favorite, beat her again - a Big Eight win. 2nd to Nasuno Chigusa in the Oaks. 8th in the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu, her first time unplaced, she avenged Oaks winner Nasuno Chigusa in the Victoria C. - top of her crop. The only filly in the Arima Kinen, she was a fine 2nd. Older, she declined, losing 7 straight without a top-5, and retired. Of her 11 foals, Espal was 4th in the QEII Cup.
3075=Debuting in September at 2, she won a maiden in January at 3, then quickly a 1-win race. 4th choice in her first graded race, the Kanto Oaks, she sat 2nd, led in the straight and won easily, then won the Breeders' Gold C. the same way - 3 straight. In autumn, 2nd in the JBC Ladies' Classic and Queen Sho; at 4 she won the TCK Jo-o Hai and Empress Hai, then 2nd again in the JBC Ladies' Classic. At 5, 2nd in the Kawasaki Kinen and 4th in the Teio Sho, then 2nd in the JBC Ladies' Classic for a 3rd straight year - a rare record.
3076=Winning his debut, 6th in the Hopeful S., at 3 he won the Kisaragi Sho. Sharing favoritism with Strong Will and Dantes View, he ran last of the three, unleashed a powerful finish and just caught leader Dantes View. Then 10th in the Yayoi Sho Deep Impact Kinen, 12th in the Satsuki Sho, 13th in the Derby. In autumn he won the Andromeda S. (L) and was 2nd in the Chunichi Shimbun Hai.
3077=Beaten badly on a turf debut, she was steady on dirt, unplaced only once, and reached the 3-win class in autumn at 4. Placing in graded races (4th Miyako S., 3rd Queen Sho), she won the Ladies' Prelude in autumn at 5 as 2nd choice, catching favorite Shonan Nadeshiko. 5th in the JBC Ladies' Classic.
3078=He won his debut in July at 2 by 2 lengths from up front, then the Kokura 2yo S. stunningly: very slow away and alone at the back, he caught the field at the 4th turn, then surged past them all as if they were standing still. Favorite in the Keio Hai 2yo S., he was 14th.
3079=A closer with a sharp finish from the back. 4th on debut in a tight finish, she won a maiden easily from up front, then the Sapporo 2yo S. as favorite, catching Do Eyes from midfield to win by a length. In the Hanshin JF she flew from last but was 6th. A classic hope at 3, she was 15th as Tulip Sho favorite and 14th in the Oka Sho. Then, 15th choice in the Oaks, she closed sharply from the back for 3rd. She won the Queen S. as favorite in summer and was 4th in the Shuka Sho - a fine revival.
3080=The 2022 champion 2yo, unbeaten in 3 incl. the Asahi Hai FS. He led on debut and drew away easily, then as 2nd choice in the Saudi Arabia Royal C., sat 2nd, surged from 200m out after runaway leader Granite, and won by 1 1/4 lengths. In the Asahi Hai FS he led in the straight and held off Danon Touchdown by a neck - a G1 already. Expected to lead the milers at 3, he was 7th as hot NZT favorite, then 12th in the NHK Mile C., 18th in the Yasuda Kinen, 13th in the Centaur S., 12th in the Sprinters S. - his juvenile sparkle gone.
3081=He shot to fame winning the 2022 Unicorn S. (G3), a huge upset with a 620,000 yen trifecta. In his first graded race after a 1-win race, 7th choice, he raced forward and won a scramble by a neck, with 9th choice Sekifu 2nd and 8th choice Battle Cry 3rd. 2nd in the Japan Dirt Derby, 3rd in the JBC Classic, he won the Nagoya GP - a top dirt horse. Winless from 4, he won the Elm S. (G3) in summer at 5.
3082=Debuting in August at 2, she won a maiden only in June at 3, her 10th start, and spent nearly 2 years in allowances, reaching the 3-win class at 5. Unfancied in her first graded race, she won the Hanshin Himba S. Last (18th) in the Victoria Mile, then unplaced (12th Queen S., 12th Keeneland C., 12th Sprinters S., 16th Swan S., 14th Galaxy S.); after a 5th in the Kyoto Himba S. she retired to breed.
