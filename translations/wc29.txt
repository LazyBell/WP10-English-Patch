280=He won the Asahi Hai Sansai S., then the Arlington C. and the NZT Yonsai S. in spring at 3. Already hailed as "the best miler," he missed the NHK Mile C. through injury. After his break he ran poorly and was written off, but at 4 he won the Yonago S., his first win in a long time, and came back to life, winning the Kitakyushu Kinen and the Mainichi Okan. At year's end he went to Hong Kong and won the Hong Kong Mile, then returned the next April to win the QE2 Cup - and won it again the following year, three straight years of G1 wins abroad. Oddly, he never won any of his 8 tries at Japan's older-horse G1s. Much was hoped of him at stud, but no standout offspring have appeared so far.
281=The horse who rewrote Japanese thoroughbred bloodlines from the ground up. He produced many heirs such as Special Week and Deep Impact, threatening to paint all Japanese pedigrees in Sunday Silence colors. He was also a superb racehorse, winning the Kentucky Derby and Preakness S. He lost the Belmont S. to rival Easy Goer, but won the Breeders' Cup Classic in autumn and was Eclipse Horse of the Year. After retiring at 4 he was little valued for his pedigree in America, so he was exported to Japan for stud. His first crop gave Fuji Kiseki and Genuine, the next Bubble Gum Fellow and Dance in the Dark - a flood of G1 winners. He kept siring G1 winners every year until his last, sparking a "Sunday Silence storm" that swept the JRA's G1s. His sire sons - Fuji Kiseki, Dance in the Dark, Stay Gold, Special Week, Agnes Tachyon, Heart's Cry, Deep Impact and more - are an elite group, and successful grandchildren and great-grandchildren are countless. His descendants have also spread abroad, and his influence now reaches around the world.
282=Sire of Triple Crown winner Narita Brian, Mayano Top Gun, Phalaenopsis, Tanino Gimlet and more, he hugely reshaped Japanese bloodlines from the 1990s into the 2000s, one of the "big three" sires with Sunday Silence and Tony Bin. Originally an American racehorse, he had no notable record beyond winning the Florida Derby, and went to stud in Japan on the strength of his pedigree. At the time Real Shadai's offspring were thriving in Japan, and Brian's Time was chosen as being of the same line. His first crop gave Narita Brian and Chokai Carol, the next Mayano Top Gun, and two years later Sunny Brian and Silk Justice - G1 winners one after another. He quickly became a top sire, at one point rivaling Sunday Silence. But his stars didn't succeed at stud, and apart from Tanino Gimlet siring Vodka, no notable descendants have emerged. Furioso, a dirt star, carries hopes as his heir.
283=A leading light of Sunday Silence's first crop, the force that swept Japanese racing. Fickle - it was said "he won't run if it's windy" - he was loved by many fans as a charming character. His moodiness showed from his youth, and he often slipped up early on. In the Wakaba S. he crossed the line 2nd, 5 lengths back, winning only after first-past-the-post Louisiana Boy was demoted, so he was only 3rd choice for the Satsuki Sho - but he held off the charging Tayasu Tsuyoshi, another Sunday Silence son, to win clearly. His explosive power at middle distances and shorter was real: besides the Satsuki Sho, he won the Mile Championship as an older horse. He stood at stud in Japan and Oceania, siring graded winners in the JRA and G1 winners in Oceania.
284=With just 4 starts and 4 wins, he swept Europe's big three: the Derby, the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. and the Arc. From Nijinsky's last crop, he is the only offspring to win the Arc, which Nijinsky never did. He won a listed race on debut, then after over six months off went straight into the Derby. With just one start, he was only 6th choice, but he shot from the back like an arrow to win. In the King George he was favorite and won a tight finish by a neck. He went straight to the Arc and held off Freedom Cry's charge in the straight to complete the European Triple Crown unbeaten. With a short career and unspectacular wins, he wasn't named Horse of the Year and was rated a modest 130 lbs in the International Classification, but Britain's Timeform said "it is impossible to rate this horse's greatness." He retired right after the Arc, stood one year in Europe, then was imported to Japan. But his offspring fell short of hopes, producing only the occasional graded winner.
285=Named in hope of winning the 2000 Dubai World Cup, he did just that. He won his first 3 starts with overwhelming power, but in the Derby, partly because the trip was too long, he suffered the only defeat of his career. Then, focused on the mile to middle distances, he romped in European mile G1s like the Prix Jacques le Marois and the Queen Elizabeth II S. At 4 he had a dirt prep before the Dubai World Cup. Overwhelming favorite, he led all the way and kept pulling away - living up to expectations and prompting Dettori to call him "the best horse I've ever ridden." Back on turf, he romped in the Prince of Wales's S., then retired through injury. He died of illness after just one crop, but it included Dubawi, winner of the Prix Jacques le Marois and a hugely successful sire.
286=So strong that some even complained "Taiki Shuttle's races are boring." He debuted late, in spring at 3, then won 3 straight. He was beaten a neck into 2nd in the Bodaiju S., but won the Unicorn S. for his first graded title. His run continued, winning the Mile Championship and the Sprinters S. at 3. Named champion sprinter, he made it clear no one in Japan could match him at short distances. As an older horse he kept rolling, winning the Yasuda Kinen for 3 straight sprint-mile G1s. Then he set off for Europe. With Seeking the Pearl having already won a G1 abroad that year, he was a confident favorite for the Prix Jacques le Marois, and beat the world's fast horses clearly to become the world's mile king in name and fact. Back home he won the Mile Championship again. His farewell, the Sprinters S., ended in a shock 3rd, but he was the undisputed Horse of the Year. At stud he sired G1 winners like Meisho Bowler, Summer Wind and Win Kluger. He retired from stud in 2017 and died of heart failure due to old age in 2022. His stall showed no sign of struggle; his end is thought to have been peaceful.
