At 4 she was 11th in the Oka Sho, but a fine 5th to Toko Elsa as 16th choice in the Oaks, and 2nd to Toko Elsa in the Victoria C. (forerunner of the Queen Elizabeth Cup). 1092=She won the Niigata Kinen and Himba Tokyo Times Hai (now Fuchu Himba S.) in 1984, the first year of graded races. At 4 (old system) she won 3 allowance races to reach open class but kept falling short; dropped back the next year, she won again, then both graded races in a row. As a dam she produced Broad Mind, JRA champion jumper twice. Her full sister Dyna Shoot is granddam of Rhein Kraft.
1099=A late-maturing stayer carrying Northern Taste's blood. Long in the lower classes, he bloomed in autumn at 4, winning the Meguro Kinen (Autumn) and Arima Kinen. The next year he fell short - 2nd in the Tenno Sho (Spring), 5th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), 2nd in the Arima Kinen - but at 6 he won the Tenno Sho (Spring). He shone even more at stud: son Mejiro Ryan, and grandchildren Mejiro Bright and Mejiro Dober, built a G1 dynasty - a hope for Japan-bred sires as imports grew stronger.
1108=A leading contender in the 1981 fillies' classics. She won the Flower C., was 5th in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu and 4th in the Oaks, won the Radio Tampa Sho, and was 4th in the Himba Tokyo Times Hai and Queen Elizabeth Cup. At 5 she beat colts in the Kimpai (East) and Nakayama Kinen, and after 10 months off won the Nakayama Kinen again at 6. Apart from the Arima Kinen (10th), always in the top five. As a dam she produced Mother Tosho.
1111=5th on debut at 2, he won 3 straight incl. the Chukyo Sansai S. Hopes were high, but after a 7th in an open race at 3, he was switched to sprints in view of his pedigree. At 4 he won 3 races at 1400m or less, and at 5 won the Hankyu Hai clearly - his first meeting with lifelong rival Nihon Pillow Winner, who beat him into 2nd in the CBC Sho after a 6-month break. At 6, graded races arrived and the JRA's sprint division was organized, a turning point for sprinters long overshadowed by middle-distance stars. When Nihon Pillow Winner, who had beaten him again in the Yodo Tankyori S. (OP), was injured, Happy Progress had no rival, winning 3 straight graded races up to the Yasuda Kinen. In autumn, with Nihon Pillow Winner back, he lost the Swan S. and Mile Championship to him, but beat Oka Sho winner Shadai Sophia in the year-end CBC Sho, and the pioneer of the new sprint era retired after that win.
1112=4th on debut, he won a maiden in his 4th start, then often fell short and missed the classics. Leg trouble kept him in his class at 4, and next to full brother Monte Prince, "Emperor of the Sun," winner of the Tenno Sho (Spring) and Takarazuka Kinen, Monte Fast came to be called "the lesser brother." But at 5 he was 3rd in the Hakodate Kinen, 4th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), then won the Meguro Kinen, his first graded win. In spring at 6, after a 4th in the Nikkei Sho, he went to the Tenno Sho (Spring). Favorite was Hollyskey, Kikuka Sho winner a year younger, at 1.5. Monte Fast was only 6th choice, but sat in midfield and held off Hollyskey's late charge to win - a historic brothers' Tenno Sho double. No one called him "the lesser brother" again.
1113=Racing until 8 (old age system), he won 2 graded races, the February H. (then a G3 handicap) and the Winter S. (now Tokai S.). At 4 he won 4 straight to reach open class, and at 5 was 2nd in both the Kabutoyama Kinen and Negishi S., good on turf or dirt. He won the year-end Winter S., then into 6 won the Alexandrite S. (OP), February H. and Keiyo S. (OP). Then came heavy weights, and his only other win was the Times Hai (OP) at 7. He retired after a 6th in the February H. at 8. At stud he sired Winter S. winner Laurier Andre and others.
1114=Winning his first 3, at 4 (old age system) he was 3rd in the Shinzan Kinen and Kisaragi Sho, then won the Mainichi Hai. But in the classics he was 5th in the Satsuki Sho and 14th in the NHK Hai. Racing mainly at middle distances, he was 3rd in the Keihan Hai and Kimpai (West), then won the Times Hai on dirt in summer. In autumn he won the Kyoto Kinen and Keihan Hai as an outsider, and at 6 won the Nikkei Shinshun Hai (then on dirt) and retired. At stud he had very few foals and none of note.
1115=Debuting at Funabashi, he won the Teio Sho at 8 (old age system). He only reached big races at 7: 11th in the Kawasaki Kinen, 6th in the Teio Sho, 9th in the Tokyo Daishoten. At 8 he improved, 5th in the Kawasaki Kinen and 2nd in the Diolite Kinen, then in the Teio Sho beat local stars Countess Up and Rocky Tiger and JRA invitee Rikisan Power as 10th choice. He won the Tokyo Kinen that autumn and retired after racing until spring at 10.
1116=Winner of the 1981 Asahi Hai Sansai S. After 2 straight wins and a 2nd in the Keisei Hai Sansai S., he was favorite for the Asahi Hai and beat Tosho Pegasus by a head, champion 2yo colt. Then injury sidelined him for 2 years. Back, he was 2nd in the Lord Derby Challenge T. and Tokyo Shimbun Hai, showing his class, and retired at 5.
1117=From Maruzensky's first crop. Debuting late, in January at 3, he won a dirt maiden by 9 lengths, and ended the spring with a 9th in the Derby. After one allowance race, he went to the Kikuka Sho as 9th choice, took the lead in the straight off Hagino Kamui O's fast pace and held on in a record 3:05.4, giving Maruzensky - barred from the classics himself - a classic win. He never won another big race, but was 2nd and 3rd in the Tenno Sho (Spring) and 3rd in a nose-and-nose finish with Minagawa Manna and Amber Shadai in the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai - a true stayer.
1118=The middle-distance king who became the first Japan-trained winner of the Japan Cup (4th running), beating crop-mate Triple Crown winner Mr. C.B., "absolute Emperor" Symboli Rudolf and world stars like Bedtime. He won his debut over 1200m at Hanshin by 8 lengths, a classic hope. With 2 more wins he headed east as Kansai's ace alongside Mejiro Monsnee, but lost to Mr. C.B.: 11th in the Satsuki Sho, 6th in the Derby. In autumn he was 2nd in the Kobe Shimbun Hai, then beat C.B. in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai for revenge. But as 2nd choice in the Kikuka Sho he was second-last of 21 (C.B. won the Triple Crown). At 4 he came good, winning the Osaka Hai, Keihan Hai and Takarazuka Kinen - a G1 winner. He beat C.B. again in the Mainichi Okan, but was 5th behind him in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), sealing his image as strong in preps, weak on the day. Then in the Japan Cup he boldly ran away and held on - his first win in a G1 that C.B. also ran in.
1119=He shocked fans by running down "absolute Emperor" Symboli Rudolf to win the 92nd Tenno Sho (Autumn) in 1985. After winning his debut he lost race after race and couldn't even run in the classics, won by Mr. C.B. and Mejiro Monsnee. At 4 he ran well in allowances, and only at 5 raced regularly in open and graded races. In the Tenno Sho (Autumn) at 5, with 6 of his wins on dirt, he was 13th choice of 17. But from last at the final turn he unleashed a furious charge, running down overwhelming favorite Rudolf in record time - the only horse ever to do so. No match for Rudolf in the Japan Cup and Arima Kinen, at 6 he won the Tokyo Shimbun Hai, was 4th in the Keio Hai Spring C., and won the Yasuda Kinen, his 2nd G1. He went to France for the Jacques le Marois and Moulin, beaten badly in both. Back home he was 4th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and 10th in the Japan Cup, but in his final race, the Arima Kinen, ran 2nd as 11th choice, showing a G1 winner's pride. At stud he sired Osumi Diner, who won a Hokkaido graded race at 13 - Japan's oldest graded winner.
1120=A mare from the crop of Mr. C.B. and Katsuragi Ace. No G1 win, but very consistent in many graded races, rarely unplaced. At 5 she won the Kokura Daishoten, Kita-Kyushu Kinen and Hanshin Himba Tokubetsu, champion older mare. She ran in the Queen Elizabeth Cup and twice in the Takarazuka Kinen, making the top five every time.
1121=A forerunner of the "Sakura stable" boom of the mid-1980s, followed by Sakura Toko a year later, Sakura Yutaka O and Sakura Sunny O 2 years later, Sakura Chiyono O 4 years later and Sakura Hokuto O 5 years later. A late bloomer, his first graded win was the American JCC early at 6 (old age system). He was 2nd in the Tenno Sho (Spring) and Takarazuka Kinen, strong over long trips. He sired no notable runners.
1122=In 1983, as Mr. C.B. became the 3rd Triple Crown winner, Southern Kanto racing had one too: San O I. He won the Haneda Hai by 4 lengths and Tokyo Derby by 7, then the Tokyo Okansho by 1.5 lengths as favorite; Celebration was 2nd in all three. Older, he moved to the JRA, 3rd in the Yasuda Kinen, showing aptitude for turf. In autumn he met Mr. C.B. in the Mainichi Okan and Tenno Sho (Autumn), 3rd and 6th. He returned to local racing and retired at the end of 7 (old age system). He left only 3 crops, incl. Kasamatsu star Tomishino Porunga.
1123=By Northern Taste out of a daughter of US great sire Bold Ruler. Her graceful bearing had people calling her "a million-dollar horse" from a young age, and she won her debut in record time. After 2 wins from 3 at 2, she was 3rd in a prep, then 3rd choice for the Oka Sho behind Shadai Farm stablemate Dyna Carle, and won from a good position. In the winner's circle she posed for photos with 3rd-placed Dyna Carle. For the 2nd classic, her camp surprised all by choosing the Derby over the Oaks, but she was 17th behind future Triple Crown winner Mr. C.B. In autumn, despite distance doubts, she was 2nd to Long Grace in the Queen Elizabeth Cup. At 4 she was repelled by G1 colts Happy Progress and Nihon Pillow Winner and slumped badly. Late in the year she showed signs of recovery, and at 5 won the Coral S. (OP), her first win in 18 months, then the Hankyu Hai by 2.5 lengths under top weight 56.5kg. In autumn at 5 she was favorite for the Swan S., her Mile CS prep, fully recovered - but fell in the race and had to be put down.
1124=A stayer of the 1980s. At 4 (old age system) he ran above his class in the Kikuka Sho, 3rd to Mr. C.B., and won the Hanshin Daishoten (then in December), his first open win. The next year graded races began, and he won the Hanshin Daishoten again as a G2, his first graded title, and later a 3rd time. He did little at stud, but his brothers include Kyoto Kinen winner Shin Chest and 2-time graded winner T.M. Jumbo.
1125=A fine supporting actor of Mr. C.B.'s crop. At 3 he won the Kobe Shimbun Hai as 7th choice, then often fell short. In autumn at 4 he won the Kyoto Daishoten by 4 lengths, but as 4th choice in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) was 7th behind C.B. His big chance came in the Takarazuka Kinen at 5: Emperor Symboli Rudolf's entry shrank the field to 11, but on the day Rudolf was scratched with leg trouble. 2nd choice, Suzuka Koban held off Tenno Sho (Spring) runner-up Sakura Gaisen to win his long-sought G1. He won the next Kyoto Daishoten and ran well in graded races, but that was his only G1.
1126=Based at Oi, he ran in the JRA's All Comers at 7 and 8 (old age system), 3rd as favorite at 7. Debuting late at 3, he was 6th in the Tokyo Okansho, a Southern Kanto classic, at 4, and won the Tokyo Daishoten at 5. At 6 he was 2nd in the Kawasaki Kinen, 4th in the Teio Sho and 3rd in the Tokyo Daishoten, and 2nd to Rocky Tiger in the Tokyo Kinen, missing a Japan Cup spot. 1127=At 4 (old age system) he ran 17 times, won the Lord Derby Challenge T., and thrilled fans with a 2nd as an outsider in the Arima Kinen. In 1984, the first year of graded races, he won the Nakayama Kinen. 17th as 2nd choice in the Tenno Sho (Spring), he was 2nd to Mr. C.B. in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), then over 2000m for the first time. 1128=Racing steadily from 3 (old age system) to 8, he won a graded race 6 years running: the Keisei Hai Sansai S. at 3, Hakodate Kinen at 4, Nakayama Kimpai at 5, Tokyo Shimbun Hai at 6, Asahi Challenge C. at 7 and Kyoto Kimpai at 8. From 4 he won just once a year, suddenly running big between poor runs - "once-a-year Dokan Yashima." Winning both the East and West Kimpai is another rare feat. At stud he sired Southern Kanto runner Kinko Batora and others, and was retired in 1995.
1129=A rare member of the Blandford line, from Mr. C.B.'s crop. Injury kept him out of the classics, but older he won the Nikkei Sho (2500m) and Sekiya Kinen (1600m), and was 5th in the Tenno Sho (Spring) (3200m) - good at any distance. Of about 70 foals, only Utsunomiya's Shimotsuke Oaks winner Dokan Satsuki stood out, and his male line died out.
1130=Mr. C.B.'s chief rival in his Triple Crown year. In the Satsuki Sho he dueled C.B., 2nd by half a length; in the Derby he swooped from outside on the early leader but fell short, 2nd again. A fracture kept him out of the Kikuka Sho, but he lit up the classics. Back as an older horse, he won the Takamatsunomiya Hai. At stud he had no notable runners and retired in 1995.
1131=Debuting late, in January at 3, she ran 3rd, 2nd and 3rd that month, then rested, missing the spring classics. 1136=A horse of Rudolf's crop who thrilled classic fans with a sharp late charge. After his debut win he often fell short: at 2, 2 wins, a 2nd and five 3rds from 9. 1137=Unbeaten in 4 up to the Queen C., she was crushed in the Oka Sho (20th) and Oaks (25th) and retired to breed. All her placings came from leading early - a classic front-runner. Her sister Aino Lady, 2nd in the Niigata Kinen, raced at the same time.
1138=She won a debut race in October at 2, and her first graded win, the Niigata Daishoten, came at 6 - unusually long-lasting for a filly. Free of major injuries, she won specials steadily every year when older, and ran 42 times before retiring to breed in autumn at 6.
1139=Debuting in August at 2, she won the Flower C. with just 1 win. In autumn at 3 she was 2nd in the Queen S. and Rose S., and in the '85 Yasuda Kinen a fine 3rd - memorable races. Alternating good and poor runs, she ran 20 times before retiring to breed in spring at 5.
