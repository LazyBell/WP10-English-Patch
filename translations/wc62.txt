1136=A horse of Rudolf's crop who thrilled classic fans with a sharp late charge. After his debut win he often fell short: at 2, 2 wins from 9. At 3 he was 2nd in the Shinzan Kinen and won the Kisaragi Sho, but was well beaten by Bizen Nishiki in the Spring S. and swallowed up in the Satsuki Sho. A long break cost him the Derby, but in the Kikuka Sho, when all others had given up, he alone chased Rudolf down the straight, closing to 3/4 of a length. His later highlight was the Mainichi Okan at 4, but that kick, which for a moment touched the Emperor's back, left a deep impression on fans.
1137=Unbeaten in 4 to the Queen C., she was crushed in the Oka Sho (20th) and Oaks (25th) and retired to breed. All her placings came from leading - a classic front-runner. Sister of Niigata Kinen runner-up Aino Lady.
1138=She won a debut race at 2, but her first graded win, the Niigata Daishoten, came at 6 - rare longevity for a filly. Free of major injuries, she won specials every year and ran 42 times before retiring to breed at 6.
1139=Debuting at 2, she won the Flower C. with just 1 win. At 3 she was 2nd in the Queen S. and Rose S., and in the '85 Yasuda Kinen a fine 3rd. Alternating good and poor runs, she ran 20 times before retiring to breed at 5.
1140=A moody front-runner, strong when allowed his own pace. At 4, 2nd in the Kimpai (East), he switched to leading every time, and results followed: the Azuma Kofuji Sho, then the Sapporo Kinen. Mainly a dirt star, his last win was the February H. at 6.
1145=The leading lady of the '84 fillies' classics. Beaten badly in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West), she was only 3rd choice for the Oka Sho but won by 5 lengths from Long Leather. In the Oaks she was 2nd, 0.2s behind Tokai Roman, and 3rd in the Queen Elizabeth Cup after Kyowa Thunder's swoop - a fine classic campaign. Champion 4yo filly that year, she ran twice in spring at 4, then retired to breed.
1146=The '84 Oaks winner. 8th in the Tulip Sho trial, she was 4th in the Oka Sho. After the Oaks win she was 5th and 6th in the autumn trials but 4th in the Queen Elizabeth Cup. Missing some races but delivering when it mattered - just like her nephew Tokai Teio. Winning the Kyoto Daishoten "out of nowhere" at 6, long after the Oaks, must run in the family too.
1147=After 2 straight wins she was 3rd in the Sansai Himba S. (OP), then rested with leg trouble. Back, she won the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East), but fell ill before the Oaks and was 12th. Often injured, she raced until 4, winning 4 of 9.
1148=The best of Daikoter's runners, strong when things went his way. 6th in the Satsuki Sho and 5th in the Derby, he won the Kyoto Shimbun Hai in autumn and was 3rd to Rudolf in the Kikuka Sho. Consistent after, he won the Naruo Kinen, Hanshin Daishoten and more. But in the Asahi Challenge C., having beaten Wakao Raiden clearly, he became the first horse ever disqualified from first in a graded race for veering and interfering. In the Tenno Sho (Spring), having finished 2nd to Mihoshinzan, he was disqualified again for veering. A strong winner of 4 G2s, he is best remembered for "veering" and "disqualification" - a quirky horse.
1149=After her debut she lost 11 straight maidens; half her 23 starts were maidens. Her first win came in late April, after the Oka Sho. Winning in summer, she began autumn with the Sapphire S., promoted to 2nd when Long Kitty was demoted, earning a Queen Elizabeth Cup spot. There she was 14th choice of 21, barely noticed. Waiting at the back and charging in the straight, she slipped past classic stars Tokai Roman and Diana Solon and crossed the line first. Her only notable win, yet it left a huge impression - a true one-hit wonder.
1150=Just 9 starts, but her brilliant burst and sharp finish left a vivid memory. She won her first 2 but illness cost her the spring classics. A close 3rd in the Sapphire S. raised hopes; she won an allowance race, then the Queen Elizabeth Cup, with no clear favorite, became a duel with Kyowa Thunder - 2nd. That race sparked her rise: she won the Aichi Hai and the Hanshin Himba Tokubetsu by over 3 lengths. At 4 she won the Chunichi Shimbun Hai, looking like the ace of middle distances, but retired after it with leg trouble.
1151=She lit up the fillies' classics, winning the Tulip Sho and Queen S., but her only G1 was the Queen Elizabeth Cup, where she pulled up before the 3rd turn. Injury-free, she ran 47 times before breeding at 7. Only 5 wins, but she was 3rd against colts in the Niigata Kinen, Kabutoyama Kinen and Fukushima Kinen. As a dam she produced 3-time winners Elegant Bouquet (90: Ile de Bourbon) and Daigo My All (91: Russian Bond).
1152=A late debut, but he kept running well, aiming for the Kikuka Sho; 4th in the St. Lite Kinen, he missed out. He then won the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai, outdueling Dyna Carle and others. No more wins.
1153=Winning his late debut in May at 3, he did little after, reaching open class only after summer at 5 - a classic late bloomer. At 6 he won the Kimpai (East), then starred in graded races, an all-rounder at home on dirt too.
1154=Struggling at 3, he came good in spring at 4, winning allowances, then the Sekiya Kinen and Keio Hai Autumn H. Outclassed in spring miles at 5, he won summer specials and set a Japanese 1200m record - a renowned speedster.
1155=After his debut win and the Shiragiku Sho, a knee fracture cost him 6 months. Back, he was 3rd in the NZ Trophy Yonsai S. and beat older horses in the All Comers in autumn. But after the Kimpai (East) he broke his knee again and missed a year. Plagued by injury, he retired with a tendon injury.
1156=She won a maiden by a wide margin in April at 3, then 2 allowance races. A blueblood bred much like Derby winner Sakura Shori, she already raced with authority. After a short break, she won the Queen S. by 5 lengths and entered the Queen Elizabeth Cup unbeaten. Waiting in midfield as usual, she easily passed front-running Elps at the final turn and kicked on in the straight. Just as an unbeaten queen seemed born, Reward Wing swooped wide and snatched the win near the line - the first time she was ever beaten. She won the Turquoise S. next and retired at year's end, her limits never found, then went to stud.
1157=Indecisive at 3-4, in summer at 4 she led into the straight in the Kinko Sho (G3: 1800m) on soft ground and powered home with authority. From 6 she raced at Mizusawa, in the top three 12 times in 15.
1158=He won his debut, but his 2nd win came in his 6th start. 4th in the Hanshin Sansai S., he was 12th in the Shinzan Kinen. He won the Kisaragi Sho, but again couldn't follow up - inconsistent. He later ran over jumps.
1159=A front-runner who broke so brilliantly that people joked "wasn't that a flying start?" Building his record at local middle-distance meetings, at 4, still in allowances, he won the Kabutoyama Kinen. But that was Kippoushi's last win. In allowances his fast pace gave others no chance, but in open class it didn't work at all - the classic "TV horse" who only shines early. Still, his bold front-running, undeterred, moved many fans. He moved to local racing at 6.
1160=After an injury, he showed his talent from autumn at 3, winning allowances, then the Kimpai (East) at 4. His camp saw him as a middle-distance horse and planned to skip the Tenno Sho (Spring), but favorite Mihoshinzan and other leaders withdrew injured, so he ran. To save stamina, he was ridden easily early and asked only late; jockey Okabe's superb ride paid off and he won.
1161=She won her debut, but after a 10th in the Daily Hai Sansai S. developed shin trouble and rested. Back the next spring, she won, then chipped her right knee. She won the Sapphire S. in autumn, showing promise, but injuries kept coming and she never fulfilled it.
1162=He came good at 5, winning an allowance and then the Milers C., his first graded win. 4th to Fresh Voice in that year's Yasuda Kinen, he was expected to do more, but repeated injuries held him back.
1163=Talented but weak on big days - that was Sunny O. He won his first 2 and was 3rd to Scrum Dyna in the Asahi Hai Sansai S. At 3 he won the Keisei Hai and was 2nd in the Yayoi Sho, a classic hope, but was 3rd to Mihoshinzan in the Satsuki Sho and 7th in the Derby, and 3rd to Mihoshinzan again in the Kikuka Sho. Older, he won the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai and more, but in G1s was 5th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) at 4 and 3rd in the Yasuda Kinen at 5. A tendon injury in summer at 5 ended his career without a G1. 1164=The Derby winner Symboli Farm produced after Rudolf. Trained alongside Rudolf from 2, he was tipped for greatness early. But a feud between owner and trainer drew more attention than his ability. Perhaps partly to escape it, after his Derby win he raced in Europe for 2 full years, laying a precious foundation for Japanese horses abroad, now common. He didn't win, but his 14 hard races against Europe's best, incl. a 2nd in the Prix Foy, were a great asset to Japanese racing.
1165=Leg trouble forced 6 months off right after his debut. Back in summer at 3, he was 2nd in the Naruo Kinen at 4, then won the Keihan Hai. A good 3rd in the Takarazuka Kinen, leg trouble forced another long break. After racing in Hokkaido, he went to stud.
1166=Debuting at local Morioka, he won 4 and moved to the JRA in autumn at 2. 4th in the Yayoi Sho, he closed only in the straight for 5th to Mihoshinzan in the Satsuki Sho. After 13 months off he did little and returned to Iwate at 6. He raced forward and kicked again in the straight, with a fine finish too.
1167=He won his debut by 10 lengths, then the G1 Asahi Hai Sansai S. by about 2. But at 3, though unbeaten 2yo champion, he was only 2nd choice for the Spring S.; unbeaten Mihoshinzan was favorite. 1168=Winning the Yayoi Sho, he earned a classic spot. In the Satsuki Sho he raced from behind as usual but lost the finishing duel, 6th, narrowly behind runner-up Scrum Dyna. In the Derby he was 2nd to Sirius Symboli. Beaten in both autumn trials, he was 2nd choice for the Kikuka Sho as a stayer, but 2nd again to Mihoshinzan. 6th, 2nd, 2nd - frustrating classics.
