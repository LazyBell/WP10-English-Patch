66=* A Meeting Born of the Name Alex\nAfleet Alex had an unremarkable pedigree and build, and was sold and resold before he ever raced. He ended up with an ownership group, and since two members' daughters were named "Alex," he was named by combining that with his sire, Northern Afleet.\nShortly before his debut, the owners heard about another girl named Alex. She was fighting childhood cancer, yet was selling lemonade to raise money for other children with cancer. Moved, the owners decided to donate part of his winnings. But just three days after Afleet Alex won a graded stakes in his third straight win from debut, the girl passed away. Her work continued as the Alex's Lemonade Stand Foundation, and the owners pledged part of his earnings to it.\nIn 2005, at 3, Afleet Alex took on the US Triple Crown. On the Triple Crown race days that year, tracks across the country promoted the foundation. Carrying those hopes, he narrowly finished 3rd in the Kentucky Derby. In the Preakness S. he raced in a good midfield spot, but as he moved up at the final turn another horse bumped him and he stumbled so badly his jockey nearly fell. Yet as if someone had reached out a hand, he quickly regained his balance, surged again, took the lead and won by four and three-quarter lengths. He then won the Belmont S. by 7 lengths, taking two legs of the Triple Crown and bringing hope to many. He was named Eclipse champion 3yo colt, and his owners received a special award for their charity work.\n\n* The Global Sprint Challenge Is Founded\nThis year the Global Sprint Challenge was founded, the world's first international sprint series to crown a world sprint champion. At first four countries took part - Australia, Britain, Japan and Hong Kong - with six international graded races at 6 furlongs (1200m) or less. Points were awarded for placings, and the first champion was Hong Kong-trained Cape of Good Hope, winner of the Australia S. and Britain's Golden Jubilee S.\nThe series continued with changing countries and races, but was suspended in 2018 when new transport rules made shipping horses between Australia and Hong Kong difficult.\n\n* The Asian Mile Challenge Is Founded\nTo raise the level of Asian milers and their international competitiveness, the Asian Mile Challenge was also founded in 2005. At first it covered two races, Japan's Yasuda Kinen and Hong Kong's Champions Mile; the next year Australia's Futurity S. and Dubai's Dubai Turf were added. Until 2010 a bonus was paid to any horse winning two or more of the races; in 2011 the bonus was replaced by a luxury car. The series ended after 2011. In 7 editions the only bonus winner was Bullish Luck, who won the Champions Mile and Yasuda Kinen in 2006; no horse qualified in any other year. With schedules, travel and other hurdles, winning two was clearly very hard.
67=* The Lady Who Reads the Future\nOuija Board was a British racehorse. Her name comes from the "Ouija board," inspired by her granddam Ouija and dam Selection Board.\nShe won 1 of 3 at 2, but blossomed at 3. She won a listed race by 6 lengths, then tried graded company for the first time in the Oaks. Only 4th choice, she left the field behind to win by 7 lengths. She added the Irish Oaks, the 10th filly ever to win both. She ran well in the Arc but was only 3rd. Straight after she traveled to America for the Breeders' Cup Filly & Mare Turf and won. With 3 G1 wins that year she was Cartier Horse of the Year.\n2005 was a year of trials. In her first race, the G1 Prince of Wales's S., she lost a shoe at the final turn and was a distant 7th, 30 lengths back. After the race she was found to have a stress fracture and a split hoof in her left foreleg and had to rest. Back in action, she was narrowly 2nd in the Breeders' Cup Filly & Mare Turf. She then went to Japan for the Japan Cup, finishing 5th, 2 lengths behind record-breaking winner Alkaased. With no time to rest, she flew to Hong Kong for the Hong Kong Vase and won convincingly by 3 lengths with a late surge from the back. Wins had been scarce, but she showed her power in the end.\nIn 2006, at 5, she kept traveling to big races. She came up short in the Dubai Sheema Classic and the Queen Elizabeth S., but avenged her heavy defeat in the Prince of Wales's S. by winning it. In autumn she ran in the Breeders' Cup Filly & Mare Turf again and won by two and a quarter lengths with a tremendous finish, her second win in the race. With 3 G1 wins that year she was Cartier Horse of the Year again - the first horse to win it twice since the awards began.\n\n* Speed Carried Into the Future\nThis year the great sire Danzig was put down due to old age, dying at 29. As a racehorse he won all 3 starts with blazing speed, but a knee injury forced early retirement. At stud, offspring who inherited his speed succeeded one after another, and he was US champion sire three years running from 1991 to 1993. His heirs include Danehill, three-time champion sire in Britain and Ireland, spreading his blood worldwide.
68=* Rags to Riches Wins the Belmont\nRags to Riches was an American filly by Belmont S. winner A.P. Indy, and grandaughter of Belmont winner Seattle Slew. On the very day she finished 4th on her 2yo debut, her half-brother Jazil won the Belmont S. - perhaps her own Belmont was destiny.\nShe raced just once at 2 and returned in January at 3 after a long break. She broke her maiden and went straight on to win her first G1, the Las Virgenes S. She kept rolling, winning the Santa Anita Oaks by five and a half lengths. In the Kentucky Oaks, where she was the clear standout, she lived up to it with a four-and-a-quarter-length win.\nFor her next race her camp chose not a fillies' race but the Belmont S., the last leg of the colts' classics. Against strong colts, she was 2nd choice at 5.3. It became a duel with Preakness winner Curlin, going for a double. After a fierce battle Rags to Riches won by a head. The commentator called it: "She beats Curlin and a hundred years of Belmont history!" She was the first filly to win the Belmont in 102 years, since Tanya in 1905, and only the third ever. Her feat was widely celebrated: besides Eclipse champion 3yo filly, she was even nominated in the athlete category of the "Teen Choice Awards," where American teenagers pick their favorite artists and celebrities.\n\n* French Racing Legend, Trainer A. Fabre\nAndre Fabre is France's leading trainer, who developed many great horses including Arc winners Carnegie and Peintre Celebre. He has close ties with major owners like Dubai's Sheikh Mohammed and trains many of their horses. He first became champion trainer in France in 1987 and then held the title 21 years running until 2007. He has won it many more times since - a true legend.
69=* Zarkava, Unbeaten Arc Winner in 7 Starts\nZarkava was an Irish-born, French-trained filly owned by H.H. Aga Khan IV of France. After an easy debut win at 2 she ran in the G1 Prix Marcel Boussac. Though blocked in the straight, when a gap finally opened she shot through it with a piercing kick to win by two and a half lengths.\nIn 2008, at 3, she won a prep and ran in the Poule d'Essai des Pouliches. On the strength of her powerful runs she was a heavy favorite at 1.3. She won by 2 lengths in a time 0.4 seconds faster than the colts' Poule d'Essai des Poulains the same day - astonishing. She won the Prix de Diane as a heavy favorite too, so easily that jockey C. Soumillon blew kisses to the crowd before the line. In the Prix Vermeille she missed the break badly and had to sit last, but she swept past the leaders wide to win, completing the French fillies' Triple Crown for the first time in 35 years, since Allez France in 1973.\nHer run wasn't over. She took on the big stage of the Arc. The only filly among 16, she was favorite, unbeaten in 6. As in the Marcel Boussac at 2, she was blocked in the straight, but again she threaded through gaps in the pack to win clearly by 2 lengths. It was a record-filled win: the first unbeaten Arc winner in 13 years, the first filly winner in 15 years and the first 3yo filly winner in 26 years. As she headed to the presentation, the commentator and crowd cried "Quelle pouliche! Extraordinaire!" (What a filly! Extraordinary!) Zarkava retired after this race and was named Cartier Horse of the Year.\n\n* Curlin, US Horse of the Year Two Years Running\nCurlin debuted late, in February at 3, but won his first 3 starts, including a G3 and a G2, and quickly became a Kentucky Derby contender. No horse who debuted at 3 had ever won the Derby, but he was 2nd choice. He couldn't catch Street Sense and others, finishing 3rd. In the Preakness S. he met Street Sense again. Street Sense led in the straight and seemed set to win, but Curlin charged wide and won by a head for his first G1. He was 2nd to the filly Rags to Riches in the Belmont S., but in autumn won the Jockey Club Gold C. and the Breeders' Cup Classic, now a two-day event, and with 3 G1 wins was Eclipse Horse of the Year.\nIn 2008 Curlin traveled to the Dubai World Cup. He took the lead 400m out and just kept pulling away, winning by a race-record seven and three-quarter lengths. His run was so overwhelming that the stands reportedly fell silent for a moment. Back in America he won 3 more G1s, becoming North America's first $10 million horse. Beating horses like Zenyatta, unbeaten in 7 that year, he was Eclipse Horse of the Year for the second straight year.
70=* Sea the Stars' British Double\nSea the Stars was named after "See the stars," words his owner's mother often said to him as a child, and his dam, Arc winner Urban Sea. Half-brother to the great Galileo and G1 winner Black Sam Bellamy, he was a true blueblood. He was 4th on debut, but then won twice, including a G2, living up to his breeding.\nIn 2009, at 3, he charged down the classic path. A fever meant he went into the 2000 Guineas without a prep, and he was only 6th choice. It became a duel with favorite Delegator, but Sea the Stars pulled clear at the end to win by a length and a half. Afterwards his camp said the Guineas had been the main goal, since his sire Cape Cross was a miler. But ignoring his camp's worries about the distance, he also won the Derby (12 furlongs = 2400m) clearly by a length and three-quarters, the first British double in 20 years, since Nashwan in 1989.\nHe raced through summer, only in G1s, and won 3 straight. Then he went for the Arc, the race his dam had won. After a good start he tossed his head, keen to go, but jockey M. Kinane managed to settle him in midfield. At the end he unleashed a powerful finish to win - only the second dam-and-son pair to win the Arc. Sea the Stars retired after this race. Running only in G1s, he won all 6 that year and was the undisputed Cartier Horse of the Year. At stud he has sired many G1 winners, living up to his blood.\n\n* Yeats, Europe's Champion Stayer Four Years Running\nYeats debuted at 2 in 2003, won 3 straight and was the hot favorite for the Derby. But days before the race he was injured, not only missing the Derby but losing a full year to recovery. Returning in May at 4, he won his first G1 in the Coronation C., but then couldn't win.\nIn 2006, in his first race at 5, he tried a true marathon for the first time in the 20-furlong (4000m) G1 Gold C. and won by 4 lengths. He added the 16-furlong (3200m) G2 Goodwood C. and was named Cartier champion stayer.\nHis talent as a stayer now in full bloom, Yeats kept racing over long distances at 6 and 7, winning the Gold C. three years running. But in 2009, at 8, age began to show: in his first race, a listed event, he faded badly late and finished 6th, 32 lengths back. Hopes were high for a first-ever 4th Gold C. in its 200-plus-year history, and he was favorite. He settled in a good spot in midfield, took the lead early at the third turn, and held on to win by three and a half lengths. Over the last furlong a huge roar of cheers rang out for him. That was his only win that year, but he was champion stayer for the 4th year running, a perfect finale - the first horse ever to win a Cartier Award four years in a row.
