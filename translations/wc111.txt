2689=A flagship of the 2019 classic crop. Posting workout times to rival older horses before his debut, he won it with ease, then the Hagi S. (OP) and the G1 Hopeful S., boxed in but splitting the field and bursting clear without the whip. Straight into the Satsuki Sho at 1.7, he won a tight finish. Even hotter Derby favorite, he missed the break and rallied to 4th, his first loss. In autumn he won the Kobe Shimbun Hai easily, then skipped the Kikuka Sho to face Almond Eye, winner of 5 straight G1s, in the Tenno Sho (Autumn); a little worked up, he was 6th. In the Arima Kinen he was 5 lengths behind Lys Gracieux but held off Kikuka winner World Premiere for 2nd. At 4 he won the Kinko Sho at 1.3; with Hong Kong called off due to COVID, he was favorite in a Takarazuka Kinen with 8 G1 winners incl. Lucky Lilac, Chrono Genesis, Kiseki and Blast Onepiece, but finished 4th, far behind crop-mate Chrono Genesis. He never raced again, retiring to stud. Out of Japanese and American Oaks winner Cesario; half-brother of Epiphaneia and Leondeux.
2690=2nd on debut to crop star Gran Alegria, she won a maiden and the Fantasy S., then with Gran Alegria in the Asahi Hai FS, won the Hanshin JF as favorite. At 3 she won the Tulip Sho and was Oka Sho favorite, but was beaten clearly by Gran Alegria, 4th. 5th to Loves Only You in the Oaks, she won the Rose S. but was 8th as favorite to Chrono Genesis in the Shuka Sho. Older, 5th in the Hanshin Himba S. and Victoria Mile, 6th in the Fuchu Himba S., she revived in the Hanshin C., shooting up the inside past G1-winning males Indy Champ and Stelvio. At 5, after a 5th, 12th and 7th, she won the Swan S. from behind and was 3rd in the Hanshin C., then retired to breed.
2691=After a debut win she was top two every time. 9th in her first open race, the Ritto S., she was 3rd in her first graded race, a G1 - the JBC Ladies' Classic. The next year she won the Sparking Lady C. as favorite, tracking in 2nd and pulling clear, then was 2nd in the Ladies' Prelude and 3rd again in the JBC Ladies' Classic. She won the Sparking Lady C. again, then the JBC Ladies' Classic at the 3rd try - a G1 - and retired at her peak to breed.
2692=Unbeaten in 2, he was 2nd to Saturnalia in the Hopeful S. at 2, but as rivals rose he was 8th in the Satsuki Sho and 18th in the Derby. Against older horses at middle-long trips he finished far back, until the Hakodate Kinen at 4, which he won as 15th of 16 with a brilliant late run; with 13th choice Duomo 2nd, the quinella paid 130,000 yen. Unplaced after, he retired after the Andromeda S. (L) at 6.
2693=The 2018 US Triple Crown winner. Debuting at 3, he won his first 2 by 9.5 and 6.5 lengths, then the G1 Santa Anita Derby. No horse unraced at 2 had won the Kentucky Derby since Apollo in 1882 - "the curse of Apollo" - but he broke it, bursting clear from handy on a sloppy track. He won the Preakness in a tight finish on slop, then dominated the Belmont - an unbeaten Triple Crown. Aiming at the BC Classic, he retired injured: 6 from 6 in about half a year. At stud from 2019, his first foals raced in 2022; by the end of 2024 he had 7 G1 winners incl. Derby winner City of Troy, and in Japan Awesome Result won 3 local graded races. A Santa Anita Derby drug violation later came to light; his disqualification was confirmed in 2024, revising his record to 5 wins from 6.
2694=4th in the St. Lite Kinen, last in the Kikuka Sho, he restarted in allowances. Winning 3 classes at 4, he was 5th as a longshot in the G2 Sapporo Kinen, then won the Fukushima Kinen as 2nd choice. At 5 he won the Kokura Daishoten, and in autumn beat Derby winner Rey de Oro and others in the All Comers - 3 graded wins.
2695=A dirt miler who won the Mile CS Nambu Hai twice. Winning 4 races at 1600-1700m on dirt, he reached open class late at 3, then won the Oasis S. (L) and Keyaki S. (OP) at 4, and the Procyon S. as 2nd choice behind defending champion Matera Sky. 2nd in the Nambu Hai, at 5 he was 9th in the February S. and 4th in the Kashiwa Kinen, then won the Nambu Hai as 6th choice - his first G1, avenging his defeat. 9th in the Champions C. and February S., short at JRA G1 level, he won the Sakitama Hai and the Nambu Hai again at 6. At 7, 7th in the February S. and 14th going for a 3rd Nambu Hai, he went to stud.
2696=Beaten on debut, he won 7 straight up to the February S. Debuting late in spring at 3, he won a maiden and 5-million race, then fetlock swelling cost a year. Back in summer at 4 with Yutaka Take, he won 3 classes, then the Tokai S. at 1.5, leading with ease. Six straight, he went to the February S., co-favorite with veteran Gold Dream; leading as usual, he won their straight duel by a neck. His streak ended; hampered, he was 15th in the Miyako S., then 3rd in the Champions C. behind 3yo star Chrysoberyl and Gold Dream. At 6, 14th defending the February S. and 9th in the Nambu Hai, he was 3rd again in the Champions C. - "signs of revival," said Take. At 7, 3rd in the Kashiwa Kinen and 4th in the Nambu Hai, he broke superbly and held 4th in a 3rd Champions C. After 2 races at 8, past his best, he retired to stud.
2697=3rd in the Tokyo Sports Hai 2yo S. and 6th in the Hopeful S., he flopped in spring at 3 (4th Kisaragi Sho, 3rd Mainichi Hai, 9th Arlington C.) and missed the G1s. Restarting, he won 3 classes in a row, then the Fuji S. in autumn at 4 with a powerful finish from midfield. 6th in the Mile CS, at 5 he was 4th in the Tokyo Shimbun Hai and 2nd in the Dubai Turf, then 12th in the BC Mile and 6th in the Hong Kong Mile. At 6 he was 3rd in the Dubai Turf; after a 15th in the Yasuda Kinen and heavy defeats in Saudi and Dubai at 7, a tendon injury ended his career, and he went to stud in Brazil.
2698=A long-time middle-to-long distance runner who won his first graded race, the Kyoto Kinen, at 7. Emerging in autumn at 3, he was 12th to Fierement in the Kikuka Sho; at 4, 3rd in the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai. Slumping from 5, he was 2nd as 17th choice in the Chunichi Shimbun Hai late at 6, then led all the way in the Kyoto Kinen at 7 as 12th choice. Seen as a fluke, he stayed unfancied and unplaced.
2699=A dirt runner at 1200-1600m in Southern Kanto, winning races like the Bloodstone Tokubetsu and April Sho. 7th in his first graded race, the Sakitama Hai, he led to win the Oval Sprint, holding off favored Oken Believe, and won it again the next year; 5th as 3rd choice in the JBC Sprint.
2700=A 2019-2021 dirt star. At 3 he was 3rd to Chrysoberyl in the Japan Dirt Derby; at 4, 4th in the Kawasaki Kinen and JBC Classic, 5th in the Tokyo Daishoten. At 5, after a 4th in the Teio Sho, he won the JBC Classic, leading early and holding off Omega Perfume, Chuwa Wizard and others.
2701=An Oi mare who emerged early at 3, 3rd in the Kanto Oaks and Totsuka Kinen (S1). In December at 4, in her 20th start and 5th JRA-local graded race, she led all the way against JRA stars in the Queen Sho. At 5 she was beaten badly in the TCK Jo-o Hai and Empress Hai, then 6th defending the Beautiful Dreamer C. at Mizusawa, and retired.
2702=Debuting on JRA turf, she kept placing without winning as a maiden, then moved to Kawasaki and won 4 straight. 6th in the Sparking Lady C., she won steadily in Southern Kanto, then at 5 won the Marine C. as 3rd choice in a field of 7, tracking in 3rd and pulling clear. 4th in the G1 JBC Ladies' Classic.
2703=Winner of the 2021 BC Classic and Whitney S., Eclipse Horse of the Year. Bought by the Korea Racing Authority under its K-Nicks program, choosing top prospects by nick patterns and pedigree theory - hence his name. He won the G1 Breeders' Futurity at 2, then went winless at 3. A fracture at 4 meant a long break; back in autumn, he won the BC Dirt Mile. At 5 he won the Pegasus World C. and Whitney S. (4 G1s), then led all the way in the BC Classic as top fancy. Horse of the Year, rated world No. 1 at 129 lbs. After a 2nd in the Pegasus World C. at 6, he retired; at stud in Kentucky from 2023.
2704=A debut winner who was 2nd in the Kyoto 2yo S. and 5th in the Hopeful S. 3rd in the Yayoi Sho for a Satsuki Sho spot, he was 11th there and 6th in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai. Aiming at autumn, he won the Radio Nikkei Sho as 3rd choice, drawing level early from midfield and bursting clear.
2705=Winner of the 2019 Kikuka Sho and, at 5, the Tenno Sho (Spring). He won his debut as favorite and was 3rd in the Kyoto 2yo S. At 3 he won a 5-million race and was 2nd to Velox in the Wakaba S. (L), earning a Satsuki Sho spot, but sore shins ruled out the spring. In autumn he was 3rd to Satsuki winner Saturnalia and Velox in the Kobe Shimbun Hai. With Saturnalia going to the Tenno Sho, Velox was Kikuka favorite and he 3rd choice; Yutaka Take said he was "in by far his best shape." From a handy spot he broke clear - Take became both the youngest and oldest Kikuka Sho-winning jockey. In an Arima Kinen with 11 G1 winners, he was 3rd. Resting long at 4, he ran only in the Japan Cup (6th, in the 3 Triple Crown winners' clash) and Arima Kinen (5th). At 5, 3rd in the Nikkei Sho, he won the Tenno Sho (Spring) as 3rd choice behind Deep Bond and Aristoteles, running on strongly from a handy spot. 11th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn).
2706=Winning 3 classes from late 3 to open class, he won the Kakitsubata Kinen and Sakitama Hai at once - a dirt force. Unplaced in the February S. and JBC Sprint, he won the Kurofune Sho, was 2nd in the Hyogo Gold T. and the Sakitama Hai. At 6 he won the Oval Sprint from a handy spot; after a 10th in the JBC Sprint, he won the Hyogo Gold T. despite gaining 22kg, catching leader Best Macho by a nose. At 7, 2nd in the Summer Champion, he won the Tokyo Hai, then was 7th as 2nd choice in the JBC Sprint. At 8, after placing but not winning, and a 4th in the Kurofune Sho at 9, he retired to stud.
2707=4th on debut, a sharp closer from midfield, he won a maiden, the Oxalis Sho and Hyogo Junior GP - a dirt talent. 2nd in the Zen-Nihon 2yo Yushun and 4th in the UAE Derby, he won the Nagoya GP in December at 3. Older, 3rd in the Kawasaki Kinen, 2nd in the Mercury C. and Nippon TV Hai, he couldn't win. From 5, occasionally top five; 2nd in the Saga Kinen at 7, he moved to local racing after heavy defeats.
