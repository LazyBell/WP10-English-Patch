725=Called the best Japanese runner by Dancing Brave, Europe's greatest horse of the '80s. Fast from the start, she won 3 of 4 at 2, including the Hanshin Sansai Himba S. At 3 she grew stronger, winning the Tulip Sho by 4 lengths and the Oka Sho as 1.3 favorite. Beaten by Lady Pastel's late charge in the Oaks, she gained revenge in the Shuka Sho for a fillies' double. Then older rivals proved tough: she won only the next year's Sapporo Kinen, losing big titles, and retired after a fracture in autumn at 5.
726=He moved from local Kawasaki to the JRA after 5 straight wins. After early struggles, once he won in the JRA he ran well on turf or dirt, rarely out of the top five. He won the Chunichi Shimbun Hai at the end of his 4yo year. The next year, 2nd in the March S., 4th in the Mainichi Okan and 5th in the Mile CS, he won the Chunichi Shimbun Hai again at year's end. Steady after, he won the '06 Swan S. and the '07 Hankyu Hai, was 3rd in the Takamatsunomiya Kinen and 2nd on dirt in the JBC Sprint. In '08 results faded and he retired.
727=She beat Lady Pastel, Rosebud and others in the Flora S., her first graded win and sire Dance in the Dark's first graded title. 5th in the Oaks, she suffered a tendon injury training for the Shuka Sho and retired.
728=His dam Home Sweet Home was a full sister of BC Distaff winner Pleasant Home. 6th on turf debut in November at 2, he then ran 4 times on dirt: 3 wins, incl. the Hyacinth S., and a 2nd. Back on turf for the classics he was 11th in the Spring S. and 12th in the Satsuki Sho, so returned to dirt. Favorite for the Hyogo Championship, he took the lead early and won by 5 lengths, then won the Unicorn S. comfortably - top 3yo dirt horse. After that he was 3rd in the Tokyo Daishoten, February S. and Teio Sho without winning, until the Akhal-Teke S. (OP), his first win in 2 years. After a nose 2nd to Grand City in the March S., he retired to stud.
729=Unbeaten in 3 including the Fairy S., she was a classic contender, but injury before the Oka Sho cost her the classics. Back in autumn, she beat older horses in the Andromeda S. The next year she won the Shunpu S. and Kokura Nikkei OP, but only won a 2nd graded race in the Centaur S. in autumn at 5. She ran in G1s without winning and left the turf after an 8th in the '04 Takamatsunomiya Kinen.
730=A Kansai horse, he debuted at Tokyo, 2nd to future graded winner Alyzeo. He won his maiden and was 4th to Victoire Pisa in the Radio Nikkei Hai. In the Wakakoma S. (OP) at 3, Air Groove's son Rulership was favored, but he slipped through on the inside to win with his experience. In the Wakaba S. (OP) he lost narrowly to Kanto hope Perusa, 2nd. In the Satsuki Sho, as 6th choice, he closed fastest for 2nd to Victoire Pisa. 3rd choice for the Derby, his kick misfired, 9th. He was 4th in the Sapporo Kinen and 7th in the Kikuka Sho, then as favorite in the Naruo Kinen was 2nd to Rulership. At 4 he kept racing in graded races, beaten by crop-mates Rulership in the Nikkei Shinshun Hai and To the Glory in the Kyoto Kinen. Then in the Osaka Hai he held off Derby winner Eishin Flash and NHK Mile C. winner Danon Chantilly - amazingly, his first graded win. In the Tenno Sho (Spring) he won a duel with Eishin Flash in the straight for his first G1. His camp went for the Arc: 2nd in the Prix Foy prep, he raised hopes, but got worked up in the paddock and was 10th. Back home he was 6th in the Arima Kinen. He raced on at 5 but had lost his old power, retiring to stud after a 3rd in the Sapporo Kinen.
731=At 3 she won the Uguisu Sho and Flower C., her first graded win. Like many Mejiro McQueen runners she grew stronger over longer trips, but perhaps lacking growth, her form collapsed in autumn and she never won again.
732=An unusual Australian-born son of Fuji Kiseki. He won his debut and an open special and was 3rd in the NHK Mile C., then always contended in sprint-mile graded races. He won the '08 Hakodate Sprint S. and that year was 2nd in both sprint G1s, the Takamatsunomiya Kinen and Sprinters S. After a dull '09, he won the Swan S. in autumn, then the Hanshin C. and the '10 Ocean S., and on that run the Takamatsunomiya Kinen - 4 straight. Consistent in sprints after, he won the Takamatsunomiya Kinen again the next year and retired to stud. His 2nd crop's Shuji won the Kokura 2yo S. and Hanshin C. His runners have long careers in sprints and on dirt, and in 2025 Shuji's foals debuted too.
733=In the JRA he did little beyond winning his first 2, incl. the Cosmos Sho, but a move to local Funabashi in autumn at 4 changed everything. He won his first race there by 4 lengths, emerging as queen of the sand. The next year she won the TCK Jo-o Hai, her first JRA-local graded race, and the Teio Sho, ruling Southern Kanto within a year of the move - a stunning rise.
734=From the widest draw in the Fairy S., she swooped from 2nd-last to win. She also won the Canna S. (OP) under the same conditions; Nakayama 1200m turf suited her. From 3, apart from a 3rd in the Falcon S., she kept finishing in double figures - a typical early bloomer - and went to stud after a February race at 5.
735=He showed promise at 3, running in the St. Lite Kinen, but often couldn't win. Hampered by leg trouble, he came good in spring at 5, winning 3 straight from the 5-million class, then the Meguro Kinen by a neck from Eye Popper. He went to Australia with stablemate Delta Blues, running in 2 G1s and finishing 2nd to him in the Melbourne Cup. Back home he was 2nd to Deep Impact in the Arima Kinen. He never won a G1, but in '07 won the Meguro Kinen again and was 2nd in the Japan Cup and 3rd in the Takarazuka Kinen, always a presence on big days. After a 12th in the '08 Tenno Sho (Spring) he slumped, and after the '10 Diamond S. (11th) moved to Ireland. He won his first race there, retired with leg trouble and went to stud in the Czech Republic.
736=From summer at 3 he ran only at 2000m+. Often closing too late, with eight 2nds, he still won his first graded try, the Diamond S., from the 10-million class. Good in graded races after, he had a long injury break. Back in '07 he did little and retired after a 14th in the Tenno Sho (Spring).
737=Leading boldly like his dam, he won his debut and the Arlington C., but led and collapsed to 13th in the Satsuki Sho. Many more fades followed, until he led all the way in the '06 Kyoto Kimpai. Unable to race steadily after, he retired after a 17th in the '07 Mainichi Okan.
738=Rose Color's first foal. Won the Fillies' Revue, then missed the Oka Sho with a fever, and was 2nd by a neck in the Oaks, by 3/4 length in the Shuka Sho and by a nose in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup - always just short. As a dam she produced Japan Cup winner Rose Kingdom, passing on the "Rose family" blood.
739=Raced mainly in dirt sprints, she won her first turf race, the Ibis Summer Dash, after reaching open class. She then won the Centaur S. to be Summer Sprint Series queen, closed well for 2nd on heavy ground in the Sprinters S. and won the Keihan Hai - a top sprinter in no time. But in '08, the year of "G1 at last," she died of heart failure, far too soon.
740=Romping in the Unicorn S. made him a core dirt horse, but he was 4th in the Japan Dirt Derby and 6th in the Derby Grand Prix, then moved to Masayuki Kawashima's Funabashi stable. Still unable to win - 3rd in the Diolite Kinen, 6th in the Kashiwa Kinen - he retired.
741=Unbeaten in 3, incl. the Nanohana Sho and Tulip Sho, she was 6th in the Oka Sho and 4th in the Oaks, unable to break the wall of Cesario and co. After a 7th in the Shuka Sho, injury kept her out. Back in October '06, leg trouble struck again and she retired.
742=With a furious late charge he won the Kyoto Kimpai at 4, then the Tokyo Shimbun Hai as heavy favorite with an astonishing 32.9, suddenly a star of the mile division. But he was 9th in the Milers C. and 15th in the Yasuda Kinen, and flopped in the Mainichi Okan and Tenno Sho (Autumn). Sharpened by racing, his kick returned in the Mile Championship, winning his first G1 in good time, then the Hong Kong Mile. After that his form dipped, and he retired after a 6th in the '07 Milers C. He went to stud at famous Walmac Farm in the US; his son Dabirsim won French G1s, the Prix Morny and Prix Jean-Luc Lagardere.
743=As a daughter of great mare Rosita, much was hoped for, but she won only 2 of 10, best a 2nd in the Elfin S. As a dam she produced Regular Member (97: Commander in Chief), winner of 3 JRA-local G1s.
744=At 3 he ran in the Kyodo News Hai and Arlington C. without success and was long stuck in the lower classes. At 6 he finally settled in open class and won the Negishi S., then was 3rd in the February S. - a self-made dirt horse. His form faded in autumn and he moved to local Oi.
745=3rd in the Spring S. to make the Satsuki Sho, he was 10th, and after a 13th in the St. Lite Kinen missed the Kikuka Sho. He improved steadily but wins were scarce, until the Epsom C. at 4, where he beat Grass Bomber and others from a good position. He then won the Niigata Kinen, a horse to watch for the autumn G1s. But he hurt a ligament, and back after a year in autumn '07 had lost his form, retiring after an 8th in the '09 Nakayama Kinen.
746=He won the Spring S. with a sharp finish, the classics' dark horse, but was crushed in the Satsuki Sho (8th) and Derby (14th). After near misses, he won the '07 New Year S., his first win since the Spring S., and was 3rd in the Nakayama Kinen. Hopes rose, but slow starts ruined him, then a tendon injury forced a long break. He struggled after his return, but at the end of his 8yo year won the Fukushima Kinen as 12th choice with an eye-opening charge from last.
747=15th on debut, he won his 3rd start and 3 straight more. After a long break he reached open class at the end of his 5yo year, won the Shirafuji S. at 6, then the Niigata Kinen and Epsom C. In autumn he lost the All Comers to Matsurida Gogh but fought Dream Journey hard for 2nd. After a 5th in the Tenno Sho a fracture was found. Back after a year, he beat Dream Journey in the All Comers - clearly talented - but more long breaks followed and he retired without the results he deserved.
748=Half-sister of 10-time winner Shinko Lovely, a Mile CS winner. By Sunday Silence, she was a classic hope, but was 4th in the Oka Sho and 7th in the Oaks. Switched to miles and sprints when older, she slowly emerged, winning the Kyoto Himba S. at 5. As a dam, her 3rd foal Codino (10: King Kamehameha) was 2nd in the Asahi Hai FS and 3rd in the Satsuki Sho, and his full sister Cecchino 2nd in the Oaks.
749=He won his debut but was crushed in the Kyodo News Hai in only his 2nd start. Building up in his own class, he reached open class only in autumn at 5. He kept trying graded races, 3rd in the American JCC at 7, then slumped. But at 8 in '05, after 3rds in the New Year S., American JCC and Shirafuji S., his honed kick finally exploded in the Nikkei Sho - his first win in 2.5 years and first graded win.
750=Based at local Funabashi. After winning the Kanto Oaks and more, she raced in the JRA's Shion S. but was 14th. Dull from 4, she retired and became a broodmare from '08.
751=With light early speed and a quick kick, she starred in middle-distance fillies' races. Master jockey Yukio Okabe often rode her early on, but later she had no regular rider - 11 jockeys in 23 races. Oddly unlucky with G1s, she first tried one in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup at 4 and, ridden by Peslier, was 2nd to Fine Motion. With 3 fillies' graded wins that year, she was named JRA Best Older Filly or Mare.
752=Debuting late, in January at 3, and slow to win, she missed the Oka Sho, but 2nd in the Flora S. earned an Oaks spot. In the Oaks she swept past T.M. Ocean, the crop's empress, to win. Just as strong when older, she won the Nakayama Himba S. and Fuchu Himba S., and never once finished out of the top five, thrilling fans with her consistency.
753=Talented enough to be favored over Mihono Bourbon in the Spring S. at 3. With then-rookie Shinji Fujita, their first ride together, he won the Radio Tampa Hai 3yo S. with a powerful charge, a first graded win for both.
754=Full sister of Queen Elizabeth II Cup winner To the Victory. With Yutaka Take she won the JRA-local Queen Sho at Funabashi.
755=2nd in his first 3, he then won 3 straight, including the Kisaragi Sho. Dull after a big Satsuki Sho loss, he regained form at 5, and after a long break won the Kokura Daishoten at 7. Age caught up and he retired in '08.
756=Debuting late, in June at 3, his dogged front-running brought 3 wins and two 2nds by autumn. In the Kikuka Sho he livened up the race leading, 6th, then won the Aichi Hai and Kimpai (West). He went to stud because he carried great Takeshiba O's blood.
757=In the Arlington C. he lived up to his name, leading from the start at his own pace to win by 4 lengths. But after a long break and more, he never won again, racing until 5.
758=He won his first 2, incl. the Kokura 3yo S., but a fractured right radius cost him 15 months. After 5 more starts, periostitis forced another long break - 2 wins from 7 up to 4. But back at 5, he won 4 straight, then the G2 Keio Hai Spring C. over Takara Steel - 5 in a row. He kept starring in graded races, winning the CBC Sho at the end of his 7yo year - a moving long career.
759=Injured in summer at 3, he rose late, getting a 3rd win only at 5. Then his talent bloomed: after a 3rd, he won 4 straight, including the Sekiya Kinen. He didn't win again but was 2nd in the Mainichi Okan.
760=He won his 1200m dirt debut, then a 5-million race, the Violet S. and Hyacinth S. - all on dirt. In May he won the JRA-local Hyogo Championship for 5 straight, then the Unicorn S., but was 3rd to Kane Hekili, ending his streak. He won the '06 Nagoya Daishoten, but was 5th in the Japan Dirt Derby, 3rd in the Derby Grand Prix, 4th in the Kashiwa Kinen, 6th in the Japan Cup Dirt and 3rd in the Kawasaki Kinen - always just short at the top level.
