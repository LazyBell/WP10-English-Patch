299=Won his first graded race, the Fuji S., at 3. As an older horse his speed sharpened: after a 2nd in the Keio Hai Spring C., he beat the monster Grass Wonder in the Yasuda Kinen for a G1. In autumn he boldly tried the Tenno Sho (Autumn), a fine 3rd behind Special Week and Stay Gold, then won the Mile Championship for spring and autumn mile G1s. He headed for the Hong Kong C. but developed a tendon injury there - a sad retirement just as he hit his prime. At stud he sired Yasuda Kinen winner Showa Modern, Agnes Raspberry and Nanayo Himawari, keeping the rare Princely Gift line alive.
300=By Tolomeo out of a No Attention mare - an obscure pedigree - yet he won 11 races (nine 2nds) before retiring at 8, a great supporting actor at middle distances. A late bloomer needing 6 starts to win, he missed the classics. He did little until a long break from autumn at 4, when he grew a lot. Back in spring at 5 he was 2nd, then won 5 straight from the 9-million class, including the Sekiya Kinen and All Comers, a sudden breakout. At 6 leg trouble kept him out and winless, but at 7 he came back to win 3 graded races including the Nakayama Kinen. 3 of his 5 graded wins (including a Niigata Kinen moved to Nakayama) came at Nakayama, and he was 3rd in the Arima Kinen - a Nakayama specialist.
301=His stayer's blood bloomed in the Kikuka Sho, which he barely got into. After taking the last classic, the next year, ridden by genius Yutaka Take, he won the Hanshin Daishoten and then the Tenno Sho (Spring) with ease - three generations of Tenno Sho winners after grandsire Mejiro Asama and sire Mejiro Titan. But he was 2nd to Mejiro Ryan in the Takarazuka Kinen; won the Tenno Sho (Autumn) by 6 lengths but was demoted to 18th for veering; was 4th in the Japan Cup, unable to beat the foreigners; and was a shock 2nd to Daiyu Saku's surprise run in the Arima Kinen. He won the Hanshin Daishoten again, then met year-younger double classic winner Tokai Teio in the Tenno Sho (Spring). The pair's quinella paid just 150 yen - "the showdown of the century." He wasn't favorite, but won clearly. So consistent was he that some joked "he always wins, so races are boring." Injury then cost him a year, but he returned to win the Osaka Hai in record time. Going for a third Tenno Sho (Spring), he lost to young stayer Rice Shower, but vented his frustration by winning the Takarazuka Kinen. In the Kyoto Daishoten that autumn he set a stunning course record of 2:22.7. He seemed to have reclaimed the title of "the best," but was injured after the race, retired and went to stud. He sired Meguro Kinen winner Hokuto Sultan but died in 2006, missing four generations of Tenno Sho winners. As a broodmare sire, though, his cross with Stay Gold, the "golden cross," produced Triple Crown winner Orfevre, Gold Ship and many more - the champion's blood lives on.
302=A miler like his speedy sire Nihon Pillow Winner. Debuting late, in March at 3, he steadily won races and at 4 tried the Yasuda Kinen. With all 4 wins at 1200m on dirt, 11th choice was fair, but he beat Daitaku Helios and Dynamite Daddy - his first graded win was a G1. That year he was 2nd in both the Centaur S. and Sprinters S. The next year he won the Keio Hai Spring C., then the Yasuda Kinen again, cementing himself as champion miler, and in autumn tried the Tenno Sho (Autumn). The field lacked a star, but his pedigree and record raised doubts about the distance. He broke clear early in the straight, and Sekitei Ryu O charged wide; after a fierce dead heat, Yamanin Zephyr won by just a nose. Where his sire tried and failed, the son took the crown. The feat earned him three JRA Awards: champion older male, champion sire-bred-in-Japan horse and champion sprinter.
303=It's no exaggeration to say he created the early image of Sunday Silence's runners: "fiery-tempered but fearsomely strong." From the first crop, he was already called the best, even "the phantom Triple Crown winner." He won his debut by 8 lengths, beat future Derby winner Tayasu Tsuyoshi easily in the Momiji S., and won the Asahi Hai Sansai S. unbeaten. Winning so easily while still immaturely charging at other horses showed his huge potential. He won the Yayoi Sho clearly at 3, and everyone assumed classic glory, even debating "how many crowns," but a tendon injury forced sudden retirement. He left the turf with the thrill of his wins and a sense of the miracle that might have been. At stud he thrived, siring dirt king Kane Hekili (7 dirt G1s including local), '06 classic star Dream Passport, Victoria Mile winner Koiuta, 2-time Takamatsunomiya Kinen winner Kinshasa no Kiseki, 5-time graded winner Daitaku Riva, Mile CS winner Sadamu Patek and Satsuki Sho winner Isla Bonita. At one point he led all Japanese-bred sires in career wins - a firmly established great sire.
304=A big-name mochikomi with high hopes before his debut, but he was born frail and had shin soreness early on, so he couldn't be fully prepared or show his ability. He missed the classics, reaching open class only at the end of his 3yo year. Then early at 4 he won the Kimpai (East), his first graded win, and was 2nd in the Meguro Kinen, but fractured a leg after it and had to rest. In spring at 5, back after 13 months, he crushed year-younger Satsuki Sho winner Genuine in the Nakayama Kinen. In the Tenno Sho (Spring) he brushed aside Triple Crown winner Narita Brian, his crop-mate, and last year's Horse of the Year Mayano Top Gun, winning his long-sought G1 and the title of best horse at once. At year's end he won the Arima Kinen too, taking '96 Horse of the Year and champion older male - the top of Japanese racing in name and fact. He then announced an Arc challenge, but was injured during the Prix Foy prep and came home without reaching the world's summit. At stud he sired 3-time middle-distance graded winner Sakura Century and Roman Empire, famous for a dead heat in the Keisei Hai.
305=This crop of colts, with stars like El Condor Pasa, Special Week and Grass Wonder, was called "the greatest generation ever." Amid them, Seiun Sky, by the obscure sire Sheriff's Star, won both the Satsuki Sho and the Kikuka Sho. His power shone brightest in the Kikuka Sho: 2nd choice behind Special Week, he went off alone over 3000m and was never challenged, leading all the way in world-record time, 3.5 lengths ahead of Special Week. At that point, before the Japan Cup, he was clearly the best of his crop. But after losing the Arima Kinen to Grass Wonder and both Tenno Sho races the next year to Special Week, opinion turned. Injury then forced a long break and retirement. Somehow his name wasn't among the "Big Three." A double classic winner of the greatest crop, overlooked - his rivals were simply too strong.
306=With a straight white blaze and smart looks, he was called a "good-looking horse" and drew attention before his debut. Needing 4 starts to win, he was said to be "more hype than horse," but after winning the Hiiragi Sho and Junior C., he crushed 2yo champion Ines Fujin in the Yayoi Sho, proving his ability was real. In the Satsuki Sho, though, he was 3rd to Hakutaisei. Backed as a horse whose pedigree "would love more distance," he was favorite for the Derby but couldn't catch front-running Ines Fujin, finishing 2nd. Spring ended in frustration. Seeking revenge in autumn, he won the Kyoto Shimbun Hai in record time and was Kikuka Sho favorite - but fellow Mejiro late bloomer McQueen stood in his way, and he ended the classics without a crown. He was 2nd in the Arima Kinen and the Nakayama Kinen at 4, and 4th behind McQueen again in the Tenno Sho (Spring) - always just short. Then came the Takarazuka Kinen. Feeling he'd never win a G1 this way, jockey Norihiro Yokoyama stayed ahead of McQueen all the way and kicked early. It worked, and at last he won a G1. At stud his first crop gave 5-time G1 winner Mejiro Dober (94: Mejiro Beauty) and Tenno Sho winner Mejiro Bright (94: Reine du Temps). He kept siring graded winners as the ace of Japanese-bred sires.
307=By great sire Sunday Silence out of double classic winner Vega - a blueblood impossible not to get excited about. On his much-hyped debut he was demoted to 4th for interference - a flashy debut, in a way. He then stepped up to win the Erica Sho, a 5-million-class special, and soon won a graded race, the Radio Tampa Hai Sansai S. But at 3 he was 2nd to Narita Top Road in the Yayoi Sho, then sank to 6th in the Satsuki Sho - due to rain, or a preparation slip shown by a 10 kg weight loss. Then the Derby, which he couldn't lose. From 14th at the final turn he unleashed a powerful kick, accelerating up Tokyo's long straight, and caught Narita Top Road by a neck right at the line, as if perfectly timed. He was 6th in the Kikuka Sho with energy to spare, then retired with leg trouble. He went to stud but died suddenly in '04 after just 4 crops - a great loss, as he sired G1 winners like Mile CS winner Blumenblatt and Oka Sho winner Kiss to Heaven.
308=In 1997 he won his first 8 starts, including 2 G1s, and was named Eclipse Horse of the Year at 2. Most of his wins were routs, and he won the big one, the Breeders' Cup Juvenile, by a record five and a half lengths. That stunning run earned him Horse of the Year over older stars like Breeders' Cup Classic winner Skip Away and double classic winner Silver Charm - the first 2yo Horse of the Year in 25 years, since Secretariat, rated 128 lbs on the US Free Handicap, close to the all-time best. But at 3 he lost momentum: he won a G3 first time out but was a dismal 8th in the Kentucky Derby. Turning to turf, he won a G2 mile easily, but was 8th in his main goal, the Breeders' Cup Mile, and retired. His stud record was poor, with just two G3 winners. In 2006, at 11, he died tragically in a barn fire.
309=He debuted fairly late, in January at 3, but ran tough until 8, winning 12 of 38 - a long career that thrilled fans. What was most remarkable about Fujiyama Kenzan was his seemingly endless capacity to improve. He won his second graded race, the Nakayama Kinen, in his first race at 7, then kept winning, finally taking the international Hong Kong International C. - a first for a Japan-bred, Japan-trained horse - and was named that year's champion sire-bred-in-Japan horse.
310=The 1989 2000 Guineas and Derby winner, and a successful sire of horses like Swain and Bago. Watched from his debut, he won both starts at 2, then went straight to the 2000 Guineas at 3. Shrugging off the layoff, he won clearly, then romped in the Derby by 5 lengths - the first British double in 19 years since Nijinsky. He then took on older horses, winning the Eclipse S. and the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. back to back. In autumn he aimed for the Arc and the first European Triple Crown since Mill Reef, but lacked his usual kick in the Prix Niel prep, stumbling to 3rd, and retired. He sired many G1 winners, notably Swain, from his second crop, who won the King George twice, and late-crop Arc winner Bago.
311=He won the Asahi Hai Sansai S. but fractured a leg in training. Surgery to insert bolts was so major that some said he'd never return. Thanks to devoted care he came back at Hakodate 82 weeks later, but couldn't run as before and kept losing. Still winless, leg trouble sent him on another long break. He seemed "completely finished," but early at 6, back from his break in the Tokyo Shimbun Hai as 10th choice, he raced forward and held on - a shock comeback win, his first in about 3 years. He then won the Hankyu Hai and ran 2nd to Shonan Kampf in the Takamatsunomiya Kinen. In the Yasuda Kinen he won his first G1 in 3.5 years - a true miracle comeback. He was also a fine 2nd to Believe in the Sprinters S. At stud his first crop included Sprinters S. winner Aston Machan (04: Wrestling Cups), and recently Majin Prosper (07: Hollywood Dream) won 3 sprint graded races. He retired from stud in 2015 and died at 21 in 2017.
312=A European mile star who won a then-world-record 7 straight G1s in 2001-02. He won his first G1, the Grand Criterium, in autumn at 2, then the Dewhurst S. At 3 he became only the 4th horse to win both the 2000 Guineas and Irish 2000 Guineas, added the St. James's Palace S., and beat older horses easily in the Sussex S. In autumn he beat rivals like Banks Hill in the Prix du Moulin de Longchamp for his 7th straight G1, Europe's best miler. He then went to America for the Breeders' Cup Mile as favorite, but missed the break and was hampered, closing well but only 2nd to Domedriver. He retired after this race and went to stud, siring Hong Kong C. winner Eagle Mountain, Eclipse S. winner Mount Nelson and, standing in Japan too, NZ Trophy winner A Shin Osman. He is also damsire of 2-time mile G1 winner Mikki Isle.
