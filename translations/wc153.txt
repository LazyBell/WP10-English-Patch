3404=A leading sire of the 1960s-70s, known as the sire of Haiseiko and Takeshiba O. An English horse, he raced in England and France, winning 7 of 25 without distinction. At stud in England he was little valued and was exported to Japan after a year. His first crop produced graded winners in both JRA and local racing, making him popular at once. His 3rd crop had Yashima National, called the best ever in local racing, his 4th the JRA monster Takeshiba O, and his 9th his masterpiece, Haiseiko; in 1973, Haiseiko's peak, he was champion sire. Displaced by Tesco Boy, he kept siring graded winners and stayed near the top. As broodmare sire: Nihon Pillow Winner, Riesengross, Take no Velvet and more. Takeshiba O and Haiseiko had some success at stud, but the line went no further and has died out.
3405=A late-blooming mare who won the 1959 Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Arima Kinen at 5 (old age system); only she and Tomei have won both as fillies or mares. Slow to start, she won her 4th race, then was 6th in the Oka Sho and 5th in the Oaks at 4. She could win open races but only placed in graded races. Still without a title by summer at 5, she ran in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) as her farewell, broke clear from midfield and held off Otemon by a nose - a shock first graded win. Invited to the Arima Kinen, 9th choice of 12, she won by 4 lengths, and retired to breed. Her foals were steady winners, mostly fillies, and her female line spread widely, producing 2006 dual classic winner Meisho Samson among others.
3406=An imported sire star of the 1970s in Japan, siring Derby winner Cornell Lancer and Tenno Sho winners Aiful and Three Giants; 6th on the sire list in 1976. A top racehorse too: at 3 in Italy he won 4 big races - the Derby Italiano, Gran Premio di Milano, Gran Premio d'Italia and Premio Principe Amedeo; at 4 the Gran Premio del Jockey Club and Premio Presidente della Repubblica - Italy's best. At stud in Italy for about 5 years with few foals, he was exported to Japan in 1964; then the foals he left behind, like Over and Mogadiscio, made him Italy's champion sire. In Japan his first crop had graded winners like Hakuei Ho; his 6th, Cornell Lancer and Aiful; his 10th, Three Giants. He also sired Sakura Sedan, dam of Sakura Toko, Sakura Chiyono O and Sakura Hokuto O.
3407=The 1959 Arc winner, imported to Japan in 1971, who defined an era siring Tenno Sho winner Taite M, Erimo George and more. French-trained, he won his first 2, then 4th in the Derby; he won the Eclipse S. and the Arc from a field of 25, and retired that year to stud in Ireland. His 4th crop had Prix Vermeille winner Casaque Grise and Italian Oaks winner Dolina, his 8th Oaks and Irish Oaks winner Altesse Royale. In Japan, beyond in-utero import Taite M, he sired Tenno Sho winner Erimo George and reached 12th on the 1978 list. As broodmare sire of Magnitude and in Special Week's pedigree, his blood lives on.
3408=Winner of 4 straight Nakayama Daishogai in 1974-1975 (then run in spring and autumn) - the only jumper in the JRA Hall of Fame. Starting on the flat, winless in 6 at 3 (old age system), he won at 4 and the Manyo S. early at 5. His stable's rookie jockey specialized in jumps, and with a granddam who had starred over fences, he was switched to jumps. Climbing steadily at 5, he peaked at 6, winning the spring Nakayama Daishogai, autumn Kyoto Daishogai and autumn Nakayama Daishogai, and at 7 won both races spring and autumn - 11 wins from 12 from late 6 through 7. At 8, weighed down by heavy imposts and injured, he retired that spring. Over jumps: 19 wins from 39, never once falling. He later stood as a riding-horse stallion.
3409=Kochi's idol, famous nationwide for losing all 113 starts. Debuting in November at 2 (modern ages), winless in 3; then 0 from 20 at 3, 18 at 4, 19 at 5, 18 at 6 and 22 at 7. Always losing but never hurt, she ran about 20 times a year, steadily earning appearance money. Her losing streak hit 100 by the end of 7, and media coverage made her a national sensation. At 8, Yutaka Take rode her, and the track set records for attendance and betting. She lost again, but Take, who had said he couldn't understand why a weak horse drew fans, said afterward, "She's definitely a star. I'd ride her again." She retired at 113 straight losses and, never bred, lives at Martha Farm in Chiba.
3410=The undisputed jumps king from 2016 to 2022, winning 9 G1s incl. 5 straight Nakayama Grand Jumps, and champion jumper 5 times in 7 years. To 4 he was ordinary: from 13 starts, only a maiden and 3 open wins. From 5 in 2016 he blossomed, winning 4 of 5 incl. the Nakayama Grand Jump and Nakayama Daishogai. At 6, unbeaten in 4, he won both again, and flat fans began to know him. At 7, after a 3rd straight Grand Jump, he invaded the flat, winning allowances with ease, and ran in the Arima Kinen, 3rd in the fan vote, making a strong move at the top of the straight. At 8 and 9 he won the Grand Jump - 5 straight. 5th at 10, he won the Nakayama Daishogai, and at 11 won the Grand Jump again, retiring with 9 G1s.
3411=A Southern Kanto star who won the Haneda Hai and Tokyo Derby of the 1973 Triple Crown. At 3 (old age system) he won 5 of 8 with a 2nd and 2 thirds, incl. a 2nd in the Zen-Nihon 3yo Yushun. At 4, with Southern Kanto hero Haiseiko gone to the JRA, he filled the gap, winning both classics and great popularity. Preparing for the Tokyo Okan Sho, he broke down in the Sumidagawa Tokubetsu and was put down.
3412=A 1979 Southern Kanto dual classic winner. He won the Haneda Hai, then the Tokyo Derby from a handy spot. Hoped to follow Hatsushiba O as the 4th Triple Crown winner, he was Tokyo Okan Sho favorite but faded from the front, unplaced. He won 3 more at 4 (old age system), none at 5 or 6, and retired.
3413=A 1980 Southern Kanto dual classic winner, rated by many fans among the best ever in local racing. Not yet a graded force at 3 (old age system), he improved greatly at 4: 2 straight wins, 2nd to Tagawa Tetsu O in the Kuroshio Hai, then crushing wins in the Haneda Hai and Tokyo Derby - best of his crop. Leg trouble kept him out of the Tokyo Okan Sho. Back at 5 he lost twice; moving to the JRA at 7, he never won and quietly disappeared.
3414=Winning a maiden in her 3rd start, she won the 1-win class in her 7th, 2-win in her 9th and 3-win in her 11th, reaching open class in autumn at 4. In her first open race, the Turquoise S., she was 3rd with the fastest finish; 9th in the Epsom C. at 5, 4th in the Sekiya Kinen and Fuchu Himba S., then she won the Turquoise S. late at 5. Half-sister to 2012 Flora S. winner Midsummer Fair, full sister to 2020 Shuka Sho 3rd Soft Flute.
3415=Winning a maiden in February at 3, she struggled in the 1-win class (12th twice), passed it early at 4, then alternated wins and losses into open class in autumn at 4. In the Kanagawa Kinen, run for one year only, she raced forward to win - also jockey Hollie Doyle's first graded win in Japan. Her third dam is Rosa Nay, making her one of the famed "Rose family."
3416=A filly who won the Arima Kinen (G1) at 3 in 2024 - the first 3yo filly winner in 64 years, since Star Roch in 1960 - giving her sire Suave Richard a big title from his first crop. Winning her debut in July at 2, 3rd in the Ivy S. (L), she took on the colts in the Hopeful S. (G1), co-favorite with Shin Emperor, and caught him from the back - the first filly winner since it became a G1. At 3 she faced colts again: 6th as Satsuki Sho favorite, 5th as 2nd choice in the Derby. Back with fillies, 5th in the Rose S., then 5th as hot 1.9-1 QEII Cup favorite, unable to catch those who raced forward. Voted into the Arima Kinen, she raced a bit closer, broke clear and outfought Shahryar to win. After a fracture she returned at 4, 11th in the Takarazuka Kinen, then won the All Comers and the QEII Cup as favorite - a true Grand Prix winner. 4th defending the Arima Kinen after a rough trip, she was champion older mare.
3417=Debuting early, in June at 3 (old age system), he won a newcomer race and was 2nd in the Hakodate 3yo S. (now Hakodate 2yo S.). He won 5 of 9 that year incl. the Hokkaido 3yo S. (now Sapporo 2yo S.), unplaced only once. At 4, on the classic path, he was 10th in the Yayoi Sho, 12th in the Satsuki Sho and 25th in the Derby. After about 3 months off he won a Hakodate open race, the new local-racing invitational and the Lord Derby CT - 3 straight to end the year. He won the Tokyo Shimbun Hai at 5, raced until 6 and retired.
3418=A Northern Dancer sire son who didn't found his own line but sired G1 winners like Opening Verse and Palace Music, and Palace Music sired Cigar. His granddam Flaming Page was Nijinsky's dam, and his sire Northern Dancer too - a pedigree very close to Nijinsky's. Unbeaten in 3 from September at 2 incl. the Dewhurst S. (G1). At 3 he won the 2000 Guineas Trial S. (G3), then was 3rd and 2nd in the English and Irish 2000 Guineas, before a storming run: the Derby, then the Irish Derby easily - only the 5th to win both - and the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. as favorite over older horses. With a huge syndicate already formed, he retired as a precaution to stud in America.
3419=US turf champion of 1978 with 12 wins incl. 2 G1s, best son of Minnesota Mac, a rare Himyar-line sire. Debuting in October at 2, he lost both starts; 8th in his first race at 3, he then won 10 straight: a maiden easily, 4 allowances, the Long Branch S. (G3) and Leonard Richards S. (G3), then the Lexington H., Secretariat S. and Lawrence Realization S. (G2s). 3rd in his first G1, the Man o' War S., he won the Canadian International S. and Washington DC International - US turf champion. He retired to stud that year; no notable foals.
3420=A great European sprinter of 1980-1981. Debuting in England in August at 2, he won his 2nd start and 2 more. At 3 he was 2nd in the Poulains (G1), then won the July C. for his first G1, the Sprint C. (G2), the Prix de l'Abbaye, the Challenge S. (G3) and the Prix de la Foret - 4 straight, 3 G1s. At 4, 2nd defending the July C., he won the Prix Maurice de Gheest (G2) in record time, 2 G3s and the 1400m Prix de la Foret again for a 4th G1, and retired. At stud his 2nd-crop Lockton won the Irish National S. (G1), his only G1 winner, but he sired steady runners in Germany and England.
3421=An Irish mare who came to Japan for the 2nd and 3rd Japan Cups, winning the 3rd after a fierce duel with Kyoei Promise. Slow to develop, she won her debut in spring at 3, then not again until spring at 4. After more near-misses she was invited to the Japan Cup, 4th as foreign horses swept the top places. At 5 she blossomed, winning 3 straight middle-distance stakes; 4th in the Eclipse S. (G1), she won the Joe McGrath Memorial S. (now Irish Champion S.), her first G1. Invited again to the Japan Cup as 3rd choice among the favored foreigners, she outfought Kyoei Promise, bidding to be the first Japanese winner - a 2nd G1. At 6, 4th in a summer G2, she retired. As a broodmare, nothing notable; her daughter Sundrine was imported to Japan as a broodmare.
