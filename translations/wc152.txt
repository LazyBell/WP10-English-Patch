3386=A Tokai-region star with 20 wins from 23 between 1981 and 1983, winner of the Tokai Triple Crown. Winning from autumn at 3 (old age system), he won 10 straight through the Chunichi Sports Hai and Tokai Derby at 4. 2nd in the Gifu Okan Sho, he won the Gifu Kinsho that autumn for the Triple Crown, then 8 straight to spring at 5. At stud he sired JRA Tokai S. winner Gold Proof and Oi Kimpai winner Gold Safe.
3387=Unbeaten in 8 at Oi in 1981-1982, he moved to the JRA and was hailed as "the second Haiseiko." Winning easily from debut at 3 (old age system), he won 8 straight to the Haneda Hai. Injury kept him out of the Tokyo Derby; moving to the JRA in autumn, he crushed Satsuki Sho winner Azuma Hunter in the St. Lite Kinen. Aimed at the Japan Cup, he was plagued by injury, won 1 of 2 more and retired; his only loss was a 2nd to Frost King in an open race. At stud he sired Satsuki Sho winner Doctor Spurt and many local stars.
3388=One of Haiseiko's best, winner of the Haneda Hai and Tokyo Derby in the 1984 Southern Kanto Triple Crown. Winning 9 straight from debut at 3 (old age system), his first loss was a 3rd in the Kuroshio Hai. He beat that race's winner, State Jaguar, in the Haneda Hai and held off Rocky Tiger in the Tokyo Derby, but was 2nd to Rocky Tiger in the Tokyo Okan Sho. Moving to the JRA at 4, 13th in the Yasuda Kinen on his first turf start, he was 2nd in the Sapporo Kinen. At stud he sired Breeders' Gold C. winner Snow Endeavor.
3389=A Hokkaido horse who moved to Southern Kanto and won the Haneda Hai and Tokyo Derby of the 1990 Triple Crown, one of Haiseiko's best. 3rd in the Hokkaido 3yo Yushun (old age system), he moved to Urawa and won 7 of 8 with a 2nd, incl. both races. 4th in the Tokyo Okan Sho, perhaps too far. Back in Hokkaido midway through 5, he won 3 more by 7 and retired.
3390=A star of Utsunomiya, a track that closed in 2006, and winner of the Tochigi Triple Crown. He won 2 quickly at 3 (old age system), then kept missing until spring at 4, when he won the Shimotsuke Satsuki Sho, the Tochigi Derby easily, and the Shimotsuke Kikuka Sho for the Triple Crown. He hit his peak with 7 straight wins late at 6 into 7, but at 8 he was killed in a traffic accident on the way back from training.
3391=An Oi horse who won his first 11 races from 1992 to 1994. Debuting late in July at 4 (old age system), he won 4 of 4 that year and 4 of 4 at 5, then kept winning at 6, 11 straight incl. the Kimpai and After 5 Star Sho. In the Grand Champion 2000 he broke down and was put down.
3392=An Oi horse who won the Haneda Hai and Tokyo Okan Sho of the 1993 Southern Kanto Triple Crown. Debuting in September at 3 (old age system), he won 7 straight to the Haneda Hai. 5th in the Tokyo Derby, he won the Tokyo Okan Sho in autumn. After that he won only the Tokyo City Hai at 6; moving to Hokkaido in autumn at 7, he was winless in 4 and retired.
3393=An Oi horse, unbeaten in 5 to the 1997 Haneda Hai. Injury kept him out of the other legs, and he went on to a turbulent career, incl. a move to Hokkaido and a comeback at 12. After about 1.5 years off, he returned late at 4 (modern ages used, as the system changed during his career) in the Tokyo Daishoten, 10th, then won the Frontier Sprint Hai at 5. Another long break, then 1 run in Hokkaido late at 6, 3 at Oi from January at 7, over 2 years off, and back in Hokkaido from August at 9, winning 3. Retired at 11, he returned at 12, running 5th, 3rd, 3rd, 4th, and retired for good at the end of 13.
3394=A Sonoda horse who won the 2001 Hyogo Triple Crown - the only Thoroughbred to do so since the series was set up. Winning 4 straight late at 2 (modern ages) into 3, he won the Sonoda Derby, Hyogo Championship and Kikusui Sho - 7 straight. JRA horses have won the Hyogo Championship every year since; he is its only local winner. Racing until early at 9 with 12 stakes wins, he retired, but with fertility problems he aimed for a comeback in Hokkaido; scratched with a fever, he moved to Kasamatsu at 10, winning 2, then to Kochi at 11, winning 2 more, and retired.
3395=Southern Kanto dual classic winner of 2005. Debuting at Funabashi, he won 2 straight and was 2nd in the Zen-Nihon 2yo Yushun. At 3 he won 2 more, the Haneda Hai, and the Tokyo Derby by 4 lengths. A Japan Dirt Derby clash with JRA star Kane Hekili was awaited, but injury ruled him out. Back in autumn, he was 8th in the Tokyo Daishoten; after another long rest, 9th in it late at 4. Winless after, he retired after an 11th in the After 5 Star Sho in August at 7.
3396=A great sire who dominated Japanese racing in the 1950s-60s, known as the sire of Shinzan. His foals won 113 JRA graded races, the national record until Sunday Silence. An English horse, he won 2 of 8; unplaced in the 2000 Guineas and Derby, he won the Irish Derby and St. George S. and retired at 3. Unimpressive at stud in Ireland, he was bought in 1955 by Japan's Hidaka breeders' association. His first Japanese crop included Tenno Sho winner Yamanin Moa; the next, 3 classic winners - Shin Tsubame, Sugihime and Hakusho. In the 1960s came 5-crown winner Shinzan, Kikuka Sho winner Daikoter, Tenno Sho winner Yamato Kyodai and more; 7 times Japan's champion sire. In an era unkind to homebred sires, only Shinzan succeeded at stud, and that line ended with Mihoshinzan and Mai Shinzan. Many daughters bred on, so his blood lives on through dams.
3397=Winner of the Satsuki Sho and Kikuka Sho soon after the war, with 21 wins from 31 incl. 10 in record time. As a brother of St Lite he was valued at stud, and held his own in the import era with 10 straight years among the top-10 sires. Dominant to the Satsuki Sho, he was hot Derby favorite but ran off in front and collapsed to 7th - a huge upset. He then won 11 straight incl. the Kikuka Sho and St. Lite Kinen. Burdened by heavy weights, he won only the Sapporo Kinen and Tokyo Hai after, and retired. His first crop had Kikuka Sho winner Kitano O, followed by Derby winner Komatsu Hikari, Tenno Sho winner Tosa O and others - 7 Big Eight winners. Against imported Hindostan and Rising Flame he never topped the list, but was twice 2nd. His foals' 1,135 JRA wins were the homebred-sire record until Fuji Kiseki.
3398=An imported sire star of the 1960s, siring Derby winners Keystone and Teito O and many more; champion sire in 1966. An Irish horse, he won 6 of 9 incl. the Irish 2000 Guineas and Cork & Orrery S. At stud in Ireland for 9 years to 1958, he sired Irish 2000 Guineas winner Lucero and English dual classic filly Sweet Solera. Imported to Japan, he quickly sired graded winners like Solona Onward and Buru Takachiho; his 3rd crop had 3 Big Eight winners - Derby winner Keystone, Oaks winner Verona and Oka Sho winner Hatsuyuki - and the next, Derby winner Teito O. In 1966 he displaced dominant Hindostan as champion sire. In an era unkind to homebred sires, no heir emerged and his line ended.
3399=Winner of the 1950 Satsuki Sho and Derby. Bidding to be the 2nd Triple Crown winner after St Lite, he was 2nd in the Kikuka Sho. Debuting in November at 3 (old age system), he first won in his 8th start, in April at 4. 2 runs later, on his beloved bad ground, he won the Satsuki Sho by 4 lengths as 4th choice; Derby favorite, he broke clear from a handy spot to win. After 2 open wins in autumn, he looked the Kikuka Sho winner from a handy spot, but Satsuki Sho runner-up High Record swooped and beat him by a head. He won 3 of 10 more to autumn at 5 and retired. His best foal was spring Nakayama Daishogai winner Kuroshio.
3400=An imported sire dominant from the late 1950s to early 1960s, rival of Hindostan; together they ruled Japanese racing. An English horse, he won 7 of 25 without a stakes win, 5th in the 2000 Guineas and Derby. At stud in England, he was imported to Japan the year after imports were allowed again. His first crop had graded winners Seiyu and Rising Winner; the next, sire son Hishi Masaru (I). Stars kept coming, and he was champion sire 3 years running from 1958. His foals were mostly sprinters and milers, then thought inferior, so people joked "Hindostan for quality, Rising Flame for quantity." Still, his 1,379 career and 176 single-year JRA wins stood until Northern Taste and Sunday Silence. His line ran through Hishi Masaru to Hishi Masahide and Hishi Speed, then declined; today his name survives mainly in pedigrees like Nihon Pillow Winner's.
3401=Unbeaten, he won the 1951 Satsuki Sho and Derby and was famous even outside racing, but died of tetanus just after the Derby - "the Phantom Horse." Rated low early, he ran unnamed as "Perfect," his farm's name, but after an easy debut win was renamed Tokino Minoru. He won 5 straight to the Asahi Hai 3yo S. and 8 straight by spring at 4 (old age system). Hot Satsuki Sho favorite, he broke the record by over 6 seconds, then won the Derby in record time too - 10 of 10, 7 in record time. Falling ill right after the Derby, he died of tetanus 17 days later. The news spread nationwide, and novelist Nobuko Yoshiya's tribute calling him "the Phantom Horse" became his epithet. Today the 3yo Kyodo News Hai carries the subtitle "Tokino Minoru Kinen."
3402=Winner of the 1952 Oka Sho and Oaks, the first filly to win both. Her name was meant to read "Sweet Sue," but a clerical slip produced this spelling. Winning 4 of her first 5, she was 5th in the Asahi Hai 3yo S. (old age system). At 4 she won the Oka Sho in record time - a Big Eight title. 8th in the Derby, she won the Yasuda Sho (now Yasuda Kinen), then the Oaks, then held in autumn, as 2nd choice - the fillies' double. 2nd against colts in the Kikuka Sho, and as an older mare she won the Yasuda Sho again. From 6 she raced at Oi, winning at least 9 of 16 on record. As a broodmare, only Golden Beam (4 JRA wins) stood out; she is 4th dam of 1998 February S. winner Gourmet Frontier.
3403=Bought from Ireland by the Shadai group in 1961, he defined an era in Japan, siring Kikuka Sho winner Knit Eight, Oaks winners Shadai Turkin and Hiroyoshi and more - the foundation of Shadai. Racing in France, he won the Poule d'Essai des Poulains, Prix de la Foret and Prix Ganay, and in England as an older horse the Coronation S. and Hardwicke S. At stud in Ireland he sired Irish St Leger winner Barclay. Bought by Shadai at 12, he became a leading sire in Japan, champion sire in 1969 over long-dominant Hindostan. The pillar of a Shadai group with no other top sire, he contributed hugely to its growth. Knit Eight failed at stud and his stars were mostly fillies, so his male line died out.
