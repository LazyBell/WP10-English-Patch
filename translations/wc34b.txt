343=Until spring at 4 he was an unremarkable horse, winning and losing dirt sprints in lower classes. But in his first turf race, the semi-open Dotonbori S., he won as 6th choice, was 3rd in the Silk Road S. (OP) and went to the Yasuda Kinen. Only 10th choice, he won by a length and a half - his first open win, in his 18th start. He later dueled Oguri Cap in the Mile Championship and won the first G1 Sprinters S., a top sprinter.
345=He won his first graded race, the Sapporo Kinen, at 4, then couldn't win and switched to jumps in November. Winning 1 of 2, he returned to the flat and led all the way to win the Niigata Daishoten as 7th choice. He then breezed in front to win the G1 Takarazuka Kinen ahead of Kamino Cresse. Half a year after jumping fences, he was the Mejiro crop's third G1 winner after McQueen and Ryan. As 15th choice in the Arima Kinen, his "second wind" held off Tokai Teio and the rest - a shock Grand Prix double, earning champion older male and champion Japanese-sired horse. His sire Mejiro Eagle was also a famous front-runner.
346=Based at local Iwate. He won his debut at 2, then developed a tendon injury in his left foreleg. After 19 months off he returned at 4 and won race after race, 18 straight from his debut, then a Japanese record. After one 2nd he won 11 more, including the Michinoku Daishoten, Tohoku Thoroughbred Daishoten, Kitakamigawa Daishoten, Nambu Hai and Toka Sho. At 7 he suffered his second loss to rival Moriyu Prince in the Michinoku Daishoten, then won 8 more. Leg trouble kept him from racing outside Tohoku, but 39 wins, three 2nds and a 3rd from 43, never out of the top two in 41 straight - worthy of "the Iwate monster."
349=He debuted early in the year, won 3 straight, then won the Satsuki Sho by 5 lengths. A father-son Triple Crown seemed certain, but a fracture after the Satsuki Sho ruled out the Derby, and on his return he was a dismal 5th in the rain in the St. Lite Kinen. Still, he won the Kyoto Shimbun Hai by 2 lengths and was a confident Kikuka Sho favorite, winning clearly with the bottomless stamina of his sire. He was 2nd to Symboli Rudolf in the Arima Kinen, but no one doubted his future. Yet at 4, partly due to a fracture, he went winless all year. Just when he seemed done, at 5 he won 2 graded races and then the Tenno Sho (Spring) by a whisker in a photo - a father-son Tenno Sho double.
357=Northern Taste's last star. Injury-prone, he had many long breaks, but at 5 he won 4 straight to reach open class, and at 7 won his first graded race, the Fuji S., giving his sire a record 20 straight crops with a graded winner.
