439=The 1988 Arc winner, who went to stud in Japan and built an era alongside Sunday Silence and Brian's Time. Until 3 he only ran well in graded races, emerging at 4: he won 4 straight including the Gran Premio di Milano and Gran Premio del Jockey Club, then was 2nd in both the Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud and the Arc. At 5 he won the Premio Presidente della Repubblica and Gran Premio di Milano, and in autumn finally won the Arc. He then went to Japan for the Japan Cup as favorite but was 5th to Pay the Butler, retiring after it to stud in Japan. His first crop gave Derby winner Winning Ticket, fillies' double winner Vega, mile queen North Flight and Tenno Sho winner Sakura Chitose O, and he was leading sire in 1994. Later came Air Groove, Jungle Pocket and many more - a huge success. But heirs were scarce; Jungle Pocket and Miracle Admire sired G1 winners, yet his direct line is now declining.
440=In his first graded race, the Derby, he was a fine 2nd to unbeaten double winner Tokai Teio. That autumn he held off Ibuki Maikagura by a length and a half in the Kikuka Sho for his first G1 - in his 7th start, then the fastest ever. But his brilliant career ended there. The incurable tendon injury struck his glass legs, and like many of his sire's runners, he couldn't escape leg trouble. Back a year later in the Arima Kinen, he was a dismal 13th, ran once more and retired to stud.
441=A highly rated foreign-bred, he won his debut but injury cost him his 2yo year and spring at 3. Returning in May at 3, he raced mainly in his class and matured in autumn at 4. Stepping up in the Keisei Hai Autumn H., he won easily in a Japanese record 1:31.5. Favorite for the Sprinters S., he was 10th, but then won the Mile Championship for a G1. Little followed, and he retired at 5 to stud, siring Fuji S. winner Meiner Seagull and Asahi Hai FS runner-up Meiner Robust.
442=Top two in 70% of his races, never out of the top five, and outside the top three only once (5th in the Derby) - yet just 5th choice for the Satsuki Sho. At 2 he was 2nd to Daishin Fubuki in the Asahi Hai Sansai S., then 3rd to filly Dyna Fairy in the Keisei Hai and narrowly beaten by Daishin Fubuki again in the Yayoi Sho, so fans saw him as a horse who couldn't win. But in the Satsuki Sho he held off Fresh Voice by a neck, shedding that image. 5th to Dyna Gulliver in the Derby, he then beat future great miler Nippo Teio by a length and a half in the Radio Tampa Sho. Leg trouble then ended his career, and he went to stud. Though minor, he sired perfect miler Trot Thunder (89: La Seine Wonder), unbeaten at a mile with 2 G1s, the Yasuda Kinen and Mile Championship, and Kabutoyama Kinen and Kokura Daishoten winner One More Love Way (89: Zengi Ghost) - a fine effort.
443=From his second maiden race he won 4 straight, including the NHK Mile C., but injury cost him over six months. He struggled for a long time after returning, but won the Keisei Hai Autumn H. in autumn at 4. At 5 he acted up in the gate just before the Lord Derby Challenge T., was injured and had to be put down.
444=Strongly carrying the blood of Lyphard, sire of many fast horses, he starred from a mile to middle distances. The turning point was the NZT Yonsai S. at 3: after 8ths in the Satsuki Sho and NHK Hai, he switched to the mile and won by three and a half lengths with his speed. He won the Hakodate Kinen and Swan S., lost the Mile Championship as heavy favorite by a nose to Takara Steel, and was named champion sprinter. At 4 he was 2nd in the Yasuda Kinen and Takarazuka Kinen, then led all the way to win the Tenno Sho (Autumn), and avenged himself in the Mile Championship by 5 lengths - champion sprinter again, plus champion older male.
445=2nd on debut, he won his next 3 including the Kyodo News Hai Yonsai S., becoming a classic contender. He was a disappointing 10th in the Satsuki Sho, but won the Japanese Derby by half a length from Grandpa's Dream to reach the top of his crop. He showed his class with a 2nd in the Kikuka Sho, then beat seasoned older horses like Miho Shinzan and Gallop Dyna in the Arima Kinen, earning Horse of the Year and champion 3yo colt.
446=Unbeaten in 3, including the Kyodo News Hai Yonsai S., he was a classic star, but injury cost him the whole spring. He was 4th in both the Kyoto Shimbun Hai and Kikuka Sho on his return. At 4 his talent bloomed: he beat crop rival Suda Hawk in the Osaka Hai; ran out of stamina in the Tenno Sho (Spring); crushed mile king Nippo Teio in the Mainichi Okan; and beat double winner Miho Shinzan and other greats in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) for a G1. Every fan regretted his early retirement that year, but given his success at stud it was the right call: he sired Sakura Bakushin O (89: Sakura Hagoromo) and many graded winners.
447=With an astonishing finish he won 3 graded races, including the Hanshin Sansai S. His fiery temper meant he always raced from the back, but his all-or-nothing charges thrilled fans. At 3 he beat Asahi Hai winner Lindo Shaver in the Yayoi Sho, then met Tokai Teio for the first time in the Satsuki Sho, finishing 4th. He won the NHK Hai (G2, 2000m) clearly, hoping for revenge in the Derby, but was injured just before it. In autumn he was 2nd in both the Kyoto Shimbun Hai and the Kikuka Sho, just short. After 3 more races, leg trouble ended his career without glory.
448=He finally won in his 7th start, in May at 3, then took off, winning 3 straight up to the Yamayuri S. After summer he was 2nd in the Kobe Shimbun Hai, his first graded try, then in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai swept past Derby winner Winner's Circle and improver Osaichi George for his first graded win, and was Kikuka Sho favorite. His sire Bamboo Atlas won the Derby but was injured after the Kyoto Shimbun Hai and went to stud before the Kikuka Sho. Carrying his sire's regret, Begin moved up from midfield and pulled away in the straight to win. Trainer Fuse, who also trained Bamboo Atlas, said, "Winning the Kikuka Sho with an Atlas foal was my dream." But injury followed, and the Kikuka Sho was his last race.
449=A leading miler of 1980s Europe and America, she won 10 G1s, including two Prix Jacques le Marois and two Breeders' Cup Miles, and as a broodmare produced Kingmambo and East of the Moon - a legendary great mare. She won 2 G1s at 2 and was France's champion 2yo filly. At 3 she won the English and French 1000 Guineas, the Prix Jacques le Marois and Prix du Moulin, then won the Breeders' Cup Mile in America in record time. At 4 she won the Prix d'Ispahan, then both the Jacques le Marois and Breeders' Cup Mile again. Dominant at a mile with 9 wins and two 2nds from 11, she retired that year. Her first foal, Kingmambo, was a huge success on the track and at stud. Her granddaughter Loves Only Me, imported to Japan, produced two G1 winners: Loves Only You and Real Steel.
450=She won 3 straight including the Sapporo Sansai S., then had two six-month breaks. Back the next year, she kept falling short until autumn, when she won the Autumn Sprint S. and Himba Tokyo Times Hai, then ran a fine 5th in the Mile Championship despite a slow start.
451=Called the greatest mare in American history by some. Unbeaten in 13, with 8 G1s including the Breeders' Cup Distaff, she was nicknamed "Miss Perfect." A serious injury in autumn at 2 cost her until autumn at 3, but at 4 she won G1 after G1 as if making up for it, beating 7-time G1 winner Gulch and other males in the Whitney H. Then came the Breeders' Cup Distaff. Among great mares like Winning Colors and Goodbye Halo, she was a clear 1.5 favorite. Far behind leaders Winning Colors and Goodbye Halo at the top of the straight, she shrugged off the sloppy track and ran them down to complete her unbeaten legend. She retired after the race. A superb broodmare, she produced 3 G1 winners including My Flag, and second foal Our Emblem, though no G1 winner, succeeded at stud.
452=Won 7 G1s in America, including the Kentucky Oaks, an idol whose battles with Derby-winning filly Winning Colors made her popular. Dominant from her debut, she won 2 G1s at 2, the Demoiselle S. and Hollywood Starlet S. At 3 she clashed with the unbeaten Winning Colors, winning the Las Virgenes S. but losing the Santa Anita Oaks. She then won the Mother Goose S. and CCA Oaks, meeting Winning Colors again in the Breeders' Cup Distaff, but older star Personal Ensign won, with Winning Colors 2nd and her 3rd. Despite 4 G1 wins that year, champion 3yo filly went to Winning Colors. At 4 she won the La Canada S. but lost narrowly in G1s, retiring after a 6th in the Breeders' Cup Distaff. As a broodmare in Japan she produced Takamatsunomiya Kinen winner King Halo.
453=From his debut on turf he kept finishing 2nd to 4th, losing 12 straight, before finally winning in his 13th start in autumn at 3. He still struggled on turf, but switched to dirt in summer at 4 he was transformed, quickly winning 3 to reach open class. In his first graded race, the Heian S., he charged from last to win. He was 11th in his first G1, the February S., but then won the Tokai S. and Elm S. After a 4th in the Japan Cup Dirt he won JRA-local races like the Nagoya Daishoten and Breeders' GC, then was 2nd to Kane Hekili in the Japan Cup Dirt. Among the best in a deep dirt division, but as a closer he dropped more races than front-runners, and won nothing from '09.
454=She ran in 35 G1s in six countries, including Japan and America, winning 8 - then a record for a European mare - and was called "the Iron Lady" for her toughness. From 3 she ran a punishing schedule: the 1000 Guineas, an Irish 1000 Guineas trial, the Irish 2000 Guineas and Irish 1000 Guineas, four weeks running, then the Oaks and Irish Derby. Her only title was the Irish 2000 Guineas - the first ever won by a filly. At 4 she won just one G1 in 11 races in six months. At 5 she matured, winning 4 G1s: the Prix Ganay, Coronation C., International S. and Phoenix Champion S. She was 3rd in both the King George and the Arc, then 4th in the Japan Cup, but won the Fuji S. with an otherworldly finish, stunning fans. At 6 she won the Coronation C. again and retired. Great things were hoped of her as a broodmare, but she died in an accident right after her first covering, leaving no offspring.
455=Raced mainly on turf until 6, winning 6 and placing 3rd in the CBC Sho at 5, but he plateaued and switched to dirt at 7. He won the Garnet S. and then the Negishi S., and in autumn added the Tokyo Hai and Hyogo Gold T., becoming a top dirt sprinter. At 8 in '07 he raced mostly in JRA-local races, winning 3 graded races including a second Tokyo Hai. From '08 he won the Sakitama Hai and was 3rd in the JBC Sprint, showing no decline, and retired after a 7th in the Tokyo Sprint in April 2010, at 11. 14 wins from 63 - a horse who truly paid his owner back.
456=A filly who matched or beat the colts, winning the Tenno Sho (Autumn), and was called "the Empress." Talented early, she was the classic star. A fever kept her out of the Oka Sho, but she romped in the Oaks to prove herself best of her crop. After a fracture, she showed her true power on returning at 4: she won the Mermaid S. and Sapporo Kinen, then beat a strong field in the Tenno Sho (Autumn). 2nd to Pilsudski in the Japan Cup, best of the Japanese, and 3rd to Silk Justice in the Arima Kinen, she became the first female Horse of the Year since Tomei. The next year she was 2nd again, close behind El Condor Pasa, in the Japan Cup. As a broodmare she produced Admire Groove, Forgettable and Rulership, cementing her status as the greatest of great mares, before dying in April 2013.
457=Small at around 420 kg, she won her debut by 9 lengths, then the Sazanka Sho and Elfin S. After a 4th in the Tulip Sho, she beat London Bridge in the Oka Sho for her first classic. The longer trip told in the Oaks, where she was 3rd, but in autumn she won the Shuka Sho for a fillies' double. As an older mare she struggled against colts, but won the Queen Elizabeth II Cup at 5, her first win in 2 years, showing her class against fillies. Her half-brother Kizuna, 15 years younger, won the 2013 Japanese Derby.
458=At a US 2yo training sale he clocked under 11 seconds for a furlong and sold for a fair $120,000 despite an obscure pedigree. He debuted in October in a 1200m dirt race at Kyoto, winning by 7 lengths, then, stepping up in distance, dominated the Hyogo Junior Grand Prix and Zen-Nihon 2yo Yushun to top his crop on dirt. At 3 he aimed for the classics, starting on turf in the Arlington C., but did nothing and finished 12th. Back on dirt he struggled to beat Nathaniel, already beaten before, in the Fukuryu S., and lost a duel by a neck to Golden Ticket in the Hyogo Championship, his first dirt defeat. After a 6th in the Japan Dirt Derby and 2nds in the Leopard S. and Tokyo Hai, he won the JRA-local G1 JBC Sprint against older horses, breaking clear from up front like a champion. But in the Japan Cup Dirt he couldn't get his usual forward position and was 14th. At 4 he lacked sparkle in JRA graded races - 4th in the Negishi S., 9th in the February S. At local tracks he won the Kurofune Sho and Tokyo Sprint, then went over a year without a win despite running well. In summer at 5, carrying 58.5 kg, he romped by 4 lengths in Saga's Summer Champion, then won the Tokyo Hai. In the big one, the JBC Sprint, against 3-time sprint graded winner Seikrimson, defending champion Summer Wind and turf convert Dasher Go Go, he unleashed a powerful finish to win it again after 2 years. He also won the Hyogo Gold T. at 5 and was 3rd in the JBC Sprint at 6 - a star of recent dirt sprinting.
459=After making her name in Argentina, she moved to America and won 12 G1s, including two Breeders' Cup Distaffs - then the record for a mare - and was Eclipse champion older mare in 1989 and 1990. In Argentina she just missed in G1s, then won the Gran Premio Palermo (G1) by 12 lengths in November at 3, earning a move to America. She struggled to win in her first year there, but showed her true worth from 1989, at 5, winning 7 G1s from the Santa Margarita Invitational H. to the Breeders' Cup Distaff. At 6 she won 5 more G1s, including another Distaff, and was champion older mare again. At 7 her form faded, and after 3 losses she retired. Her 4 foals disappointed - 2 unraced, 1 maiden, 1 single winner - but her grandchildren include G1 winners Affluent and Fort Larned.
460=She won the Suzuran Sho by 6 lengths and the Hakodate Sansai S. by 5 - a talent called a sure classic bet. But her temper was the miscalculation. At 3 she refused to enter the gate for the Sumire Sho, had to retake a training exam and missed the Oka Sho. After the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East) she made her long-sought Oaks, finishing 3rd. Winless at 3, she slumped in spring at 4 too. But teamed with master Yukio Okabe, she tried a late charge for the first time in the Keio Hai Autumn H. and won in a time equal to the then world record. She lost the Tenno Sho (Autumn), Japan Cup and Arima Kinen, but at 5 won the Sprinters S. (G2) and Keio Hai Spring C., starring in graded races. In the Yasuda Kinen she dueled Nippo Teio, losing by just 0.2 seconds in 2nd. After a 4th in Tamamo Cross's Tenno Sho (Autumn), she retired. As a dam she produced Stage Champ (90: Real Shadai).
