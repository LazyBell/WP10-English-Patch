3676=The 1970 Kikuka Sho winner. His crop was ruled by Satsuki Sho and Derby winner Tanino Moutiers, but he took the final classic, denying him the Triple Crown. Debuting late at 3 (old age system), he climbed through allowances by spring at 4 and joined the classics in the Derby, a close 2nd, half a length behind Tanino Moutiers - suddenly famous. He won the Mainichi Hai; with champion Tanino Moutiers unwell, he was fancied in the Kikuka Sho and seized the chance. 3rd in the Arima Kinen, best of the 4yos. At 5, 2nd in the spring Kyoto Kinen and Milers C., then injuries limited him, and he retired after a 5th in an open race in autumn at 7. Not a success at stud, he died at 11.
3677=The 1971 Oka Sho winner, leading filly of that year's classics and champion 4yo filly (old age system). Debuting in July at 3, she won her 2nd start, then the Salvia Sho over later Oaks winner Kane Himuro, and an open race - 3 wins from 7, nearly always top 3. At 4, after the Hanshin 4yo Himba Tokubetsu, she was Oka Sho favorite despite worries about bad ground, and won. She won the 4yo Himba Tokubetsu, but was 10th as Oaks favorite, finding nothing. Quiet the rest of that year, she won 3 at 5 incl. the BSN Hai and Niigata Nippo Sho, 2nd in the Fukushima Daishoten, and retired. Few foals, but daughter Nasuno Aroma's line - Magic Morning, Magic Victoria - continues.
3678=The 1971 Oaks winner. Oka Sho winner Nasuno Kaori was the star, but as she struggled on bad ground, this filly won as 10th choice - a huge upset. Debuting in August at 3 (old age system), she won easily, then her 4th start and her first race at 4. Then a slump: 12th Queen C., 18th Hanshin 4yo Himba Tokubetsu, 15th Oka Sho, 8th in the 4yo Himba Tokubetsu - ignored for the Oaks. On bad ground she flew, with what jockey Okabe called a demon's finish: "She's just brilliant in the mud." 4th in the Victoria C., she won no more and retired in March at 6. As a broodmare she produced 1978 Arima Kinen winner Kane Minobu.
3679=A late bloomer who won the 1972 Tenno Sho (Autumn). Debuting at 4 (old age system), he won a maiden and an allowance from 14; at 5 he won 4 more allowances with 5 seconds. At 6 he won 2 in spring, reached open class, was 4th in the Hankyu Hai and 2nd in the Asahi Challenge C., then won the autumn Kyoto Kinen. As 7th choice in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), while 8th choice Passing Goal ran off in front, he sat patiently at the back and swept past in the straight - a Big Eight win. Winless after, but 2nd in the Hanshin Daishoten, the autumn Kyoto Kinen at 7 and the Nihon Keizai Shinshun Hai at 8, then he retired. No notable foals.
3680=Winner of the 1967 Oaks, and as an older mare the Sankei Osaka Hai, Naruo Kinen and more. At 3 (old age system) she won 4 straight to the Daily Hai 3yo S.; hot favorite in the Hanshin 3yo S., she was 3rd, her first loss, but champion 3yo filly. At 4, 12th in the Oka Sho, she won the Oaks; 15th against colts in the Kikuka Sho, but champion 4yo filly. At 5 she beat males in the Sankei Osaka Hai and Naruo Kinen and won the Yuzuki Tokubetsu by 8 lengths - champion older mare, 3 straight years as top filly. Retired to breed, she left just one foal before dying young.
3681=Winner of the 1968 Sprinters S. Unbeaten in 2 at 3 (old age system), he raced at a mile to middle distances from 4, winning the Fukushima Daishoten in autumn. Unplaced often at 5, he won the Sprinters S. back at a sprint for the first time since 3, then the Sekiya Kinen in autumn. Pulled up in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), he left the turf.
3682=Winner of 3 graded races in 1968: the Nakayama Kinen, Yasuda Kinen and Mainichi Okan. Debuting at 4 (old age system), he soon won his way into the Derby (11th) and Kikuka Sho (10th). He won the Kumohata Kinen, then again at 5; at 6, at his peak, he won the Nakayama Kinen, Yasuda Kinen and Mainichi Okan. Over 10 years at stud, no notable foals.
3683=A late-blooming mare who won the 1968 Chukyo Daishoten and CBC Sho. Debuting at 3 (old age system), she was 2nd in the Hanshin 4yo Himba Tokubetsu and Oaks at 4 - talented, but winless, until her first open win in the Chukyo Daishoten in summer at 5. She won the CBC Sho that autumn and retired in spring at 6. A broodmare until 22, her foals never rose above allowances.
3684=Winner of the 1968 Asahi Hai 3yo S. (old age system). With stablemates Wild Moa, Hakuei Ho and Mejiro Asama, he was one of trainer Ogata's "Four Kings," a big classic hope, but was 4th in the Satsuki Sho and 2nd in the Derby. 2nd choice in the Kikuka Sho, he struggled on soft ground, 17th. At 5 he won the Keio Hai Spring H.; with the Tokyo 4yo S. at 4, 3 graded wins. Not a success at stud.
3685=Winner of the 1969 NHK Hai. First winning at 4 (old age system), he won the Renge Sho and Green S. in spring, then the NHK Hai as favorite. 12th to Dai Shinboru Guard in the Derby, he kept losing, 8th in the Kikuka Sho. Rarely racing from 5, he retired after an allowance in January at 6.
3686=A mare who won 3 graded races incl. the 1969 Yasuda Kinen. Winning her debut easily in January at 4 (old age system), she won 3 straight to the 4yo Himba Tokubetsu, then the Queen S. in autumn; last (11th) in the Arima Kinen. Struggling at 5, she won the Yasuda Kinen as 10th choice in record time, and retired that summer. Her only foal never rose above allowances.
3687=A mare with 5 graded wins incl. the 1970 Shorai S. (forerunner of the Takamatsunomiya Hai). Winning 5 of 9 with 3 seconds at 3 (old age system), she won the Hanshin 4yo Himba Tokubetsu at 4; 9th in the Oka Sho and 6th in the Oaks, she won the Hanshin Himba Tokubetsu that December. At 5 she won the Kimpai (West) and Naruo Kinen, 2nd in the Swan S.; at 6 she won the Shorai S. for a 5th graded win and retired. She left 4 daughters.
3688=Winner of the 1970 Sankei Osaka Hai and 1971 Takamatsunomiya Hai, by English sire Parthia, raising his name in Japan. Winning his debut at 3 (old age system), he climbed allowances at 4 and battled in stakes without winning, then won the Sankei Osaka Hai that spring. At 5 he won the Takamatsunomiya Hai, and retired in spring at 6. His half-brother, a year younger, Shunsaku Ryu, won the spring Kyoto Kinen.
3689=Winner of the 1970 Victoria C. (forerunner of the QEII Cup), created that year as the fillies' final classic; she was its first winner. Debuting late at 3 (old age system), she first won at 4, 16th in the Oka Sho. Climbing through allowances, she beat Oaks winner Jupiq in the autumn Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu, then won the Victoria C. Winless after, she retired in spring at 5. No stars of her own, but via daughter Kunino Kiyoko came grandson Daiyusaku, an Arima Kinen winner.
3690=Winner of 4 graded races incl. the 1970 Asahi Hai 3yo S. (old age system). After 4 straight 2nds, he won 3 straight to the Asahi Hai 3yo S. A classic contender at 4, he missed the Satsuki Sho, short of work, was 20th in the Derby, then 3rd in the Kikuka Sho. At 5 he won the Hakodate Kinen, 4th in the Arima Kinen; at 6 the AJCC and spring Meguro Kinen. Placed but winless in Big Eight races, he raced steadily until early at 8. No notable foals.
3691=Winner of the 1970 Hanshin 3yo S. (old age system). Winning his 2nd start, he won the Hokkaido 3yo S. (forerunner of the Sapporo 2yo S.) and Hakodate 3yo S., an open race, and the Hanshin 3yo S. - 5 straight. At 4 the classic trips didn't suit him; just 2 open wins. Older, he won the Milers C. and Keihan Hai, strong at a mile to middle distances. He left few foals at stud.
3692=Winner of the 1971 Sankei Osaka Hai. Debuting at 4 (old age system), he reached open class by summer in 13 starts, then tried stakes from autumn, 4th in the CBC Sho - 19 starts that year, a tough campaign. Improving at 5 and 6, he won the Kimpai (West) early at 7, then 3rd in the spring Kyoto Kinen, 2nd in the Chukyo Kinen, and won the Sankei Osaka Hai. 4th to Mejiro Musashi in the Takarazuka Kinen, he retired.
3693=A mare who won the 1971 Yasuda Kinen. Debuting in September at 3 (old age system), she won 1 of 6. At 4, overshadowed by Oka Sho winner Tamami, she was 3rd in the Oka Sho and 2nd in the 4yo Himba Tokubetsu, then won the Queen S. in autumn and beat Tamami in the Himba Tokyo Times Hai (forerunner of the Fuchu Himba S.). At 5 she won the Yasuda Kinen, strong at a mile, and retired at year-end. Her daughters Harbor Never, Harbor Shine and Harbor Freesia carried on the family; a great-grandchild, Arrow September, starred in Southern Kanto.
