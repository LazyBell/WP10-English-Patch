1009=Gelded before his debut, he bloomed in summer at 4. Inconsistent but powerful, he won the '05 Hakodate Kinen and again the next year. After a 5th in the Sapporo Kinen, a tendon injury cost him nearly a year, but in his 2nd race back he won the Hakodate Kinen a 3rd time. 3rd in the All Comers and 4th in the Mainichi Okan, he was 4th going for a 4th Hakodate Kinen in '08, showing his age.
1013=In her first graded race, the Shuka Sho in autumn at 3, she stayed at the back, 12th. Stuck in the lower classes for 18 months, she reached open class at 5, leading all the way in the Minami Hokkaido S. on a light weight. 9th in the Kyoto Daishoten, she then set her own pace in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup and held off Buena Vista - the first Kanto-trained winner since Mejiro Dober in '99. In her last race, the Hong Kong Cup, she led but tired, last of 10.
1016=A tough mare who ran 48 times on turf and dirt. She settled in open class only at 6, then the next spring won the Fukushima Himba S. with a powerful late charge - her first and last graded win.
1024=She won the Flora S. and, with 3 wins from 4, was Oaks favorite in the absence of Vodka and Daiwa Scarlet, but lost by a nose to Robe Decollete. 2nd in the Rose S. and 4th in the Shuka Sho, at 4 she was 3rd in the Fuchu Himba S. and Queen Elizabeth II Cup. With a powerful kick, always among the fastest finishers yet falling short, she was frustrating. In her last race, the '09 Fuchu Himba S., Mood Indigo outkicked her. Her G1 dream passes to her foals.
1029=Slow to rise, he emerged in spring at 5, winning the Niigata Daishoten in a tight finish, then 2nd in the Epsom C. and Asahi Challenge C. After a 10th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) he faded, retiring after the '09 Kyoto Kimpai.
1033=The 1973 Oaks winner. Polished early, with 5 wins and two 2nds from 7 at 3 (old system), she was 2nd in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu, then won the Oaks over its winner Lady Sport and Oka Sho winner Nittoh Chidori, who beat her in the Victoria C. in autumn. Older, she won the Niigata Kinen at 5 and Keio Hai Autumn H. at 6, then retired. Her 6 foals incl. Niigata Daishoten runner-up Nasuno Prince and Meguro Kinen runner-up Nasuno Taizan.
1043=He won his debut, the Daily Hai 2yo S. and Kyoto 2yo S. impressively, but injury kept him out of the Satsuki Sho. He made the Derby but was 4th. After that his best was a 2nd in the Naruo Kinen, below expectations. After a stable move early the next year he suffered a twisted intestine; the surgery succeeded but meant a long break. Back after about a year, he led all the way in an open special, then won the Sekiya Kinen in summer. He later thrilled mile fans with last-to-first charges after slow starts, but never won another graded race, retiring to stud at the end of 7.
1044=He won 3 straight at 3, but was 2nd in the Kokura Daishoten, 4th in the Chukyo Kinen and 5th in the Niigata Daishoten, graded-winless through 4. At 5 he was 2nd in the Kyoto Kimpai and Kokura Daishoten, then won the Hankyu Hai and the Keio Hai Spring C., a mile contender. 3rd in the Yasuda Kinen, he never won again.
1045=A foundation mare of Mejiro Farm whose family produced Mejiro McQueen, Mejiro Durren, Mejiro Phantom and many more. She bred 16 foals over 18 years, from 6 (old age system) to 23, building a great family. A daughter of sire Bostonian, she raced poorly but made her sire's name as a broodmare. She won just 2 allowance races from 25, retiring at the end of 4. Eleven of her foals won, and sons Mejiro Suisei and Mejiro Hawk won graded races. Her daughter Mejiro Iris produced Mejiro Aurora, dam of 2 G1 winners, Mejiro McQueen and Mejiro Durren. Mejiro Harima produced graded winners Mejiro Phantom and Mejiro Jupiter, and later descendants include Shonan Kampf and Shonan Tachyon. Mejiro Farm closed in 2011, but Asama Yuri's blood lives on as a native Japanese family.
1046=Dam of 1980 Satsuki Sho winner Hawaiian Image and graded winners Meiwa Kimiko and Pro Maid. Imported from America, she began breeding in 1973 at 5 (old age system). Her first foal Meiwa Kimiko won the Sprinters S. in 1977 and 1978, and her 3rd, Hawaiian Image, won the Satsuki Sho, one of the 8 big races. He also won the Radio Tampa Sho and Fukushima Kinen - an unusual classic winner who starred at local meetings and on dirt. Her 6th foal Pro Maid won the Kabutoyama Kinen, her 3rd graded winner. After her 7th, Out Power (unraced), she was bred every year until 22 without another foal. Meiwa Kimiko carried on the family, her great-grandson King Joy winning the Nakayama Daishogai twice.
1047=4th in the Oka Sho and 3rd in the Oaks, she went to America for the American Oaks, 2nd. In autumn she was 2nd in the Shuka Sho and 4th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup - superb every time. Older, she was 2nd in the Victoria Mile, then won the Queen S., her long-awaited first graded win, and was 2nd in the Fuchu Himba S. After nearly a year off, she was last in the '08 Fuchu Himba S., a fracture was found, and she retired to breed.
1048=The 1972 Oaks winner; her half-brother a year younger was Derby and Tenno Sho (Spring) winner Take Hope. She won her debut and was 3rd in the Keisei Hai Sansai S., her only 2 starts at 3 (old age system). At 4 she struggled in allowances, won once and went to the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu as 12th choice, but was 2nd to Takai Homa, rising to 3rd choice for the Oaks. There she beat favorite Takai Homa clearly for her only title. She never won again, placing in some open races but crushed in graded races, e.g. 12th in the Lord Derby Challenge T. and 11th in the Kimpai (East). She retired at 6 with 3 wins from 20. Of her 9 foals none won a graded race, but Take Masaru was 2nd in the Kabutoyama Kinen and Take Fighter 2nd in the Mainichi Hai, and 3 daughters carry on her family.
1049=Winning 4 straight to the Hanshin Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (old age system), she was Oka Sho favorite but 7th to Titania. In autumn she was 5th as 3rd choice in the Queen Elizabeth Cup. Older, she was 3rd in the Azuma Kofuji Sho and Fukushima Daishoten, then 2nd choice for the Yasuda Kinen, which she won by 3.5 lengths - a big-race win at last. She retired after it. A prolific dam with 15 foals, she produced no stars.
1050=A mare who won 10 G1s, including the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. twice. She won it at 3 and 4; at 3 she crushed stars like Roberto and Rheingold by 6 lengths, still the race's widest margin, and she is often called the greatest mare. She also won the Benson & Hedges Gold C. twice, the Irish Oaks and Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud, and in America the Washington DC International and Man o' War S. But her crop included Allez France, French fillies' Triple Crown and Arc winner, and in 8 meetings she never beat her. She raced until 6, retiring with 15 wins from 48. At stud she had 13 foals, incl. G1 winners Dahar, Riverman's son Rivlia and Delegant. Rivlia sired Satsuki Sho winner Narita Taishin in Japan, and Dicky Dream sired Sengoku Silver there - a family of note.
1051=A legendary horse who excelled on turf and dirt at any distance. Emerging early, he won 6 straight from autumn at 3 (old age system) to spring at 4, by 7 lengths in the Asahi Hai Sansai S. and 8 in the Tokyo Yonsai S. (now Kyodo News Hai), the top classic hope. But then came a frustrating run of 2nds: Yayoi Sho, Spring S., Satsuki Sho, NHK Hai and Derby - no spring title. In autumn he went to America for the Washington DC International, last of 8. At 5 he showed his true worth, winning 8 straight incl. the Tokyo Shimbun Hai (dirt 2100m), Kyoto Kinen (Spring), Tenno Sho (Spring), Mainichi Okan (dirt 2100m) and the British Fair Commemorative (now Sprinters S.) - 4 in record time, the Tenno Sho with a sprinter's kick. Unstoppable. In autumn he went back to America for the DC International, but fell ill and finished last. Still tired on his return, he retired. Excluding America, he won 16 of 27 in Japan, his losses ten 2nds and one 3rd - superbly consistent. At stud, in an era hard on Japan-bred sires, he produced many graded winners like Dokan Yashima and Ajishiba O, and local stars like Hatsushiba O.
1052=The sire who ruled Japanese racing in the 1970s-80s with Symboli Rudolf, Mejiro Asama, Sakura Shori and more. An Irish racehorse, he won only 2 of 14, but ran respectably even over long trips, 6th in the Irish Derby and 4th in the Irish St. Leger. Bought jointly by Tomohiro Wada of Symboli Farm and Toyokichi Kitano of Mejiro Farm, he was imported to Japan to stand. His son Mejiro Asama soon won the Tenno Sho (Autumn), followed by Derby winner Sakura Shori and 7-time G1 winner Symboli Rudolf. Mejiro Asama led via Mejiro Titan to Mejiro McQueen, Sakura Shori to Sakura Star O, and Symboli Rudolf to Tokai Teio - the great Partholon line. His male line is now nearly gone, but as a broodmare sire of Sirius Symboli, Mejiro Dober and others, his blood lives on widely.
1053=While TTG captivated fans in 1976-77, an astonishing speedster appeared a year behind them: Maruzensky, a Nijinsky colt conceived abroad. Such horses then faced many limits, including a ban from the classics, so after the Asahi Hai Sansai S. he went at 4 (old age system) to the Nihon Tampa Sho, the "consolation Derby." Along the way he faced Satsuki Sho favorite Hishi Speed and later Kikuka Sho winner Press Tosho and toyed with them. Far beyond the classic crop, his jockey Nakanowatari famously pleaded: "Put him in the widest gate. He won't get in anyone's way and needs no prize money. Just let him run in the Derby to test his ability." It never happened, and after 8 wins from 8 with extraordinary power, injury ended his career - the dreamed-of Arima Kinen clash with TTG never came. At stud he sired many G1 winners, including Derby winner Sakura Chiyono O, Kikuka Sho winners Hollyskey and Leo Durban and Takarazuka Kinen winner Suzuka Koban. As a broodmare sire he produced Special Week, Winning Ticket, Mejiro Bright and many more - an extraordinary record as a sire too.
1054=Winner of the 1974 Satsuki Sho and Kikuka Sho. At 3 (old age system) he won his first 4, incl. the Hanshin Sansai S., showing talent early. At 4 he won the Kisaragi Sho and Spring S., and as heavy Satsuki Sho favorite - one of the first horses given a single-bracket entry, introduced that year - he won with authority from a good spot, unbeaten in 7. In the Derby he lacked his usual kick, 3rd to Cornell Lancer. In autumn he won the Kobe Shimbun Hai and Kyoto Shimbun Hai, then the Kikuka Sho as favorite for his 2nd classic, Horse of the Year. At 5 he beat Arima Kinen winner Tanino Chikara in the Milers C., but the trip beat him in the Tenno Sho (Spring), 2nd. Troubled by his legs, he ran anyway in the Arima Kinen, 8th; a fracture was found and he retired. At stud he had no stars but steady graded winners like Kyoto Yonsai Tokubetsu winner Takano Kachidoki and Kinko Sho winner Towa Kachidoki. Sumo wrestler Kitakachidoki (active 1987-2000) took his name after this horse's strength.
1055=Imported to Japan amid the 1970s Northern Dancer boom, this son of his was a huge success, siring G1 winners like Amber Shadai, Dyna Gulliver and Gallop Dyna and transforming Japanese pedigrees. He overturned the dominance of sires like Tesco Boy and Never Beat, champion sire 10 times in 11 years from 1982. As a broodmare sire he was just as dominant, with countless stars like Sakkah Boy, Sakura Bakushin O, Air Groove and Daiwa Major. He was also famous for his durability, standing 25 years from 1975 to 1999. A fine racehorse too: 4th in the 2000 Guineas and 5th in the Derby, he won a G1 in France, the Prix de la Foret.
1056=Winner of the 1973 Tenno Sho (Spring). Talented early, he won no classic (7th Satsuki Sho, 3rd Derby, 2nd Kikuka Sho) but won the trials: Spring S., Kobe Shimbun Hai and Kyoto Shimbun Hai. Older, he won the Milers C., then the Tenno Sho (Spring) as favorite, and retired after a 2nd in the Takarazuka Kinen. At stud he sired graded winners like Kimpai (West) winner Western George and Nakayama Kinen winner Kosei - a fair success for a Japan-bred sire in hard times.
1057=With Ten Point and Green Grass he defined 1976-77, beloved as "TTG." His solid build and flying stride earned him the name "Pegasus." His duels with Ten Point are still told as classics. The first came in the Satsuki Sho: Ten Point was Kansai's star, while in Kanto Tosho Boy rose as his rival after 3 straight wins - a much-anticipated clash. Tosho Boy won by 5 lengths with superior speed. They met again in the Derby and Kikuka Sho, neither winning but both fighting as favorites. In the Arima Kinen they swept aside the older horses to finish 1-2, Tosho Boy winning again to be the best horse at 4 (old age system). After a break, he faced Ten Point in the Takarazuka Kinen on his return; Ten Point, in steady form, was favored, but Tosho Boy won again. Their final clash came in the Arima Kinen, ending with Tosho Boy a narrow 2nd after a fierce duel, and Ten Point took Horse of the Year. Green Grass was 3rd in both races. At stud he sired Triple Crown winner Mr. C.B. and G1 winners like Sakura Hokuto O, Daiichi Ruby and Sister Tosho, showing the potential of Japan-bred sires in an era of imports.
1058=Sire of Tosho Boy, Sakura Yutaka O and more, the great sire of 1970s Japan. A British racehorse whose main win was the Queen Anne S., on a disqualification, he was imported to Japan and rewrote its racing history. His first crop gave Satsuki Sho winner Land Prince, his 3rd Kitano Kachidoki, his 4th Tesco Gabby and his 5th Tosho Boy - top runners almost every year. Later came Inter Gushiken, Hagino Kamui O and Sakura Yutaka O, and he was champion sire 5 times from the 1970s to the early '80s. Tosho Boy and Sakura Yutaka O succeeded at stud too, producing Mr. C.B., Sakura Hokuto O, Sakura Bakushin O, Air Jihad and others - the great Tesco Boy line that continues today.
1059=A French horse famous as sire of Sakkah Boy. Though from stayer bloodlines, he starred at a mile, winning the G1 Prix Jacques le Marois. At first he raced over long trips, but after a big loss in the Grand Prix de Paris (then 3100m) and a 4th in the Champion S. (about 2000m), he switched to miles. At 4 he won the G3 Prix d'Evry, then the summer highlight, the Jacques le Marois. In the Queen Elizabeth II S. he met superstar Brigadier Gerard and was well beaten, 8 lengths 2nd, and retired. Not quite top class as a racehorse, he was a gifted sire, soon a regular near the top of the French sire table. Bought by the Shadai group in 1980, in Japan he sired Asahi Hai Sansai S. winner Scrum Dyna and 2-time G1 winner Sakkah Boy, among many. His male line has faded, but as broodmare sire of Stay Gold his blood lives on.
1060=The 1972 Derby winner, who with crop-mates Land Prince, Tai Tem and Ishino Hikaru lit up 1972. Debuting at 4 (old age system), he won his first 5, incl. the Yayoi Sho, beating Land Prince into 2nd in both his 4th and 5th starts. Satsuki Sho favorite, he was 3rd behind Land Prince and Ishino Hikaru. In the Derby he gained revenge, edging Land Prince and Tai Tem in a 3-way straight duel in a record 2:28.6. From autumn he lost his spark: 6th to Tai Tem in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai, 5th to Ishino Hikaru in the Kikuka Sho and 8th in the Arima Kinen, his last race. At stud his 2nd crop produced Takarazuka Kinen winner Teruten Ryu, plus graded winners Speed Hero and Wide O. Not a leading sire, but a fair success for a Japan-bred sire in hard times.
1061=In the late 1970s-80s, when imported sires ruled, he led the Japan-bred sires, producing 2-time classic filly Titania, Oka Sho winner Riesen Gross and more, and in 1980 and 1981 became only the 2nd Japan-bred champion sire, after Kumohata. On the track he had a fierce rivalry with Tanino Moutiers in the 1970 classics. At 3 (old age system) he won his first 5, incl. the Asahi Hai Sansai S., and a clash with Kansai's Hanshin Sansai S. winner Tanino Moutiers was eagerly awaited. In the classics he was 2nd to him in the Satsuki Sho and 5th in the Derby, but beat him in the NHK Hai. Both flopped in their last meeting, the Kikuka Sho, after which Tanino Moutiers retired. Arrow Express ran on in the Arima Kinen and next year's Sprinters S., 4th in both, and retired. At stud his first crop gave Titania, plus many graded winners, and his son Lead Wonder succeeded as a sire, building a sire line - turning the tables on the less successful Tanino Moutiers.
1062=In 1964 he became only the 2nd Triple Crown winner, then won the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Arima Kinen as an older horse - the first "5-crown" horse, a legend of Japanese racing. 15 wins and four 2nds from 19. His wins were never flashy, so his image as the greatest is a little fainter than later Triple Crown winners', but his way of doing just enough to win with plenty in hand hinted at boundless strength. His four 2nds came only in open races and preps: he hated training, so trainer Takeda used races as workouts. Horse of the Year at 4 (old age system) and at 5, his feats made "Beat Shinzan" Japanese racing's catchphrase. He retired at the end of 5 to stud. In an era hard on Japan-bred sires he got few good mares, yet sired graded winners like Shingun, Sugano Homare and Silver Land, ranked among the imports every year, and later produced 2 big-race winners, Minagawa Manna and Mihoshinzan. His male line is gone, but his legend will be told for ages.
1063=He moved from Oi to the JRA and won 10 straight, including the Satsuki Sho, sparking a national boom - an idol known even to people who knew nothing of racing. Debuting at Oi in summer at 3 (old age system), he won all 6 starts by 7 lengths or more. At 4 he joined the JRA - "the local monster storms the JRA" - and won the Yayoi Sho, Spring S. and Satsuki Sho, quickly famous nationwide, then the NHK Hai for 10 straight. But in the Derby, with an astonishing 66% of the win betting, he lost to Take Hope, his lifelong rival, 3rd. In autumn he beat Take Hope in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai but lost a duel to him in the Kikuka Sho, 2nd, and won no more titles. Older, he won the Nakayama Kinen, Takarazuka Kinen and Takamatsunomiya Hai, but was 6th to Take Hope in the Tenno Sho (Spring). In their shared farewell, the Arima Kinen, he beat Take Hope but was 2nd to Tanino Chikara, retiring as a horse strong up to middle distances but never a winner beyond 2400m. At stud he was a huge success for a Japan-bred sire, producing Derby winner Katsurano Haiseiko, Satsuki Sho winner Hakutaisei, Queen Elizabeth Cup winner Sand Piaris and many more - easily beating Take Hope, a flop at stud.
1064=With Tosho Boy and Ten Point he ruled racing in 1976-77 as the "Big Three," or "TTG" from their initials. The latest bloomer of the three, he burst onto the stage in the Kikuka Sho in autumn at 4 (old age system), beating hugely popular Tosho Boy and Ten Point. Winning as 12th choice remains an unmatched upset. Older, he raced at the top with the other two, and at 5 the three finished 1-2-3 in both the Takarazuka Kinen and Arima Kinen - that Arima Kinen is called the greatest race ever by many fans. After Tosho Boy and Ten Point left the turf at 6, he fought on as the last of the Big Three, winning the Tenno Sho (Spring) at 6 and the Arima Kinen at 7, a fitting farewell. Fragile and lightly raced, he was out of the top five only once after reaching open class. At stud he sired Queen Elizabeth Cup winner Reward Wing and American JCC winner Tosho Falco, among others.
