3242=The first Irish-trained winner of the St Leger; at 4 in 1958 he won the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. and the Arc, British Horse of the Year. A modest sprinter early at 3 with just a maiden win, he won the 12f Trigo S. that spring, was judged a stayer, ran 2nd in the Derby and won the Irish Derby by 4 lengths. That autumn he won the St Leger. At 4 he won the Coronation C. and Eclipse S. easily, the King George by 3 lengths and the Arc - 4 straight. 3rd in his farewell Washington DC International, he was Horse of the Year with Timeform's top rating of 136. At stud he was 2nd and 3rd on the British-Irish sire list in 1967-1968; his best include Derby and King George winner Royal Palace, Irish Oaks winner Ancasta and Japan's Asahi Challenge C. winner Ballymoss Nisei.
3243=The sire of the great Northern Dancer, and also of Nonoalco and Icecapade, founding the powerful Nearctic line. A Canadian racehorse, he was champion 2yo in Canada; quieter at 3, at 4 he focused on the mile and won 9 of 18, Canadian Horse of the Year. He retired after racing at 5. His first crop produced his masterpiece, Northern Dancer, who reshaped world pedigrees and raised Nearctic's fame. Icecapade, from his 9th crop, sired Wild Again, whose line thrives; Nonoalco, from his 11th, succeeded at stud and, imported to Japan, sired Daiyusaku and Kashima Wing. Through dams, Nonoalco also produced Hishi Amazon and Admire Moon, so Nearctic's blood spreads without Northern Dancer too.
3244=A 1950s US horse who thrilled fans with incredible stretch runs, winning from dozens of lengths back - an American legend. He won his debut from behind, and that December the Golden Gate Futurity after making up 27 lengths. At 3 he won a 6.5f sprint after trailing by 41 lengths. His style drew a record 61,123 fans to the Santa Anita Derby; racing last as usual, he surged after 5 furlongs and swept past everyone from 28 lengths back to win easily. In the classics his kick deserted him: 12th in the Kentucky Derby, 8th in the Preakness. Battling poor health, he raced until 4 and retired. A poor sire with just 4 stakes winners.
3245=The 1958 US dual classic winner. Winning 8 straight incl. the Kentucky Derby and Preakness, he was 2nd, 6 lengths behind Cavan, in the Belmont with the Triple Crown at stake. Debuting in autumn at 2, he first won at 3; after 2 allowances, a stakes win in the Everglades S. began a winning run: Flamingo S., Fountain of Youth S., Florida Derby, an allowance, then the Derby Trial S. In the Kentucky Derby he held off sensation Silky Sullivan, then won the Preakness as favorite. Bidding to be the first Triple Crown winner since Citation 10 years earlier, he fractured during the Belmont and was 2nd, and retired. At stud he sired Hall of Fame filly Tosmah and, as broodmare sire, Known Fact and Davona Dale - strongest through dams.
3246=The 1959 Prix du Jockey Club winner, who as a sire made history with Grey Dawn, Sea Hawk and Coined Silver. Emerging at 3, he won 3 straight to the French Derby and beat older horses in the Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud, but broke down in the Arc, 10th, and retired. At stud he quickly sired Grey Dawn and Sea Hawk, 2nd on the French sire list in 1966. Grey Dawn sired Morning Frolic; Sea Hawk, imported to Japan, sired Monte Prince, Monte Fast, Winners Circle and Ines Fujin. Herbager later moved to the US, where Big Spruce sired 43 stakes winners. His male line eventually faded, but he appears as broodmare sire of Rainbow Quest and Devil's Bag.
3247=Winner of the 1959 Belmont S. and US Horse of the Year, known as the sire of the great Damascus. He struggled early, first winning a maiden in his 8th start. Improved at 3, a Kentucky Derby contender, he lost by a nose to Tomy Lee, was 2nd to Royal Orbit in the Preakness, and won the Belmont. With 8 wins that year incl. the Travers S. and Jockey Club Gold C., he was Horse of the Year. At 4 he won the Suburban H. and Woodward S., but was injured and 3rd in the Man o' War S., and retired. Only 13 stakes winners at stud, but one was the historic Damascus, whose son Private Account carried on the line.
3248=A sire son of Grey Sovereign. His best include 1000 Guineas and Sussex S. winner Humble Duty and Eclipse S. winner Wolver Hollow, who was champion sire in Britain and Ireland in 1976. Another son, Spanish Express, succeeded in Japan, siring Asahi Hai 3yo S. winner Arrow Express, whose foals included Oka Sho winner Titania and Oaks winner Noano Hakobune; only Lead Wonder followed at stud, and the male line has faded. Sovereign Path himself won 8 of 25, incl. 3 stakes such as the Queen Elizabeth II S.
3249=Winner of the 1959 Santa Anita Derby and US champion 3yo filly that year, one of Triple Crown winner Citation's best. Unbeaten in 2, she won 3 more at 3, the last, the Santa Susana S., by 10.5 lengths. Encouraged, her camp ran her against colts in the Santa Anita Derby as favorite; she won by 2.5 lengths, only the 2nd filly winner, the first in 20 years. Aiming to be a filly Kentucky Derby winner, she was 5th to Tomy Lee, but later beat him in the Cinema H. and was named champion 3yo filly. She raced until 4. As a broodmare she produced Demoiselle S. winner Inca Queen and Silver Coin, dam of sire Coined Silver.
3250=A great sire who founded the worldwide Hail to Reason line through Halo and Roberto. Halo, ordinary on the track, sired Sunday Silence; Roberto won the Derby and sired Real Shadai, Brian's Time and more. In Japan today it is hard to find a horse without his blood. Brilliant on the track, at 2 he beat later dual classic winner Carry Back 3 times; injured in September at 2, he retired early, but won 7 stakes in that short time, champion 2yo. At stud his first crop included Belmont winner Hail to All and Derby winner Straight Deal (run at Newmarket); he was US champion sire in 1970. With Horse of the Year Personality, then Halo and Roberto, he built a great empire.
3251=The 1961 US dual classic winner. At 2 he couldn't beat crop-mate Hail to Reason, but with Hail to Reason retired early, he was classic favorite at 3, winning the Kentucky Derby as favorite and the Preakness easily. Injured in the Belmont, he was 7th, 15 lengths behind. Champion 3yo, though Horse of the Year went to older star Kelso. At 4 he beat Kelso in the Metropolitan H. and Monmouth H. In autumn he became the first Kentucky Derby winner to try the Arc, a big story, but was 10th. Retired to stud, he surprisingly returned to racing after the next breeding season and won twice, then retired for good that autumn. No stars at stud, but 12 stakes winners.
3252=A sharp closer who dominated French racing in 1963. A closer already at 3, he kept placing: 3rd in the French Derby, 2nd in the Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud, 6th to Soltikoff in the Arc. At 4 he stepped up, winning the Prix Boiard, Prix Ganay, Coronation C. and Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud - 4 straight - beating Val de Loir and Arc winner Soltikoff to top the older horses. In the Arc he made amends with a brilliant late run. French champion older horse, he retired that year. At stud his best was Zamazaan, later champion sire in New Zealand, whose son Beau Zam stood in Japan, but the male line faded.
3253=An American filly star from 1961 to 1963, honored 3 years running: champion 2yo filly in 1961, champion 3yo filly the next year, and champion handicap mare the year after. Emerging in summer at 2, she won 6 straight stakes by year-end. At 3 she won the Kentucky Oaks easily - some said she could have won the Kentucky Derby. Winning the Acorn S. and Mother Goose S., 2nd in the CCA Oaks, she just missed the New York Filly Triple Crown, then won the Beldame S. in record time - top of her crop. At 4 she won 4 handicaps by summer; injured in autumn, she was still champion handicap mare and retired to breed. Barren at 5, she oddly raced once more, then produced 6 foals incl. Juvenile S. winner Cicada's Pride.
3254=An American mare who blossomed with age; at 5 she was the first female champion sprinter. Racing mainly at Aqueduct in Queens, New York, she was called "The Queen of Queens." She debuted with 6 straight wins at 2, then stalled at 3, but from summer at 4 won 6 straight to year-end. Her peak was at 5: she won the Toboggan H. easily, the first filly winner in 55 years; beat previous champion handicap mare Old Hat easily in the Top Flight H.; and won the Vagrancy H. under 137 lb (about 62.1kg), sealing champion sprinter and champion handicap mare (shared with Old Hat). She retired that year. As a broodmare she produced 1970 US Horse of the Year Personality, later a sire in Japan.
3255=From Nasrullah's final crop, one of his sire sons, he founded the powerful Never Bend line through Mill Reef, Riverman and Bravest Roman. A fine racehorse, he won 7 of 10 at 2, US champion 2yo. At 3, 2nd in the Kentucky Derby, 3rd in the Preakness, he won 5 stakes and retired at the end of 3. At stud he sired European great Mill Reef, becoming champion sire in Britain and Ireland; Mill Reef succeeded at stud with Mill George and Magnitude. Riverman sired Irish River and Riverlea, and Bravest Roman sired Calista Glory and Meisho Homura, spreading Never Bend's blood worldwide.
3256=Known as the sire of the great Mr. Prospector. A brilliant speedster, he retired injured after 4 wins from 4 at 2, setting or equalling track records in 3; US champion 2yo. At stud from 3, he sired Exclusive Native in his first crop, then dual classic winner Majestic Prince and English champion 2yo Crowned Prince, and in his 6th crop Mr. Prospector, who reshaped world pedigrees. Mr. Prospector and Alydar, from his 11th crop, both succeeded at stud, spreading Raise a Native's blood worldwide. In Japan, in-utero import Nifty Niece won the Centaur S.
