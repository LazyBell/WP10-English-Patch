754=Full sister of QE II Cup winner To the Victory. Won Funabashi's Queen Sho under Yutaka Take.
761=Beaten by Sakkah Boy in his first graded race, the Chunichi Sports Sho, he won in his class and ran in the Kikuka Sho (11th). After a break he won the Niigata Daishoten as 2nd choice. Steady in open class.
762=By Kentucky Derby winner Fusaichi Pegasus, he sold for 100 million yen as a foal. 7th on his turf debut in February at 3, he blossomed on dirt, reaching open class in his 5th dirt start. In his first graded race, the JRA-local Diolite Kinen, he raced forward and held on, an easy graded winner. All expected him to rise to the top on dirt, but since then his best are two graded 3rds.
763=Running above her class, she was 3rd in the Rose S. As 12th choice in the Shuka Sho, she moved early from behind and held on to win. Winless after, she switched to jumps - almost unheard of for a G1-winning mare - and won her 4th jump race. Back on the flat she ran 4 graded races, all unplaced, and retired.
765=With a reliable late kick, at 3-4 she had a near-perfect 7 wins, two 2nds and two 3rds from 13. From 5 she faced top colts and ran poorly more often, but still added 2 graded wins, incl. the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu at 6.
767=A late bloomer, she won the Himba Tokyo Times Hai with a sharp charge in autumn at 4.
768=Winning the Tulip Sho made her a classic hope, but she was 9th in the Oka Sho and crushed in the Oaks and Shuka Sho. Often beaten when older, she won the Nakayama Himba S. at 5.
769=With crop-mates King Haiseiko and State Jaguar, he lit up Southern Kanto racing. At first he trailed them, 2nd in the Tokyo Derby. Then he won 6 straight, incl. the Tokyo Okansho and Diolite Kinen, and took the Teio Sho. In that autumn's Japan Cup, as local racing's representative, he beat world stars to finish 2nd to Rudolf - a famous feat.
770=She won her debut, the Marigold Sho and the Niigata 2yo S. After a 13th in the Keio Hai 2yo S. and 17th in the Hanshin JF she looked an early bloomer, but as an outsider was a narrow 2nd in the Oka Sho and Oaks and a close 5th in the Shuka Sho.
772=She won her debut in June at 3 and the Queen S. in her 4th start. 6th in the Shuka Sho and 12th in the Fuji S., she then had leg trouble and retired after just 6 starts.
775=He won his debut, was 3rd in the Radio Tampa Hai 2yo S. and won the Keisei Hai. In the Yayoi Sho he used his early speed to trouble heavy favorite Deep Impact, caught by a neck at the very end. In the Satsuki Sho Deep Impact and Six Sense swept past, 3rd. In the "third time lucky" Derby he was swallowed by the pack, 10th. In the Kikuka Sho his camp led boldly to blunt Deep Impact's kick, and just before he escaped, Deep's power overwhelmed him. Still, 2nd from the front in the Kikuka Sho was impressive. After 2 poor runs, a tendon injury ended his career early. At stud he had no notable runners, but as a broodmare sire produced Hanshin JF winner Circle of Life. He retired from stud in 2018 and appeared in a 2022 Yogibo commercial.
776=From the crop of Derby winner Bamboo Atlas and record-priced Hagino Kamui O. In 1984, the first year of graded races, he won the Chukyo Kinen and Naruo Kinen, beating later Japan Cup winner Katsuragi Ace and Suzuka Koban in the latter. He then kept running unplaced and retired at 7.
781=He won 4 straight up to the Hanshin Sansai S., champion 2yo colt. A classic clash with Symboli Rudolf was awaited, but shin trouble cost him 10 months. At 4 he won the Heian S.; after another break he won the Milers C., was 3rd in the Yasuda Kinen and won the Hankyu Hai.
784=She won her debut but was crushed in the Wakatake Sho. As 6th choice in the Queen C. she beat champion filly Shino Cross and others, but a leg injury ended her career. As a dam she produced miler Bell Winner (92: Nihon Pillow Winner).
785=2nd to Mejiro Ramonu in the Oka Sho. At 4, with jockey Minai aboard, she won the Kita-Kyushu Kinen, then the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu the next year. She retired after a 7th in the Yasuda Kinen.
790=A part-bred with Arab blood, yet brilliantly fast, she won 7 incl. the Sapphire S. and Fukushima Kinen - "star of part-breds." As a dam, only Narita Meister (93: Sunday Silence) raced in the JRA.
791=His 2 turf runs - 16th on his late debut in April at 3, 15th in a maiden - were a model of mediocrity. But after winning on dirt at Monbetsu in his 3rd start, he was a new horse. He won 4 straight to reach open class, was 2nd in the JRA-local Hokkaido Sprint C., Procyon S. and Tokyo Hai, then won the Musashino S. for his first graded title. On dirt, local and overseas included, he had 9 wins and seven 2nds from 20.
792=The mare who made jockey Koichi Sumida "Sumida of the mares." As 9th choice at 6 she upset the Kinko Sho, his 2nd graded win in 2 weeks. She held her own against colts at middle distances.
793=Slow away, she always raced from behind. Stuck in the 9-million class until 4, she bloomed at 5, winning the Hakodate Kinen, and at 7 won the Tokyo Shimbun Hai, moved to dirt due to snow.
794=After starring at local Oi and Kasamatsu, he joined the JRA at 4. He was 2nd to top miler Nihon Pillow Winner in the Milers C., then in the Osaka Hai beat Triple Crown winner Mr. C.B. by a nose after a straight-long duel. 4th in the Takarazuka Kinen, he was caught up in a doping scandal and retired.
