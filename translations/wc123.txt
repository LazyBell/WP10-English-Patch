2906=Winner of the 1976 Oka Sho and Oaks, following Tesco Gaby as a 2-classic filly. Early to shine, she won 3 open races at 3 (old age system), champion 3yo filly. At 4 she won the Queen C., then the Oka Sho as joint favorite with Squash Solon, charging wide with a superior finish despite a slow start, and the Oaks as favorite, breaking clear from a handy spot - top of her crop. 4th in the All Comers and 4th to Diamante in the QEII Cup, she missed a 3rd crown, but held her own against older horses with Tosho Boy and Ten Point, 5th in the Arima Kinen. Older she faded: far back as 2nd choice in the Tokyo Shimbun Hai and Nakayama Himba S., she retired winless after a 6th in the Kabutoyama Kinen. No notable foals.
2907=Known as the dam of Ten Point, she won the 1966 Oka Sho. 12th on debut, she won 3 allowances, then the Oka Sho as 4th choice, beating Hiroyoshi and Mejiro Bosatsu by a neck after a fierce duel. 14th in the 4yo Himba Tokubetsu and 7th in the Oaks, she raced in open and local races, winning the Kokura Kinen and 6 open races, racing until the end of 7 (old age system). As a broodmare she produced JRA Hall of Famer Ten Point and 1982 champion jumper King's Point. Her origins are famous too: her dam Kumowaka was caught in an outbreak and nearly put down, escaped to Hokkaido, cleared her name in court, and was re-registered as "Okataka" - and Wakakumo was born.
2908=Winner of the 1977 Oka Sho and QEII Cup. Debuting at 4 (old age system), she won 3 straight: her debut, the Wakaba Sho and the Oka Sho as favorite by 3 lengths over Fine Nissei. Unwell, she was 14th in the Oaks, but after a 3rd in the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu won the QEII Cup, dispelling doubts about the trip, and the Hanshin Himba Tokubetsu - champion 4yo filly. At 5 she won the Milers C. and Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu, and lost the Arima Kinen by a length to Kane Minobu. At 6, 3rd in the Chukyo Kinen, 2nd in the Milers C., 12th of 13 in the Takarazuka Kinen, she won the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu again and retired after a 9th in the Arima Kinen. No notable foals.
2909=The 1979 Satsuki Sho winner. Debuting at 3 (old age system), winning his 2nd start, the Rindo Sho and an open race, he won the Asahi Hai 3yo S. as 6th choice behind imported Kentucky Fair, champion 3yo colt. At 4, 2nd in the Tokyo 4yo S. (now Kyodo News Hai) and 5th in the Spring S., he was 3rd choice in the Satsuki Sho and held off Katsurano Haiseiko. In the Derby Katsurano Haiseiko turned the tables; he closed for 4th. After the Keio Hai AH and St. Lite Kinen, he was hot favorite in a Kikuka Sho without the Derby winner, but moved too soon and was 3rd. Pulled up injured in the Arima Kinen, he retired. No notable foals.
2910=The 1980 Kikuka Sho winner. Winning his 3rd start and the Shiragiku Sho, he was 2nd in the Hanshin 3yo S. (old age system). At 4, 3rd in an open race, 10th in the Derby, he improved in autumn: Kobe Shimbun Hai 1st, Kyoto Shimbun Hai 2nd, then the Kikuka Sho as 5th choice by a neck over favorite Monte Prince. Injuries limited him after; he retired at 7. His best foal was Tomino Goran, a Niigata local star.
2911=Legend for beating 2 US Triple Crown winners, Seattle Slew and Affirmed, in the 1978 Jockey Club Gold C. Originally French, he won the Coronation C. and Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud. When disease broke out in France, his connections, planning US stud duty, sent him to America before a travel ban. Losing at first at 4, at 5 he won 6 G1s on any surface - the turf Hollywood Invitational H., the dirt Hollywood Gold C. - above all that Jockey Club Gold C., a duel of 2 Triple Crown winners which he won after a fierce fight. Retired at 6, he disappointed at stud, went to Sweden, and after his owner's bankruptcy was slaughtered. The shock led to the Exceller Fund and other groups to protect retired racehorses.
2912=A gelding who ruled Australian sprints from 1977 to 1983. Sprints were then low-status; his success, it's said, built their prestige today. Dominant from 3 at sprint-mile trips, he was Australian Horse of the Year. At 4, with the new grading system, he won the Futurity S. and George Ryder S., now G1s. Despite injuries he raced until 7, winning the G2 William Reid S. 5 years running. With few G1s then, he won only 4, but by today's grading it would be 20+. His success is said to have paved the way for later sprint stars like Black Caviar. The Freeway S., which he won twice, became the Manikato S., still a sprint G1.
2913=A legend of Australian racing: 30 wins from 41 from 1979 to 1982, 14 G1s, the first in Australia to earn A$1 million. Gelded after a heavy debut defeat, he won 6 straight; after some near-misses, he won 11 in a row from late at 3, from 1200m sprints to 3200m - versatile. He won the Cox Plate in October at 4 and was unbeaten for a year, 8 straight, until winning it again at 5. In the Melbourne Cup, Oceania's biggest race, leg trouble left him 20th. After a long break, back in August at 6, he won a 3rd Cox Plate, the first ever. Leg trouble recurred; after 3 years of trying to return, he retired at 9 as a pensioner.
2914=Winner of 4 graded races incl. the 1972 All Comers and 1974 Kimpai (East); with 76 starts, the JRA record for graded-race runs - "the working horse." Winning allowances in autumn at 4 (old age system), he won his first graded race, the All Comers, then raced 11-14 times a year, winning the Kimpai (East) and Meguro Kinen (Autumn) at 6 and the Aichi Hai at 7. Winless in 19 starts at 8, he retired after a 5th in the Aichi Hai. With partbred blood on his dam's side there was no stud demand, and he became a lead pony at Tokyo.
2915=Dam of 1977 Kikuka Sho winner Presto Toko and 5-time graded winner Noboru Toko. Her first foal Noboru Toko won the Sprinters S., winning her early praise, but unexplained cycle problems then left her barren for years. Told "a change of scene may help," she was sent to a small Hokkaido farm, where a cheap imported sire, Gustav, stood nearby - "cheap, so we can try again and again." She conceived at once, and Presto Toko was born - virtually Gustav's only notable foal. After that she conceived almost every year, producing 9 foals until 1986, at 23 (old age system).
2916=Winner of graded races incl. the Sprinters S., Fukushima Kinen and Tanabata Sho; tough, with 68 JRA starts until 8 (old age system). 2nd in the Niigata 3yo S. and Keisei Hai 3yo S. at 3, he flopped in the classics at 4 but won the Sprinters S. One win at 5, then at 6 the Kokura Daishoten, Sekiya Kinen and Fukushima Kinen; at 7 the Tanabata Sho. He retired after a 7th in the Fukushima Minpo Hai in October at 8. Few foals; he died at 16.
2917=The 1974 Oaks winner. Only just past the 2-million class before the Oaks, she drew attention as jockey Isao Shimada sought a 3rd straight Oaks, and won as 9th of 26. In autumn she won the Queen S. and Victoria C. (forerunner of the QEII Cup), champion 4yo filly (old age system). Older, she was 3rd to Fujino Perseus in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Takarazuka Kinen against top males. Few foals, none beyond allowance level.
2918=5th on debut, he won a maiden and 2-million race, then 2 open races at 4 (old age system). 5th in the Satsuki Sho and 14th in the Derby, he turned to sprints, 2nd in the Lord Derby CT and CBC Sho. After a year off injured, at 6 he won 4 straight: an open race, Yasuda Kinen, BSN Hai and Sekiya Kinen, then was 2nd to Nippo King under 60kg in the Kumohata Kinen. He raced until 8. No notable foals.
2919=Winner of the 1975 Arima Kinen. First winning in March at 4 (old age system), he won 2 allowances and was 5th to Kaburaya O in the Derby. In autumn he won the St. Lite Kinen and an open race, then was 4th as favorite to Kokusai Prince in the Kikuka Sho. In an Arima Kinen with Fujino Perseus, Kitano Kachidoki and others, he won - then a rare feat for a 4yo. Older, 2nd in the Meguro Kinen and 3rd in the Tenno Sho (Spring), but only 1 open win. Few foals.
2920=A 1975 classic fancy: 2nd choice each time, he was 2nd in the Satsuki Sho, 6th in the Derby and 5th in the Kikuka Sho - no crown, but wins in the Spring S., Asahi Challenge C. and Hanshin Daishoten showed his class. From late at 4 (old age system) to spring at 5 he won 6 straight incl. the Nihon Keizai Shinshun Hai and Sankei Osaka Hai, then was 2nd in the Tenno Sho (Spring) and Takarazuka Kinen, 3rd in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and 2nd in the Hanshin Daishoten. From 6, only 1 open win; 11th in his farewell Tenno Sho (Autumn). His best foal was Shinshun 4yo Himba S. winner Juji Lady.
2921=Winner of the 1979 Tenno Sho (Spring), a force in the TTG (Tosho Boy, Ten Point, Green Grass) crop. Missing the classics at 4 (old age system), he emerged with the Meguro Kinen in February at 5, won the Keio Hai AH and was 4th to Hokuto Boy in the Tenno Sho (Autumn). At 6 he won the AJCC and Meguro Kinen, was 3rd to Green Grass in the Tenno Sho (Spring) and to Tenmei in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), then won the Tenno Sho (Spring) at 7 as 4th choice. Winless after, he retired at 9. No notable foals.
2922=Of the TTG (Tosho Boy, Ten Point, Green Grass) crop, a sprint star. 9th in the Derby and 13th in the Kikuka Sho at 4 (old age system), he won the St. Lite Kinen, then turned to shorter trips: the Kumohata Kinen (a December Nakayama 1800m handicap until 1980) late at 4, the Keio Hai SH at 5, and at 6 a 1400m open race and the Yasuda Kinen. He retired after a 5th in the Kumohata Kinen. No notable foals; his half-sister Itto won the Takamatsunomiya Hai and Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu.
