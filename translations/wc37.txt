395=Brian's Time's third Satsuki Sho winner, after Narita Brian and Sunny Brian. Many called that win a fluke, and he was never rated highly; indeed it was his only graded win, and he never won again. But he was hit by mishaps - a fracture, a fall that stopped him mid-race, a tendon injury - and never had a smooth career. It's also true he never got to show his real ability.
396=He won his debut as expected, then finished 2nd, 3rd and 3rd, missing the Satsuki Sho. He won the Yamabuki Sho in his 4th start, romped in the Aoba Sho and went to the Derby, losing to Tanino Gimlet in 2nd. In autumn he won the Kobe Shimbun Hai, then skipped the Kikuka Sho for the Tenno Sho (Autumn) against top older horses, beating Narita Top Road by three-quarters of a length - and won it again the next year. He also won the Arima Kinen twice, only the 4th to do so, taking 4 JRA Awards: Horse of the Year twice, champion 3yo colt and champion older male. Fearsome as an older horse, in his farewell Arima Kinen he crushed Tap Dance City by 9 lengths, repaying his Japan Cup defeat. "The best horse of recent years" retired with plenty left, to much regret. Syndicated for 1.95 billion yen, he sired many graded winners: February S. winner Success Brocken, Yasuda Kinen winner Strong Return, 4-time graded winner Sun Carlo and more. Epiphaneia, out of Cesario, won the Japan Cup by 4 lengths, fulfilling the dream of his sire, twice 3rd in it. In 2018 Le Vent Se Leve won the Champions C. at just 3.
397=Offered at a training sale, he stood out in neither pedigree, build nor workout, selling for just 6.5 million yen. Not much was expected; he debuted in August at 2 but kept running poorly, breaking his maiden only 9 months later, at the end of May at 3, in his 10th start. That win changed everything. He improved amazingly, his running far more consistent. That autumn he stepped up to the Kikuka Sho and, as 10th choice, won - a "miracle" true to his name that left fans stunned. The next year, again lightly fancied, he won the Tenno Sho (Spring) and Takarazuka Kinen. All 3 of his graded wins were G1s, and all paid 10,000-yen-plus quinellas. But injury sidelined him in autumn at 4. Back in the next Tenno Sho (Autumn), he kept losing heavily, and another injury forced retirement.
398=Sunday Silence's great rival on the track. They fought fiercely in all three US classics: 2nd to Sunday Silence in the Kentucky Derby and Preakness S., he finally beat him in the Belmont S. for the last classic. In their 4th clash, the Breeders' Cup Classic, he lost by a neck, so Eclipse Horse of the Year went to Sunday Silence. At 4 he won the Suburban H. and retired with 14 wins, five 2nds and a 3rd from 20. While Sunday Silence, retiring about the same time, was sold to Japan, Easy Goer, by Alydar out of a Buckpasser mare, went to stud in America with high hopes. He died at 8 after just 4 crops, which included CCA Oaks winner My Flag and Travers S. winner Will's Way, who went to stud.
399=He combined rare speed with a strong finish, and inherited the Tokyo-course aptitude of many Tony Bin runners. At Tokyo he won 5 of 20, with four 2nds, including the NHK Mile C., but elsewhere he managed just one 2nd from 17, never winning. He raced abroad boldly - 3rd in the Prix Jacques le Marois, 13th in the Prix du Moulin, 7th and 14th in the Hong Kong Mile - without results; his clumsy late-charge-only style likely cost him. He raced until 7 but never won after the Mainichi Okan at 5, and retired to stud. His first crop included Niigata 2yo S. winner Meine Isabel.
400=Won 6 G1s, including the Derby, Irish Derby and two Breeders' Cup Turfs, and sired stars like So You Think - a great legacy. Emerging at 2, he won the Racing Post T. for his first G1. At 3 he won twice, then went to the Derby as 2nd choice behind stablemate Hawk Wing, but beat him clearly by 2 lengths. He romped in the Irish Derby too, then went straight to the Arc, finishing 3rd. But he won the Breeders' Cup Turf to end his 3yo season in style. At 4 he won the Irish Champion S. and tried the Arc again, but struggled on heavy ground and was 3rd once more. Still, he went back for the Breeders' Cup Turf and dead-heated for the win with Johar after a thrilling finish, retiring in glory. 10 wins from 13, losing only a 2nd on debut and two Arc 3rds - very consistent. He stood as a shuttle sire in Europe and Oceania, siring stars in both; in Oceania, notably 10-time G1 winner So You Think.
401=He trailed Fusaichi Zenon in the Yayoi Sho, but as 2nd choice in the Satsuki Sho his late charge from the back made him a classic winner. Going for a double in the Derby, he lost to Agnes Flight by a nose, then boldly went to Britain's King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S., finishing 5th against Montjeu and Europe's top older horses. 3rd in the Kobe Shimbun Hai, he was said to be tired from the trip, but won the Kikuka Sho narrowly as 2nd choice - two classics plus a nose 2nd in the Derby, a "near Triple Crown." He ran well after but never won, and went to stud at the end of '02. But the next spring, out at pasture, he kicked the paddock fence, fractured a leg and was put down.
402=US double classic winner of 2002. After retiring he went to stud in Japan as a hoped-for successor to Sunday Silence, but his extreme pickiness limited his matings, and he didn't greatly succeed. As a racehorse he was erratic: early at 3 he was crushed when intimidated by the pack, then won his next 2 easily at his own pace. In the Kentucky Derby, only 9th choice, he romped again, then won a tight Preakness for a double. But a slow start in the Belmont left him a heavy-beaten 8th, no Triple Crown. He won the Haskell Invitational H. easily, but was crushed by older horses in the Pacific Classic S. and Breeders' Cup Classic - an up-and-down career. Bought by the Shadai Group, looking for Sunday Silence's successor, he went to stud in Japan. But he showed interest only in certain mares, so covering was a struggle; in 12 years he left under 100 offspring. Still, they included Shuka Sho winner Black Emblem and other graded winners, hinting at his quality as a sire.
403=16 starts, 9 wins, his only G1 the Takarazuka Kinen. By the numbers, not enough to be called great. But his fearsome ability to win in record time just by running his own race made him a true monster, and his career is full of legends: ducking under the gate in the Yayoi Sho; an unprecedented 6 straight wins, 5 graded, after Yutaka Take took over at 4; winning the Kinko Sho by a distance; the switch to jockey Minai in the Takarazuka Kinen because Take rode Air Groove; toying with year-younger El Condor Pasa and Grass Wonder in the Mainichi Okan; and the Tenno Sho (Autumn) that ended the legend... Ending unfinished through a fatal breakdown, his reputation is not settled; some say "tragedy has glamorized him." On records, perhaps. But no one can deny the impact of his running or the thrill it brought. That surely makes him worthy of being called a legend.
404=Called "the black assassin from Kanto" and "the last stayer," he was loved by fans. Seen as a late bloomer, he actually debuted early, in August at 2, and won the Fuyo S. in his third start. Then injury cost him 6 months; back in the Spring S. he was 4th, then 8th in both the Satsuki Sho and NHK Hai (G2, 2000m turf), and only 16th choice for the Derby. But perhaps helped by the longer trip, he held on for 2nd to Mihono Bourbon, sparking a 10,000-yen-plus quinella. In autumn he was 2nd in both Kikuka Sho trials, the St. Lite Kinen and Kyoto Shimbun Hai. In the Kikuka Sho itself, his stayer's power finally beat Mihono Bourbon, shattering his Triple Crown dream for his first crown. Riding that momentum, the next year he denied Mejiro McQueen a third straight Tenno Sho (Spring) and Yutaka Take a fifth, beating the champion by two and a half lengths. A long slump followed, but at 6 he won the Tenno Sho (Spring) again, his first win in two years. Fans loved the comeback, voting him first for the Takarazuka Kinen, but he broke down in the race and had to be put down. By fate, that Takarazuka Kinen was held at Kyoto. The greatest stayer bloomed at Yodo, and fell at Yodo.
405=In the star-studded 1988 crop, Nice Nature was a standout character loved by fans. He had the guts to match any rival, but a soft finish that let lesser horses beat him, and after winning the Naruo Kinen in autumn at 3 he went 2 years and 7 months without a win. Meanwhile he set odd records like 3rd in the Arima Kinen three years running, earning unwanted nicknames like "Mr. Close Second" and "bronze collector." He raced until 8 thanks to a strange charm that made you think "maybe next time," and fans' warm support. He stood 5 years at stud, then retired as an honored horse. From 2017 a retired horse association held "birthday donation" drives for him, and from around 2021, with a revival of popularity, donations far exceeded the goals. He died peacefully at 35 in 2023.
406=Half-brother to Tenno Sho winner Biwa Hayahide, the best horse of the 1990s, "the shadow-roll monster." He ran some poor races early, but from the Kyoto Sansai S., when the shadow roll went on, he won race after race. He won the Satsuki Sho by three and a half lengths, the Derby by 5 and the Kikuka Sho by 7 - a Triple Crown by huge margins. He then beat Hishi Amazon and Rice Shower by 3 lengths in the Arima Kinen to reach the top of racing. But after winning the next spring's Hanshin Daishoten, his smooth career darkened. A hip inflammation cost him 7 months; back in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), the old sparkle was gone and he was a dismal 12th. Things never came right, and he retired after the Takamatsunomiya Hai at 5, his sprint entry controversial. He went to stud but died young after just 2 crops - the greatest Triple Crown winner, dogged by tragedy at the end.
407=Debuted at local Oi. Ordinary at 3, he came into his own after moving to Funabashi at 4, winning 7 straight including the Oi Kinen. At 5 he won the JRA's Tokai Winter S. and kept improving, and from 6 he was a monster, with 10 wins and a 3rd from 11. In JRA-local races packed with stars like Kyoto City, Meisei Opera, Taiki Sherlock and M.I. Blanc, he showed overwhelming power, winning 9 graded races (4 G1s), and was NAR Horse of the Year in 1997 and 1998. In his prime he ranks among the best dirt horses in Japanese history. A Dubai World Cup trip was naturally hoped for, but it never happened for various reasons - still a cause for regret.
408=Brother of Sakura Chiyono O, by Tosho Boy - a blueblood watched from the start. As hoped he won 3 straight, including the Asahi Hai Sansai S., but as favorite in the Yayoi Sho he sank to 12th, then a dismal 19th in the Satsuki Sho. Many blamed the heavy ground, but he was 9th in the Derby on good going too. In autumn he won the St. Lite Kinen and staked everything on the last classic, the Kikuka Sho. But at the final turn he ran wildly off course. Into the straight he charged boldly along the outside rail with a tremendous kick, but the lost ground was too much and he could only finish 5th. Gifted as he was, he ended the classics without a crown.
409=Losing his dam soon after birth, Sakura Star O was raised by hand - an unusual start. He won the Yayoi Sho and became a classic favorite. 2nd choice behind Material in the Satsuki Sho, he won with overwhelming power, but leg trouble forced a long break and he missed the Derby. His preparation fell further behind, and he returned after six months in the Kikuka Sho itself - an unheard-of route. But he blew away all doubts, winning to announcer Kiyoshi Sugimoto's famous call, "Cherry blossoms in full bloom in chrysanthemum season!" - "the miracle double winner." Then the Arima Kinen, where he topped the fan vote, brought tragedy: he caught a leg in a hollow in the track, fractured it and was pulled up. Bad enough to be put down at once, he got devoted treatment from his connections, but it was in vain, and 4.5 months later he was put down.
410=His sire Tudenham King was a fine supporting player who won the Nakayama Kinen and more. The late trainer Tameo Toyama, who adored the sire, raised Dojima Muteki with great care as his best son. He ran tough until 10, winning 4 of 57, and raced not only in the JRA but at local tracks and abroad - a pioneer of his time. But big titles eluded him: no G1, and his only graded win was the Keio Hai Autumn H. at 5, a record short of his fame. In later years his form faded, but the sight of him racing happily among young horses inspired and moved many fans.
411=Twin Turbo thrilled fans by dashing to the lead and setting off in a reckless, all-or-nothing runaway. His extreme style made his record anything but steady, but when it worked, his wins were pure joy. His best race was the All Comers at 5. All the leading runners had question marks that year, a wide-open race. As the others watched each other, he built a huge lead at his own pace until it was a safe one, cruising home as the other jockeys went pale - fooling stars like Rice Shower and Sister Tosho. He never won again, but many fans loved his single-minded front-running. After moving to local Kaminoyama, he retired to stud.
412=He won twice at 3, was 2nd in the Chunichi Sports Sho Yonsai S. and ran in the Kikuka Sho, finishing 11th. At 4 he won his first graded race, the Kokura Daishoten, then the Chukyo Kinen. After some poor runs he won the Tanigawadake S. in spring at 5, regained form with 2nds in the Kitakyushu Kinen and Kokura Kinen, won the Fukushima Minpo Hai and went to the Tenno Sho (Autumn). With Tokai Teio a clear favorite, Let's Go Turkin was 11th choice. Held up, he won with a sharp late kick - a huge upset.
413=The one-hit wonder whose shock win in the 1991 Arima Kinen stunned everyone. Last and over the time limit on debut, he was never a big hope, but gradually improved from 3. At 6 he won the Kimpai (West), his first graded win, and ran in that year's Arima Kinen. With the dominant Mejiro McQueen the favorite, he was ignored at 14th choice of 15. But in the straight he unleashed the kick of his life, overpowering McQueen to win - in record time. The announcer's cry, "Daiyu Saku, to everyone's amazement!", summed up every fan's feelings. He died of old age in 2013.
414=One of America's best of the 1990s. He won 11 G1s, including the Breeders' Cup Classic, matched legendary Citation's 16 straight wins, earned a then-record $9.99 million, and ranked 18th in The Blood-Horse's "Top 100 US Racehorses of the 20th Century." Until late at 4 he raced mostly on turf without success, but after winning on dirt at the end of his 4yo year, he was transformed. He won his first G1 in the NYRA Mile H. (renamed the Cigar Mile H. after his retirement), and at 5 won all 10 starts, including 8 G1s like the Jockey Club Gold C. and Breeders' Cup Classic. At 6 he won the first Dubai World Cup and the Citation Challenge, equaling the record of 16 straight. He lost his next race, ending the streak, but was Eclipse Horse of the Year at 5 and 6. Syndicated for $25 million, he went to stud but was found infertile, leaving no offspring, and died in 2014.
415=Won the 1987 Derby and King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. and was Britain's Horse of the Year. 3rd on debut, he won his next 2, including the William Hill Futurity S. (G1), becoming the 2yo to watch. At 3 he won a G2, then went to the Derby as favorite, beating Most Welcome and others. In his first race against older horses, the Eclipse S., he was narrowly 2nd to Mtoto, but won the King George to prove he was No. 1. In autumn he won the St. Leger and took on the Arc, but sank in the straight after a fast pace, a heavy-beaten 8th. Named British Horse of the Year, he retired after the Arc. He died young at 7, leaving few offspring, including Italian Oaks winner Ivyanna.
416=His temper was extraordinary - he even tried to bite horses racing alongside him - so despite his ability he lost many close races. Still, in autumn at 3 he won his first graded race, the Unicorn S., and the next year the Heian S. Then in the February S., newly a G1 that year, he ran a good time on Fuchu's dirt, turned to mud by rain, and won the JRA's first-ever dirt G1. Afterwards long injury breaks and a worsening temper kept him from winning again.
