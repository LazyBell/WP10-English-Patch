3301=Unbeaten in 10, she won the 1983 Kentucky Oaks, then the inaugural BC Distaff the next year easily - a great mare of 1980s US racing. She won her debut in May at 2, then by 18 lengths in her 3rd start and 12 in her 4th, and the Frizette S. (G1) by 8 - unbeatable in the East. 5 more wins by spring at 3 incl. the Kentucky Oaks. 2nd to Ski Goggle in the Acorn S., then a fracture meant a long rest. After a spell of losses she revived in summer at 4 with the Vanity Invitational H. (G1), then the Chula Vista H., an allowance and Spinster S. (G1), and won the first BC Distaff by 7 lengths, her last race. Bred to Danzig in her first year, she raised high hopes but produced no notable foals.
3302=A durable US star from 1983 at 2 to 1988, winning 6 G1s; at 4 he won the BC Sprint, Eclipse champion sprinter. 3 wins from 5 at 2, no stakes. At 3 his first G1, the Swaps S., 5 wins from 12. At 4 he won the San Fernando S. and Charles H. Strub S. (G1s), then the BC Sprint - champion sprinter. At 5 he won the Californian S. and Woodward S. for a 6th G1, 4 wins from 9, no title. Retired to stud, he proved infertile, sired no foals, and after a year off returned to racing at 7, winning 3 incl. the Cabrillo H. (G3), then retired for good. With fertility problems he left just 4 foals in 3 years, but daughter Preciseness produced graded winner Draw Again.
3303=Winner of the 1985 Kentucky Derby in a stunning wire-to-wire display, who stirred controversy by skipping the other classics for the richer Jersey Derby. At 2 he won 5 of 8 incl. the Arlington-Washington Futurity (G1). At 3 he won the Cherry Hill Mile H. and Garden State S., then led throughout at a hot pace to win the Kentucky Derby by 5 1/4 lengths. Hopes of a Triple Crown rose, but his camp went for the Jersey Derby and won $2.6 million. A sound Derby winner skipping the classics was unheard of; reportedly it led to a $5 million Triple Crown bonus. He won the Monmouth H. (G1), then was injured in training and retired. 5 wins from 7 with 2 G1s - few wins, but his brilliance made him Eclipse Horse of the Year. At stud he sired about 30 stakes winners incl. Santa Anita Oaks winner Antespend, many in Brazil.
3304=In 1985 she became the 6th New York Filly Triple Crown winner, ridden by Abigail Fuller, daughter of her breeder-owner - the first female jockey to win a fillies' Triple Crown race. Often slow away at 2, she still won the Selima S. (G1), 4 of 7. At 3 she won her opener by 19 lengths, then the Cherry Blossom H. and Comely S., and the Triple Crown: Acorn S. by 3, Mother Goose S. by 5.5 and CCA Oaks by 2.5 - flawless. 2nd in the Test S., she won the Alabama S. easily for a 5th G1, then was injured in training and retired. Eclipse champion 3yo filly. Of 15 foals, no stars, but colt Jonesboro won 4 graded races.
3305=The 1986 French Derby winner, who as a sire produced Pennekamp and American Post, continuing grandsire Sea-Bird's male line. Debuting in autumn at 2, he won his 2nd start, then the Prix Noailles and Prix Hocquart at 3, and the French Derby as favorite in record time. Back in autumn, he won the Prix Niel easily, then in the Arc swept past from the back into the lead, only to be overtaken by Dancing Brave's devastating run, 2nd, and retired. At stud in the US he sired 2000 Guineas winner Pennekamp and French 1000 Guineas winner Matiara; back in France from 1992 he sired Poulains winner American Post. Also broodmare sire of Harbinger.
3306=A rare carrier of a minor Blenheim branch, and a top racehorse who won the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. and Eclipse S. Debuting in summer at 2, he won only a maiden from 7 by the end of 3. At 4 he blossomed, winning the Brigadier Gerard S., Prince of Wales's S. and Eclipse S. - his first G1. At 5 he won the Prince of Wales's S. and Eclipse S. again, then the King George with an eye-opening run from last. After a break he won the Select S. and ran in the Arc; closing from behind as usual, he was boxed in at the key moment and lost by a neck to Tony Bin. He retired after it. His best son was Derby winner Shaamit, who failed at stud, so the male line is fading.
3307=A US turf star of 1986-1987, winner of the BC Turf, Arlington Million and Turf Classic S. Starting on dirt, he needed 4 tries to win. Switching to turf in his 2nd start at 3, he won an allowance by 7 1/4 lengths, the Cinema H. and Lexington S., then the United Nations H. in record time for his first G1. After a G3 he won the Turf Classic S., then the BC Turf, beating Europe's jewel Dancing Brave and Theatrical - America's top turf horse. 9 straight through the next year's United Nations H.; 2nd in the Bernard Baruch H., he then won the Arlington Million for a 5th G1. A fracture ended his career. His first crop included 4-time G1 winner Bien Bien, raising hopes, but few followed; Bien Bien sired a G1 winner, just keeping the line alive.
3308=A 1986 European sprint star who won the July C., and a hugely successful Danzig sire son. Debuting in England in May at 2, he won 2 sprint stakes from 5. At 3 the 2000 Guineas (English and Irish) and St. James's Palace S. were too far, so he focused on sprints and won the July C. (G1), then the Sprint C. In America he ran in the BC Sprint, last on his first dirt start, and retired. At stud he passed on his speed, siring BC Sprint winner Sheikh Albadou in his first crop, then G1 winners Owington, Cape Cross, Desert Prince and Japan's Takamatsunomiya Kinen winner Shinko Forest. Blessed with sire sons, he is a pillar of the Danzig line.
3309=A sire son of Mr. Prospector who produced Hansel, Hector Protector, Timber Country and many more. Modest on the track, he debuted in Ireland and won 3 straight incl. 2 G3s, then was 5th in the Dewhurst S. (G1) and retired after a 3rd in the Mill Reef S. early at 3. Little was expected at stud, but his first crop produced G1 winners Hansel and Hector Protector, and his stock soared. Timber Country, Bosra Sham, Hawk Wing and Japan's Sprinters S. winner Hishi Akebono followed - over 100 stakes winners. Few of them succeeded at stud, so his sire line has not grown.
3310=A sire son of Mr. Prospector whose own sire sons - Zafonic, Elusive Quality, Grand Slam and more - built a powerful family. Debuting in the US in September at 2, he won only a maiden from 4. At 3 he won 2 G2s; 6th in his target, the Belmont, he won the Dwyer S. by 12.5 lengths for a G1. Winless after, he retired at the end of 3. His 2nd crop included 2000 Guineas winner Zafonic, sending him soaring, followed by G1 winners Da Hoss, Grand Slam, Speightstown and Johar - about 100 stakes winners in all. With Elusive Quality and others succeeding at stud, his line is thriving.
3311=She beat the colts in the 1988 Kentucky Derby, only the 3rd filly to win it after Regret (1915) and Genuine Risk (1980). Unbeaten in 3, 2nd in her first G1, the Las Virgenes S., she won the Santa Anita Oaks by 8 lengths and the Santa Anita Derby against colts by 7.5, and her camp went for the Kentucky Derby, where she beat Forty Niner and the rest. 3rd in the Preakness and 6th in the Belmont, she was 2nd by a nose to Personal Ensign in the BC Distaff - Eclipse champion 3yo filly. At 4, troubled by injury and a breathing problem, she won 2 of 7 without a stakes win and retired. No stars as a broodmare; her best was Golden Colors, a foreign-bred runner-up in Japan's Daily Hai Queen C.
3312=Winner of the 1988 Oaks, Irish Oaks and Yorkshire Oaks - top of Europe's 3yo fillies. Debuting in England in June at 2, she won 4 straight incl. a G3 and G2. At 3, 2nd in the Nell Gwyn S. (G3) and 3rd in the 1000 Guineas, she won the Musidora S. (G3) easily, then the Oaks by 4 lengths, dead-heated with Melodist in the Irish Oaks after a fierce duel, and won the Yorkshire Oaks by 5. 2nd to Minster Son against colts in the St Leger, she ran in the Arc 2 weeks later, 10th to Tony Bin, and retired. Of 9 foals, 3 won; her best was Calando, winner of England's May Hill S. (G3).
3313=An Australian star of 1989-1991, familiar in Japan as winner of the 10th Japan Cup - still the only Australian winner. To the 1988/89 season at 3 he was a mere handicapper. In 1989/90 at 4 he blossomed, winning 5 of 9 incl. 4 G1s. At 5, after 4 straight wins, he won the Japan Cup over Belmez, Kakoi Seeds, Oguri Cap and others. Back home he added another G1 and was Australian Horse of the Year. Injury kept him out long; back late at 6 he had lost his edge, winless until retiring at 7. A gelding, he left no foals, but entered the Australian Racing Hall of Fame in 2004 and is still loved.
3314=He ruled English mile racing in 1989. His career lasted just 6 months, from May to November at 3, but his stunning dominance made him British Horse of the Year. Winning his debut by 10 lengths, he won the Jersey S. (G3) and Criterion S. (G3) easily, the Sussex S. (G1) in record time, and the QEII S. (G1) - unbeatable. In the US, unsettled, he was 6th in the BC Mile and retired. He was Horse of the Year over 2000 Guineas and Derby winner Nashwan. With poor fertility he left under 400 foals, but they included milers like Sussex S. winner Among Men and French 1000 Guineas winner Always Loyal. As broodmare sire: Hong Kong mile champion Good Ba Ba.
