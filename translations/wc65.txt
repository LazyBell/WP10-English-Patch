1237=Crossing the line 3rd in the Wakaba S., a Satsuki Sho trial, he got in when winner Louisiana Boy was demoted. In the Arima Kinen at 4 he was a superb 3rd, ahead of Mayano Top Gun, Hishi Amazon and others - one of 6 graded 3rds.
1238=As 4th choice he held off Royal Touch to win the Satsuki Sho. 6th in the Derby and beaten in both trials, his camp made the unusual call to skip the Kikuka Sho for JRA-local dirt racing, and he won the Derby Grand Prix. He then boldly tried G1s of every kind: Kawasaki Kinen, February S., Tenno Sho (Spring), Yasuda Kinen.
1239=His G1 at 6 and late debut make him seem a late bloomer, but he showed promise early, 3rd in the Swan S. and CBC Sho at 3. 1244=He won the St. Lite Kinen but was 11th in the Kikuka Sho. Older, he won the American JCC and Nikkei Sho, then was 5th in the Tenno Sho (Spring). With 4 graded wins but short of G1 class - mid-ranking among Sunday Silence's stars.
1246=The first G1 winner for Thoroughbred Club Ruffian ("Meiner"). He won the Hakodate Sansai S., Keisei Hai Sansai S. and Asahi Hai Sansai S., champion 2yo colt. At 3, behind in training, he was 13th in the NHK Mile C. and 15th in the Derby, and kept running poorly. At 5 he won the Christmas S., his first win in 3 years, and the Milers C. at 6, racing until 7. He died young in '08 as his stud career began.
1248=Several breaks slowed his rise, but he won the Asahi Challenge C. in autumn at 4, then beat Mejiro Bright in the Kyoto Daishoten. 8th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), he was a superb 4th, half a length behind T.M. 1252=Set back by injury, he missed the spring classics. 2nd to Biwa Takehide in the Radio Tampa Sho, he was injured again after the Kobe Shimbun Hai. After 11 months off, he was 2nd in the Asahi Challenge C. and lost a nose duel to Grass Wonder in the Mainichi Okan. He won the Osaka Hai at 5 and Naruo Kinen at 6.
1253=He won 5 straight from the allowance ranks in autumn at 4, up to the Tokyo Daishoten. The next year he represented Japan in the Dubai World Cup but was 6th. He never regained form and moved to local racing at 7.
1254=Winning the January S. at 5 to reach open class, he won the Negishi S. on heavy ground, then the February S. by 1.25 lengths from Wing Arrow, going for a repeat - 3 straight and the dirt crown. 1255=By Park Regent out of a Goodly mare - a minor pedigree - yet a dirt specialist who starred in JRA-local races. Slow to win at first, with below-average results, he climbed the classes slowly and showed his ability at 5, winning the Diolite Kinen, and the next year the Kawasaki Kinen, a G1. At 8 he ran in the Dubai World Cup as part of Japan's team (9th).

He pulled up in the next Nakayama Kinen and, unable to race again, became a riding horse.
1257=He won his first 3, up to the Daily Hai Sansai S., was 2nd in the Asahi Hai Sansai S. and Kisaragi Sho, then won the Arlington C. That was the end of his run: after a 7th in the NZ Trophy Yonsai S. he never won again.
1258=Won the NZ Trophy Yonsai S. by half a length and was 2nd in the NHK Mile C., then ran dismally and retired.
1259=A middle-distance dirt horse who emerged at 5 and ran well in JRA-local races. He won the Tokai S. twice and the Antares S., and was 2nd in the Kawasaki Kinen. Injured at 7, he came back well, but another injury forced a long break. Back in autumn '04, he retired winless.
1260=He won the Keisei Hai, split T.M. Opera O and Narita Top Road for 2nd in the Satsuki Sho, and was 4th in the Derby. He won the Kobe Shimbun Hai in autumn - a fine supporting player - but did little after the Kikuka Sho. He tried jumps at 7 without success and retired at 8.
1261=Beaten in the trials, he missed the classics and raced mainly on dirt, often placing without breaking through. Reaching open class in autumn at 4, he matured: the next year he won the Procyon S. and Sirius S., then raced at local tracks. Beaten in the Nambu Hai, he outdueled Novo True in the JBC Sprint for a G1. He kept running in graded races, JRA and local, winning the Procyon S. again and running well in dirt sprints.
1262=With Novo True, a year older, he formed the "Novo-Novo" duo of JRA-local racing. From his first JRA-local race, Kochi's Kurofune Sho, he won 6 straight across Japan - Takasaki, Hokkaido, Morioka, Oi - capped by the JBC Sprint, one of local racing's top races. He kept touring: Utsunomiya, Kasamatsu, Nagoya, Funabashi, Urawa, Sonoda. In the '03 Kurofune Sho, Novo True was 2nd - a same-prefix 1-2. At stud from '05, his best was Kurofune Sho winner Blason d'Iris (Kochi).
1263=3rd in the Spring S., he was crushed in the Satsuki Sho, missed the Derby and switched to dirt. Stalled in allowances, he became consistent once he mastered front-running. Reaching open class at 5, he won the JRA-local Teio Sho, and the next year the Kawasaki Kinen - a race his dam Rosita had won. 4th in the February S., he then won the Diolite Kinen and Oguri Cap Kinen, both in record time. He retired to stud in '04.
1264=With 1 win and two 2nds from 3, he won the Principal S. and was a fine 4th in the Derby. A close 2nd in the St. Lite Kinen raised Kikuka Sho hopes; over 3000m he broke clear from a good spot but was narrowly beaten by Air Shakur, 2nd. A fracture after his 3yo season cost him over a year, and chronic hoof heat limited him to the Nakayama Kimpai and an open special when older. He raced until 8.
1265=By Wild Rush, sire of dirt king Transcend. Raced only on dirt from his debut in autumn at 2, he reached open class in summer at 4. He won 4 open specials and was 2nd in 3 graded races like the Capella S., a steady dirt sprinter, finally winning the Hyogo Gold T. at 6 and the Kakitsubata Kinen at 7.
1266=Unstoppable from his debut, he was too late for 2yo races but won the Yayoi Sho easily over Air Shakur and others, the Derby favorite. But injury just before the classics forced a long layoff. Breeding from '04, he has had no notable runners.
1267=At 3 he alternated wins and losses like Othello pieces. At 4 he was 2nd twice in 4 starts, incl. the Yasuda Kinen, then injured. Back in summer at 5, he won the Keisei Hai Autumn H. easily, then ran poorly with leg trouble, before winning it again in good time the next year. A 4th long break followed. Talented as a sprinter, his career fell a bit short due to leg trouble.
1268=Won the Japan Dirt Derby, beating Agnes Digital, but never won again and moved to jumps.
1269=First foal of Tsurumaru Girl, who won the Asahi Challenge C. at 3 with her sharp kick. Older, his talent bloomed: 2nd in the Takarazuka Kinen at 4 and 5, and 2nd to Symboli Kris S in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) at 5. At 6 he beat a star-studded field in the Yasuda Kinen with a brilliant straight-long charge, his long-sought G1. In autumn, without a prep, he was a fine 4th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn). 1270=Versatile in any company, pace, distance or tactics, he lit up JRA-local dirt racing. In 30 dirt races he finished worse than 5th only 3 times - remarkably consistent. His graded wins were just the Kakitsubata Kinen and Tochigi Marronnier C., but he was 2nd in G1s - Tokyo Daishoten, February S. and Teio Sho. Valued for his ability, he went to stud.
1271=He won the Komakusa Sho (OP) on Derby day and the Tomoe Sho easily, then beat older horses and Derby winner Jungle Pocket in the Sapporo Kinen. In the Kobe Shimbun Hai he beat future G1 winners Kurofune and Dantsu Flame for 4 straight, but was 3rd to Manhattan Cafe in the Kikuka Sho. After a year off injured, he won the Hakodate Kinen and All Comers, but leg trouble persisted, and he retired without a G1 to stud, later exported to France.
1272=Half-brother of Mejiro Bright. By Sunday Silence instead, he was a speedier type. He needed 4 starts to win a maiden, but next, in the Asahi Hai Sansai S., easily defied 10th choice of 16 to win a G1. But soon after becoming 2yo champion, injury cost him 14 months. Back at 4 for just 2 races, he rested again over 2 years and retired without returning.
1273=With 2 wins from 3 on dirt, he ran in the Kisaragi Sho but, perhaps unsuited to turf, was 6th. Back on dirt, he won the Hyacinth S. and Fukuryu S., then the Hyogo Championship. He won the Nagoya Yushun, then beat Utopia by a nose in the Japan Dirt Derby, a G1 against the nation's best. He stalled after, moved to Sonoda in '05, and broke a leg in a race at the end of '05 and was put down.
1274=Winner of 7 US G1s incl. the Woodward S. and Jockey Club Gold C. twice each; 58th in The Blood-Horse's "Top 100 US Racehorses of the 20th Century." He won his first G1, the Wood Memorial S., in spring at 3, was 4th in the Kentucky Derby and 2nd in the Belmont S. In autumn he won the Woodward and Jockey Club Gold C. At 4, after an allowance win, he won 4 G1s in a row: Whitney H., Woodward, Marlboro Cup Invitational H. and Jockey Club Gold C. In the first BC Classic that year he was 2nd to Wild Again, but was Eclipse champion older male. 1275=One of Northern Dancer's most successful sire sons. He sired Galileo, Montjeu, Opera House and many more, and was champion sire in Britain and Ireland a record 14 times. His runners excel worldwide, founding the Sadler's Wells line. A fine racehorse too, he won the Irish 2000 Guineas and Phoenix Champion S. and was 2nd in the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. His first crop gave G1 winners Old Vic and In the Wings, followed every year by stars like Salsabil and Opera House. Galileo sired Frankel and Montjeu Hurricane Run - his family swept Europe's great races. Thought unsuited to Japanese turf, he still has many successful descendants there, like T.M. 1276=Winner of the 2000 Guineas and Irish Derby, with 7 wins and a 2nd from 8. In 1984, at 3, he was rated the world's best at 138 lbs. His crop, packed with stars like future Arc winner Rainbow Quest, great sire Sadler's Wells and 2-time G1 winner Chief Singer, was called one of the strongest ever. He lost the Derby to Secreto by a short head, but beat all those rivals, earning the world's top rating. He was to run in the King George and the Arc after the Irish Derby, but leg trouble forced his retirement. At stud he sired King George winner Belmez and Rodrigo de Triano, winner of the English and Irish 2000 Guineas and a sire in Japan. 1277=Winner of the Kentucky Derby and Preakness and 13 US G1s. Called the last great horse of the 1970s, he was rated "better than Secretariat up to 10 furlongs." Winning his 5th start by 15 lengths, he went on a streak, champion 2yo colt, and won all the way through the Kentucky Derby and Preakness at 3 - 12 straight. But he then won his last 10 races until retiring at 4. So dominant was he that no one challenged him in the Woodward S. at 4, making it a walkover. 26 wins from 30, 8 records, and 10th in The Blood-Horse's "Top 100 US Racehorses of the 20th Century." 1278=An American horse who made his name beating Japan's aces Tamamo Cross and Oguri Cap in the 8th Japan Cup. 1279=One of Danzig's best, a dominant European sprinter. After winning his 1200m debut and losing badly at 1400m, he was kept to 1000-1200m.
