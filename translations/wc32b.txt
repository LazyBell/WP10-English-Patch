317=He won his debut and the Sumire S., then made the Derby his third start, chosen because he was frail and needed the shortest route. In the Derby straight, favorite Dance in the Dark surged from a good spot; just as everyone was sure he'd win, Fusaichi Concorde unleashed a "supersonic" kick and ran him down wide. Fans called the 3-for-3 Derby winner "Japan's Lammtarra." In autumn he took another odd route, the Cassiopeia S. then the Kikuka Sho, running well but losing both. He then developed periostitis and retired after just 5 races. At stud he sired stars like Blue Concorde, Balance of Game and Osumi Haruka.
325=The late trainer Tameo Toyama trained his horses with Spartan rigor, believing "you build the best horse through training." Mihono Bourbon was the ultimate masterpiece of that belief. With speed honed on the hill course, he won his debut, his powerful late burst making light of a slow start. He then led all the way in a 5-million race, and held off Yamanin Miracle by a nose in the Asahi Hai Sansai S., unbeaten 2yo champion. From 3, doubts about distance, jockey and more swirled before every race, but his power never wavered: never letting rivals near, he won the Satsuki Sho and the Derby, an unbeaten double. Triple Crown hopes rested on the Kikuka Sho, but he was 2nd to Rice Shower, who emerged as a stayer. Leg trouble then forced a break, and he never returned to the turf.
322=From Sunday Silence's first crop, he missed the classics through injury. Back in spring at 4, he won 6 straight, including 4 graded races - the Epsom C., Sapporo Kinen, Asahi Challenge C. and Kyoto Daishoten - a "late-arriving star." 4th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and 2nd in the Arima Kinen, he was rated in a Big Three with Sakura Laurel and Mayano Top Gun the next spring, and won the Takarazuka Kinen, shutting out Bubble Gum Fellow, Dance Partner and Taiki Blizzard. At stud he sired Silk Famous, a late bloomer just like him who won 5 straight at 4 and was 2nd in the Takarazuka Kinen, and Nikkei Sho winner Never Bouchon.
324=He won his first graded race, the Arlington C., then was 8th in the Mainichi Hai, partly due to the distance. Skipping the classics, he went to the NHK Mile C. and, as 9th choice, raced boldly forward to win. He then slumped badly and never won again on the flat. In '06 he had colic surgery and a long recovery; back in '07 he lacked sparkle, finishing in double figures. In July he switched to jumping and won a maiden hurdle, his first win in 4 years, and was 4th in the Hanshin Jump S., but retired in November. At stud he sired Ever Rose, winner of 3 straight 1000m straight-course races in lower classes.
326=On debut at Hakodate (1800m, good turf), 6th choice of 6, he came from last to win in a remarkable 2:01.6. He won the Radio Tampa Hai Sansai S., but in the classics he stuck to late charges and kept losing narrowly. At year's end, with Hiroshi Kawachi aboard for the Stayers S., he raced from a good spot and won by a distance. Now he knew how to win: the next year he took the American JCC, the Hanshin Daishoten and the Tenno Sho (Spring), beating favorite Silk Justice, last year's Arima Kinen winner - 4 straight graded wins and three generations of G1 winners after Amber Shadai and Mejiro Ryan. With the "greatest generation" a year younger starring, he won only one more G2. He went to stud but died of a heart attack in spring '04.
