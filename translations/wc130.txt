3031=A mare who blossomed at 5, winning 4 dirt graded races in a row incl. the Kashiwa Kinen. After a 1-win race in spring at 3, she struggled in the 2-win class until spring at 4, then quickly reached open class. At 5, 2nd in her first graded race, the TCK Jo-o Hai, she won the Empress Hai, then the Marine C., Kashiwa Kinen and Sparking Lady C. - 4 straight. Favorite in the Ladies' Prelude, JBC Ladies' Classic and Queen Sho, she was 3rd each time. 6th in the Tokyo Daishoten, 15th in the February S. at 6, then retired.
3032=After a turf maiden win she switched to dirt, doing best on heavy and bad going. Unable to break through in open races, she moved to local racing: 4th in the Ladies' Prelude, then helped by rain-soaked bad ground, she won the Queen Sho - her first graded title. Moving to Kawasaki in Southern Kanto, she won no more and retired to breed.
3033=Beaten by a record margin on debut at Sapporo, then 7 2nds and a 3rd in 9 more starts, he moved to Sonoda, won 2 straight and quickly returned to the JRA. 12th in his first graded race, the Heian S., he improved as distances grew and won the Nagoya GP as favorite that December - his first graded title. At 5, 3rd in the Kawasaki Kinen, then out of contention; he moved to local racing in June at 6.
3034=An explosive closer fancied for the 2022 classics. 5th on debut, he won a maiden by 7 lengths, was 2nd in the Hagi S. (L), then won the Hopeful S. (G1) as 2nd choice, breaking clear from up front in record time. Classic favorite at 3, he went straight to the Satsuki Sho as 4th choice and was 13th. 8th choice in the Derby, he closed from the back for 6th, about 6 lengths behind Do Deuce. In autumn, 8th in the Argentine Republic Hai, he won a blanket finish in the Chunichi Shimbun Hai (G3). Placed 3 times in graded races at 4 without a win. At 5, 2nd in Saudi Arabia's Neom Turf C. (G2), then 15th in the Osaka Hai, 10th in the All Comers and 8th in Bahrain's International T.
3035=2nd on debut, he won a 1-win race stepping up that December. At 3 he won the Shinzan Kinen, holding off a charging Solitario. Favorite in the NZT, he couldn't catch front-runner Jungle Rock, 2nd; in the NHK Mile C. he was 2nd again, a neck behind Danon Scorpion. 17th in the Derby; unplaced in the Swan S. and Mile CS.
3036=A great US horse with huge influence on today's pedigrees. His son Raise a Native founded the dominant Mr. Prospector line; he also sired Diesis's grandsire Etan, Oguri Cap's sire Dancing Cap, Sea-Bird's sire Dan Cupid, Dancer's Image, Kauai King and more. As broodmare sire of Northern Dancer, his blood runs in most horses today. A rare champion on the track: 21 wins from 22, his only loss a 2nd in the Kentucky Derby. Unbeaten in 9 at 2 incl. the Futurity S., then top 2yo race; at 3 he won the Preakness and Belmont; at 4 he won 3 incl. the Metropolitan H., Horse of the Year, and retired. A huge grey who often won from up front almost unnoticed, he stood out on black-and-white TV and was called "The Gray Ghost."
3037=Nearly 80% of today's Thoroughbreds descend from Phalaris; of other lines, the most notable is Ribot's. It produced sires like His Majesty, Graustark and Tom Rolfe; His Majesty's line flourished through Pleasant Colony. In Japan, descendant Tap Dance City won the Japan Cup and Takarazuka Kinen. With Nearco, Ribot was breeder Federico Tesio's greatest horse, winning the 1955 and 1956 Arc - unbeaten in 16. He also won the Gran Premio del Jockey Club at 3, and the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. and Gran Premio di Milano at 4. His 2nd Arc was won by a record 6 lengths - film suggests 8.5 - a legendary monster.
3038=A tragic champion filly who won the US Filly Triple Crown unbeaten in 10, then broke down fatally. 35th in The Blood-Horse's top 100 of the 20th century, the highest for a filly. Dominant from her debut, Eclipse champion 2yo filly, she won the Acorn S. by 8 lengths and Mother Goose S. by 13, and the 2400m CCA Oaks by 2 3/4 - the Triple Crown. Then came a match race vs. Kentucky Derby winner Foolish Pleasure; she was favored but broke down mid-race and was put down. Still named champion 3yo filly, she entered the Hall of Fame the next year. Famously, Mr. Okada, who cared for her as a foal, returned to Japan and named his club "Thoroughbred Club Ruffian" after her.
3039=The 1959 Satsuki Sho winner, bred from English parents and raced in Japan as an in-utero import. Winning his debut easily in December at 3 (old age system), he won 2 more early at 4, was 2nd in the Spring S., then won the Satsuki Sho by 4 lengths in record time - a Big Eight win. He took the NHK Hai in record time too, but was 15th in the Derby as 2nd choice on bad ground. In autumn he won the Kyoto Hai (now Kyoto Shimbun Hai) and, skipping the Kikuka Sho, the Kumohata Kinen - Horse of the Year. At 5 he won the Osaka Hai, but was 4th to Kuripero in the Tenno Sho (Spring), 3rd to Otemon in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), and scratched injured before the Arima Kinen. Back at 6, he ran once and retired. At stud he sired Takarazuka Kinen winner Date Horai and Kikuka Sho winner Date Tenryu.
3040=The 1958 Satsuki Sho winner. At 3 (old age system) he won just a maiden from 5. At 4 he won 2 dirt races and an open race, then another, and entered the Satsuki Sho as 8th choice, behind Katsura Shuho (7 of 10, never out of the top 2) and in-form Southern Kanto import Daigo Homare. He beat them by a neck in record time - a huge upset. 4th choice in the Derby, he was a good 3rd behind those two. After 2 summer races at Chukyo he rested; back at 5 he won once, then was 7th of 8 in the Tenno Sho (Spring) and retired. He left few foals at stud.
3041=Winner of the 1975 Tenno Sho (Spring). At 4 (old age system) he ran in the classics: 4th Satsuki Sho, 2nd Derby, 3rd Kikuka Sho; that summer he won the Nihon Tanpa Sho (now Radio Nikkei Sho). Winless for a while at 5, he won the All Comers, then at 6 beat favored Kitano Kachidoki in the Tenno Sho (Spring) - a Big Eight win. He retired after the autumn Meguro Kinen; little success at stud.
3042=After years in allowances, he blossomed in 1965 at 6 (old age system), winning 6 straight incl. the Tenno Sho (Spring) - a famous late bloomer. Debuting in July at 3, he first won at 4 and stayed in allowances to the end of 5. A move to local racing was mooted, but trainer Fujimoto refused. 2nd stepping up in the autumn Meguro Kinen, he surged: winning an allowance at year-end, at 6 he won the Kimpai, AJCC, Keio Hai Spring H. and Swan S. - 4 graded races in a row - then the Tenno Sho (Spring) by 7 lengths on bad ground. A clash with Triple Crown winner Shinzan was hoped for, but Shinzan was scratched unwell; Shinzan's groom later said, "Asahoko then would have been even with Shinzan at his best." Fading after, 2 losses and a long rest, he won 2 open races at 7 and retired. At stud he got few mares and little success.
3043=The 1969 Derby winner - a race famous for incidents. Satsuki Sho winner Wild Moa was out with a fracture, so 3rd-placed Takatsubaki became favorite, but unseated his rider just after the start, causing uproar. In the straight, Dai Shinboru Guard, Minoru and Hakuei Ho fought it out, when Dai Shinboru Guard's groom broke past officials onto the track, waving the horse's hood and yelling "That's my horse!" Dai Shinboru Guard edged ahead to win by a neck. His only other graded win was the Diamond S. at 5 (old age system), but he was 3rd in the Arima Kinen at 4 and in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Arima Kinen at 6, thrilling fans in big races. At stud for 14 years, mostly with local runners, he sired no graded winner.
3044=The 1968 Derby winner, leading all the way to beat the much-touted big three, Takeshiba O, Asaka O and Mahchis. Raised by Country Farm and trainer Tameo Toyama on a "train them hard to make them strong" ethos, he ran 10 times at 2 (old age 3). Without a graded win, he won 3 open races by spring at 4 and was in the top 5 every time except a 6th in the Satsuki Sho. 9th choice in the Derby, he led boldly and, as the big three watched each other, won by 5 lengths. He won the Asahi Challenge C. and Kyoto Hai (now Kyoto Shimbun Hai), then was 6th in the Kikuka Sho, only his 2nd unplaced run. 9 wins from 30 by spring at 5. He is the only Derby winner to run 18 times before the Derby. Little success at stud.
3045=The 1967 Derby winner. Beaten twice at 3 (old age system), he transformed at 4: a maiden by 6 lengths, then an allowance and open race, and the Yayoi Sho - suddenly a classic lead. After another open win he was Satsuki Sho favorite, but a strike delayed the race 2 weeks, upset his preparation, and he was 7th. 5th choice in the Derby, he showed his class: a thunderstorm just before the race caused chaos and a furious pace, and he waited calmly at the back to win. A fracture found right after ended his career. He was donated to Malaysia as a stallion and died there.
3046=The 1962 Derby winner. He won his 2nd start, a newcomer race, in record time, and an open race in record time too - fast from age 3 (old age system). Winning 3 open races by spring at 4, he was 4th choice in the Satsuki Sho but 11th of 16. 6th choice in the Derby, he fought it out with Satsuki Sho winner Yamano O and 11th choice Korehisa, and won by 3/4 length. Poor in autumn: 2 open losses, 17th in the Kikuka Sho, 10th in the Arima Kinen. At 5, 3rd in the AJCC and Nihon Keizaisho, but won only 2 open races, and retired that year. Unremarkable at stud, he stopped covering at 12.
3047=A Southern Kanto star who moved to the JRA and won the 1958 Derby - the 2nd local-bred Derby winner after Golden Wave 4 years earlier. Dominant in Southern Kanto, favorite every time, he won 8 of 8, incl. the Zen-Nihon 3yo Yushun (old age system) by 7 lengths in record time. Moving to the JRA at 4, 2nd in his first open race, he won 3 straight incl. the Yayoi Sho and Spring S., proving himself on JRA turf. 3rd to Taisei Hope in the Satsuki Sho, he won the NHK Hai, then beat Katsura Shuho by a nose in the Derby - a Big Eight win. In autumn he won just 1 open race, last in the St. Lite Kinen and Kikuka Sho, winless to the end of 5, and retired. No graded winners at stud.
3048=An in-utero import by Bois Roussel who, at stud, sired many graded winners incl. Kikuka Sho winner Great Yoruka and Tenno Sho winner Asahoko - a homebred sire holding his own in an era of imported stallions. He also won the 1957 Derby. Slow to win at 3 (old age system), he blossomed at 4, winning 3 straight incl. the Spring S., then an open race. On soft going in the Satsuki Sho he was 2nd to mudlark Kazuyoshi, but beat him in the Derby; in-utero imports could then still run in the classics. Tendon trouble followed; back at 5, he won 2 open races and retired. From his first crop came spring Meguro Kinen winner Asa Ryu, then Big Eight winners Great Yoruka and Asahoko; twice a top-10 sire.
