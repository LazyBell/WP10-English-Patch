511=An extreme front-runner: good when she got the lead, crushed when she didn't. After winning the Hakodate Sansai S. and TV Tokyo Sho Sansai Himba S., she won the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West) at 3, with Royal Cosmo half a length behind. 2nd choice in the Oka Sho, she led again, and though Royal Cosmo closed at the end, held on. She was 15th in the Oaks, beaten by the distance - proven when she led all the way against older horses in the Keio Hai Autumn H. After a 2nd in the Rose S., her camp chose the 2400m Queen Elizabeth II Cup. She got the lead but finished 11th, and with an injury, left the turf.
512=A filly who won the Southern Kanto triple crown: the Haneda Hai, Tokyo Derby and Tokyo Okan Sho. She ran in the Japan Cup as a local representative, but an international turf race was too much for a 3yo filly, and she lost by a distance. But at year's end she beat seasoned older horses in the Tokyo Daishoten - arguably the greatest filly ever on dirt. The next year she was retired to breed, choosing the Kawasaki Kinen at home as her farewell. Her 1.0 odds were no surprise; astonishingly, every other horse paid over 100 to 1. She swept forward down the backstretch and won easily, living up to mythical odds and leaving a legend. Breeding began chapter two: Ibuki Government (96: Commander in Chief) won 8 including 2 G3s, Kanetsu Fleuve (97: Paradise Creek) won the Teio Sho and Kawasaki Kinen, and her first foal Sister Sono (91: Nasr El Arab) produced Regular Member (97: Commander in Chief), winner of 3 dirt G1s - the Derby Grand Prix, Kawasaki Kinen and JBC Classic.
513=The only star by her sire Tosho God. Tough, she raced until 6, winning a graded race every year: the Sapphire S., Asahi Challenge C., Kitakyushu Kinen, Aichi Hai and Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu. She twice tried the Tenno Sho (Autumn), finishing 5th and 6th, holding her own against top colts. She often met fellow tough mare Ikuno Dictus in local graded races - true rivals. Sadly she produced no notable offspring.
514=With her fine pedigree she was always a classic contender, but fell just short: 4th in the Oka Sho, 5th in the Oaks, 3rd in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. Her ability held up as an older mare, and racing to the end of her 4yo year she won 6 of 21, including 4 graded races - more than enough for a filly. But her success didn't end there. Her son Daiwa Major (01: Sunday Silence) won 5 G1s including the Satsuki Sho and Tenno Sho (Autumn), and his sister Daiwa Scarlet (04: Agnes Tachyon) won the Oka Sho, Shuka Sho and Queen Elizabeth II Cup - a top-class dam.
515=A mochikomi by great sire Raise a Native, watched from before her debut. Debuting late, in July at 3, she broke her maiden, and after a 2nd won 3 straight up to the open Autumn Sprint S. After an 8th in her first graded race, the Swan S., leg trouble sent her on a break, but back in July the next year she won 3 straight including the Sekiya Kinen and Centaur S. Another injury cost her a year; back in the Centaur S. she couldn't win, and retired after 3 races. Much was hoped of her as a broodmare, but her best was 5-time winner Win Gloria (99: Sunday Silence).
516=Out of Alcea, 1983 US Eclipse champion 2yo filly, by great sire Danzig - a true blueblood. She won her first 3 in record time: 1:08.2 for 1200m at Chukyo on debut, 1:34.7 for 1600m at Tokyo in the Icho S., and 1:34.7 for 1600m at Hanshin in the Hanshin Sansai Himba S. She slumped after, but as a dam produced '02 Keisei Hai winner Yamanin Seraphim (99: Sunday Silence) and '03 Hanshin JF runner-up Yamanin Alcyone (01: Sunday Silence).
517=8th in the Oka Sho and 5th in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East), she was 10th choice for the Oaks. She always drifted in races, so her jockey took her widest to avoid trouble - and it worked: she swooped on favorite Shadai Kagura in the straight for a huge upset.
518=Right after winning his debut he was 2nd in the Shinzan Kinen and 3rd in the Kisaragi Sho, but injuries then held him back. He came back to win the Diamond S. at 6, but after a 7th in the Tenno Sho (Spring) a tendon injury, a long break and a failed comeback forced retirement.
519=With one win, she ran a close 2nd to Peace of World in the Hanshin JF. The next year she won the Fillies' Revue and ran a fine 4th in both the Oka Sho and Oaks. In autumn, after a 2nd in the Rose S., she was 3rd in the Shuka Sho.
520=Steadily winning dirt allowance races, she reached open class, and at 5, in her first graded race, the Garnet S., ran down Limitless Bid and others to become a graded winner.
521=Remarkably consistent: at 3 in 5 G1s he was 2nd in the Satsuki Sho, 3rd in the Derby, 2nd in the Kikuka Sho, 2nd in the Japan Cup and 4th in the Arima Kinen. Wins in the Kisaragi Sho and Kobe Shimbun Hai set him apart from the usual silver collector. He was also famous for changing jockeys: 7 riders in 16 starts, a different one in each of those 5 G1s - unheard of for a horse this good. In '07 Katsumi Ando rode him again for the first time since the Kisaragi Sho, 2nd in the Hanshin Daishoten, but a fracture then sidelined him. Back in autumn for a G1, he was 14th in the Japan Cup and 6th in the Arima Kinen, his steadiness gone. In '08 he ran as if burned out, and his retirement was decided.
522=Won the Sweet Pea S. and improved over middle distances on turf, winning the Aichi Hai at 4.
523=Debuted at local Oi. A Southern Kanto fillies' classic contender, she was 3rd as favorite in the Urawa Oka Sho, then boldly ran 4th in the Tokyo Derby against colts. Winning 4 straight as an older mare, she moved to the JRA. A closer at the mercy of the pace, her form was erratic, but she won two graded races, the Niigata Kinen and Hakodate Kinen - both narrow wins grabbed by grit at the very end.
524=Winless except on dirt, she couldn't even make the top five in the 9-million class - then, lucky to get into the Queen Elizabeth II Cup by draw, she won it. Last choice of 20, she paid 430.6 to win, still the record payout for a graded race. Naturally called a fluke, as an older mare she was 3rd in the Kyoto Daishoten and 2nd in the Kyoto Kinen - oddly good only over 2400m on Kyoto turf. As a dam she produced Tamamo Strong (95: Scan), 4-time dirt graded winner, and died of old age in June 2007.
525=He won the Shinzan Kinen and Yayoi Sho, was 4th as Satsuki Sho favorite and 3rd in the Derby. His path mirrored Admire Moon, a year older, of the same owner, and growth was expected, but a fracture ruled out the autumn classics. Back with a 3rd in the Naruo Kinen, he then beat Derby-winning crop-mate Vodka in the Kyoto Kinen. Following Admire Moon, he tried the Dubai Duty Free but was boxed in all the way, 9th. More bad luck, including a fracture, kept him from returning to form. At stud he sired dirt runners like first-crop Procyon S. winner Novo Baccarat, Nambu Hai winner Arctos and Leopard S. winner Cross Krieger, but sadly died young in 2015 after fracturing a leg at pasture.
526=She won in her 8th start, then as 14th choice ran a stunning race to win the Flora S., paying a 250,000-yen exacta. She ran a fine 3rd in the Oaks as an outsider, but then did nothing and retired after heavy defeats.
527=She raced at around a mile on turf or dirt, so erratic she might finish in double figures then suddenly run well. After 5 wins from 41 in the JRA, she moved at 7 to Southern Kanto, where she won the JRA-local Empress Hai.
528=Won the Himba Tokyo Times Hai at 5, her first graded race, and was 4th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn).
529=At 2 he was 2nd in his first 3 starts, then won his maiden as favorite at 3. In the Kisaragi Sho he showed a sharp kick, just holding off Reve d'Orient's charge for his first graded win. After a refreshing break he went straight to the Satsuki Sho, but, keyed up, faded from a good spot to finish 17th of 18. He later won the Miyakooji S. (OP) twice and was a regular in middle-distance open and graded races. Close relatives include 3-time graded winner Tuscata Sorte.
530=A speedy front-runner, best in dirt sprints. He won the JRA-local Cluster C. and Tokyo Hai, but was beaten in double figures in the Negishi S., and all 5 races until his retirement were double-figure defeats.
531=Her first win came in her 3rd start, on dirt, and all her wins were on dirt. In JRA graded races her best was 2nd in the Unicorn S., but she loved Kawasaki, winning the Empress Hai at 4 and 5 and running 2nd in the Sparking Lady C. - never out of the top two there.
532=She won 3 at 2, including the Fukushima 2yo S., and classic success was hoped for, but she was 12th in the Oka Sho. Skipping the Oaks for distance reasons, she targeted sprints of 1200m or less, finishing 3rd in the Ibis Summer Dash and winning the Keeneland C., her first graded race. Little followed but an open-special 2nd, and she retired in spring '09 to breed at her birthplace.
533=An early-maturing speedster who ran well around a mile on turf or dirt at 2 and 3. Winning in her 4th start, she then won 3 straight to reach open class, won the Flower C., and was 2nd in the NZT Yonsai S. and 3rd in the NHK Mile C.
534=She won the Elfin S. and was 3rd in the Tulip Sho, but 17th in the Oka Sho and 9th in the Oaks. Older, she was transformed, winning a tight Kyoto Himba S. as 8th choice for her first graded win, and later 2nd in the Aichi Hai, Mermaid S. and Lord Derby CT. As a dam she produced Meguro Kinen winner Cryptogram (12: King Kamehameha).
535=The 1969 Oaks winner. Her crop held stars like future Tenno Sho and Arima Kinen winner Tomei and Oka Sho winner Hide Kotobuki; overshadowed, she ran the race of her life in the Oaks. At 2 (old system) she won her debut and 2 more, a fine start, but slumped from year's end into early 3, missing the Oka Sho. She improved with a 3rd in the Flower C. and 2nd in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu, and was 5th choice for the Oaks. In the straight she passed Tomei and won a duel with Light Palais by a nose for the Oaks title. Winless after, she retired after an open win at 4. Of her 10 foals, her 9th, Dyna Artemis, won the Himba Tokyo Times Hai (now Fuchu Himba S.), and her 5th, Dyna Turkin, produced Tenno Sho winner Let's Go Turkin; with her other daughters her family flourished.
536=He won the Arlington C. but was crushed in the Satsuki Sho, then finished in double figures in the NHK Mile C. and Ibis Summer Dash. But as 10th choice in the Keisei Hai Autumn H., his stock fallen, he ran a stunning race for his second graded win. Lameness scratchings and other troubles kept him out nearly a year until his return in the Cassiopeia S. in November '07 (9th). In spring at 5 he won an open race, his first win in 18 months, but slumped again and retired - and went to stud in, of all places, the Czech Republic.
