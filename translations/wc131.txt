3049=The 1955 Derby winner. The JRA had been founded the previous September, taking over from state-run racing, and he won its first Derby. Unremarkable early, he won his 2nd start but was 7th in the Asahi Hai 3yo S. (old age system). At 4 he won 2 allowances and was 10th in the Satsuki Sho, then won an open race by a wide margin and, as 10th choice, won the Derby by 8 lengths - his first graded win. 5 more open wins followed; 2nd to Meiji Hikari in the Kikuka Sho, he was Horse of the Year. Unbeaten in 4 at 5 incl. the Diamond S. and Tokyo Hai (now Tokyo Shimbun Hai), he retired with 14 wins from 22. At stud he sired Kyoto Hai winner King Speed and Oi star Aoi Raiko.
3050=The 1954 Derby winner. State racing passed to the JRA that September, so he won the last state-run Derby. He first raced in Southern Kanto as Nentakara, winning 6 of 8 at 3 (old age system) incl. the Zen-Nihon 3yo Yushun. Moving to state racing at 4 and renamed Golden Wave, he won 3 straight, was 7th in the Satsuki Sho, then won the Derby by 3 lengths as 12th choice. He also won the Nakayama 4yo S. (now Radio Nikkei Sho), but was 7th of 9 in the Kikuka Sho. Older, he won only 2 open races; back in Southern Kanto in spring at 6, he ran 3 times and retired. At stud he sired spring Nakayama Daishogai winner Golden Oza.
3051=Winner of 3 graded races incl. the 1973 Takarazuka Kinen, he broke down in the Takamatsunomiya Hai and was destroyed. Blossoming in autumn at 4 (old age system), he won 3 straight incl. the Hanshin Daishoten; at 5 he won the spring Kyoto Kinen and Takarazuka Kinen. After his fatal fracture, it emerged he had been sent to a slaughterhouse instead of being euthanized - a scandal that reportedly led to today's rule of euthanasia as standard.
3052=A late bloomer who won the 1971 Tenno Sho (Spring) at 5 (old age system). He won a maiden only in February at 4, then 2 allowances; 3rd in the Spring S., 5th in the Satsuki Sho, 18th in the Derby. Stronger in autumn: 2nd St. Lite Kinen, 3rd Kyoto Hai (now Kyoto Shimbun Hai), 4th Kikuka Sho. At 5 he won the spring Meguro Kinen, then the Tenno Sho (Spring) on swamp-like ground, outfighting Okura - his first Big Eight win - and the Takarazuka Kinen with Mejiro Asama 2nd: 3 straight. He won the Hakodate Kinen under 62kg. At 6 he was only the 2nd Japan-trained horse in the Arc, 18th, and 7th in the Washington DC International. Winless after, he retired at the end of 7. His best foal was Anglo-Arab star Light Oscar.
3053=Winner of the 1964 Tenno Sho (Spring) and Takarazuka Kinen at 6 (old age system) - the first to win both in one year. Emerging midway through 4, 2nd in the Kobe Hai (now Kobe Shimbun Hai), 3rd in the Kikuka Sho, he began a fierce rivalry with fast-improving Ryu Forel. After a fracture at 5, back in autumn he was 2nd to Ryu Forel in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), then won the Hanshin Daishoten. At 6 he beat 2-classic winner Meizui in the Tenno Sho (Spring) - a Big Eight win - and Ryu Forel in the Takarazuka Kinen, top older horse. After 6 straight wins, he was 5th to Yamato Kyodai in the Arima Kinen, who also took Horse of the Year. At 7, peak Shinzan beat him in the Takarazuka Kinen and Arima Kinen, and he retired. At stud he sired Maruichi King, winner of 5 Southern Kanto graded races.
3054=The 1971 Kikuka Sho winner. Winning a maiden at 4 (old age system), he quickly won an allowance and the Mainichi Hai. 10th in the Satsuki Sho, 8th in the Derby, he improved, winning an open race in summer, then the Kobe Hai (now Kobe Shimbun Hai) and Kyoto Shimbun Hai in autumn. Kikuka Sho favorite, he led early in a slow pace and held on to win. Older, leg trouble limited him to the Chunichi Shimbun Hai at 5 and Sankei Osaka Hai at 6.
3055=One of the 1968 big three with Takeshiba O and Mahchis: 3rd in the Satsuki Sho and Derby, then winner of the Kikuka Sho. Called "the Young Prince" for his blue blood and beauty, he was 2nd on debut, then won 2 straight; in his first clash with Takeshiba O, the Asahi Hai 3yo S., he was 3rd. At 4 (old age system) he beat both rivals in the Yayoi Sho, but was 3rd behind them in the Spring S. and Satsuki Sho, and 3rd in the Derby, ahead of Mahchis but caught out by Tanino Hallo Moa - no spring title. In autumn he won the St. Lite Kinen, was 2nd in the Kyoto Hai, and with Takeshiba O abroad, beat Mahchis in a two-horse duel in the Kikuka Sho - a Big Eight win. 6th as Arima Kinen favorite, his first time unplaced, he was Horse of the Year. At 5 he won the AJCC; in another big-three clash in the Tenno Sho (Spring), 2nd to Takeshiba O but ahead of Mahchis. After a long break he lost 4 straight at 6 and retired. At stud for 10 years in Aomori, a declining breeding area, he had few foals and no stars.
3056=Winner of the 1969 Tenno Sho (Autumn). Debuting at 4 (old age system), he won easily and steadily climbed through allowances by summer; stepping up in the NHK Hai, he was 11th as 10th choice. Stronger at 5, he won the autumn Meguro Kinen and was 4th to Nit Eight in the Tenno Sho (Autumn). At 6, 6th in the Kimpai (East), 7th in the AJCC, he won the Argentine JCC, then placed without winning (4th Nihon Keizaisho, 5th Sapporo Kinen, 3rd Stayers S.). 5th choice in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), he broke clear by 2 lengths as fancied Mahchis and Ryuzuki faded - a Big Eight title. 4th to Speed Symboli in the Arima Kinen, he retired after a 9th in the AJCC at 7. 8 years at stud, no stars.
3057=The 1967 Kikuka Sho winner, who added the 1968 Tenno Sho (Autumn). His big blaze and wild temper earned him the nickname "White-faced Rogue Monk." After a debut win he struggled, taking his 2nd win the day before the Derby. Too late for the spring classics, he won 2 open races and was 2nd in the St. Lite Kinen. 9th choice in the Kikuka Sho, in the shadow of stablemate Satsuki Sho winner Ryuzuki, he beat him by 1.5 lengths - a Big Eight win. Erratic at 5 (old age system) with 1 open win from 9, he won the Tenno Sho (Autumn) as 6th choice, in record time. At 6 he won 1 open race and retired after a 5th in the Arima Kinen. No notable foals.
3058=A Southern Kanto horse who moved to the JRA and won the 1961 Tenno Sho (Autumn), sharing a fierce rivalry with fellow Southern Kanto import Onslaught. At 3 (old age system) he won 2 of 11; Onslaught was far above him, beating him all 4 times. Moving to the JRA in spring at 4, he was 10th to Kodama in the Derby. He blossomed at 5, winning the Tokyo Hai (forerunner of the Tokyo Shimbun Hai) and Tokyo Kinen in spring, then the autumn Meguro Kinen and the Tenno Sho (Autumn), beating Onslaught into 2nd; 2nd to Homareboshi in the Arima Kinen, ahead of Onslaught. At 6, 10th in the Washington DC International, then 2nd to Onslaught in the Arima Kinen. 4th defending the AJCC at 7, he retired. Few foals in an era unkind to homebred sires; he later became a lead pony at Hakodate.
3059=A poor sire, but a racehorse who won the 1954 Satsuki Sho and Kikuka Sho and the next year's Tenno Sho (Autumn) - 3 Big Eight wins. Tiny, reportedly in the 380kg range, he blazed from the front and was called "the Brown Bullet Train." Racing first under his foal name Tamasan, he won 8 of 8 at 3 (old age system); renamed Dainana Hoshu at 4, he won 3 straight to the Satsuki Sho. His 11 straight wins from debut still tie the JRA record. 4th to Golden Wave in the Derby, he won the Kikuka Sho easily for 2 classics - champion 4yo colt. At 5 he won 6 of 7 incl. the Tenno Sho (Autumn), champion older horse. Worsening leg trouble ended his career after an 11th in the Nakayama Grand Prix (now Arima Kinen) at 6. At stud, only a few local graded winners.
3060=The 1968 Satsuki Sho winner, battling versatile Takeshiba O and Kikuka Sho winner Asaka O in the classics. At 3 (old age system) he won 3 of 4, then the Hanshin 3yo S. as Kansai's juvenile champion. At 4 he won the Kisaragi Sho; 3rd behind Asaka O and Takeshiba O in the Yayoi Sho, he beat both in the Spring S. and again in the Satsuki Sho - a Big Eight win. He won the NHK Hai, but in the Derby the three fought it out and outsider Tanino Hallo Moa stole it; he was 4th. He won the Sapporo Kinen and Hollywood Turf Club Sho (now Kyoto Daishoten); with Takeshiba O abroad, he was 3rd in the Kikuka Sho behind Asaka O and Date Horai. Older, he won the Sapporo Kinen and autumn Meguro Kinen, but was 5th in both Tenno Sho and 10th in the Arima Kinen. He retired after an open win at 6. No notable foals; he stood until 21.
3061=Rival of stablemate 2-classic winner Meizui in the 1963 classics, he won the final crown, the Kikuka Sho, as Meizui ran off and faded. Early on he won 4 straight from his 3rd start incl. the Asahi Hai 3yo S. (old age system). At 4 he beat Meizui in the Tokyo Kinen (forerunner of the Yayoi Sho), but was 2nd to him in the Spring S., Satsuki Sho and Derby. In autumn he won the St. Lite Kinen, then the Kikuka Sho, as Triple Crown certainty Meizui went too fast and stopped - a stable Triple Crown. 7th in the Arima Kinen, he rested with leg trouble. Back in autumn at 6 he won an open race; at 7 he won the Keio Hai Spring H. and was 3rd in the Tenno Sho (Autumn). Racing mostly at local tracks from 8, with an open win at Niigata, he retired early at 9. No notable foals.
3062=A fierce rival of crop-mate Hakuchikara, winner of the 1956 Kikuka Sho and 1957 Tenno Sho (Spring), he tragically died of acute pneumonia in 1958 at 6 (old age system) while still racing at the top. Debuting in August at 3, he beat Hakuchikara in the Asahi Hai 3yo S., champion 3yo colt. At 4, 2nd to outsider Hekiraku in the Satsuki Sho and to Hakuchikara in the Derby, he beat both in the Kikuka Sho for his title. Top of the fan vote for the Nakayama Grand Prix (now Arima Kinen), he was 2nd to older champion Meiji Hikari. At 5, 5th to Hakuchikara in the spring Meguro Kinen, he won the Tenno Sho (Spring) in record time, beat Hakuchikara in the All Comers and was 2nd to him in the autumn Meguro Kinen. At 6 he died of pneumonia after a summer break. Hakuchikara entered the JRA Hall of Fame; Kitano O did not, but is remembered as a great rival.
3063=A late bloomer who won the 1973 Arima Kinen at 5 (old age system). 13th in the Derby at 4, he restarted in allowances and moved up in summer at 5. After near-misses (3rd Mainichi Okan, 2nd autumn Meguro Kinen, 4th Tenno Sho (Autumn)), he won the Arima Kinen as 10th choice, slipping away as Haiseiko, Tanino Chikara and other fancies watched each other. He won the Naruo Kinen at 6 and AJCC at 7, proving it no fluke, and retired after the AJCC.
3064=A Southern Kanto star who moved to the JRA and won the Tenno Sho (Spring) and Arima Kinen, in a fierce rivalry with fellow import Takamagahara. Debuting at Oi, he won 7 straight, incl. the Zen-Nihon 3yo Yushun (old age system) by 15 lengths. At 4 he kept placing, then regained form to win the Aki no Kura (now Tokyo Daishoten). Moving to the JRA midway through 5, he won quickly, but Takamagahara, who had moved first, beat him: 2nd in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and 3rd in the Arima Kinen. At 6 he won the Tenno Sho (Spring), beat Takamagahara in the Nihon Keizaisho, then won the Arima Kinen easily with Takamagahara 2nd - settling the rivalry, Horse of the Year. He retired the next year; in an era unkind to homebred sires, he left few foals.
