327=3rd in the Satsuki Sho behind Seiun Sky and King Halo, he was still Derby favorite and won by 5 lengths. Normally that makes a horse king of his crop, but not this year: there was injured 2yo champion Grass Wonder and unbeaten NHK Mile C. winner El Condor Pasa. Then he lost the Kikuka Sho to Seiun Sky, far from the best of his crop, and was 3rd in the Japan Cup behind El Condor Pasa and Air Groove. But as an older horse he grew stronger. He won the American JCC, outdueled Mejiro Bright in the Hanshin Daishoten and won the Tenno Sho (Spring). Crushed by Grass Wonder in the Takarazuka Kinen, he won the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and the Japan Cup, then lost to Grass Wonder again after an epic duel in the Arima Kinen. Few horses have won the Derby, both Tenno Sho races and the Japan Cup; earning such honors against the greatest generation's rivals proves him a great horse. At stud he sired two queens, Cesario and Buena Vista, as a pillar of the Sunday Silence line, plus dirt stars Roman Legend and Gold Blitz and record-time Kikuka Sho winner Toho Jackal. Cesario produced three G1 winners - Epiphaneia, Leontes and Saturnalia - all fine sires too, so his excellent blood carries on.
328=The first "monster" since Maruzensky. Winning by wide margins, he took the Asahi Hai Sansai S. unbeaten in a stunning record 1:33.6. But a fracture cost him his 3yo spring. Back after a long break, he was 5th in the Mainichi Okan behind Silence Suzuka and fellow foreign-bred rival El Condor Pasa. After another poor run in the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai he was called finished, but in the Arima Kinen he beat Tenno Sho winner Mejiro Bright and Kikuka Sho winner Seiun Sky to make a comeback. The next year he lost the Yasuda Kinen by a nose to Air Jihad, then won the Takarazuka Kinen for two straight Grand Prix wins, and won the Arima Kinen again after a historic photo with Special Week. He raced on after his rivals retired but lacked sparkle and retired. At stud he sired graded stars from his first crop, and in winter 2008 Japan Cup winner Screen Hero and Asahi Hai FS winner Seiun Wonder emerged. In 2011 Earnestly won the Takarazuka Kinen, a father-son Grand Prix double. Screen Hero sired mile king Maurice, so the monster's sire line looks set to continue.
329=An Irish horse who won 6 G1s, including the Breeders' Cup Turf and Eclipse S., well known in Japan for beating Air Groove and others in the 1997 Japan Cup. He was winless at 2 and broke his maiden in summer at 3 - a slow start. At 4 he won 2 graded races, then his first G1, the Grosser Preis von Baden. He was 2nd to Helissio in the Arc and beat Singspiel and others in the Breeders' Cup Turf, joining the elite. At 5 he won 3 G1s including the Eclipse S., then came the Japan Cup. He startled Japanese fans by getting aroused in the paddock, yet still beat Air Groove, whose jockey Yutaka Take said "I rode a perfect race and still got caught." He was named Cartier champion older horse and retired to stud in Japan, but sadly sired only a few local graded winners.
330=He won his first 3 starts, including the Daily Hai Sansai S., but was 2nd by a nose in both the Asahi Hai Sansai S. and Kyodo News Hai. With master Yukio Okabe aboard he won the Wakaba S. easily, but in the classics he lost the Satsuki Sho to Narita Taishin and the Derby to Winning Ticket - 2nd both times. In autumn he won the Kobe Shimbun Hai, then in the Kikuka Sho left both rivals behind, winning by 5 lengths in record time for his long-sought classic. In the Arima Kinen he was 2nd again, to Tokai Teio's miracle comeback, but at 4 he grew to win 4 straight graded races, including the Tenno Sho (Spring) and Takarazuka Kinen. Aiming for both Tenno Sho races, he developed a tendon injury during the autumn race, finishing 5th, and it ended his career.
331=In 1991 he won 4 French and American 2yo G1s and became Horse of the Year at 2 in the first Cartier Awards. He won in his second start, then 2 graded races, then France's 2yo G1s: the Prix Morny, Prix de la Salamandre and Grand Criterium. He then went to America and won the Breeders' Cup Juvenile on dirt for the first time, earning Cartier Horse of the Year in Europe and Eclipse champion 2yo colt in America. But at 3 he faded: 8th in the Kentucky Derby, then 5th back in Europe in the St. James's Palace S. He won a G2 in autumn, was well beaten in the Breeders' Cup Mile and retired. At stud he sired 5-time G1 winner Congaree, but little else; in Japan his best was imported son Dollar Arabian, winner of the Oi Kinen. He stood in Japan from 1998 without success, was exported to Switzerland 5 years later, and died at 32 in Australia in 2021.
332=A blueblood, half-brother to Breeders' Cup Turf winner Theatrical. Rated highly from his arrival at the stable, he won his first 2 starts but then kept finishing 2nd - in the Mainichi Hai, Radio Tampa Sho and Hakodate Kinen - showing talent but a soft finish. The next spring he won the Tanigawadake S., his first win in about a year, but kept falling short, and his first graded win came only in spring at 5, in the Osaka Hai. At 6 he tried the Yasuda Kinen a third time after a 3rd and a 2nd. Unfazed by A Shin Berlin's fast pace, he ran with a champion's strength to beat Genuine by a neck for his long-sought G1. He also boldly raced abroad, including the BC Classic at 5 and 6. Results didn't follow, but the know-how laid the foundation for stablemate Taiki Shuttle's G1 win abroad.
333=He won the Kisaragi Sho and Yayoi Sho to become the top classic contender, but was a close 3rd in the Satsuki Sho and 2nd by a neck in the Derby - narrow losses. 2nd by a neck again in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai, he went into the Kikuka Sho with his back to the wall. Racing boldly from 4th, he beat T.M. Opera O by a neck for his long-sought G1. But as Opera O matured, Top Road ran well without winning, ending his 4yo year winless. He won 4 more middle-to-long graded races and was always a contender in G1s, but never won another, retiring at the end of his 6yo year. He died young of heart failure in '05, leaving only 3 crops, yet his first included Bella Leia (04: Maristar II), Flora S. winner and Oaks runner-up.
334=After winning his first graded race, the Mainichi Hai, his camp paid a supplementary fee to enter the classics - and he won the Satsuki Sho. He ran well after that: 3rd in the Derby, 2nd in the Kikuka Sho, 3rd in the Arima Kinen. The next year was stunning. He won the Kyoto Kinen and Hanshin Daishoten, then the Tenno Sho (Spring) for his second G1, and the Takarazuka Kinen to cement himself as No. 1. In autumn he won the Tenno Sho (Autumn), Japan Cup and Arima Kinen, an unprecedented autumn sweep - 8 straight graded wins, unbeaten all year. The next year, perhaps tired, he was 4th in the Osaka Hai, but beat Meisho Doto by half a length in the Tenno Sho (Spring) for a third straight Tenno Sho. He then had three G1 2nds, adding no more titles, and retired with 7 G1s, equal with Rudolf. Some fans hesitate to call him "the greatest" for lack of routs, but he is surely a historic great. At stud, despite his brilliant record, his offspring did poorly: no flat stars, just two jump graded winners, T.M. Toppazure and T.M. Ace.
335=A middle-distance turf horse with a strong kick who raced well from off the pace. Blooming at 4, he won 3 straight to rise through the classes, was 2nd in his first graded race, the Niigata Kinen, and as 2nd choice in the year-end Keihan Hai won his long-sought graded title.
336=3rd in the Yayoi Sho earned him a Satsuki Sho ticket, but he was a poor 4th in the Wakaba S. and only 11th choice for the Satsuki Sho. Yet he took the lead at the third turn and no one could catch him. As the favorites watched each other behind, he held on - a shock Satsuki Sho win. Called a fluke, he was only 6th choice for the Derby. Freed by the low expectations, jockey Onishi and Sunny Brian took the lead from Silence Suzuka and set their own pace. The favorites, sure he'd tire, only panicked after the final turn. Silk Justice and Mejiro Bright closed in the low 34s, but too late - another shock wire-to-wire win and a double, at the top of his generation. But injury ended his career after the Derby. His true worth remains unknown - maybe he'd have won the Triple Crown with ease.
337=He won in his third start, but in his next he threw jockey Minai and failed to finish. He kept falling short, winning again only in his 9th start, in October at 3. That win was by 7 lengths, but he was still just an ordinary allowance horse. Switched from dirt to turf, though, he was strong. He won his next special by 8 lengths, then the Naruo Kinen by 6 in course-record time. Three straight wins drew sudden attention and an Arima Kinen run was mooted, but he went instead to the Kimpai (West). Slowly away and last, he passed 15 horses in the straight alone - so strong some said he'd have romped in the Arima Kinen too. Despite doubts about the distance, he dead-heated with Dyna Carpenter in the Hanshin Daishoten. In his first G1, the Tenno Sho (Spring), he shut out stars like Mejiro Durren, and in the Takarazuka Kinen he beat "mile emperor" Nippo Teio - 7 straight wins, 5 graded. In autumn, in the much-hyped grey clash in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), he beat the younger "grey monster" Oguri Cap, becoming the first to win both Tenno Sho races. In the Japan Cup outsider Pay the Butler ended his streak, but he was 2nd by half a length, the best Japanese finisher - strong in defeat. In his farewell Arima Kinen, his third grey duel, Oguri Cap finally beat him by half a length - a changing of the guard between two great greys. From a modest sire, and surviving his birthplace farm's bankruptcy, Tamamo Cross returned to Hokkaido as the last great horse of the Showa era. A steady sire and leading Japanese-bred stallion, he sired graded winners like Mysore Sound and Kanetsu Cross, but died of colic on April 10, 2003.
338=He won the Hanshin Sansai S. and was champion 2yo colt. Classic hopes were high, but leg trouble kept him out of the Satsuki Sho and he was a dismal 15th in the Derby. In his comeback race, the Chunichi Sports Sho Yonsai S., he ran down Satsuki Sho winner Yaeno Muteki, then won the Hakodate Kinen in Japanese record time for 2000m. He was to go to the Kikuka Sho, but leg trouble struck again, so he ran in the Mile Championship, crushing top older milers by 4 lengths with a powerful finish. He was a close 3rd behind Oguri Cap and Tamamo Cross in the Arima Kinen. Great duels with crop-mate Oguri were expected the next year, but leg trouble forced retirement - a talent who leaves you wondering "if only he'd stayed sound." He later became a very successful sire.
339=Steady - 4th in the Satsuki Sho and Derby, 3rd in the Kikuka Sho - but rarely a winner, he was a fine supporting player called "the bronze collector." His first graded win was the Nikkei Sho at 4, on extremely heavy ground, as 3rd choice. He was also 2nd on heavy ground on debut at 2, in the Yayoi Sho at 3 and the Niigata Kinen at 7 - never out of the top two on soft going. 3rd and 5th in two Tenno Sho (Spring) runs and 3rd in the Diamond S., he was a true stayer at heart.
340=A blueblood, half-brother to Air Dublin and full brother to Dance Partner, seen as a leading classic contender before his debut. He won the Yayoi Sho as hoped, but a fever forced him out of the Satsuki Sho just before; with favorite Air Groove also scratched from the Oka Sho for the same reason, it was a "wild classic" year in another sense. Recovered, he won the Principal S. and went to the Derby, but lost narrowly to Fusaichi Concorde's late swoop. In autumn he won the Kyoto Shimbun Hai, then in the Kikuka Sho waited far back, 12th at the final turn - seemingly hopeless - and unleashed an ultimate 33.8 finish to win his long-sought classic. A tendon injury followed and he retired to stud. He has sired G1 winners like That's the Plenty, Delta Blues, Turu Maru Boy and Three Rolls, and more recently Dark Shadow and Clarente - one of the most successful heirs of the late Sunday Silence.
341=He won his first 2 starts, but back trouble kept him from showing his ability and he went on a long break. On his return he ran with new power and rose fast, winning 3 graded races within a year. At 5, as the ace of Kanto's older horses in the Yasuda Kinen, he lost by a nose to foreign raider Heart Lake, but in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) he swooped from far back to become a G1 winner.
342=One of the most brilliant horses of the Fairway line. He won the Derby by a record 10 lengths, then the Irish Derby and King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. He was Britain's Horse of the Year at 3, rated 140 lbs in the International Classification, equal to the best then. In autumn he aimed for the Arc and the European Triple Crown, but was 4th in the St. Leger prep and retired to stud. Great things were expected, but after his first season he was kidnapped, leaving just one crop. The kidnappers wanted money; after the ransom demand was refused, Shergar was never seen again. That single crop included Irish St. Leger winner Authaal, but he failed at stud, and the direct line died out.
