567=At first a plain front-runner relying only on speed, she needed 6 starts to win. But once she learned to settle, she unleashed a fearsome kick, romping in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East) and charging into 3rd in the Oaks. Outclassed against colts, she was astonishing when things fell right, winning the Tokyo Shimbun Hai with a furious finish. Retired in spring at 4, she produced Derby 4th Kyowa Splendor (01: Sunday Silence).
568=A late-maturing sprinter. Stuck in allowance races until spring at 5, from June she won in the 9-million and semi-open classes and was 3rd in the Centaur S. At 6 she charged past favorites like Scarlet Bouquet and My Superman in the Swan S. for her first graded win.
569=She won an Oka Sho shared at the top of the betting with Admire Groove, then in the Oaks, 2nd choice behind her rival, unleashed the field's fastest 33.5 finish for a double. In autumn, as heavy favorite, she was 5th in the Rose S., clouding her Triple Crown path. But in the last leg, the Shuka Sho, she won a tight duel with Admire Groove by three-quarters of a length - only the second fillies' Triple Crown winner, after Mejiro Ramonu. Going for a 4th crown in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, she ran superbly but lost to Admire Groove's grit by a nose. As an older mare she struggled, and the Shuka Sho was her last win. Great things were hoped of her as a broodmare, but she died in August '07, leaving just one colt (by King Kamehameha), Judah. He debuted as favorite in March '10 but was 4th; he won twice after moving to local racing, then was crushed on returning to the JRA and retired.
570=Her style was to race forward and hang on, with first-class grit. Apart from her debut, all her wins were by a length or less - never a rout. When she got her pattern, her tenacity showed, as when she held on for 2nd to Admire Groove in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup at 4. She was 2nd in it again in '05 - proven quality. As a dam she produced Diolite Kinen winner Osumi Ichiban (09: Agnes Tachyon).
571=Running well in the mile division, he won his long-sought first graded race, the Tokyo Shimbun Hai, at 5. 3rd in the Hankyu Hai, he won the Takamatsunomiya Kinen as favorite, a G1 winner. After that he kept losing G1s as a fancied runner, and retired to stud after the '08 Mile Championship. Always producing a sharp finish, usually from the back, he was rarely beaten badly, but his style may explain the races he let slip. His first crop's Meiner Hoo won the NHK Mile C. with a brilliant finish from last, recalling his sire.
572=She improved fast as an older mare, winning two graded races for fillies and mares, the Nakayama Himba S. and Fuchu Himba S. Always fiery, she was so bad loading for the Tomoe Sho at 5 that she was banned for 3 months, then failed her gate retest and sadly had to retire.
573=She missed the classics, but as an older mare was 2nd in both the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu and Nakayama Himba S., then won the Mermaid S. as favorite in summer. After a 3rd in the Kitakyushu Kinen, she beat colts by 3 lengths in the Asahi Challenge C., and was 2nd in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, ahead of Air Groove. As a dam she produced '10 Oka Sho runner-up Oken Sakura (07: Bago) and '03 Hakodate 2yo S. winner Fille de Reve (01: Sunday Silence).
574=She held off Air Deja Vu in the Queen C. for her first graded win, but was 6th as 2nd choice in the Oka Sho. Skipping the Oaks for the mile-to-middle-distance route, she never won again, but was strong against fillies: 2nd in the Nakayama Himba S. and Queen S., 3rd in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup.
575=Just 2 wins from 13, but one was the graded Queen S. In the classics she ran well in every race: 3rd in the Oka Sho, 2nd in the Oaks, 3rd in the Shuka Sho. As a dam she produced American JCC winner Air Shady and Shuka Sho winner Air Messiah. Her half-brother is Air Shakur, 2000 Satsuki Sho and Kikuka Sho winner.
576=She debuted at local Kasamatsu and won 10 straight with overwhelming ability. Lucky to turn 3 just as JRA-local exchange began in earnest, she ran in the Oka Sho trial Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West) to try the newly opened classics. 10 straight wins, but at local tracks - would that ability work on JRA turf? She stunned JRA fans with an astonishing finish to win and earn her Oka Sho spot. Ridden by Katsumi Ando, then a Kasamatsu jockey, the local filly won huge support: favorite for the Oka Sho, she was 4th on soft going. Favorite again for the Oaks, she hit the distance wall in 13th. The dream of a local classic winner in year one of exchange didn't come true. Fans still backed her, making her favorite a third time in the Rose S., but she was 3rd in a battle of finishes, then 13th as 6th choice in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. No title, but in exchange's first year she pried open the trials' heavy door herself and ran in all three classics - a feat worth celebrating.
577=Strong from her debut, she won a fillies' maiden by 6 lengths, then the Kaede Sho easily and the Fantasy S. dominantly. As heavy 1.5 favorite in the Hanshin JF she came from the back of midfield to win, 2yo queen unbeaten in 4. But a fracture was found afterwards; after a long break she went straight to the Oaks, finishing 13th, then 4th in the Shuka Sho. Never regaining her 2yo shine, she retired in spring at 4.
578=With a sharp kick she won the Kobai Sho (5M) and Flower C., a graded win in her 5th start. In the Oka Sho a slow start left her 6th despite closing. In the Oaks she charged from last, diving to the rail at the final turn - a surprise tactic that worked perfectly - and her explosive finish won it, the one shining moment of her career. She never won again. She produced no notable offspring and died at 18 in summer, in foal to Daiwa Major.
579=Based in Hokkaido local racing, she was 3rd in the JRA-local Suzuran Sho, then moved to the JRA. Straight away she was a fine 2nd in the Hanshin JF, and the next spring won the Oka Sho as an outsider. She never won again and retired.
580=Winner of the Breeders' Cup Classic and Dubai World Cup, and Eclipse Horse of the Year in 2007 and 2008. Debuting late, in February at 3, he was 3rd in the Kentucky Derby, then won the Preakness S. for his first G1. In the Belmont he was 2nd to Rags to Riches, the first filly winner in 102 years. In autumn he won the Jockey Club Gold C. and romped in the Breeders' Cup Classic by four and a half lengths - 6 wins from 9 and Horse of the Year. At 4 he won the Dubai World Cup and 3 more G1s at home, including the Jockey Club Gold C. Going for another Breeders' Cup Classic, now on synthetic, he was 4th behind two lightly-regarded Europeans; his owner fumed, "He'll never run on plastic again." Still Horse of the Year again, he retired to stud. His first crop included Belmont S. winner Palace Malice, then Breeders' Cup Classic winner Vino Rosso and many G1 winners; 2nd on the 2016 North American sire list, he has been a top regular since.
581=He won his debut by 6 lengths, then the Fairy S. in a 2yo record that still stands. After a break he won the Crystal C. too, but lost the NZ Trophy by a head to Taiki Lion. Illness ruled out his goal, the NHK Mile C. He won the Akikaze S. in his 3rd race back, but never recovered, retiring after the Takamatsunomiya Kinen at 5.
582=She won her debut as favorite, then the TV Tokyo Sho Sansai Himba S. and Queen C., 3 straight. But she fractured a leg during the Queen C., losing the spring. Back, she was 13th as 3rd choice in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, then won the Christmas S. Going for a third graded win in the next Tokyo Shimbun Hai, she fractured again on sloppy going. She won 2 open races later, but never regained her old burst. She produced no notable offspring.
583=He led all the way to win his debut at Niigata in July at 2 as 8th choice. After winning the Kaede Sho in his 4th start he reached open class, and was 5th to A Shin Apollon in the Keio Hai 2yo S. After a break he led at a perfect pace to win the Arlington C. 7th in the NZ Trophy and 11th in the NHK Mile C., he tried dirt in the Unicorn S. He lost his rider at the start but, with his speed, crossed the line first riderless. Trying more dirt against older horses, he was 16th in the Procyon S. Back on turf at a mile, he was 3rd as 15th choice in the Yasuda Kinen and won the New Year S. (OP) twice. He met crop-mate Garbo in over 10 races, and in 2012 they finished 1-2 in both the New Year S. and Tokyo Shimbun Hai.
584=Debuting late, in April at 3, she won in her 2nd start and, after one race, won 4 straight. On a roll in the Niigata Kinen she was 3rd, 0.3 seconds behind Dyna Fairy, and kept running well, 2nd in the Himba Tokyo Times Hai (G3, 1600m). Her talent then bloomed over long distances: 3rd to Kashima Wing in the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai at 4, she won the Diamond S. early the next year, her first graded race. In the Nakayama Kinen she was 9th, her first finish outside the top five, and retired.
585=She won in her 3rd start, then ran the next week in the Kokura 2yo S. Last in the betting, the only runner over 100 to 1, she charged from the back in the straight to win her first graded race. She was crushed in the Fantasy S., but as 7th choice in the Hanshin JF she again swooped down the straight to win. From 3 she slumped badly, finishing in double figures in the classics and almost every race - an early bloomer who shone for a moment at 2.
586=Her close relatives include Broad Appeal, a 13-time winner including the Silk Road S. She won her first graded race, the Fillies' Revue, an Oka Sho trial, at 3, but was a non-factor 16th in both the Oka Sho and Oaks. Back after about six months, she kept finishing in double figures without any sign of improvement and left the turf. As a dam she produced Success Brocken, winner of the February S. and Tokyo Daishoten.
587=Unbeaten in her first 3 up to the Edelweiss Sho, her first graded win, she then won the Hokkaido 2yo Yushun for 4 straight. From then on she lost race after race, as if a different horse. She moved to local Oi at 4 and to America at 5, where she won the Dahlia H., her first win in about 3 years.
588=She won the Tulip Sho and Rose S. and ran well in the classics: 2nd in the Oka Sho, 4th in the Oaks, 4th in the Shuka Sho, 5th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, then won the Aichi Hai against older mares. At 4 she was 4th in the Yasuda Kinen and 5th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup - steady, but "just short in G1s." In '08 she won the Kyoto Himba S., her first win in about a year, raising hopes of a G1, but fractured a leg. While resting she developed colic, and thrashing in pain suffered a new open fracture. Beyond saving, she was put down, to much regret.
589=From the first crop of double classic winner Neo Universe, he won the 2009 Japanese Derby like his sire. Unbeaten in 4 from his debut, including the Yayoi Sho, he was clear Satsuki Sho favorite but finished 14th as the fast pace collapsed the leaders, him included - a shock result. Seeking redemption in the Derby, he raced forward on heavy going and pulled away in the straight to win by 4 lengths. Leg trouble then kept him from racing properly; 2nd in the Sapporo Kinen at 4, he rested again, and after 2 more years off was 14th in the Sapporo Kinen, his last race. He went to stud from 2014.
590=Winner of the 2010 Japanese Derby and 2012 Tenno Sho (Autumn), with just 6 wins from 25 - no glamorous path. Not highly rated in the classics, he was 3rd in the Satsuki Sho as 11th choice, and only 7th choice for the Derby, where an astonishing 32.7 finish won him the crown. A long slump followed, 2.5 years without a win. He bounced back in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) at 5: as 5th choice, he waited at the back in a fast race and slipped out perfectly to win. It was an imperial-attended race, and jockey M. Demuro's dismounting after his victory lap, removing his helmet and bowing deeply to the Emperor and Empress, became a moving talking point. He raced until the end of his 6yo year, retiring on Arima Kinen day 2013.
591=At 3, teamed with Mirco Demuro, she stole the Tulip Sho from the front, outfoxing favorite Osumi Cosmo. In the Oka Sho she tracked in 2nd and showed amazing grit, a fine 5th, 0.4 seconds back. She did little after and retired.
592=With early speed typical of a Danzig x Mr. Prospector cross, and the versatility to race from anywhere, she came from behind to win a 5-million race as 8th choice, then led all the way to win the Zen-Nihon 2yo Yushun despite a loose horse harassing her throughout. At 3 she aimed for the Oka Sho via the Fillies' Revue, but was last in both, then raced only on dirt. She won the Cluster C. in August at 4 for a second graded win and raced until August at 5.
593=As 11th choice on debut she won with a stunning late charge. In spring at 3, ridden by Funabashi's jockey Sakai, she charged again to win the Flora S., her first graded title. Those were her only 2 wins.
594=After a 2nd in the Queen C., she ran steadily: 7th in the Oka Sho, 4th in the Oaks. In the Queen S., a Shuka Sho trial, as 3rd choice she won from the front. She was 12th in the Shuka Sho and won only once more, the BSN Open at 5, though she ran a close 4th in her last Queen Elizabeth II Cup. As a dam she produced Admire Main (03: Sunday Silence), '06 Aoba Sho and Mainichi Hai winner and Derby runner-up.
595=His brother Risk Me won the Grand Prix de Paris and starred at middle distances in France. Mainly on dirt, he raced tough until 7, consistent against stars: 2nd to Hokuto Vega in the Teio Sho and February S. (G2) at 6, 2nd to Kyoto City in the Winter S. at 5. He won 7 of 32; 6 of 26 on dirt and just 1 of 6 on turf - but that 1 was his only graded win, the Kabutoyama Kinen.
596=She won the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East), but injury kept her out of the Oaks. In autumn she won the Rose S. but did nothing in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, 7th. She ran well in middle-distance graded races without winning, and retired at the end of her 4yo year. Silent Melody, by Symboli Kris S., won the March S.
