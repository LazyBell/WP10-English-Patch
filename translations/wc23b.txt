125=* Japan's Greatest Takes On the Arc\nThe year before, Deep Impact won an unbeaten Triple Crown and became a social phenomenon. As an older horse this year he kept running in a class of his own. In the Hanshin Daishoten he handled good-to-soft going for the first time with ease. In the Tenno Sho (Spring) he drew a race-record 73.5% of win bets. He missed the break again, then launched a long run before the third turn and led at the final turn. Still he produced the fastest finish in the straight, showing that wherever he moved, it didn't matter. His time was a world record for 3200m on turf. In the Takarazuka Kinen he again drew a race-record 75.2% of win bets and won easily; in the photo afterwards Yutaka Take raised five fingers for five crowns.\nHis camp, long planning a trip abroad, set his next race as the Arc, the world's greatest. Many Japanese fans traveled to Longchamp dreaming of glory. As favorite he broke well, and unlike his usual style, sat 2nd or 3rd. He led 400m out, but in the straight Rail Link, on a 4-race winning streak, passed him after a fierce duel, and he crossed the line 3rd. He was praised for his effort on his return, but over a month later he was disqualified from the Arc: ipratropium, a drug used for respiratory problems, had been detected. It wasn't banned in Japan then, but was added the next year after this. Japan's greatest horse's Arc challenge ended in an unexpected way.\nBack home, announcing his retirement at year's end, he ran in the Japan Cup and Arima Kinen. As if to vent his frustration, he won both easily, leaving the turf with the pride of Japan's best intact. His overwhelming power lives on in his offspring, still making a huge impact on racing.\n\n* Taking On G1s Abroad\nBesides Deep Impact, many other horses took on G1s abroad this year.\nLocal horse Cosmo Bulk, who stormed the classics in 2004, ran in the Singapore Airlines International Cup on his first trip abroad. Racing forward, he won - the first G1 abroad by a local-based horse.\nAmong JRA horses, Delta Blues traveled to Australia. He was narrowly 3rd in the Caulfield Cup, then won the Melbourne Cup, called the greatest festival of Australian racing (Japan's Pop Rock was 2nd) - the first G1 in the distant Southern Hemisphere won by a Japan-trained horse.
128=* "To the Top of the View" Buena Vista\nHer sire was Derby winner Special Week, who walked the royal road; her dam was G1 winner Biwa Heidi, and half-brothers Admire Japan and Admire Aura shone in the classics. Buena Vista lived up to expectations, sweeping past the field in the straight to win the Hanshin JF and champion 2yo filly.\nIn her first race this year, the Tulip Sho, she drew a race-record 72.3% of win bets and won easily with a powerful kick. In the Oka Sho she was a clear favorite. Nearly last into the straight, over 10 lengths behind the leader, she surged wide to win. In the Oaks she again unleashed a devilish finish from the back, beating Red Desire, who had broken clear first, by a nose after a photo for the double.\nAn Arc trip was scrapped after a 2nd in the Sapporo Kinen prep, and she aimed for the fillies' Triple Crown in the Shuka Sho. She sat in midfield, marking Red Desire. In the straight they dueled again and hit the line together. In another photo, this time Red Desire won by a nose. Not only did Buena Vista miss the Triple Crown, she was judged to have interfered and was demoted to 3rd.\nShe was 3rd in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup behind the runaway Queen Spumante and T.M. Precure, and narrowly 2nd in the Arima Kinen after a duel with Dream Journey, proving she could match older horses. Named champion 3yo filly, she carried great hopes as a future star of Japanese racing.\n\n* Vodka, the Greatest Mare Ever\nThe year before, Vodka won an epic duel with Daiwa Scarlet in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and was Horse of the Year. This spring she tried the Dubai Duty Free (G1) again after a 4th the year before, but was 7th.\nBack home her first race was the Victoria Mile. She had lost 3 straight since the Tenno Sho, but fans never doubted her, making her 1.7 favorite. She won by 7 lengths in record time for her 5th G1, and passed Air Groove to become the top-earning female ever.\nBut she wasn't done. In the Yasuda Kinen she waited in midfield. In the straight she tried to come up the inside but was walled in by a crowd of horses. Just when all seemed lost, she shot through a tiny gap, surged forward and caught double winner Deep Sky, who had broken clear first, before the line. With this win she became the first female to win the Yasuda Kinen twice, set a new record for G1 wins by a female with 6, and became the first female to earn 1 billion yen.\nIn autumn she narrowly lost the Mainichi Okan and the Tenno Sho (Autumn). The big one, the Japan Cup, was a race she longed to win after finishing 4th and 3rd before. She led early in the straight and broke fully clear at one point. Kikuka Sho winner Oken Bruce Lee charged at the end, but she held on by a nose - the first Japan-trained female to win the Japan Cup. Her 7th G1 tied the record held by Symboli Rudolf and others, and she was Horse of the Year for the second straight year.\n\n* Dream Journey Returns to Glory\nDream Journey was by Stay Gold out of a Mejiro McQueen mare. This cross later produced Orfevre and Gold Ship and was called the "golden cross" - and Dream Journey was its pioneer. He won the Asahi Hai FS at 2 and was seen as a future star, but went through the classics without a win, and G1 titles stayed out of reach.\nAt 5 this year he beat favorite Deep Sky in the Sankei Osaka Hai for his 5th graded win. He ran well for 3rd in the Tenno Sho (Spring), then went for his spring goal, the Takarazuka Kinen. Favorite was Deep Sky again, at 1.6; 2nd choice Dream Journey was far behind at 7.1. But in the straight he swept wide past Deep Sky and Sakura Mega Wonder, who were driving hard, to win his first G1 in about two and a half years.\nAfter a 2nd in the All Comers and 6th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), he went to the Arima Kinen. After those losses, favorite was double winner Buena Vista. Waiting nearly last, he swept forward, and in the straight fought a fierce duel with Buena Vista, who had broken clear from a good spot, catching her by half a length. He became only the 6th horse since graded races began to win both the spring and autumn Grand Prix. With his great partner Kenichi Ikezoe, "Mr. Grand Prix," he is a horse remembered in both records and memories.
