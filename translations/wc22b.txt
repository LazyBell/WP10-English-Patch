122=* Still in Love: The Second Fillies' Triple Crown\nThis year's fillies' classics were split between two daughters of Sunday Silence. Favorite in all three legs was Admire Groove, a blueblood out of "noble empress" and Horse of the Year Air Groove; she won 3 straight against colts before the Oka Sho. 2nd choice in all three was Still in Love. With a graded-winning brother (Big Bayamon, winner of the Radio Tampa Sho), she was watched early, won her first two, and was a gallant 2nd despite trouble in the Tulip Sho trial to earn her Oka Sho spot.\nIn the Oka Sho, Admire Groove shockingly missed the break and had to race from behind. Still in Love raced in a good spot, burst powerfully out of the middle of the track in the straight and won by a length and a half. Admire Groove closed wide but could only finish 3rd - a clear win for Still in Love.\nThe Oaks held special meaning for Admire Groove: a chance for three generations of winners after her granddam Dyna Carl and dam Air Groove. Three generations or a fillies' double? Again Admire Groove missed the break - in her 3yo spring this habit was her great trial. She was nearly last at the final turn, and the gap to Still in Love, who surged sharply from midfield to win, only grew. A double won on ability over popularity - it was clear to all that the true heroine was Still in Love.\nBoth camps chose the Rose S. for their autumn start. Still in Love was favorite for the first time, but Admire Groove won and she was 5th. The heroine's crown seemed to pass to Admire Groove. Then came the last leg, the Shuka Sho. Still in Love stuck to her own style, going forward, breaking clear and holding on, holding off Admire Groove by three-quarters of a length. Showing unmatched big-race grit, she became only the second fillies' Triple Crown winner, after Mejiro Ramonu in 1986. The Shuka Sho was created in 1996 as the third leg when the Queen Elizabeth II Cup opened to older mares; this was the first Triple Crown since.\nAmong colts, Neo Universe won the Satsuki Sho and the Japanese Derby but missed the Triple Crown, so no colt-and-filly double Triple Crown. Still, double classic winners among both colts and fillies in the same year was a first in 28 years, since 1975 (Kabuto Yao and Tesco Gaby).\n\n* Hishi Miracle's Two Long-Shot Wins\nIn the previous Kikuka Sho, with favorite No Reason losing his rider at the start, Hishi Miracle swept forward from around the third turn and held on to win as 10th choice - a "miracle" true to his name. But he was 11th in the Arima Kinen, 12th in the Hanshin Daishoten and 7th in the Osaka Hai this year, and was being branded a "fluke Kikuka Sho winner."\nThen came the Tenno Sho (Spring). Despite a weak field with only one other G1 winner, Dantsu Flame, he was only 7th choice. But he defied the odds, holding off Sunrise Jaeger's charge by half a length.\nThe Takarazuka Kinen drew a stellar field, including last year's Horse of the Year Symboli Kris S. and Agnes Digital, a G1 winner on turf and dirt. The day before, one person's huge bet made Hishi Miracle the 1.7 favorite (the bet did win), though he settled at 6th choice (16.3). Racing in midfield, he rounded the final turn 8th, then charged to the front in the straight. Turu Maru Boy came flying wide and drew level at the end, but he held on by a neck. As if to say "don't call it a fluke anymore," he won his third G1, proving his ability.\n\n* Symboli Kris S. Wins the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Arima Kinen Again\nThe year before, Symboli Kris S. won the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Arima Kinen at 3 and was Horse of the Year. Big things were expected, but in his first race, the Takarazuka Kinen without a prep, he ran into Hishi Miracle's long-shot win and was a surprise 5th. But again he came good in autumn. In the Tenno Sho (Autumn), shrugging off the widest draw and the fast pace set by Lohengrin and Go Steady fighting for the lead, he burst clear a third of the way out from the rail in the straight to become the first to win the Tenno Sho (Autumn) twice (Mejiro McQueen and T.M. Opera O had won the spring race twice).\nIn the Japan Cup he let Tap Dance City get away alone and was well beaten in 3rd, over 9 lengths back. Then his farewell, the Arima Kinen. Racing in midfield, he moved up from before the third turn, drew level with the leaders easily at the final turn, and simply drew away, winning by 9 lengths - the biggest margin in a G1 since graded races began in 1984.\nThe first to win the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Arima Kinen twice each, he was Horse of the Year for the second straight year, matching his great namesake Symboli Rudolf (1984 and 1985), and left the turf in glory.
