141=* Equinox: An Autumn Charge Erasing Spring's Frustration\nEquinox was from the first crop of 7-time G1 winner Kitasan Black. He won his second start, the Tokyo Sports Hai 2yo S., with an astonishing finish and became a leading classic contender.\nAt 3 he went straight to the Satsuki Sho without a prep, 3rd choice behind previous champion 2yo colt Do Deuce and unbeaten Kyodo News Hai winner Danon Beluga. He moved from midfield to a good spot and pulled clear in the straight, but was caught at the end by 5th-choice stablemate Geoglyph. He raced at the back and produced the fastest finish, but fell a neck short of Do Deuce under Yutaka Take - 2nd again.\nNext, following Efforia's path from the year before, he went to the Tenno Sho (Autumn). Panthalassa, a dramatic Dubai Turf winner in spring, set off in his trademark huge lead, with Equinox tracking in midfield. As Panthalassa turned into the straight over 10 lengths clear, the stands roared, "He's going to hold on!" But Equinox unleashed an astonishing kick, closed fast and ran him down. He then won the Arima Kinen in just his 6th career start, the fewest ever, completing a father-son double. Like Efforia the year before, he turned the frustration of narrow classic defeats into triumph among the older horses.\n\n\n* Stars on Earth's Double\nStars on Earth was from the second crop of double classic winner Duramente. She broke her maiden in her second start, then was favorite three times running but kept just missing.\nGiven that record she was only 7th choice for the Oka Sho. But she surged from midfield and drew level with Water Navillera right at the line. After a photo, Stars on Earth was crowned queen of the cherry blossoms. In the Oaks she broke well, settled in midfield and swung widest of all in the straight, powering down the middle to win clearly by a length and a quarter - the fillies' double.\nBut after the race a chip fracture in her right leg was found. After a break she made it to the Shuka Sho, but just missed in 3rd, falling short of the Triple Crown.
142=* Liberty Island Wins the Fillies' Triple Crown\nLiberty Island (by Duramente) showed outstanding speed from her very first race. Her final 600m of 31.4 seconds on debut tied the fastest in JRA history, and was naturally the fastest ever for a debut. She narrowly lost the Artemis S. after finding no room in the straight, but won the Hanshin JF clearly in her third start and was unanimously champion 2yo filly.\nShe went straight to the Oka Sho for her first 3yo race, overwhelming 1.6 favorite over the horses who came through the trials. She was a bit slow away, but unleashed a tremendous finish widest of all in the straight to win. The new jockey camera, introduced that spring, captured her astonishing run and jockey Yuga Kawada calling her "young lady" after the race. Next was the Oaks. Racing much further forward than before, she left the field behind in the straight for the double, winning by 6 lengths - beyond Gentildonna's 5 (2012), the biggest Oaks margin since graded races began (1984).\nShe went straight to the last leg, the Shuka Sho. Her dominant wins made her an incredible 1.1 favorite. She broke well, sat in midfield, broke clear early at the final turn and shut out the late chargers - 4 straight G1 wins and the 7th fillies' Triple Crown ever.\n\n* Equinox Retires After 6 Straight G1s\nThe year before, Equinox dominated the older horses in autumn at 3. This year he tried the Dubai Sheema Classic, his first race abroad. All eyes were on how he'd do against the world's best - and he broke well and, for the first time, led. He never gave up the lead, winning easily. Lemaire never even used the whip, yet he broke the record by a second. This made him world No. 1 at 129 lbs in the international ratings (World's Best Racehorse Rankings) - the world's best horse in name and fact.\nBack home he ran in the Takarazuka Kinen, which his sire never won, holding off late-improving Through Seven Seas by a neck to avenge his sire. Then he went for a second Tenno Sho (Autumn). Like Panthalassa the year before, Jack d'Or set a fast pace in front. But where Equinox had closed from midfield the year before, this time he raced near the front. As Jack d'Or and others tired, he kept going and pulled away to win easily, showing his versatility and again setting a record time - the overwhelming power of the world's best horse.\nHe went straight to the Japan Cup, where the entry of this year's fillies' Triple Crown winner Liberty Island made it a summit clash. With Panthalassa again in a huge lead, he raced from a good spot, shook off Liberty Island 200m out to lead with ease and won by 4 lengths. Liberty Island's jockey Kawada called Equinox "the answer of modern Japanese racing, the greatest horse in Japanese racing history." The win took his earnings past 2.2 billion yen, passing Almond Eye as the all-time leader.\nAfterwards his camp announced his retirement. Having moved past his classic disappointments, he left the turf with an astonishing 6 straight G1 wins.\n\n* Big Feats on Dirt\nPanthalassa, who thrilled racing with his runaway style the year before, tried a dirt G1 for the first time in the Saudi Cup. Leading at a fast pace, he couldn't pull clear as usual, but showed tremendous grit in the straight to win - the first Japanese horse to win the Saudi Cup, and, with his Dubai Turf win, the first Japan-trained horse to win G1s abroad on both turf and dirt. He was 10th in the Dubai WC, won by Japan's Ushba Tesoro, who until autumn at 5 had won just 3 races on turf, but rose to G1 level after switching to dirt and showed his power abroad.\nLemon Pop, ridden by Ryusei Sakai, won both the February S. and the Champions C. - only the 4th horse ever to do so.\nWith the 3yo dirt triple crown to start the next year, a star also emerged among the 3yos: Mick Fire, who won the Haneda Hai, Tokyo Derby and Japan Dirt Derby for the Southern Kanto triple crown. He was the first to win it since these three races became the triple crown in 2002, and as it was reorganized into the dirt triple crown from 2024, he was both the first and only winner.\nMany dirt heroes were born this year, raising great hopes for dirt racing's future.
143=* Do Deuce's Shock, Regaleira's Brilliant Run\nIn 2024, after Equinox retired, 13 straight JRA G1s had different winning jockeys - a wide-open year. But the star was still Do Deuce, Equinox's great rival and the previous Arima Kinen winner. Unlucky with pace and going in his two spring races, he won his first autumn race, the Tenno Sho (Autumn), clearly by over a length from last year's Derby winner Tastiera. In the Japan Cup he moved up wide and won a fierce battle near the line by a neck, one step from the autumn older horses' triple crown, last done by Zenno Rob Roy in 2004. With the Arima Kinen his last race, hopes were sky-high, but two days before it he was scratched with lameness in his right foreleg, sending shockwaves through racing.\nThe Arima Kinen was turned into a historic occasion by Regaleira, winless since the previous year's Hopeful S. Unlike before she raced from a good spot, and in the straight fought a fierce duel with Shahryar. They hit the line together, and the photo went to Regaleira - the first 3yo filly to win the Arima Kinen in 64 years, since Star Roch in 1960, and only the second ever.\n\n* Year One of the 3yo Dirt Triple Crown: Forever Young Shines\nThis year the 3yo dirt triple crown began. The first leg, the Haneda Hai, went to Amante Bianco; the second, the Tokyo Derby, to Ramjet; and the third, the Japan Dirt Classic, to Forever Young - three different winners in its first year.\nForever Young was especially brilliant abroad, winning the Saudi Derby and UAE Derby and running 3rd in both the Kentucky Derby and the Breeders' Cup Classic, America's greatest races. At year's end he beat Japan's top older horses in the Tokyo Daishoten.
