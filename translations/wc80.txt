1798=Galileo's masterpiece: unbeaten in 14, mainly at a mile, with 10 G1s, most won by wide margins, he dominated European racing from 2010 to 2012. Brilliant at 2, he was Cartier champion 2yo; at 3 he won 4 G1s incl. the 2000 Guineas and St. James's Palace S., Cartier Horse of the Year. At 4 he won the Queen Anne S. by 11 lengths, then stepped up to 10 furlongs to win the International S. and Champion S. with ease - Horse of the Year again, rated 140 lbs, the highest ever in the World Thoroughbred Rankings. At stud he was just as superior: his first crop gave G1 winner Cracksman, and in Japan Soul Stirring and Mozu Ascot started a Frankel boom. In 2021 Adayar won the Derby and Hurricane Lane the Irish Derby, and he took the British and Irish sire title from his sire Galileo. In 2023 he sired a northern hemisphere record 11 G1 winners, and in 2024 his fee equaled Dubawi's as Europe's highest at 350,000 pounds. He also reached 100 graded winners in a record 3,144 days, faster than Galileo, Dubawi or Japan's Deep Impact.

1807=Winner of the 2013 Mile Championship. Originally a middle-to-long distance horse, he was 3rd to Orfevre in the Kikuka Sho at 3, and at 5 won the Kyoto Kinen and was 2nd in the Tenno Sho (Spring). But often falling short, his camp suddenly switched him to a mile that autumn. In the Mile CS he waited at the back and swept past them all with the fastest finish. Kyoto suited him, but a G1 win at an unfamiliar distance was remarkable - and it gave Yutaka Take an unprecedented 100th JRA G1 win. 1808=Winner of the 2011 Kentucky Derby and 2013 Dubai World Cup. He raced on turf and all-weather early, winning the Spiral S. (G3) in March at 3, then tried dirt for the first time in the Kentucky Derby. Only 11th choice, he moved early from midfield to win clearly - the first horse to win the Kentucky Derby without a dirt race. 2nd in the Preakness and 6th in the Belmont, he then rested with a fracture. Aiming for the Dubai World Cup at 4, he broke down again and missed it, ending the year with a 2nd in the BC Mile. At 5 he finally went to Dubai and, racing forward, pulled clear in the straight to win the big title. He was 11th in England's Queen Anne S. and retired, standing as a shuttle sire in Australia and America with G1 winners in both, then in Japan from 2020; his first Japanese crop was born in 2021.

1813=An Australian sprint mare, unbeaten in 25 with 15 G1s.
