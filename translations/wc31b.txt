300=By Tolomeo out of a No Attention mare - an obscure pedigree - yet he won 11 races (nine 2nds) before retiring at 8, a great supporting actor at middle distances. A late bloomer needing 6 starts to win, he missed the classics. During a long break from autumn at 4 he grew a lot; back at 5 he was 2nd, then won 5 straight, including the Sekiya Kinen and All Comers. Leg trouble kept him winless at 6, but at 7 he won 3 graded races including the Nakayama Kinen. 3 of his 5 graded wins came at Nakayama, and he was 3rd in the Arima Kinen - a Nakayama specialist.
301=His stayer's blood bloomed in the Kikuka Sho, which he barely got into. The next year, ridden by Yutaka Take, he won the Hanshin Daishoten and the Tenno Sho (Spring) - three generations of Tenno Sho winners after Mejiro Asama and Mejiro Titan. But he was 2nd in the Takarazuka Kinen; won the Tenno Sho (Autumn) by 6 lengths but was demoted to 18th for veering; was 4th in the Japan Cup; and a shock 2nd to Daiyu Saku in the Arima Kinen. He won the Hanshin Daishoten again, then met double classic winner Tokai Teio in the Tenno Sho (Spring), "the showdown of the century" with a 150-yen quinella. Not favorite, he won clearly. So consistent was he that some joked "he always wins, so races are boring." After a year out injured he won the Osaka Hai in record time. Going for a third Tenno Sho (Spring) he lost to Rice Shower, but then won the Takarazuka Kinen. In the Kyoto Daishoten that autumn he set a stunning course record of 2:22.7, but was injured after the race and retired. At stud he sired Meguro Kinen winner Hokuto Sultan, but died in 2006. As a broodmare sire, though, his "golden cross" with Stay Gold produced Triple Crown winner Orfevre, Gold Ship and more - the champion's blood lives on.
303=It's no exaggeration to say he created the early image of Sunday Silence's runners: "fiery-tempered but fearsomely strong." From the first crop, he was called the best, even "the phantom Triple Crown winner." He won his debut by 8 lengths, beat future Derby winner Tayasu Tsuyoshi in the Momiji S., and won the Asahi Hai Sansai S. unbeaten - easy wins despite immaturely charging at rivals. He won the Yayoi Sho clearly at 3, and people debated "how many crowns," but a tendon injury forced sudden retirement. He left the turf with a sense of the miracle that might have been. At stud he thrived, siring dirt king Kane Hekili (7 dirt G1s), Dream Passport, Victoria Mile winner Koiuta, 2-time Takamatsunomiya Kinen winner Kinshasa no Kiseki, Daitaku Riva, Mile CS winner Sadamu Patek and Satsuki Sho winner Isla Bonita. He once led all Japanese-bred sires in career wins - a true great sire.
304=A big-name mochikomi with high hopes, but frail from birth and with sore shins early on, he couldn't be fully prepared or show his ability. He missed the classics, reaching open class at the end of his 3yo year. Early at 4 he won the Kimpai (East), his first graded win, then was 2nd in the Meguro Kinen but fractured a leg. At 5, back after 13 months, he crushed Satsuki Sho winner Genuine in the Nakayama Kinen. In the Tenno Sho (Spring) he brushed aside crop-mate Triple Crown winner Narita Brian and Horse of the Year Mayano Top Gun, winning his long-sought G1 and the title of best horse at once. He added the Arima Kinen and was '96 Horse of the Year and champion older male. He announced an Arc challenge, but was injured in the Prix Foy prep and came home. At stud he sired 3-time graded winner Sakura Century and Roman Empire, known for a Keisei Hai dead heat.
306=With a white blaze and smart looks, he was a "good-looking horse" watched before his debut. Needing 4 starts to win, he was called "more hype than horse," but after winning the Hiiragi Sho and Junior C. he crushed 2yo champion Ines Fujin in the Yayoi Sho. In the Satsuki Sho he was 3rd to Hakutaisei, and as Derby favorite he couldn't catch Ines Fujin, finishing 2nd. In autumn he won the Kyoto Shimbun Hai in record time and was Kikuka Sho favorite, but fellow Mejiro late bloomer McQueen beat him, and he ended the classics uncrowned. 2nd in the Arima Kinen and Nakayama Kinen, 4th behind McQueen in the Tenno Sho (Spring) - always just short. Then in the Takarazuka Kinen jockey Norihiro Yokoyama stayed ahead of McQueen and kicked early, and at last he won a G1. At stud his first crop gave 5-time G1 winner Mejiro Dober (94: Mejiro Beauty) and Tenno Sho winner Mejiro Bright (94: Reine du Temps), and he kept siring graded winners as an ace among Japanese-bred sires.
307=By Sunday Silence out of double classic winner Vega - a blueblood impossible not to get excited about. On his debut he was demoted to 4th for interference - a flashy start, in a way. He then won the Erica Sho and soon the Radio Tampa Hai Sansai S. But at 3 he was 2nd to Narita Top Road in the Yayoi Sho, then 6th in the Satsuki Sho - due to rain, or a preparation slip shown by a 10 kg weight loss. Then the Derby, a must-win. From 14th at the final turn he unleashed a powerful kick up Tokyo's long straight and caught Narita Top Road by a neck right at the line. He was 6th in the Kikuka Sho, then retired with leg trouble. He died suddenly in '04 after just 4 crops - a great loss, as he sired G1 winners like Mile CS winner Blumenblatt and Oka Sho winner Kiss to Heaven.
309=He debuted fairly late, in January at 3, but ran tough until 8, winning 12 of 38. Most remarkable was his seemingly endless capacity to improve. He won his second graded race, the Nakayama Kinen, at 7, then kept winning, finally taking the international Hong Kong International C. - a first for a Japan-bred, Japan-trained horse - and was that year's champion Japanese-sired horse.
311=He won the Asahi Hai Sansai S. but fractured a leg in training; surgery to insert bolts was so major some said he'd never return. He came back at Hakodate 82 weeks later but kept losing, and leg trouble sent him on another long break. He seemed finished, but at 6, as 10th choice in the Tokyo Shimbun Hai, he raced forward and held on - a shock first win in about 3 years. He then won the Hankyu Hai, ran 2nd in the Takamatsunomiya Kinen, and won the Yasuda Kinen, his first G1 in 3.5 years - a miracle comeback. He was also 2nd to Believe in the Sprinters S. At stud he sired Sprinters S. winner Aston Machan (04: Wrestling Cups) and sprint graded winner Majin Prosper (07: Hollywood Dream). He retired from stud in 2015 and died at 21 in 2017.
