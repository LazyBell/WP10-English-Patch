334=After winning his first graded race, the Mainichi Hai, his camp paid a supplementary fee to enter the classics - and he won the Satsuki Sho. He ran well after that: 3rd in the Derby, 2nd in the Kikuka Sho, 3rd in the Arima Kinen. The next year was stunning. He won the Kyoto Kinen and Hanshin Daishoten, then the Tenno Sho (Spring) for his second G1, and the Takarazuka Kinen to cement himself as No. 1. In autumn he won the Tenno Sho (Autumn), Japan Cup and Arima Kinen, an unprecedented autumn sweep - 8 straight graded wins, unbeaten all year. The next year, perhaps tired, he was 4th in the Osaka Hai, but beat Meisho Doto by half a length in the Tenno Sho (Spring) for a third straight Tenno Sho. He then had three G1 2nds, adding no more titles, and retired with 7 G1s, equal with Rudolf. Lacking routs, he isn't always called the greatest, but he's a historic great. At stud his offspring did poorly: no flat stars, just two jump graded winners, T.M. Toppazure and T.M. Ace.
339=Steady - 4th in the Satsuki Sho and Derby, 3rd in the Kikuka Sho - but rarely a winner, he was called "the bronze collector." His first graded win was the Nikkei Sho at 4, on extremely heavy ground, as 3rd choice. He was also 2nd on heavy ground on debut at 2, in the Yayoi Sho at 3 and the Niigata Kinen at 7 - never out of the top two on soft going. Placed in the Tenno Sho (Spring) and Diamond S., he was a true stayer at heart.
340=A blueblood, half-brother to Air Dublin and full brother to Dance Partner, seen as a leading classic contender before his debut. He won the Yayoi Sho as hoped, but a fever forced him out of the Satsuki Sho just before. Recovered, he won the Principal S. and went to the Derby, but lost narrowly to Fusaichi Concorde's late swoop. In autumn he won the Kyoto Shimbun Hai, then in the Kikuka Sho waited far back, 12th at the final turn - seemingly hopeless - and unleashed an ultimate 33.8 finish to win his long-sought classic. A tendon injury followed and he retired to stud. He has sired G1 winners like That's the Plenty, Delta Blues, Turu Maru Boy and Three Rolls, and more recently Dark Shadow and Clarente - one of the most successful heirs of the late Sunday Silence.
335=A turf middle-distance horse with a sharp kick, best off the pace. Blooming at 4, he won 3 straight, was 2nd in the Niigata Kinen, then won the Keihan Hai as 2nd choice.
338=He won the Hanshin Sansai S. and was champion 2yo colt, but leg trouble kept him out of the Satsuki Sho and he was 15th in the Derby. He then ran down Satsuki Sho winner Yaeno Muteki in the Chunichi Sports Sho Yonsai S. and won the Hakodate Kinen in Japanese record time for 2000m. Leg trouble ruled out the Kikuka Sho, so he ran in the Mile Championship and crushed top older milers by 4 lengths. He was a close 3rd behind Oguri Cap and Tamamo Cross in the Arima Kinen. Duels with crop-mate Oguri were expected, but leg trouble forced retirement - "if only he'd stayed sound." He became a very successful sire.
