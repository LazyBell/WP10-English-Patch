491=She won her debut, then injury cost her six months. 2nd on her return, she won 3 straight, and in her 6th start, her first graded try, beat colts by 2 lengths in the Nikkei Shinshun Hai. She later won the Nakayama Himba S. at 5.
493=Ridden by "Tsunoda of the fillies," she won her first 3 with overwhelming power, beating Scarlet Bouquet by two and a half lengths in the Tulip Sho. But that year's fillies' classics were a wide-open, unusually strong field. Unbeaten Isono Roubles (5 for 5) was Oka Sho favorite, Northern Driver, who beat colts in the Pegasus S. (G3, 1700m), 2nd choice, and Sister Tosho only 4th choice behind Scarlet Bouquet, whom she'd beaten. Isono Roubles lost a shoe just before the start. Sister Tosho tracked the leaders, led at the final turn and burst clear, winning by 2 lengths - an unbeaten Oka Sho winner. Isono Roubles got revenge in the Oaks, but Sister Tosho dispelled doubts about the distance in 2nd and was named champion 3yo filly. Injury followed, and she retired without another win.
495=Along with A Shin Washington, a year older, she cemented the image "A Shin = foreign-bred, sprinter, front-runner." She won her debut by a distance in record time, and at 3 won two G3s, the Queen C. and Arlington C. After an 18-month break she slumped, but at 5 won the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu and Silk Road S. She was 2nd in the Takamatsunomiya Hai (now Kinen), never winning on the big stage. As a broodmare she retired without producing a star with her speed.
498=After beating Daiichi Ruby in a trial, she was 6th in the Oaks and ran poorly in autumn. But in a wide-open Queen Elizabeth II Cup, she was 8th choice at 13.6, a fancied outsider. Waiting at the back, she closed in the straight and crossed the line to Kiyoshi Sugimoto's famous call: "Number 18, one week late!" The week before, her jockey Norihiro Yokoyama had lost the Kikuka Sho as favorite on Mejiro Ryan - also number 18.
499=3rd in the Oaks and Queen S., she was consistent. In the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, with the betting focused on fillies' double winner Max Beauty, she was 4th choice, but swooped on Max Beauty in the straight and won by 2 lengths. She was put down in '08 due to old age.
501=She ran a gutsy 3rd in a debut hailed as "the strongest maiden race ever." Her jockey said "she'll win a G1," and she did in her 3rd start, the Hanshin JF. With amazing finishes she won the Oka Sho and Oaks, earning the nickname "the female Deep Impact." An Arc trip was planned, but after a 2nd in the Sapporo Kinen she stayed home. Going for the fillies' Triple Crown in the Shuka Sho, she trailed rival Red Desire and was demoted to 3rd for interference. In the Queen Elizabeth II Cup lightly-fancied Queen Spumante and T.M. Precure held on in front, leaving her 3rd, and in the Arima Kinen she was 2nd to Dream Journey's sharp finish. At 4 she proved herself world-class with a 2nd in the Dubai Sheema Classic, won the Victoria Mile with a devilish kick for her 4th G1, was 2nd to outsider Nakayama Festa in the Takarazuka Kinen, then won the Tenno Sho (Autumn) by 2 lengths - her long-sought G1 against colts. In the Japan Cup she crossed the line first but was demoted to 2nd behind Rose Kingdom for interference, and in the Arima Kinen she just failed to catch Victoire Pisa, 2nd by a nose - frustrating defeats. Still she was named Horse of the Year, the 4th female ever, after Vodka the year before. At 5 she was 8th in the Dubai World Cup, her first heavy defeat. In the Victoria Mile she met year-younger fillies' Triple Crown winner Apapane; Apapane perhaps suited the mile better, and she was 2nd. She was 2nd again to Earnestly's record run in the Takarazuka Kinen, and 4th to Tosen Jordan trying to repeat in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) - over a year without a win. In the Japan Cup, for the first time not favorite in Japan (Arc winner Danedream was), she raced a little closer than usual, unleashed a 33.9 finish and won, avenging last year. In the Arima Kinen, a summit clash with Triple Crown winner Orfevre, she couldn't quicken from midfield and was 7th. If Vodka was a queen with the strength to keep challenging, Buena Vista was a queen with the strength to keep reigning.
503=She led all the way to win her debut by 5 lengths and a 5-million race by 6, then won the Fantasy S. from a good spot with a 34.0 finish - speed on another level. But leg trouble ruled out the Hanshin Sansai Himba S. Back at 3, she raced forward and faded to 4th in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West), and was caught by Phalaenopsis at the end of the Oka Sho, 2nd. After a 10th in the Oaks, which didn't suit her, a tendon injury ended her career. On paper, 3 wins from 6 and one G3 - but a speedster who starred in the classics. As a broodmare she produced Oaks winner Daiwa El Cielo (01: Sunday Silence), her daughter fulfilling her classic dream, then 2-time graded winner Big Planet (02: Brian's Time), and Greater London (12: Deep Impact), born when she was 17, who was 4th in the Yasuda Kinen after 6 wins from 7.
504=Debuting in January at 3, she missed the Oka Sho. She won the Wasurenagusa Sho and, with 2 wins from 4, won the Oaks with a sharp run from midfield. Later she won 3 graded races at 4.
506=He improved rapidly late at 4. At 6, though, he powered past future Takamatsunomiya Kinen winner Ore wa Matteruze to win the Tokyo Shimbun Hai, a graded winner at last. Poor runs followed, and he retired after a 13th in the '09 CBC Sho.
510=Leading in nimble style, she won the Flower C., her first graded race. 2nd choice in the Oka Sho, she was hampered and finished 14th, and her injuries kept her out of the Oaks. 17th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup on her return, she then won the Turquoise S. The next year she won the Nakayama Himba S., then beat hot favorite Mejiro Ryan in the Nakayama Kinen. Retiring at 4, she produced '98 Queen C. winner Eidai Queen (95: Mejiro McQueen).
