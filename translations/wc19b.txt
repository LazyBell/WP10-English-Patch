104=* Beyond Shinzan\nThe year before, Symboli Rudolf became unbeaten Triple Crown winner, and though beaten in the Japan Cup, won the Arima Kinen. Now he set out on the older horses' main path, aiming to be the first five-crown winner since Shinzan in 1965.\nIn the Tenno Sho (Spring), the third clash of Triple Crown winners with Mr. C.B., Rudolf let Mr. C.B. go ahead for the first time, then easily ran him down as he tired. Jockey Yukio Okabe raised five fingers.\nNo rival seemed left, but he was injured before the Takarazuka Kinen. On his return in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) he was beaten by outsider Gallop Dyna. Still, trainer Yuji Nohira declared, "They say there are no certainties in racing, but with Rudolf there are," showing total faith in him. As if in reply, Rudolf shrugged off bad weather to win the Japan Cup, a sixth crown, beyond Shinzan.\nWith a trip abroad planned for the next year, the Arima Kinen was set as his last race in Japan. Nohira asked Okabe not just to win but to "win impressively." As told, Rudolf calmly took the lead at the third turn and won by 4 lengths from that year's double classic winner Miho Shinzan. He was the first to win the Arima Kinen twice since Speed Symboli in 1970 - and Japan's first-ever seven-crown winner.\n\n* Nihon Pillow Winner Dominates the Mile\nWhile the older horses' main path was thrilled by Triple Crown clashes, one horse kept fighting over a mile: Nihon Pillow Winner. In 1982 he won his first 3 starts, showing his talent, but in the next year's Satsuki Sho he was last of 20, nearly 5 seconds behind winner Mr. C.B. Giving up on the classics, his camp turned to the mile division, which now had new G1s thanks to graded races. There he showed his full talent, piling up wins including the G1 Mile Championship.\nIn '85 he won two sprint G2s, the Milers C. and the Keio Hai Spring C., then won the Yasuda Kinen, which he had missed the year before with a fracture. In his farewell race, the Mile Championship, he won clearly by 3 lengths, winning it twice and bowing out in style. That year he was champion sprinter for the third straight year.\nA great miler who starred from the very first year Japan's sprint and mile program was established, he was called "the Emperor of the Mile" after Emperor Symboli Rudolf. His trainer boasted, "At a mile he wouldn't lose even to Symboli Rudolf," but they met only once, not at a mile but in the 2000m Tenno Sho (Autumn). Rudolf was 2nd and Pillow Winner 3rd, just 0.1 seconds behind. On a stage that favored Rudolf, that shows his strength.\n\n* Racing Tilts West\nBack then Kanto horses were far stronger. In the Japanese Derby, for example, winners from 1973 to 1985 were 10 from Kanto and just 3 from Kansai.\nWestern trainers believed "Kanto horses are strong because they train on the uphill straights at Tokyo and Nakayama." In response, in 1985 a hill training course opened at Ritto Training Center.\nWithin a few years its effects became clear, shaping the "strong West, weak East" balance that followed.
