1597=Near the top in allowances, he used his light weight to lead all the way in the '08 Tanabata Sho, winning the Summer 2000 Series. Later he won the Meguro Kinen on heavy ground by 5 lengths over Jaguar Mail, then the Tanabata Sho again. After a big loss in the Sapporo Kinen, he won the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai. He retired after the Nikkei Sho at 8 and became a lead pony at Chukyo.
1605=Half-brother of Danon Velvet, 2nd to Buena Vista in the 2008 Hanshin JF. Unlike his early-peaking sister, he emerged in summer at 4, winning the Christmas C. 4th to Danon Ballade in his first graded race, the American JCC, he won the Chunichi Shimbun Hai with the fastest finish.
1608=Winner of the 1982 Tenno Sho (Spring) and Takarazuka Kinen. Up to 4 he was often favored but fell short - 2nd in the Derby, Kikuka Sho and Tenno Sho (Autumn), 3rd in the Arima Kinen - "the uncrowned emperor." At 5 he finally bloomed, winning both. He disliked heavy ground, so was also called "the Emperor of the Sun."
1617=Won a debut race and the Sapporo Sansai S., then never won again in the JRA; raced in Iwate, Tochigi and Nakatsu.
1630=Leading alone and controlling the pace, he won big; otherwise he lost big, regardless of odds or rivals - longshot fans loved him. In his first G1, the Takarazuka Kinen, he was last, over 5 seconds behind Mejiro McQueen; next, in the Takamatsunomiya Hai, he cruised to a record win over Narita Taishin, Machikane Tannhauser and others. Late in his career he raced in Iwate and Utsunomiya without winning.
1632=A foreign-bred by Mt. Livermore, a fast mid-level sprinter. Much like his sire, he used powerful early speed to shine in dirt sprints. An accident at 3 cost him nearly a year, but about 6 months after his return, in autumn at 4, he won the Negishi S. Just as he seemed to be coming good, he broke down in the next year's Kan'etsu S. and did not survive.
1633=Prominent early, 2nd in the Shinzan Kinen, but as a gelding he watched the classics from the sidelines. When he settled he had a sharp kick, winning the Fukushima Kinen and Chukyo Kinen. He moved to Kanazawa at 6.
1634=He won his first 2, then did little - strong in open specials but crushed in graded races. Then at 5 he suddenly ran big in the Osaka Hai, beating previous Tenno Sho winner Nehai Caesar and others at over 100 to 1.
1635=Hard to believe given his later fame, but he was last choice on debut. He led all the way to win by 7 lengths, then beat Rugger Champion and others in the Niigata Sansai S., a leading 2yo contender, but injury then cost him over 6 months. His comeback was the Satsuki Sho, straight off the break; a miracle was hoped for, but he was 16th, then 13th in the Derby. Never his old self, he ran big again as 10th choice to win the Fukushima Kinen. In 19 more races, double-figure finishes were common and 4th his best, and he retired after the Diamond S. at 7.
1636=Beaten in the Wakaba S., his first middle-distance race, he gave up the classics and, the day before the Satsuki Sho, led all the way in the Crystal C., a sprint - his first and only graded title. He later moved to Iwate.
1637=A superb blueblood: granddaughter of Oaks winner Agnes Lady, niece of Oka Sho winner Agnes Flora, among other classic winners. In her 3rd start she beat Oguri Roman by half a length in the Tulip Sho, a classic leading lady, but was 8th in the Oka Sho and 3rd in the Oaks. 3rd in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, she was 2nd in the Hanshin Himba Tokubetsu, Kimpai (West) and Chunichi Shimbun Hai, but never won another graded race.
1638=He needed 11 starts to win, but grew enough to run in the Kikuka Sho that autumn. Older, he won the Nikkei Shinshun Hai, then beat Agnes Parade and others with authority in the Chunichi Shimbun Hai.
1639=From the 2nd crop of Arima Kinen and Dubai Sheema Classic winner Heart's Cry, related to Horse of the Year Sakura Laurel. A classic hope from a foal, he debuted late and was 9th as 2nd choice in the Aoba Sho, giving up the spring classics. 9th in the Kobe Shimbun Hai, he skipped the Kikuka Sho - perhaps for the best. Fresh from a 10-million win, he ran in the Nikkei Shinshun Hai and, helped by a light weight, raced forward and powered home to win. 2nd to Fenomeno in the Nikkei Sho, his talent was blooming, but after the Meguro Kinen he rested long, running just once in 2014.
1640=Debuting in Hokkaido racing, he won 7 of 17 and moved to the JRA, where he struggled with the higher level. But on heavy ground he was 2nd in the Niigata Daishoten and Epsom C., then on a bog in the Mainichi Okan led all the way over a star field incl. Genuine and Sakura Chitose O. He also won the Kabutoyama Kinen on soft ground - a rare mudlark, "the horse with webbed feet." Injured at 7, he returned to Hokkaido racing and raced until 9.
1641=She rose on dirt, but late in her career was a fine turf sprinter, winning the Sapporo Sprint S. at 4 and 5. Her 2nd win gave jockey Tomio Yasuda the first-ever graded wins at all 10 JRA tracks, making headlines. She raced tough until the end of 6 without another win. As a dam, hopes of a sprinter beyond her have not yet been met.
1642=Suited to flat left-handed tracks, 5 of his 7 wins came at Chukyo. He clicked with jockey Jotaro Horai: from late at 4, with Horai his regular rider, he improved sharply, winning the Chunichi Shimbun Hai twice and the Aichi Hai - 3 graded races for Japan-sired horses at Chukyo. Injured at 6, he rested 8 months and returned in the next Chunichi Shimbun Hai, going for a 3rd straight win, but his old kick was gone - last all the way.
1643=Slow to leave the lower classes, he won the March S. at 5 and again the next year, then the Antares S. He tried the Teio Sho and Tokyo Daishoten, but the G1 wall was high; he never even made the top five.
1644=Improving from autumn at 4, he won the Kabutoyama Kinen and Fukushima Kinen, then was a fine 6th to Silk Justice in the Arima Kinen. At 5 and later he won the Kokura Kinen and was 2nd in the Chunichi Shimbun Hai - outstanding at local tracks.
1645=He starred around a mile on dirt. Often 2nd at 3, stuck in allowances, he came good at 4, winning 3 straight to the March S., then the Procyon S. - both with furious charges from the back at the final turn.
1646=Full brother of Oka Sho winner Cheers Grace, much hoped for from the start. At 3 he was 2nd by half a length to Tanino Gimlet in the Shinzan Kinen, then won the Kyodo News Hai and Mainichi Hai, a classic contender. But he was 12th in the Satsuki Sho and 16th in the Derby. Older, back from 8 months off, he was beaten badly, and in autumn '03 died suddenly on the way back to his stable from pasture.
1647=Coming good at 4, he quickly left the allowances and won the Kabutoyama Kinen, then the Sakuranbo Kinen in autumn at 4 and the Gumma Kinen easily in spring at 5. Oddly, all 3 graded races he won were abolished the following year. In '05 he broke the jinx by winning the Kashiwa Kinen, just promoted to G1, then was 2nd in the Teio Sho. Repeated long breaks with leg trouble followed, and he retired without regaining his form.
1648=He won the Keio Hai 2yo S. at 2. In the classics he was 4th in the Spring S. and 13th in the Satsuki Sho. Switching to dirt sprints in autumn, he won the Shimotsuki S., then struggled until the Galaxy S. late at 4. In form, he won the Procyon S. by 2.5 lengths, then the Sirius S. and JBC Sprint. In '06 he won the Kurofune Sho, Nambu Hai, JBC Mile and the big year-end Tokyo Daishoten. In '07 he won the Kashiwa Kinen and Nambu Hai and was 2nd in the February S. and Teio Sho, and in '08 the Nambu Hai again - his 7th G1, equal to Deep Impact and T.M. Opera O. Still going at 8, he kept trying G1s, barely making the top five, and retired after an 8th in the '09 JBC Classic without breaking the record.
1649=After 2 dirt wins he switched to turf in autumn at 3, won 2 allowances and was a nose 2nd in the American JCC, then beat Zenno Rob Roy by a neck in the Nikkei Sho. He then struggled, fractured a leg in the '06 Arima Kinen, was injured again training for a comeback, and retired to stud in '08. Blind in his right eye from an injury, yet battling on in graded races, he still has many fans.
1650=Won 3 in Southern Kanto racing, incl. the Kanto Oaks. Dam of Hokkaido Sansai Yushun winner Takino Special (97: Sumako Bakuriku).
1651=15th on debut, he recovered to win a maiden next time. Missing the spring classics, he won the Shirayuri S., his first open win, then ran in the Kobe Shimbun Hai for a classic spot and swept past "Big Three" members Unrivaled and Reach the Crown down the wide outside with a powerful finish. So impressive was it that he was 2nd choice for the Kikuka Sho, but 4th, then 4th as favorite in his first race against older horses, the Naruo Kinen. Struggling after, he died after breaking down in a race.
1652=A Southern Kanto star who won the 1981 Tokyo Daishoten. Moving to the JRA early at 6, he was 14th in the Swan S. and then moved to Iwate, winning 9 there until summer at 8. He died suddenly after just one year at stud, but his foals included Toukei Nisei, a local legend with 39 wins from 43.
1653=Talented but plagued by fractures, in only his 2nd graded race, the Asahi Challenge C. in September at 4, he won when Nishino Raiden, first past the post, was disqualified. 2nd in the Takamatsunomiya Hai (2000m: G2), he battled on in graded races at 5, then moved to local racing - and that's where he shone: he won Kanazawa's Hakusan Daishoten, then Nagoya's Tokai Kikkasho and Nagoya Daishoten easily, and won at Kasamatsu too, sweeping the graded races. He retired at 7 to stud.
1654=Debuting at 4 (old age system), long stuck in allowances, he won the Sapporo Nikkei Sho above his class at 5, then in the Sapporo Kinen beat that year's Satsuki Sho winner Tosho Boy and Derby winner Climb Kaiser - no ordinary horse. In autumn he won the All Comers and ran in the Arima Kinen, 14th behind Tosho Boy over his first long trip. He won the Lord Derby Challenge T. at 6 and raced until 8.
1655=Debuting at local Mizusawa, he was ordinary at 2, with 2 wins from 6. At 3 he came good fast, winning Kaminoyama's Tohoku Yushun and Iwate's derby, the Kozukata Sho, and was 3rd against older horses in the Nambu Hai. He also won the big local Derby Grand Prix. Moving to the JRA at 4, he was 10th in the Nakayama Kinen, then injured; after a long break he moved to Hokkaido racing, raced until 9 (9 wins from 54) and went to stud.
1656=As a local horse in the All Comers at 5, he was 2nd to runaway leader Twin Turbo, part of an upset. Last in 2 straight Japan Cups, he left a poor impression on JRA fans, but his record at home in Southern Kanto was truly first-class. Moving to the JRA at 7, he chased a first JRA win in vain, switched to jumps, and broke down fatally in his 2nd jump race.
1657=Based at local Kanazawa. A strong horse with 23 wins, incl. the Thoroughbred Challenge C. and Hakusan Daishoten.
1658=Based at Oi. Much hoped for, but debuting late, in February at 3, he couldn't run in the Southern Kanto classics. Free of major setbacks, he raced steadily until 7: 15 wins and eight 2nds from 40. No big titles, but his consistency won many fans, and winning graded races at all 4 Southern Kanto tracks, incl. the Urawa Kinen and Tokyo Hai, made him a star.
1659=Debuting in Hokkaido racing (3 wins from 13), he blossomed after moving to Kiyomatsu Akama's stable at Oi, winning the Tokyo Kinen and Tokyo Daishoten at 4. The next year he tried JRA turf races, beaten badly.
1660=Debuting late, in April at 3, he won by 9 lengths. Shin trouble cost him 5 months; he won on return, but a fracture was found, costing 11 months. Back in autumn at 4, 5th first time, he won 2 more, then never got right and couldn't race until 6 - 5 wins from 6 - and moved to Hokkaido racing. There he raced steadily, winning 16 as a top local horse, incl. the Mizuho Sho 4 years running, from 9 to 12. He also raced in JRA-local graded races like the JRA's Elm S., winning his first, the Hokkaido Sprint C., at 12. At 13 he won Hokkaido's Etoile Sho, setting a new record as Japan's oldest graded winner.
1661=His sire San O I was an unbeaten Southern Kanto Triple Crown winner. Inheriting his talent, Tomishino Porunga starred at Kasamatsu, winning most of its major races. At 5 he tried JRA races: 3rd in the Heian S., then his first JRA win in the turf TV Aichi Open, and a fine 4th in the All Comers - NAR's best older horse that year. The next year he tried the Takarazuka Kinen, 10th to Dantsu Seattle.
1662=His name, similar to a great sire's, made him surprisingly well known. A local horse who ran 78 times across Niigata, Kasamatsu, Nagoya, Kanazawa and Saga, strong on heavy ground. No G1-class wins, but 21 wins is a fine record.
1663=A blueblood of the Star Roch family, but needing 7 starts to win and then stuck, with 1 win he moved to local Nagoya in summer at 3 - and was transformed. Winning powerfully, he swept the Tokai area's graded races and was NAR's best older horse in '95 and '96. Representing local racing, he fought great battles with JRA stars like Hokuto Vega and Kyoto City, lighting up JRA-local racing, and boldly tried JRA turf graded races like the Nikkei Sho and Kyoto Daishoten. With 19 wins from 35 locally, he retired at 6 and went to stud.
1664=Meant to debut in the JRA, leg trouble sent him to local Kanazawa, where he won and won, including the Derby Grand Prix in record time with a furious charge - called Kanazawa's greatest ever. Leg trouble plagued his later career.
1665=Based at local Funabashi. Winning his first 7, he drew attention and won the Kawasaki Kinen the next year. But JRA-local racing had begun, pitting him against JRA stars like Hokuto Vega and Lively Mount. He tried turf races like the All Comers without success, and never won a JRA-local graded race, but raced until 9 with 17 wins. Late on he moved to Hokkaido racing without more wins.
1666=Based at local Kawasaki. Turning a fierce temper into explosive runs, he won the Tokyo Okansho, the last Southern Kanto classic, and the Tokyo Daishoten. He announced a bid for the Hanshin Daishoten, but an accident in training forced a long break, and he broke down fatally in his comeback, the Urawa Kinen.
1667=Based at local Kasamatsu. Not invincible, but he won the Tokai Derby, Thoroughbred Challenge C. and more, then the Derby Grand Prix, making his name nationwide. At 4 he tried the JRA's Hanshin Daishoten, a superb 3rd behind Narita Brian and Mayano Top Gun.
1668=Winner of the Tokyo Hai at 4 and 5. Racing at Oi, Kaminoyama and Hokkaido, he ran 73 times. Winless from 7, he won once more at 10, and his last race was over 1800m on Monbetsu dirt in autumn at 11.
1669=Based at local Niigata. From his 3rd start he won 12 straight and the JRA-local Gumma Kinen. A monster at home, he struggled against the JRA's wall in JRA-local races.
1670=Based at local Iwate. A fast horse on dirt and turf. At 2 he raced in the JRA, 2nd in the Dahlia Sho and 3rd in the Niigata Sansai S. At 6 he won Oi's Tokyo Hai, unbeatable in sprints. Moving to Kochi at 8, he raced 104 times until 10.
1671=Debuting in Hokkaido racing (4 wins from 6), he moved to Funabashi and won the Tokyo Okansho and Tokyo Derby. Older, he starred in JRA-local races, winning the Kashiwa Kinen and Nippon TV Hai, though never a G1.
