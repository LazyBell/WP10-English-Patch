71=* Zenyatta, America's Hard-Charging Heroine\nZenyatta was named after an album by the British rock band The Police. Using her big frame and long stride to charge from the back, she had won 14 straight since her 2007 debut.\nHer camp first announced she would retire after winning the 2009 BC Classic and even held a grand farewell, but in January the next year they suddenly said she would race on. The public's surprise and confusion soon turned to excitement: calls were growing for a clash with Rachel Alexandra, 2009 Horse of the Year.\nTo make it happen, April's Apple Blossom H. changed its purse and conditions, becoming the Apple Blossom Invitational H. under set weights. Both camps declared, but Rachel Alexandra lost her prep in 2nd and skipped the race. Without her rival, Zenyatta won by over 4 lengths.\nCalls for the clash never stopped, but Rachel Alexandra announced her retirement that September, and the two never met.\nZenyatta added 3 more G1 wins, and her camp headed for the BC Classic to win it again. She was overwhelming favorite, and racing fans worldwide hoped for the queen's 20th straight win.\nShe missed the break badly and had to trail at the very back - a tough race. But she was the queen: she steadily closed the gap and in the straight charged with a furious finish. She ran down leader Blame... but fell short by the narrowest of margins, 2nd by a head. After this agonizing loss, her camp announced her retirement for good.\nDespite that final defeat, she won 19 of 20 starts. Her overwhelming record still shines in American racing history.\n\n* Goldikova Wins a Third BC Mile\nGoldikova had already won the BC Mile twice and was famous worldwide. She lost Cartier Horse of the Year to Sea the Stars, but no one could stop her run in the mile division.\nIn 2010 she opened with a win in the Prix d'Ispahan and kept winning. She was 2nd in August's Prix Jacques le Marois, but in the Prix de la Foret she broke well, led and held off all challengers. Some had feared she couldn't handle Longchamp's soft ground, but the win blew those fears away.\nThen came the BC Mile. The last two years it had been held at Santa Anita Park, but in 2010 it was at Churchill Downs. Goldikova was favorite at 1.3, with Gio Ponti, previous year's Eclipse champion older male and champion turf male, next in the betting.\nThe pace was fairly slow, and Goldikova watched the leaders from midfield. In a grueling race where the front-runners wouldn't stop, she made her move in the straight and swept past the others in an instant. Gio Ponti charged from behind with a tremendous kick, but she shrugged off his challenge to win her third straight BC Mile.\nIt was an unprecedented feat in Breeders' Cup history on the flat. Goldikova became a champion whose name will live on in world racing history.
72=* Frankel: The Legend Begins\nThis year a "monster" dominated European racing: Frankel, a British-bred colt named after great trainer Robert Frankel, who died in 2009.\nHis sire was Galileo, winner of the Derby and Irish Derby. Carrying enormous expectations as his son, Frankel debuted and beat Nathaniel, a future winner of the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. and the Eclipse S., by half a length. His run continued, and in 2011 he went into the G1 2000 Guineas unbeaten.\nOverwhelming favorite, he won by 6 lengths from Dubawi Gold. That untouchable style - like his 10-length win in the Royal Lodge S. the year before - is Frankel's trademark.\nSeeing such power, people hoped for a father-son Derby double. But his camp chose the St. James's Palace S. in the mile division instead, where he gave a controversial performance.\nRunning in the second group, he made an early move mid-race, took the lead and kept pulling away. The crowd gasped at what looked like a runaway. Everyone thought he'd be swallowed up. But he didn't tire as expected and held off Zoffany's fierce charge by three-quarters of a length. The narrow win drew criticism, but holding on through sheer ability was impressive in itself.\nThen came the Sussex S. Frankel was favorite again, with Canford Cliffs, who had beaten mile queen Goldikova, close behind in the betting. Billed as the "Duel on the Downs," it drew huge attention.\nBut when it came to it, Frankel made the running and won without letting anyone near, showing the crowd his tremendous power.\n\n* Great Hoofprints Left at Longchamp\nThis year a Cinderella story was written into racing history. Its heroine was Danedream, a filly bred at Germany's Brummerhof Stud. She was naturally small, and sold as a yearling for 9,000 euros (about 1 million yen) - a sign of how little she was rated.\nShe won her debut, but then only ran well without winning. She bravely tried G1s like the Prix Marcel Boussac, but victory wouldn't come.\nLooking for a breakthrough, her camp took her to Italy. Her third race there, the Italian Oaks at San Siro, was the race of destiny. Racing in midfield, she kicked from the final turn and blew past her rivals with an explosive finish, winning by six and a half lengths. It was the moment the 9,000-euro filly's sleeping talent awoke.\nNow soaring, she won the Grosser Preis von Berlin and the Grosser Preis von Baden, and finally took on the Arc. She had shown her ability, but the field included defending champion Workforce, last year's runner-up Nakayama Festa from Japan and many other stars, and she was only 10th choice.\nBut whoever she faced, her kick never dulled. In the straight she accelerated, swept elegantly past the leaders and won by 5 lengths from Shareta.\nIt was the first Arc win by a German horse, and jockey A. Starke became the first German jockey to win it. Helped by unusually good ground at Longchamp that year, she won in record time. Setting so many records, Danedream marched on as Europe's queen.
73=* The "Monster" Rated 140 lbs\nUnbeaten since his debut and winning race after race in the mile division, Frankel - known worldwide as "the monster" - soared even higher this year.\nHe ran only in G1s and won all 5, every one by a wide margin. In the Queen Anne S. he won by an incredible 11 lengths, earning a rating of 140 lbs in the World Thoroughbred Rankings. He also won the International S. by 7 lengths and the Sussex S. by 6; over 8-10 furlongs (1600-2000m) he was simply unbeatable, and was Cartier Horse of the Year for the second straight year.\nThe extraordinary Frankel even shook the ranking system itself. 140 lbs was normally the ceiling; only Dancing Brave, at 141 in 1986, had ever exceeded it. There had long been criticism that past ratings were too generous, and Frankel's arrival led to the drastic step of lowering all past ratings across the board.\nThis drew a strong backlash. Harwood, trainer of Dancing Brave, who was "downgraded" from a record 141 to 138, fumed: "Utter nonsense. If they want Frankel on top, just rate Frankel higher." Former jockey W. Swinburn agreed, questioning the rewriting of history.\nIn the end the review was made unofficial, and it was announced that Dancing Brave's 141 remained the highest ever. Frankel's 140 became the second highest, but horses from different eras and distances can't simply be compared - both are once-in-a-generation greats.\n\n* Camelot Tries for the British Triple Crown\nAnother talking point this year was Ireland's Camelot trying for the British Triple Crown. The last horse to win the 2000 Guineas, Derby and St. Leger was Nijinsky in 1970, and with the long St. Leger's prestige declining, most horses now aimed for the Arc instead. But having won the 2000 Guineas and the Derby, Camelot ran in the St. Leger, trying for the first Triple Crown in 42 years.\nHe just failed to catch front-runner Encke and was 2nd - no Triple Crown. Still, he also won the Irish Derby that year and was named Cartier champion 3yo colt.\n\n* Black Caviar's Winning Streak Down Under\nIn the Southern Hemisphere, Australian mare Black Caviar set an unprecedented record: 25 straight wins unbeaten from her debut, 15 of them G1s. Rated 130 lbs in the World Thoroughbred Rankings, she was ranked 3rd in the world behind Frankel (140) and Cirrus des Aigles (131).\nThe Australian season runs from August to the following July, and in the 2013 end-of-season rankings she became the first Australian-trained horse ever to top the world.
74=* America's Turf Titan\nWise Dan. To explain his achievement, we must start with how turf and dirt racing are ranked in America.\nDirt racing has long been mainstream in America and considered above turf. Indeed, almost every Horse of the Year has been a dirt winner, and even turf winners tended to handle dirt as well. Wise Dan was the first "turf miler" to be named Eclipse Horse of the Year in America - and the first turf-based horse to win it two years running.\nFrom his 2010 debut through 2011, Wise Dan showed ability on all-weather, dirt and turf, but won 8 of 14, with just one G1, the Clark H. A fine record, but something was missing.\nThe turning point was the 2012 Stephen Foster H. After losing to Ron the Greek there, he was switched to turf only.\nThe decision seems to have paid off. He won the Fourstardave H. and the Woodbine Mile S. in a row, and from there began to dominate the turf mile division. Favorite for the BC Mile, he lived up to it, breaking a 15-year-old course record. For this unmatched run he won three Eclipse Awards.\n2013 was his golden age. His only loss was a 2nd in the Shadwell Turf Mile S. He won the Woodbine Mile again in record time and the BC Mile again, a 14-race turf winning streak, and won three Eclipse Awards for the second straight year.\nWise Dan won 23 of 31 in his career. Of his 8 losses, only one came on turf, before the switch. No horse deserves the title "Titan of the Turf" more.\n\n* The Sprint Queen Leaves the Turf\nWho is the world's greatest sprinter? Many names come up, but many would say Australia's great mare Black Caviar. Since her 2009 debut, 25 starts, 25 wins - an unbeaten sprinter in the truest sense.\nAustralia has a rich program of sprint races, with fierce competition. Yet Black Caviar kept winning from her debut on.\nWith no rivals left at home, in June 2012 she traveled to faraway Britain for the Diamond Jubilee S. at Royal Ascot. She had an unexpectedly hard fight but won. Some were puzzled by such a narrow win, but the next day it emerged she had injured a leg - perhaps the strain of travel.\nShe then rested, and in February 2013 ran in the Black Caviar Lightning, named after her. She never gave up the lead and won in track-record time. Running like a champion despite the long break, she left great hoofprints on Flemington's straight course, "The Straight Six."\nShe kept winning, set a record 25-race winning streak, and after winning the T.J. Smith S. suddenly announced her retirement, leaving the turf with many legends behind her.\nThis year she was rated 130, level with Arc winner Treve, becoming the first Australian-trained horse to finish the year No. 1 in the world rankings.
75=* Treve Wins Back-to-Back Arcs\nA brilliant comeback - no words fit the 2014 Arc better. The star was Treve, a filly who won the 2013 Arc by 5 lengths over Japan's Orfevre and was named Cartier Horse of the Year.\nTreve was an unbeaten Arc winner. But after that Arc her glittering record began to dim. She was 2nd in the Prix Ganay, 3rd in the Prince of Wales's S., then 4th in the Prix Vermeille. After three shock defeats, the public turned critical and some doubted her ability. Facing that headwind, she headed for the Arc again.\nGerman Derby winner Sea the Moon, expected to run, suddenly retired after losing the Grosser Preis von Baden, and with strong runners from Britain and Japan the betting was wide open. The favorite was Taghrooda, winner of the Oaks and the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. - curiously, a 3yo filly like Treve the year before. Stars like Japan's Just a Way, who romped in the Dubai Duty Free, and Prix Niel winner Ectot were also well backed, and Treve was only 12.0.\nUnder a chilly autumn sky, very different from the days before, the Arc began. Treve settled in the pack, watching the leaders and never getting too keen. Out of the false straight, as the field fanned out and kicked, she slipped up the inside and gradually moved forward. With 400m left she swept past the others and pulled away, coasting home by 2 lengths. She was only the 6th horse ever to win the Arc twice.\nFans worldwide cheered her brilliant comeback. The champion's form that silenced all doubts was back.\n\n* The Kentucky Derby: A Dream 52 Years in the Making\nThis year one horse achieved a remarkable feat: California Chrome, a racehorse born, as his name says, in California on the West Coast.\nAmerican racing history reveals an East-West rivalry. In the late 19th century, the center of American racing was Kentucky in the East. Led by Kentucky, organizations were formed and laws put in place modeled on British racing, rapidly modernizing American racing.\nThe West Coast - California and other western states - didn't like this. Western power brokers pushed modernization to rival the East. In 1937 Del Mar Racetrack opened, backed by Hollywood actor Bing Crosby and others.\nBut with transport problems and more, West Coast-bred horses struggled to win in the East. No California-bred had won the Kentucky Derby since Decidedly in 1962.\nAgainst this background, a West Coast-bred winning the Kentucky Derby carried special meaning. In 2014 California Chrome went into the Derby carrying the West Coast's pride and hopes. Fresh from winning the Santa Anita Derby, he was favorite at 3.5, though some dismissed him simply for being West Coast-bred.\nThe pace was slow. Sitting 3rd and moving up, he passed the leader at the final turn and powered away in the straight. It's easy to imagine the cheers in California and across the West Coast at this first win in 52 years.
