2498=He won the Swan S. on 4 straight, a sprint name to watch. 3rd in the Daily Hai 2yo S. and 5th in the Hopeful S. without winning, he restarted in allowances at 3. Winning in May, he won 2 more classes strongly, then the G2 Swan S. as 2nd choice behind Let's Go Donki, closing brilliantly from the back. 3rd as 7th choice in the Mile CS, at 4 he won the Milers C., then the 2000m Sapporo Kinen, and was 2nd as 4th choice in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) - effective at middle trips too.
2499=The 2017 Derby winner. With Satsuki Sho winner Al Ain and Kikuka Sho winner Kiseki, he led racing in 2017-2019, proving his crop's strength. Unbeaten in 3 to the Hopeful S. as hot favorite, he was the classic focus. Straight into the Satsuki Sho, 5th, he then won the Derby, best of his crop. In autumn he won the Kobe Shimbun Hai easily, then skipped the Kikuka Sho for the Japan Cup, 2nd, ahead of champion Kitasan Black. Older he led his crop: 3rd in the Kyoto Kinen, 4th in the Dubai Sheema Classic, then the All Comers as favorite and the Tenno Sho (Autumn) over crop-mates Al Ain, Kiseki and others. 2nd to 3yo Blast Onepiece in the Arima Kinen, still a force. At 5 he faded: 6th in Dubai, 5th in the Takarazuka Kinen, 4th in the All Comers, 11th in the Japan Cup as crop-mate Suave Richard won. In his farewell Arima Kinen he was 7th as 9th choice, but the paddock was full of fan banners. Now a sire.
2500=Favorite in every allowance race, but often 2nd or 3rd, he reached open class only when older. In the Challenge C., 2nd choice in a 2-horse race with Rey Enda, he broke clear from a handy spot with the fastest finish and won easily.
2501=The 2017 Satsuki Sho winner, a flagship of the crop with Rey de Oro and Kiseki. Unbeaten in 2, he was 6th in the Shinzan Kinen on heavy ground, then won the Mainichi Hai from 2nd. 9th choice in the Satsuki Sho amid stamina doubts, he was swallowed up at the 4th turn, recovered, split the pack and won. 5th in the Derby, close up, 2nd in the St. Lite Kinen, he was 2nd choice behind Kiseki in the Kikuka Sho but, in a storm-soaked quagmire (final 600m near 40s), finished 7th. At 4, at middle trips, no G1 but 3rd in the Osaka Hai, 4th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), 3rd in the Mile CS. At 5, 9th choice as Blast Onepiece and Wagnerian rose, he won the Osaka Hai in a tight finish with Kiseki 2nd - his crop's strength again. 4th in the Takarazuka Kinen, he was 14th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and 16th in the Mile CS, then 11th in his farewell Arima Kinen, reunited with Satsuki rider Matsuyama.
2502=A Heart's Cry filly in the strong 2017 crop. Losing her debut narrowly, she won 3 straight incl. the Daily Hai Queen C., beating later Yayoi Sho winner Cadenas in the Hyakunichiso Tokubetsu and later NHK Mile C. winner Aerolithe in the Queen C. In the Oka Sho, billed vs. Hanshin JF winner Soul Stirring, she floundered on soft ground, 12th. Her camp's stated goal was always the Oaks; 2nd choice there, she was 3rd. A tendon injury before the Rose S. ended her career.
2503=The first mare to win both Grand Prix races in one year, 2019 JRA Horse of the Year ahead of younger star Almond Eye. Noticed early, she was 2nd in the Hanshin JF, Oka Sho and Shuka Sho, 5th in the Oaks - always short of a title until the QEII Cup at 4 shed her "silver collector" tag. At 5, 2nd in the Kinko Sho and 3rd in Hong Kong's QEII Cup, she won the Takarazuka Kinen, sweeping past Al Ain, Rey de Oro, Kiseki and Suave Richard. In Australia she won the Cox Plate. Her camp named the Arima Kinen as her last race - a showdown with Almond Eye, with the JRA specially allowing her Takarazuka and Cox Plate rider Damian Lane - and excitement peaked. As Aerolithe set a hot pace, she raced on the rail in midfield watching Almond Eye, then swung wide in the straight with superb agility, caught the leaders and won by 5 lengths from Saturnalia - the first mare to win both Grand Prix races. Horse of the Year and champion older mare, she retired. Her first foal Schwertlilie was fatally injured in a race, but much is hoped from foals by top sires.
2504=A dirt miler who won his first graded race at 6. Winning 3 straight at 5 to the Ritto S. (OP), he was 3rd in his first graded race, the Procyon S., then a top-five regular: 4th in the Musashino S. and Negishi S. 11th in the G1 February S., he won the Procyon S. on his 6th graded try, charging powerfully from the back. In autumn he was 3rd in the Nambu Hai and Miyako S.
2505=Debuting at Kawasaki, he won the Sapporo 2yo S. as a local horse. He won his debut at 1.1 by 4 lengths and the Wakakusa Tokubetsu by a huge margin, then in JRA races was 2nd in the Clover Sho and led all the way in the Sapporo 2yo S. Moving to the JRA, he placed in graded races: 5th in the Tokyo Sports Hai 2yo S. and Asahi Hai FS, 4th in the Shinzan Kinen, 5th in the Mainichi Hai. In the G1s - 13th in the Satsuki Sho, 8th in the NHK Mile C. and Derby - he struggled.
2506=A rare pedigree: by Danzig's son Langfuhr. As of 2018 he was the sire's only runner in Japan and only graded winner. Emerging at 4, he won 3 straight - a 16-million race, the Jinsen S. and Brilliant S. (both OP). 3rd in the Sirius S., he won the Miyako S. in a tight finish, was 5th as 7th choice in the Champions C., then won the Tokyo Daishoten, a G1. 9th in the Dubai World Cup, 5th in the Teio Sho, he won the Nippon TV Hai, a 3rd graded win, then kept falling short despite a 2nd in the Kawasaki Kinen and 3rd in the Kashiwa Kinen.
2507=A pure sprint pedigree: by Kinshasa no Kiseki out of a Sakura Bakushin O mare. After a debut win, he was 1.8 favorite in the Hakodate 2yo S. but couldn't catch Levante Lion, 2nd. In the Keio Hai 2yo S. he beat him, swinging wide from behind midfield with a finish from another dimension. In the Asahi Hai FS he closed the same way but lost by half a length to Satono Ares. At 3 he faded: 10th in the Spring S., 9th in the NHK Mile C., 6th as favorite in the Keeneland C., 14th in the Sprinters S.
2508=Winner of the Kyoto 2yo S. at 2 and Yayoi Sho at 3, a 2017 classic fancy, but 9th as 3rd choice in the Satsuki Sho and 11th as 8th choice in the Derby. A 2-year slump followed until a 3rd in the Fukushima Minpo Hai (L) in spring at 5; that summer he was 2nd in the Kokura Kinen and 3rd in the Niigata Kinen. At 6 he won the Kokura Daishoten in February, his first graded win in 3 years, with an overwhelming late charge. 4th in the Osaka Hai and Mainichi Okan, he faded at 7 (10th Mainichi Okan, 16th Tenno Sho). At 8 he was 5th on his dirt debut in the Tokai S. and 3rd in the Kokura Daishoten, retiring to stud after the Tenno Sho (Autumn).
2509=From the first crop of Frankel, unbeaten in 14 with 10 G1s; she and Soul Stirring quickly won graded races, gifting their sire success in Japan. She won her debut at 1.3, then the Fantasy S. at 1.4, closing fastest from the back with ease. Choosing the Asahi Hai FS over the Hanshin JF, she was favorite - hope for the first filly winner in 36 years - but lacked her kick, 4th. Hard to get right, she went straight to the Oka Sho as 5th choice (11th), then was 7th in the NHK Mile C. and 15th in the Swan S. - her early brilliance gone.
2510=A filly who held her own with crop-mates Rey de Oro and Kiseki. Soul Stirring and Admire Miyabi drew the early attention, but her NHK Mile C. win changed that: 2nd choice in an open race, she broke clear from up front with authority. She won the Queen S. that summer. At 4 she was 2nd in the Nakayama Kinen, 4th in the Victoria Mile and 2nd in the Yasuda Kinen, then won the Mainichi Okan as favorite, a force against males. At 5, the year of younger star Almond Eye, she beat her for 2nd in the Yasuda Kinen and was 3rd behind her in the Tenno Sho (Autumn). In her farewell Arima Kinen, her longest race, she led boldly as always (14th).
2511=A character who blasts out of the gate and races far clear. 3rd in the Radio Nikkei Sho at 3, winless after, he restarted in allowances, won 2 classes in summer at 4, then the Fukushima Kinen, followed by the Kokura Daishoten and Sekiya Kinen - 3 graded wins. A fan favorite for his all-out front-running at middle trips on turf.
2512=Winning 3 straight in summer at 3 to open class, he was fancied in graded races but 5th in the Kokura Daishoten was his best. Falling short in open races, he finally won at that level at 5 in the Naruo Kinen, breaking clear from up front in a course-record 1:57.2.
2513=After 35 allowance races, he reached open class in autumn at 5 and won his first graded race, the March S., as 8th choice, leading all the way and laughing off favorite Buster Type. He won the Marine S. (OP) that summer.
2514=Improving at 5, 2nd in the CBC Sho and Hanshin C., he won the Silk Road S. the next year as 2nd choice, tracking in 3rd and passing leader Laurel Veloce to win by 1.5 lengths - a first graded win at 6 in only his 16th race, after injuries. Slow to win after, he won the Silk Road S. again at 7.
2515=Winner of the Teio Sho at 4, Kawasaki Kinen and JBC Classic at 5 - a 2016-2019 dirt star. He won his first graded race, the Hyogo Championship, in spring at 3, then was 2nd in the Japan Dirt Derby, Leopard S. and Radio Nippon Sho (OP), and won the Hakusan Daishoten and Urawa Kinen. At 4 he won the Nagoya Daishoten, then the Teio Sho as 6th choice behind Awardee, Apollo Kentucky and Sound True, charging from the back to crush them - a star born. 2nd in the JBC Classic and 4th in the Champions C., he led all the way in the Kawasaki Kinen at 5 for a 2nd G1. Favorite defending the Teio Sho, he was 2nd to Gold Dream, then won the Nippon TV Hai and the JBC Classic, beating Sunrise Soar and Omega Perfume for a 3rd G1. At 6, 2nd in the Kawasaki Kinen, he was scratched from the Dubai World Cup when unwell; back in autumn he won the Urawa Kinen easily. After an 8th and 6th, he was last choice of 16 in the February S. at 7, yet ran 2nd, then 2nd in the Kashiwa Kinen. 6th in the Teio Sho, after a break and stable move, his best was 5th in the JBC Classic at 8; injured after the February S. at 9, he retired to stud.
2516=He beat favorite Marialite in his first graded race, the Meguro Kinen. A stayer who won allowances at 2300-2400m, he won the Osaka-Hamburg C. (OP) as favorite, then was 3rd choice in the Meguro Kinen behind Marialite and Touching Speech; from midfield he swung wide and caught Marialite on the rail by a neck. A fracture meant a long break; back a year later in the same race, he was 4th and retired.
2517=An unusual record: at 3 he won the UAE Derby, then ran in the US Triple Crown - 9th in the Kentucky Derby, 5th of 6 in the Preakness, but 3rd in the Belmont. Back home he was 3rd in the Brazil C. (OP) and 9th in the Champions C. At 4, 8th in the Dubai World Cup, 4th in the Brilliant S. and 5th in the Brazil C. (both OP), he retired.
2518=He won the Shinzan Kinen early at 3, then a fracture cost him about 2 years. Back late at 4, he won a 16-million race and the Rokko S. (OP); late at 5 he was 2nd in the Chukyo Kinen and 3rd in the Keisei Hai AH, then won the Fuji S. as 2nd choice behind Air Spinel, tracking in 2nd and holding everyone off.
2519=Blooming at 5, he won the Tokai S., then was 3rd as 7th choice in the February S. 2nd in the Antares S., he won the Heian S., a dirt force; 3rd choice in the Teio Sho and Nambu Hai, he was 6th and 4th. In the Champions C. he held 3rd from up front as a longshot. From 6 he slumped, rarely in the top five, winless for over 2.5 years.
2520=Named after the Beatles song "A Day in the Life." 3rd in the Keisei Hai and Yayoi Sho, he was beaten badly in the Satsuki Sho and restarted in allowances. After a long struggle he won 2 classes at 5, back to open class, and won the Niigata Kinen, swinging wide from last and grinding down favorite Albert Dock.
