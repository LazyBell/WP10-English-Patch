417=Debuting in Hokkaido local racing at 2, he emerged after moving to Southern Kanto, finishing 2nd in all three legs of its triple crown: the Haneda Hai, Tokyo Derby and Tokyo Okan Sho. He then won 3 straight - the Hochi Grand Prix C., Kinpai and Mile Grand Prix. Best of all was the 20th Teio Sho, the first as a national G1, where he held off stars like Southern Kanto's Abukuma Poro and JRA dirt regular Battle Line by a neck.
418=Named after yokozuna Akebono for his huge frame of nearly 560 kg. It took 6 starts to win, but then his speed carried him through 4 straight wins into open class. After winning the Swan S. he lost to Trot Thunder's powerful finish in the Mile Championship, but romped in the Sprinters S. as favorite, sprint king at 3. He never won as an older horse.
419=A late bloomer who reached open class by winning the Fukushima Kinen in autumn at 3. Winless at 4, at 5 he was 2nd to Tamamo Cross in the Tenno Sho (Spring). The next year his talent bloomed, winning the American JCC and Nikkei Sho in a row.
420=With an astonishing finish he won the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. and the Arc, earning a world-best 141 lbs in the International Classification. Ratings were later reviewed and his was cut to 138, but his crushing power is still legendary. He won his first G1 in the 2000 Guineas, then in the Derby, though 2nd, closed from last over the final 2 furlongs to within half a length of Shahrastani, showing his potential. He won the Eclipse S. and King George, and was a solid favorite for the Arc, where his explosive late kick swept past the whole field in a flash for a record win. He retired at year's end to stud, but after his first season contracted Marie's disease, and with his runners doing little, the farm sold him. The JRA bought and imported him to Japan, and then his remaining offspring produced stars like Commander in Chief and White Muzzle, changing his image. In Japan he sired Kyoei March, King Halo, T.M. Ocean and more, a top-ranked sire. He died at 16 in 1999; Commander in Chief, White Muzzle and King Halo carried on, but his direct sire line is near extinction in Japan.
421=His maiden win came on turf, but all 12 other wins were on dirt - a true sand specialist. He ran in many JRA-local races, winning his first graded race, the Hokkaido Sprint C., at 5. At 6 he won 5 graded races including the Garnet S. and Kashiwa Kinen, and by retiring at 9 had won 8 dirt graded races.
422=He emerged at 5, after stepping up from the 10-million class to finish 3rd in the Nikkei Shinshun Hai; until then he had won 2 of 15. He then won 2 in his class and was a close 2nd in the Nikkei Sho. That autumn he won the Asahi Challenge C. in record time, and in the Arima Kinen his bold forward run earned 2nd, joining the elite. The next spring he won the Kinko Sho and was 3rd in the Takarazuka Kinen; in autumn he won the Kyoto Daishoten. In the Japan Cup, against the best in training, Symboli Kris S., he won a legendary wire-to-wire race by 9 lengths for his first G1. After a heavy loss in the Arima Kinen, he romped in the '04 Takarazuka Kinen and announced an Arc bid. But his transport plane broke down, forcing a delay, and a replacement flight just before the race followed. He ran, but the punishing schedule left him 17th. He was 2nd to rival Zenno Rob Roy in the Arima Kinen. Racing on at 8 in '05, he won a third straight Kinko Sho but was 7th trying to repeat in the Takarazuka Kinen, and after more heavy defeats retired that year.
423=3rd choice in the NHK Hai (G2, 2000m turf), he was 2nd to Rugby Ball, then 7th in the Derby. The Kyoto Kinen at 4 was his only graded win. His best offspring was Asahi Challenge C. winner Shinkaiun (92: Shin Miss Brown).
424=The 1990 Satsuki Sho winner. He won 5 straight including the Kisaragi Sho, was a Satsuki Sho contender, and beat favorites Ines Fujin and Mejiro Ryan for the crown - a father-son double after his sire Haiseiko. 2nd choice in the Derby, he couldn't quicken and was 5th to Ines Fujin. A tendon injury followed, and after a long break he retired without racing again.
425=Raced mainly on dirt from his debut, he shone in JRA-local races, winning the Derby Grand Prix by a distance. In 2001 he won the Kawasaki Kinen in record time, a race his granddam Rosita had won; two years later his uncle Kanetsu Fleuve won it too - a family race. He kept running hard on dirt, including a Dubai trip (10th), and won the first JBC Classic. Yet oddly he flopped in JRA graded races: 10th and 14th in two Japan Cup Dirts and 15th in the February S. Apart from a 5th in the Unicorn S. at 3, he never even made the top five. At stud he sired 2009 Tokyo Derby winner Silent Stamen. He covered mares until 2010 and died in July 2017.
426=Unbeaten in 3 from his debut, he won the Asahi Hai FS, a G1 double with his brother Admire Vega. At 3 he struggled in the classics - 7th, 6th and 4th - so, given his pedigree too, he switched to dirt. In his first dirt race, the JBC Classic, he won by 7 lengths as 2nd choice. He lost the Japan Cup Dirt and February S., but after a break swept Japan's big dirt G1s - Nambu Hai, Teio Sho, February S. - building an era as dirt king. He never won the Japan Cup Dirt, but had no real rivals. Declaring "we're done with dirt," his camp ran him in the Arima Kinen, his first turf race in 2 years 2 months; he was 7th. Taking that back, he ran in the February S. and was a dull 5th, then 6th in the Sankei Osaka Hai and 4th in the Kashiwa Kinen, and retired to stud. He was exported to Korea in 2011, but his Japanese offspring include graded winners Admire Deus and Albert.
427=Too late for the spring classics, he improved rapidly and won the Kobe Shimbun Hai, his first graded race. In the Kyoto Shimbun Hai he then beat double classic winner Narita Brian by a neck - a huge upset. Even in a prep, beating Narita Brian made Starman a household name. He was 5th in the Kikuka Sho, but brushed aside older horses for the first time in the Naruo Kinen, proving his ability. Then a fracture cost him 18 months. Back in summer at 5, after a dirt race, he was 2nd in the Kokura Kinen and Asahi Challenge C., showing signs of recovery, but a third injury ended his career.
428=A late-blooming middle-distance dirt horse who changed the image of his sire Soccer Boy. Lacking a finishing punch early, he was 2nd 3 times before winning his 5th start, and often fell short after that, only taking off in summer at 4. He then rose fast, winning the Winter S., his first graded race, and then local Nagoya's Nagoya Daishoten. He ran in many JRA-local races, battling stars like Hokuto Vega, Lively Mount and Amazon Opera and showing fans how exciting dirt racing could be. At 5 he won the Tokyo Daishoten, and was 2nd in it the next year as a national G1. At 7 he tried the Dubai World Cup, finishing 6th, and kept running tough on dirt until 8, winning 13 races in all.
429=After a 5th in the Asahi Hai Sansai S., a 2nd in the Keisei Hai and a 3rd in the Yayoi Sho, he was 8th in the Satsuki Sho, 3rd in the NHK Hai (G2, 2000m) and, as 12th choice, 3rd in the Derby. Always running well, never quite winning. Then in autumn he won the St. Lite Kinen, his first graded race and first win since his maiden. 2nd in the Kikuka Sho, he was 4th in the Japan Cup, best of the Japanese ahead of Oguri Cap. In Oguri's farewell Arima Kinen he was favorite and ran 3rd. He kept running in graded races - 28 in a row from the Asahi Hai Sansai S. - his finishes slipping year by year. Still, White Stone remained an essential supporting player in graded races.
430=The monster of local Utsunomiya, whose 43 wins set a local thoroughbred record. At 9 he was entered in the All Comers as "the best in North Kanto," but in his first turf race finished 7th. He went to stud but retired in '07 for poor results, and has since lived quietly at Araki Training Farm in Hokkaido.
431=He won 3 straight from the Hiiragi Sho, including the Keisei Hai, but leg trouble ruled out the NHK Mile C. His comeback race was the Yasuda Kinen, of all things; showing his sharp kick, he was a fine 3rd at 3 behind Taiki Blizzard and Genuine. He was 3rd to Bubble Gum Fellow in the Mainichi Okan in autumn, but then ran poorly as if burned out.
432=He debuted late, in September at 3, and was a dismal 15th in his hyped debut. But he steadily improved, reaching open class with 3 straight wins at the end of his 3yo year. At 5 he grew remarkably, winning the Aichi Hai, Kitakyushu Kinen and Mainichi Okan in a row. In the Tenno Sho (Autumn) he was a fine 3rd behind T.M. Opera O and Meisho Doto, ahead of Narita Top Road and Stay Gold. But an injury to his right foreleg was found afterwards, and despite long treatment he never returned.
433="The demon of Kyoto": all 8 of his wins came at Kyoto. He won his debut and the Shiragiku Sho, but injury cost him the spring classics. He was 3rd in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai and 10th in the Kikuka Sho, then won in his own class twice. At 5 he won the Subaru S., then led all the way in the Kyoto Kinen for his first graded win, and won the Kyoto Daishoten in autumn. In the Takarazuka Kinen at 6, while others swung wide to avoid the torn-up inside, he alone dove to the rail and nearly won, finishing a thrilling 3rd.
434=An allowance horse, he was crushed in double figures in the Spring S., ending his classic dream. After 6 months off he went on a run in autumn, winning 3 straight through the classes, and just 3 months after returning won the Nakayama Kimpai to become a graded winner. A promising start, but among strong rivals that was his only win of the year. At 5 he won the Nakayama Kimpai and Tokyo Shimbun Hai in a row, seeming to turn a corner, but a tendon injury forced his retirement to stud. His daughter Gina Fontaine (Empress Hai winner) is the dam of Funabashi star Casino Fontaine, who led all the way to beat JRA horses in the 2021 Kawasaki Kinen.
435=At 3 he was a fine 3rd against older horses in the Takarazuka Kinen. The next year he won the Nakayama Kinen, his first graded race, then the Milers C. in record time. He even went to France and was a gallant 2nd by half a length in the Prix du Moulin de Longchamp - a fine record. Erratic like many front-runners, he also had many heavy defeats. Winless in autumn at 5, he tried dirt again in the Japan Cup Dirt, but blasted off in front and collapsed. At 6 he won the Milers C. for his first win in 2 years, then struggled again, until in his first race of '07, the Nakayama Kinen, he led and won easily. When it worked, he was formidable. At stud his second crop's Logotype won the Asahi Hai FS and Satsuki Sho, with fellow son Gottfried 3rd in that Asahi Hai FS.
436=He won the Aoba Sho and went to the Derby, losing to Neo Universe by half a length in 2nd. He won the Kobe Shimbun Hai in autumn but was 4th in the Kikuka Sho. Near misses continued: 3rd in the Arima Kinen, 2nd in the Nikkei Sho and Tenno Sho (Spring), 2nd in the Kyoto Daishoten. Then in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), with Peslier aboard, he showed new power and won his long-sought G1. As if releasing his frustration, he won the Japan Cup too, and in the Arima Kinen beat Tap Dance City, who had beaten him in the Takarazuka Kinen - the autumn older horses' triple crown, the best in training. In '05 he went straight to the Takarazuka Kinen, finishing 3rd, then was 2nd in the International S. in summer; 2nd in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), 3rd in the Japan Cup and 8th in the Arima Kinen followed as his form faded, and he retired. At stud he sired Oaks winner Saint Emilion and many middle-distance stars like Perusa, Animate Bio, Cosmo Mnemosyne and Trailblazer.
437=Favorite for the Asahi Hai FS, he lost by a neck to A Shin Champ's record 1:33.5. In the classics he was a close 2nd to Neo Universe in the Satsuki Sho, but was crushed in the Derby and Kikuka Sho. Yet he won the Sapporo Kinen against older horses like Air Eminem, and the next year crushed Lohengrin and others in the Nakayama Kinen in record time - at middle distances he was hard to beat. In autumn at 4, just as his prime began, a tendon injury forced retirement. At stud he sired Ocean S. winner Sakura Gospel and Flower C. winner Sakura Plaisir.
438=5th on turf on debut, he won his second start on dirt by 9 lengths. He won the Cyclamen S. too, then the Zen-Nihon 2yo Yushun to reign as dirt champion of his crop. In spring at 3 he tried the NHK Mile C., finishing 4th. Back on dirt he won the Unicorn S. and Derby Grand Prix. Then he went about a year without a win, until in the Nambu Hai at 4 he beat dirt king Admire Don to bounce back, and won it again the next year. In '06 he went to Dubai and romped in the Godolphin Mile, prompting a sudden sale to Dubai's Godolphin and a move abroad. Fans hoped he'd return to race in Japan, but he retired in December '07 and went to stud in America.
