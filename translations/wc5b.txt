35=* "Queen of Longchamp" Allez France\nTrue to her name, "Go, France," Allez France had swept the French fillies' Triple Crown (Poule d'Essai des Pouliches, Prix de Diane, Prix Vermeille) the year before - France's top mare. But in the Arc, Europe's greatest race, she had tasted bitter defeat in 2nd.\nIn 1974, at 4, she began in the G2 Prix d'Harcourt and won easily. She took two straight G1s, won the G3 Prix Foy prep, and headed to the Arc for revenge.\nAll seemed set, but 10 days before the race her regular jockey Yves Saint-Martin fell and broke his hip. A substitute was lined up, but Saint-Martin refused to give up the ride and took painkillers to get in the saddle. She was expected to come from behind as usual, but from the final turn she swept past the field and hit the front early - perhaps because of Saint-Martin's injury. Pressed by others near the line, she just held on to win by a head. As a daughter of Sea-Bird, often called France's greatest horse, she completed a father-daughter Arc double,.\nThat year Allez France won all 5 of her starts at Longchamp and was named France's champion older mare and Horse of the Year. With 12 of her 13 career wins at Longchamp, fans came to call her "the Queen of Longchamp."\n\n* "The Black Butterfly Dancing Around the World" Dahlia\nThe year before, the French filly Dahlia had stunned fans by winning the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. by a record 6 lengths. She also traveled to America and became the first filly to win the G1 Washington DC International, making her name known worldwide.\nThis year she opened in the Prix d'Harcourt, her 5th meeting with rival Allez France, and lost by six and a half lengths. In the Prix Ganay she was well beaten again, 5th behind her rival. Traveling to England for the Coronation C., she was only 3rd - three straight losses, a poor start.\nBut back in France, in the G1 Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud, with Allez France absent and a weaker field, she won convincingly. Dahlia then flew back to England for the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. Despite a strong field including Highclere, winner of the 1000 Guineas and Prix de Diane, she cruised out of the back of midfield to become the first horse ever to win the race twice.\nShe kept racing around the world, winning 10 G1s in her career. At a time when shipping horses abroad was still far from routine, she raced in six countries with great success - truly one of the horses who opened the road for international racing.
