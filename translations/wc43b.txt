540=She won twice on dirt and tried the classics without success, then turned to dirt and bloomed, winning the Kanto Oaks and Sparking Lady C. From '04 she ran only 3 of 31 races in the JRA, winning JRA-local races like the Empress Hai, Hakusan Daishoten and Queen Sho.
544=From her second maiden she won 3 straight - the Hopeful S. and Flower C. - but was crushed in the Oka Sho and Oaks. After 11 months off she showed grit with a 2nd in the Fukushima Himba S., but then ran poorly and retired at the end of her 5yo year.
545=A familiar runner for trainer Hashiguchi. Early to shine, he won the Daily Hai 2yo S. and was 3rd in the NHK Mile C. at 3. A Tokyo specialist, he won the Fuji S., Tokyo Shimbun Hai and Epsom C., plus the Sekiya Kinen and Keisei Hai AH at left-handed Niigata - 6 graded wins. At 6, as 12th choice, he held on for 3rd behind Maurice in the Yasuda Kinen, and later placed as a double-figure outsider in the Milers C. at 7 and Keio Hai Spring C. at 8. He retired after the Yasuda Kinen at 8.
551=She won the Rose S. and was favorite for the Queen Elizabeth II Cup over Vega, but finished 9th. As a dam she produced '01 Chunichi Shimbun Hai winner Grand Pas de Deux (97: Fuji Kiseki) and '04 Tokai S. winner En Dehors (99: Brian's Time).
552=A late riser, she reached open class with 2 wins at the end of her 3yo year, then alternated good and bad runs until winning the Himba Tokyo Times Hai in autumn at 4. As a dam her best was 4-time winner Tsukiakari (94: Northern Taste).
557=A foreign-bred hope, he ran steadily over 1400-1800m on turf from 3 to 5. 3rd in his first graded try, the Nakayama Himba S. in spring at 4, he won the Keio Hai Autumn H. and Fuchu Himba S. in autumn. Never in a G1, he still ran well, 3rd in the Tokyo Shimbun Hai and Nakayama Himba S.
560=The Anemone S. made her a classic contender, but she finished in double figures in both the Oka Sho and Oaks. In autumn she won the Negishi S. and then starred in dirt sprints, a close 2nd in the JRA-local Cluster C.
561=Strong or poor, she needed 7 starts to reach open class. At 3, with a 2nd in the Kobai Sho and 3rd in the Elfin S., she steadied and took off, winning the Oka Sho trial Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West). 4th in the Oka Sho, she won the Oaks over hot favorite Agnes Flora and blueblood Daiichi Ruby. Little followed, and she retired after the Meguro Kinen in February at 4. As a dam, her best was 5-time winner A Shin Harimao (98: Tokai Teio).
