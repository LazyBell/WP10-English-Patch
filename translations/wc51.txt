797=Many horses improve at tight, flat local tracks, but Maillot Jaune was extreme: she only ran big at Niigata. Of her 7 wins from 39, 4 came from 6 starts at Niigata - 3 of them graded - and her 2 losses there were by just 0.2s. She even won the Fukushima Kinen when it was moved to Niigata for rebuilding.
798=Half-brother of Admire Hope (2 graded wins incl. the Zen-Nihon 2yo Yushun) and Admire Fuji (3 graded wins incl. the Nikkei Shinshun Hai). 2nd on debut, he moved to local Nagoya, won twice, returned to the JRA and won 5 straight up to the Fukushima Kinen. But in the Nakayama Kimpai he fractured his right 3rd metacarpal. Retired to stud, his condition worsened at the farm and he was put down.
799=He kept trying graded races at 2 without success, but at 3 was a nose 2nd in the Shinzan Kinen. 8th over further in the Wakaba S., he won the NZ Trophy. After that he mostly ran poorly, as if burnt out, though he was 3rd in the Lord Derby Challenge T. at 5. He broke down in the '09 Aurora C. and was put down.
800=A mare who won 2 graded races as an outsider. First, the Fairy S. at 3, edging past the favorites in the straight. 2nd in the Flower C. and Fukushima Himba S., she went a long time without a win. Then at 6, as 10th choice in the Niigata Kinen, she stayed on as the favorites faded and won by a neck - her first win in 3.5 years.
801=Close but winless at 3, he won the Chunichi Shimbun Hai and the Kabutoyama Kinen as an older horse. He then lost form, moved to Hokkaido racing and retired without another win.
802=A talented horse always near the top in graded races. He won the Heian S. at 5, then the March S. and Elm S. at 6. But after a 2nd in the Nambu Hai he developed hemangiosarcoma and was put down - a shocking end.
803=2nd in the Radio Tampa Hai 2yo S., he won the Mainichi Hai, but was 9th in the Satsuki Sho and 8th in the Derby. He was 3rd in the Kikuka Sho and a popular force in older graded races. At 5 he won the Chukyo Kinen, his first win since the Mainichi Hai, and the Kinko Sho, showing the "Rose family" strength of granddam Rosa Nay. Going for a repeat in the '08 Kinko Sho, he broke down and was put down - he shone at Chukyo and fell there.
804=He won 3 straight, incl. the Kisaragi Sho and Mainichi Hai, but then fractured a leg. Back after 9 months, he never regained his form, and moved to local Funabashi at 6 without a win.
805=Close but winless on turf, he suffered repeated injuries - 1 win from 7 by spring at 5. Back on dirt he won 4 straight and the Heian S. at 6, and despite leg trouble won 2 more graded races. He moved to Iwate at 9, racing until the end of '06.
806=Debuting in Hokkaido racing, he won by wide margins, 4 straight including the Hokkaido 2yo Yushun. He moved to Futoshi Kojima's JRA stable for the classics, but was crushed in the Keisei Hai, ran poorly after and returned to local racing.
807=Not overwhelming in ability, nor blessed with rare race sense - but undeniably strong. Her Oaks run, pushing Cesario to a neck, and her neck win in the Shuka Sho showed Air Messiah at her best: "strong without being overwhelming." Her straight 2nds in the Hanshin Himba S. and Victoria Mile reflected that too, if less kindly. As a dam she produced Air One Piece (08: Rock of Gibraltar), and her 4th foal Air Spinel (13: King Kamehameha) won 3 graded races, while his full brother Air Windsor won 4 straight up to the Challenge C.
808=Often close but winless at 3, he came good after injury in summer at 4, winning 4 straight to the Nikkei Shinshun Hai, then the Kyoto Kinen - 5 in a row. His late surge recalled his sire, "late-blooming star" Marvelous Sunday. In G1s he was 3rd in the Tenno Sho (Spring), 2nd in the Takarazuka Kinen and 3rd in the Arima Kinen. He won the '06 American JCC but, racing on until 10, never won again.
809=A head 2nd to Rhein Kraft in the Fillies' Revue and 3rd in the Oka Sho, she beat colts for 2nd in the NHK Mile C., a contender with just 1 win. After a 12th in the Shuka Sho she slumped, but at 4 was 6th in the Victoria Mile and 4th against colts in the Epsom C. In autumn she won the Queen S. and Fuchu Himba S. She was 3rd in the Victoria Mile and won the Fuchu Himba S. again, a leader among mares. She retired to breed after a 7th in the '08 February S.
810=A regular in low-class dirt races, with surprisingly many fans. His partnership with jockey Takashi Hirame, as low-key as the horse, was a hidden favorite: Hirame rode him in 50 of his 55 flat races. Slowly adding wins, at 6, in his 44th start, he tried his first graded race, the March S. A perfect start let him lead, and as 14th choice of 14 he pulled off a huge upset - Hirame's first graded win too.
811=Beaten badly in 2 turf races, he won his 3rd start on dirt like a new horse. Racing only on dirt after, he won the March S. in spring at 5, then the Tokai S. over Time Paradox and others - he had come good. He was 5th in the Teio Sho and JBC Classic, but a fracture was found. Back in June '05, he was 3rd in the Onuma S. but had lost his old strength. He finally won again in the Shiwasu S. at the end of '06. Still racing at 9 in '08, aiming for a full comeback, he was retired without running.
812=Half-sister of graded favorite Columbus Circle. She won her debut over a mile at Tokyo in November at 2 in a good 1:34.7. 2nd choice in the Fairy S., she broke clear early but was caught by a neck by 11th choice Cosmo Mnemosyne. As favorite she won the Queen C. with authority, an Eastern classic hope with 2yo queen Apapane. But 2nd choice in the Oka Sho, she faded to 5th behind Apapane, then was dull in the Oaks, 6th. In summer she beat older mares in the Queen S., but was 3rd behind Apapane again in the Shuka Sho. Older, she was 2nd in both the Keisei Hai AH and Fuji S. and won the Capital S. (OP).
813=By autumn triple crown winner Zenno Rob Roy out of Motek, a French middle-distance graded winner. She won her debut and a special. 1.6 favorite for the Flower C., she couldn't quicken, 3rd to Oken Sakura. Aiming only for the Oaks, she won the Flora S. as 1.9 favorite. 5th choice in the Oaks, she dueled Oka Sho winner Apapane to the line, noses level; after a long photo, it was the first dead heat in classic history. She didn't win again and went to stud in 2013.
814=She beat older mares in the Mermaid S. and was 3rd in the Aichi Hai. A closer, not consistent, she was still 3rd in the Mermaid S. at 4 and 5. She retired to breed in February '09.
815=Winner of 11 US G1s from 2002 to 2004; in 2002 at 4 she was only the 3rd female Eclipse Horse of the Year. Debuting late, in November at 3, she won her first G1, the Santa Margarita Invitational H., in spring at 4 and went on a winning run, taking 5 G1s that year incl. the BC Distaff - the first female Horse of the Year since Lady's Secret in 1986. At 5 she won 4 more G1s on the same path, but after a 2nd in September's Lady's Secret H., a tendon injury forced a long break. Back, she won less often but took 3 more G1s, incl. a 3rd straight Apple Blossom H., retiring with 11. At stud she produced graded winner Wine Princess, and from 2010 lived in Japan, bred to Deep Impact and Heart's Cry without notable success.
816=Winner of the 2004 Breeders' Cup Classic by a dominant margin, one of the best horses of the 2000s. He raced as a sprinter until 3: 3rd in his first G1, the King's Bishop S., he won the Vosburgh S. by 6 lengths. At 4 he stretched out with the BC Classic in mind, winning a 9-furlong G3 by over 10 lengths and the G1 Woodward S. as favorite. In the 10-furlong BC Classic, against stars like Pleasantly Perfect, then rated the world's best, and dual classic winner Funny Cide, he led all the way and won easily, earning Eclipse Horse of the Year. Racing on at 5, he won the Metropolitan H. but then hurt a leg and retired. At stud he sired many top fillies like Contested, Judy the Beauty and Goodnight Olive, plus colts like 2021 Dubai World Cup winner Mystic Guide and 2024 Santa Anita Derby winner Stronghold. As a broodmare sire he gave 2018 US Triple Crown winner Justify and Drefong, now a sire in Japan.
817=6 wins, three 2nds and three 3rds from 15. Never out of the top five in 5 graded races incl. the Nikkei Sho and Kyoto Daishoten, he won a graded race in his final start, the Kokura Daishoten. At stud he sired steady runners like Kokura Kinen winner Sunrey Jasper.
818=Close but winless in lower classes, at 4 she ran above her class for a 2nd in the Mermaid S. She was 4th in the Kokura Kinen, 2nd in the Niigata Kinen and Fuchu Himba S., but 7th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. She broke through in summer at 5: 2nd in the Mermaid S., then beat colts in the Kokura Kinen. Flu and hoof trouble halted her, and after her return she ran poorly, but had a long career.
819=Beaten in the trials (4th Spring S., 7th Aoba Sho), he missed the classics but won the Radio Nikkei Sho, unfazed by heavy ground. After an 11th in the Kobe Shimbun Hai he rested, returning in the Niigata Daishoten at 4. Struggling in older graded races, he won the Capital S. in autumn '08, then beat Admire Aura, Fallichat and others in the Kyoto Kimpai, his first graded win in 2.5 years. But he never made the top five in 8 more starts and retired after the '10 Niigata Daishoten.
820=After 2nds and 3rds, he won in his 5th start, then was 2nd in the Radio Nikkei Sho. 3rd in the Kobe Shimbun Hai in autumn, but graded-winless, he was 8th choice for the Kikuka Sho. In the race he ran down front-running Admire Main near the line with a powerful finish - a brilliant first G1. After a 4th in the Hong Kong Vase, a tendon injury ended his career. At stud he sired Hanshin JF 3rd Rice Terrace and others.
821=At 4 he was 2nd in the Kimpai (West), Kyoto Kinen, Osakajo S. and May S., but winless. The next year he won the Kimpai (West), catching later G1 winners Marvelous Crown and Nehai Caesar. A broodmare after retiring, she died in a pasture accident in '08.
822=In his 27th start, at 5, he tried his first graded race, the Hakodate Sprint S., and as 13th choice blew away See Is Tosho and others with a storming finish. A flat-track lover, he was also 3rd in Sapporo's Keeneland C.
823=Raced on dirt from his 2nd start, he was 2nd in the Japan Dirt Derby and Derby Grand Prix at 3. He won his 2nd open special in the Ritto S. at 4 but fractured his left knee and was out over a year. Back in July at 5, he was 4th in the Procyon S., then won 4 straight to the JBC Sprint - suddenly the ace of dirt sprints. At 6 he was 4th in the Dubai Golden Shaheen, the best yet by a Japan-trained horse. In autumn he won the Cluster C. and Tokyo Hai, but a tendon injury ruined his JBC Sprint repeat, and he retired to stud.
824=He led all the way in his first dirt race, the Poinsettia Sho (5M), then again in the big Zen-Nihon 2yo Yushun. Invited to the UAE Derby, he went and was 8th. Back home he was beaten badly, his limits exposed, and after the Topaz S. at 3 moved to Sonoda.
825=By modest sire Carista Glory, but with top speed. At 3 he focused on turf sprints and won 3 straight incl. the Ibis Summer Dash. Struggling after, he switched to dirt and won the Keiyo S. (OP), then was 2nd in the JRA-local Kakitsubata Kinen. Winless in sprints on any surface after, he moved to Arao after the '09 Ibis Summer Dash, and won his first race there from the front.
826=Too late for the Oka Sho, she won the Sweet Pea S. and ran in the Oaks and Shuka Sho, then won the Hanshin Himba Tokubetsu at year's end as 10th choice. As a dam she produced '05 Nakayama Himba S. winner Winglet (01: Taiki Shuttle).
827=3rd in the Kokura 2yo S., she showed promise but missed the classics and was 15th in the NHK Mile C. After a long break, she focused on sprints of 1200m or less and won the Kita-Kyushu Kinen in summer at 4. After a 9th in the Centaur S. she lost her rhythm, finishing in double figures, and went to stud in '08.
828=He romped on debut and won the Niigata Sansai S. easily in record time, but a fracture kept him off the turf for 19 months. Back in April at 4, he beat Hokuto Helios, Material and others in the Paradise S., then was 3rd in the Tanabata Sho. Another injury cost him a year, and after one more race he retired. Everyone wondered what he could have been without injuries.
829=With just 1 win, he ran above his class to win the St. Lite Kinen, but was 16th in the Kikuka Sho. He then ran in the Japan Cup, Arima Kinen and February S. without making the top five. A tendon injury followed, and after a comeback the next summer he retired.
830=Bred at US Dell Ridge Farm, like Capella S. winner Victory Tetsuny. After 2 turf losses in summer at 2, she returned in a Kyoto 1400m dirt race and won by 4 lengths, then romped against colts in the Poinsettia Sho, a dirt contender. 3rd in the Hyacinth S. and 6th in the Fillies' Revue aiming for the Oka Sho, she went back to dirt and crushed colts in the Shoryu S. In the Kanto Oaks she raced forward and won by 5 lengths - top 3yo dirt filly. As jockey Yasunari Iwata said, she was a match for colts: dominant in fillies' JRA-local races, 2nd against colts in the Kashiwa Kinen. After duels with Miracle Legend, a year younger, she was 4th to Transcend in the Japan Cup Dirt as 10th choice, then retired to breed.
831=Raced on dirt from debut, he beat older horses in the Taisetsu H. (10M), then won the G1 Derby Grand Prix by a neck. He never ran well again, crushed even in open specials, and moved to Southern Kanto in '08.
832=A true miler who ran almost only at 1400-1600m on turf. Crushed in the NZ Trophy and NHK Mile C. at 3, he slowly improved, and at 7 won the Fuji S. as 16th choice, holding on against the odds.
