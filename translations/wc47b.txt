670=Her first win came in her 3rd start and her first special in her 7th, but she improved with every race, and in her 8th start, the Queen C., she raced forward and overpowered Danon Velvet and the rest. Going for a second graded win in the Flower C., she was caught out by Viva Vodka's runaway and lost by a neck. Skipping the Oka Sho, she won the Flora S. from up front and challenged queen Buena Vista as Kanto's hope. But in the Oaks, against the Oka Sho stars she met for the first time - winner Buena Vista and runner-up Red Desire - she was well beaten in 5th.
671=Debuting fairly late, in February at 3, she ran tough until 6, winning 5 of 31, including 2 graded races, the Kabutoyama Kinen and Kokura Daishoten. All her wins came at local meetings; she loved tight, flat tracks but struggled on hills.
673=A speedy front-runner. She won her second maiden by 7 lengths and the Sapporo Sansai S. by 4, becoming a classic contender, and ran a good 4th in the Oka Sho. But leading boldly in the Oaks she was 13th, lost form and retired without another win.
674=At 2 he was 2nd in the Hyogo Junior Grand Prix and 3rd in the Zen-Nihon 2yo Yushun on dirt, but then kept finishing unplaced, and switched to jumps in autumn at 4. It paid off: he won a maiden hurdle in his 2nd start, and the next year won the Nakayama Grand Jump, one of jumping's top races. He then won the Kokura Summer Jump and Nakayama Daishogai too. But from 6 his great rival Oju Chosan emerged, taking both the Nakayama Daishogai and Grand Jump two years running, leaving Up to Date 2nd or 3rd each time. He fought back, winning the Hanshin Jump S. twice, but fell at the last fence in the 2018 Nakayama Daishogai. He aimed to return after treatment but couldn't, and retired.
681=Debuting in Hokkaido local racing, he won once and moved to the JRA. He won the Kusunoki Sho, was 2nd in the Kyodo News Hai, and as 9th choice won the Mainichi Hai. After the Derby, injuries disrupted him, and at 6 he moved to local Kasamatsu, then Kanazawa the next year, without a win.
686=He won his debut at Kyoto in January at 3 as favorite, then made the top five twice in 5-million specials. In the Spring S., his first graded race, as 10th choice he raced forward and held on for 2nd, ahead of 2yo champion Rose Kingdom. He was a fair 7th in the Satsuki Sho, then won the Kyoto Shimbun Hai for his first graded win, and was a good 4th in the Derby, 2 lengths behind Eishin Flash in a sprint finish. He kept running well in turf graded races without winning.
689=At 3 he ran fairly well, 4th in the Aoba Sho, but from 4 alternated wins and losses in lower classes, reaching open class only in September at 5. In his first older-horse graded race, the Mainichi Okan, he unleashed a stunning kick, sweeping past Daiwa Major, A Shin Deputy and the rest in a record 1:44.2. He was 8th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), 6th in the Japan Cup and 13th in the Arima Kinen, but his ability was real, until he broke a leg in training in winter '08 and was put down.
663=Nephew of Silence Suzuka. He won the Keisei Hai in his 4th start and was 3rd in the Mainichi Hai, but missed the Satsuki Sho and was crushed in the Aoba Sho and Derby. After a tendon injury, he broke a leg in training in March '05 and was put down.
