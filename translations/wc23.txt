123=* "The Greatest Great King" King Kamehameha\nTrainer Kunihide Matsuda was known for racing horses with their future breeding careers in mind. For top colts, to raise their stud value, he aimed for G1 wins at two different distances: the 1600m NHK Mile C. and the 2400m Japanese Derby. Some called it an odd schedule, but it would showcase early maturity and versatility. Still, with just two weeks between the races and an 800m jump in distance, few horses could manage it. His Kurofune in 2001 and Tanino Gimlet the next year tried, but each won only one.\nThis year, after seeing King Kamehameha (by Kingmambo) run on debut, Matsuda thought, "I want to go for the Derby with this horse," and planned the double. After winning his first graded race, the Mainichi Hai, he went to the NHK Mile C. Despite doubts as he'd never run a mile, he settled smoothly in midfield, tracked the fast pace with ease and won by 5 lengths - a race record margin, against strong rivals like the previous Asahi Hai FS 1-2, in race-record time.\nThen, two weeks later, the Japanese Derby. His NHK Mile win made him favorite despite the 800m step up. The pace was fast again, and as the front-runners faded one by one, he swept forward widest of all and simply held on, winning by a length and a half from Heart's Cry. His time broke Ines Fujin's 1990 race record by 2 seconds. The first to win the NHK Mile C.-Derby double, he was, just as Matsuda planned, highly valued at stud and sired many G1 winners.\n\n* "Star of Local Racing" Takes On the Classics\nCosmo Bulk, based in Hokkaido local racing, made headlines by taking on the JRA classics. He debuted at 2 in Hokkaido, then ran in the JRA's Hyakunichiso Tokubetsu. In his first turf race he won in course-record time, then won the Radio Tampa Hai 2yo S. at year's end, drawing huge attention.\nIn 2004 he set out on the JRA classics. His earnings were ample, but as a local horse he needed priority entry, so he won the Satsuki Sho trial, the Yayoi Sho, easily. With two graded wins and the excitement of a local horse in the classics, he was Satsuki Sho favorite. He closed in the straight but couldn't catch Daiwa Major, losing narrowly in 2nd. In the Derby he faced NHK Mile C. winner King Kamehameha. He led briefly but faded in the straight, finishing a well-beaten 8th behind the double winner. In autumn he won the St. Lite Kinen but was 4th to Delta Blues in the Kikuka Sho, and his classic dream ended.\nHe kept showing his pride, finishing 2nd in that year's Japan Cup. He kept trying JRA and even overseas G1s, and at 5 finally won a G1 in Singapore. He raced on until 8. No local-based horse has yet won a JRA classic, but his relentless challenges against the JRA's best stirred many fans.\n\n* Durandal, Who Changed the Rules of Sprinting\nIn sprints, front-runners often hold on even at a fast pace, so racing forward was the rule. Durandal (by Sunday Silence) changed that, charging from the back in a class of his own.\nVery hot-tempered, he had trouble at the start. Yutaka Take suggested to his trainer, "This horse should come from behind and go wide," and that became his style.\nWhen Ikezoe became his jockey in autumn of his 4yo year, he came into his own, winning the Sprinters S. and the Mile CS from the back, and was named champion sprinter. In 2004 he was narrowly 2nd in the Takamatsunomiya Kinen and Sprinters S., unable to catch the leaders. But in the Mile CS he again swept past the field with an amazing kick for his third G1, and was champion sprinter for the second straight year.\nThe sharpness of his finish truly deserved his name, a legendary "famed sword."\n\n* Trifecta Betting Begins\nThe trifecta - picking 1st, 2nd and 3rd in order - is hard but pays big. Hugely popular today, it was introduced in JRA racing in September this year, at first only on the last 4 races of a day. From 2008 it was sold on all races.
124=* Deep Impact: Unbeaten Triple Crown\nBorn on March 25, the same birthday as his sire Sunday Silence, Deep Impact shocked Japanese racing even more than his sire and became a social phenomenon.\nIn his second start, the Wakakoma S., he was about 10 lengths behind the leader at the final turn, then surged to win by 5 lengths. So dominant was he that people already called the Triple Crown a certainty. In the Satsuki Sho he was favorite with 63.0% of win bets, second only to Tokino Minoru in 1951. He stumbled badly at the start, nearly losing his rider, and dropped to last, but won clearly by two and a half lengths. No one doubted the Triple Crown now. Yutaka Take's description, "he runs as if flying," became his trademark.\nIn the Japanese Derby he drew 73.4% of win bets, far beyond Haiseiko's 1973 record. He missed the break again, but led with 200m left and pulled away to win by 5 lengths, equaling King Kamehameha's record time from the year before.\nThen the Kikuka Sho, unbeaten. Over 130,000 fans (182% of the year before), a Kikuka Sho record, packed Kyoto to see the Triple Crown. He drew 79.03% of win bets, and paid just 1.0 - money back. He broke well and waited in midfield. In the straight Norihiro Yokoyama on Admire Japan broke clear early and it seemed over, but again he flew home, winning by 2 lengths. The first unbeaten Triple Crown since Symboli Rudolf in 1984 was covered by media far beyond racing and grew into a true social phenomenon.\n\n* Cesario Wins the Japanese and American Oaks\nCesario, by Special Week, who walked the royal road, won her first two starts, then the Flower C. prep in race-record time. But in the Oka Sho she was 2nd to Rhein Kraft, a future NHK Mile C. winner.\nFrom here she shone. With Rhein Kraft absent from the Oaks (going to the NHK Mile C. as the distance didn't suit her), Cesario was heavy favorite. Racing from the back, still 12th at the final turn, she split the pack in the straight and caught Air Messiah to win by a neck.\nNow the best 3yo filly, she was sent to the American Oaks. Oka Sho winner Dance in the Mood had been 2nd the year before, so it was a chance for revenge. Favorite was American filly Melhor Ainda, unbeaten in 4, with Cesario 2nd. She was keyed up before the race at her first foreign track and the huge crowd, but broke well. Moving early from the third turn, she led into the straight and kept everyone away, beating Melhor Ainda by 4 lengths - race records for both time and margin.\nThe first Japan-trained horse to win a US G1, Cesario was expected to do more and further trips were planned, but after returning she suffered a suspensory injury, went on a long break, and retired the next year without racing again.
125=* Japan's Greatest Takes On the Arc\nThe year before, Deep Impact won an unbeaten Triple Crown and became a social phenomenon. As an older horse this year he kept running in a class of his own. In the Hanshin Daishoten he handled good-to-soft going for the first time with ease. In the Tenno Sho (Spring) he drew a race-record 73.5% of win bets. He missed the break again, then launched a long run before the third turn and led at the final turn - unlike his usual style. Still he produced the fastest finish in the straight, showing that wherever he moved, it didn't matter. His time was a world record for 3200m on turf. In the Takarazuka Kinen he again drew a race-record 75.2% of win bets and won easily; in the photo afterwards Yutaka Take raised five fingers for five crowns.\nHis camp, long planning a trip abroad, set his next race as the Arc, the world's greatest. Japan's long-held Arc dream would be carried by perhaps its greatest horse ever. Many Japanese fans traveled to Longchamp dreaming of glory. As favorite he broke well, and unlike his usual style, sat 2nd or 3rd. He led 400m out, but in the straight Rail Link, on a 4-race winning streak, passed him after a fierce duel, and he crossed the line 3rd. He was praised for his effort on his return, but over a month later he was disqualified from the Arc: ipratropium, a drug used for respiratory problems, had been detected. It wasn't banned in Japan then, but was added the next year after this. Japan's greatest horse's Arc challenge ended in an unexpected way.\nBack home, announcing his retirement at year's end, he ran in the Japan Cup and Arima Kinen. As if to vent his frustration, he won both easily, leaving the turf with the pride of Japan's best intact. His overwhelming power lives on in his offspring, still making a huge impact on racing.\n\n* Taking On G1s Abroad\nBesides Deep Impact, many other horses took on G1s abroad this year.\nLocal horse Cosmo Bulk, who stormed the classics in 2004, ran in the Singapore Airlines International Cup on his first trip abroad. Racing forward, he won - the first G1 abroad by a local-based horse.\nAmong JRA horses, Delta Blues traveled to Australia. He was narrowly 3rd in the Caulfield Cup, then won the Melbourne Cup, called the greatest festival of Australian racing (Japan's Pop Rock was 2nd) - the first G1 in the distant Southern Hemisphere won by a Japan-trained horse.
126=* Father and Daughter Derby Winners\nThe Japanese Derby is open to both sexes, but with the Oaks run over the same distance, very few fillies try it. The last filly to win was Kurifuji in 1943. Vodka boldly took on the race everyone avoided.\nHer sire was Derby winner Tanino Gimlet. She was named "Vodka," stronger than a gimlet, and without the owner's usual prefix - a stylish touch.\nThe year before, she won the Hanshin JF in a Japanese 2yo record for 1600m on turf and was JRA champion 2yo filly, heading into the 2007 classics as the star. But in the Oka Sho she couldn't catch Daiwa Scarlet, whom she had beaten in the Tulip Sho prep.\nEveryone expected the Oaks, but she went to the Japanese Derby. Her camp longed "to win the Derby again with a child of Derby winner Tanino Gimlet," and Vodka was chosen. But no filly had run in the Derby in 11 years, since Biwa Heidi in 1996, who was a well-beaten 13th, and some called it "reckless." Still, she was 3rd choice. She waited at the back, then in the straight swept past leader Asakusa Kings before he could react and burst down the middle to win - the third filly ever to win the Derby, the first in 64 years since Kurifuji. Of course, it was also the first father-daughter Derby double. Her powerful finish at Tokyo convinced everyone she would be at the center of racing.\n\n* Japan Promoted to Part I\nRacing countries and regions are graded Part I to Part III by the International Cataloguing Standards Committee (ICSC), based on how open their races are to foreign-trained horses.\nSince founding the Japan Cup in 1981, Japan gradually opened up to foreign-trained horses, and this year it was promoted from Part II to Part I - a turning point in Japanese racing's worldwide recognition.
127=* A Three-Way Tenno Sho (Autumn)\nIn the 2008 Tenno Sho (Autumn), all 17 runners were graded winners, yet the betting centered on a Big Three.\nFavorite was Vodka. After her Derby win the year before she lost 7 straight (excluding a scratch), but won the Yasuda Kinen this year, her first win in a year. 2nd choice was her nemesis Daiwa Scarlet, who had beaten her in the previous Oka Sho and Shuka Sho - 7 wins and three 2nds from 10, never out of the top two. Next came 3yo colt Deep Sky, winner of this year's NHK Mile C.-Derby double.\nDaiwa Scarlet set a fast pace, with Vodka tracking in midfield. In the straight Deep Sky joined and the three broke clear, spread across the track. Vodka seemed to edge ahead, but Daiwa Scarlet fought back and the two hit the line together, with Deep Sky just behind in 3rd. The photo took a full 13 minutes; it looked like a dead heat. As the crowd buzzed, Vodka was declared the winner by a nose - just 2 cm. Many fans name this duel between two mares as the greatest race ever. Winning this epic, Vodka was named Horse of the Year.\n\n* Yukichan: World's First Graded Win by a White Horse\nOguri Cap and Tamamo Cross in the late 1980s disproved "greys don't win." But white horses were so rare and none had succeeded, so the belief that "white horses don't win" remained strong.\nThen came Yukichan (by Kurofune), a white filly out of white mare Shirayuki Hime. She stumbled on debut, but won the Mimosa Sho in her first 3yo race, the first turf win by a white horse. She aimed for the classics but gave up after a 7th in the Flora S., and targeted the dirt graded Kanto Oaks. Her entry made huge news; Kawasaki drew 127% of the previous year's attendance, and a special window was set up just for bets on her. She led from the start, set a slow pace, pulled away from the third turn and won by 8 lengths - the first graded win by a white horse, not just in Japan but in the world. Yukichan overturned "white horses are weak." But the white horse story wasn't over: later, Shirayuki Hime's family would produce even greater stars.
128=* "To the Top of the View" Buena Vista\nHer sire was Derby winner Special Week, who walked the royal road; her dam was G1 winner Biwa Heidi, and half-brothers Admire Japan and Admire Aura shone in the classics. Watched from before her debut, Buena Vista lived up to expectations, sweeping past the field in the straight to win the Hanshin JF and champion 2yo filly.\nIn her first race this year, the Tulip Sho, she drew a race-record 72.3% of win bets and won easily with a powerful kick. In the Oka Sho she was a clear favorite. Nearly last into the straight, over 10 lengths behind the leader, she surged wide to win. In the Oaks she again unleashed a devilish finish from the back, beating Red Desire, who had broken clear first, by a nose after a photo for the double.\nShe then announced her long-planned Arc challenge, but after finishing 2nd (to 4yo colt Yamanin Kingly) in the Sapporo Kinen, meant as a prep, she gave it up and aimed for the fillies' Triple Crown in the Shuka Sho. Unlike the first two classics, she broke well and sat on the inside in midfield, marking Red Desire. In the straight they dueled again and hit the line together. In another photo, this time Red Desire won by a nose. Not only did Buena Vista miss the Triple Crown, she was judged to have interfered and was demoted to 3rd.\nShe was 3rd in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup behind the runaway Queen Spumante and T.M. Precure, and narrowly 2nd in the Arima Kinen after a duel with Dream Journey, proving she could match older horses. Named champion 3yo filly, she carried great hopes as a future star of Japanese racing.\n\n* Vodka, the Greatest Mare Ever\nThe year before, Vodka won an epic duel with Daiwa Scarlet in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and was Horse of the Year. This spring she tried the Dubai Duty Free (G1) again after a 4th the year before, but was 7th.\nBack home her first race was the Victoria Mile. She had lost 3 straight since the Tenno Sho, but fans never doubted her, making her 1.7 favorite. She won by 7 lengths in record time for her 5th G1, and passed Air Groove to become the top-earning female ever.\nBut she wasn't done. In the Yasuda Kinen she waited in midfield. In the straight she tried to come up the inside but was walled in by a crowd of horses. Just when all seemed lost, she shot through a tiny gap, surged forward and caught double winner Deep Sky, who had broken clear first, before the line. Her powerful finish and grit in splitting the pack, worthy of any colt, stunned fans. With this win she became the first female to win the Yasuda Kinen twice, set a new record for G1 wins by a female with 6, and became the first female to earn 1 billion yen, among other records.\nIn autumn she narrowly lost the Mainichi Okan and the Tenno Sho (Autumn). The big one, the Japan Cup, was a race she longed to win after finishing 4th and 3rd before. She led early in the straight and broke fully clear at one point. Kikuka Sho winner Oken Bruce Lee charged at the end, but she held on by a nose - the first Japan-trained female to win the Japan Cup. Her 7th G1 tied the record held by Symboli Rudolf and others, and she was Horse of the Year for the second straight year.\n\n* Dream Journey Returns to Glory\nDream Journey was by Stay Gold out of a Mejiro McQueen mare. This cross, trained by master trainer Yasuo Ikee, later produced Orfevre and Gold Ship and was called the "golden cross" - and Dream Journey was its pioneer. He won the Asahi Hai FS at 2 and was seen as a future star, but went through the classics without a win, and G1 titles stayed out of reach.\nAt 5 this year he beat favorite Deep Sky in the Sankei Osaka Hai for his 5th graded win. He ran well for 3rd in the Tenno Sho (Spring), then went for his spring goal, the Takarazuka Kinen. Favorite was Deep Sky again, at 1.6; 2nd choice Dream Journey was far behind at 7.1 - it looked like Deep Sky's race. But in the straight he swept wide past Deep Sky and Sakura Mega Wonder, who were driving hard, to win his first G1 in about two and a half years.\nAfter a 2nd in the All Comers and 6th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), he went to the Arima Kinen. After those losses, favorite was double winner Buena Vista. Waiting nearly last, he swept forward, and in the straight fought a fierce duel with Buena Vista, who had broken clear from a good spot, catching her by half a length. He became only the 6th horse since graded races began to win both the spring and autumn Grand Prix. With his great partner Kenichi Ikezoe, "Mr. Grand Prix," he is a horse remembered in both records and memories.
