287=Bought in America and raced by Mr. Sekiguchi, owner of the "Fusaichi" horses. His price, $4 million, the highest of his crop, made headlines. He was 2nd on debut in December at 2, then won 5 straight at 3, taking the Kentucky Derby to top his generation. He was 2nd to Red Bullet in the Preakness S. and missed the Belmont S. after hurting a leg just before, but he gave an Asian owner a first Kentucky Derby win. He later won a G2 and retired after a 6th in the Breeders' Cup Classic. Sold to Coolmore for $70 million, he went to stud in America. His best offspring include Haskell Invitational H. winner Roman Ruler and Queen Anne S. winner Haradasun, but few others did much - not results worth $70 million. Still, Roman Ruler sired a G1 winner, so his blood lives on.
288=An American idol who thrilled fans by leading and pulling away in the straight. He won 6 G1s, including the Florida Derby and Metropolitan H., in dominant style. At 2 he won his first 4, including the Futurity S. (G1). At 3 he showed some immaturity, losing when pressed early and finishing 12th after a slow start in the Kentucky Derby, but after romping in the Metropolitan H. he was unstoppable. From summer he won 3 straight G1s - the Haskell Invitational H., the Travers S. and the Woodward S. - and was named Eclipse Horse of the Year ahead of Breeders' Cup Classic winner Concern. At 4 he won a listed race easily, but was pulled up injured in the Donn H. and retired. The Donn H. winner, Cigar, went on to win a record-equaling 16 straight. At stud he sired Kentucky Derby winner Giacomo and Breeders' Cup Juvenile winner Macho Uno, carrying on the rare Himyar line. He retired from stud in 2012 and died of old age in 2017.
289=Won 10 G1s in America from 1996 to 1998, including the Breeders' Cup Classic, earning $9.61 million, then second only to Cigar. At 3 in 1996 he won no classic, but then won 3 straight G1s against older horses, outdueling the veteran Cigar in the Jockey Club Gold C., and was named Eclipse champion 3yo colt over the classic winners. At 4 he won the Jockey Club Gold C. and Breeders' Cup Classic to be champion older male. At 5 he won 5 straight G1s, including the Hollywood Gold C., and was finally named Eclipse Horse of the Year. He then tried the Jockey Club Gold C. and Breeders' Cup Classic again but lost both, retiring just short of Cigar's $9.99 million earnings record. At stud he sired graded winners like Skipshot and Skipaslew, and from horses brought to Japan came Sonoda Kinpai winner Meiner Exon.
290=Dominant from his debut, he won the NHK Mile C. unbeaten in 5. He was beaten by Silence Suzuka in the Mainichi Okan, but then crushed Air Groove, Special Week and the foreign raiders in the Japan Cup. Reigning over Japanese racing after Silence Suzuka's death, he went to France the next year. Narrowly 2nd in the Prix d'Ispahan, he then won the Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud, his long-sought G1 abroad. In the Arc, the world's summit, he lost narrowly to Montjeu after a brave run, showing the world Japan's quality. Back home, fans begged for an Arima Kinen run, but he retired, leaving the "Big Three" battle with Special Week and Grass Wonder unsettled. He died young of colic in 2002, leaving just 2 crops, which still produced dirt champion Vermilion, Kikuka Sho winner Song of Wind and Japan Cup Dirt winner Alondite.
291=A speedster who raced around the world. At 2 he won graded races like the Hakodate Sansai S. and the Zen-Nihon Sansai Yushun, but proved his huge speed in his 11th start, the Kitakyushu Tankyori S.: through the first 600m in 32.3, he never slowed, closing in 34.2 for a Japanese record of 1:06.5. Perhaps that made him, as he won the Kokura Nikkei Open next and went to France for the Prix de l'Abbaye de Longchamp. Unfazed on his first trip abroad, he held off Imperial Beauty to win his first G1 overseas. At 5 he went to Britain, was 2nd in the G2 King's Stand S., then won the G1 July C. for a second G1 abroad. That year he also tried the BC Sprint in America; whatever the result (8th), his endless challenger's spirit deserves praise. At stud he sired dirt sprinter Agnes Jedi (6 JRA-local graded wins, including the Tokyo Hai) and Falcon S. winner Kazusa Line.
292=US double classic winner of 1997. Agonizingly 2nd in the Belmont S., he won fans with his fierce will to win close duels. Never out of the top two from his debut, he held off favorite Captain Bodgit by a head in the Kentucky Derby for his first G1, then won a three-way duel with Free House by a head in the Preakness S. But in the Belmont, as he dueled with Free House, Touch Gold passed them both and he was 2nd - no first Triple Crown in 19 years since Affirmed. At 4 he won a duel with Swain in the Dubai World Cup for his third G1. In autumn he met Swain again in the Breeders' Cup Classic and beat him, but Awesome Again won and he was 2nd. At 5 he lost 4 straight and retired. After 4 crops in America he was imported to Japan, but sired no notable runners.
293=By the speedy Sakura Yutaka O out of a full sister to Tenno Sho winner Amber Shadai, this blueblood was seen as a classic contender before his debut. He won his debut by 5 lengths, then after a 2nd in the Kurotake Sho, blasted 4 lengths clear in the Sakurasou Tokubetsu. But with 2 wins from 3, he was a shock 12th in the Spring S. His camp gave up on the classics and switched him to sprints. It was a perfect fit: he won the Crystal C. next, and running mostly at 1200-1600m, won 11 of 21, including two Sprinters S. At 1400m or less he won 11 of 12, losing only his 3yo Sprinters S. His blazing speed, treating even top miler North Flight and Tenno Sho winner Yamanin Zephyr like children, truly made him "the charging king" - rarely has a name fit so well. At stud, Shonan Kampf won the Takamatsunomiya Kinen for three straight generations of Japanese-bred G1 winners. He also sired sprint stars like 2-time G1 winner Grand Prix Boss, Takamatsunomiya Kinen winner Big Arthur, She Is Tosho and Dasher Go Go. Hopes rest on sires Big Arthur and Grand Prix Boss to carry on great sire Tesco Boy's now rare Princely Gift line. Fans also noted that Kitasan Black, despite a sprinter damsire, won middle-to-long G1s like the Kikuka Sho, Tenno Sho (Spring) and Arima Kinen.
294=Son of "Emperor" Symboli Rudolf and winner of the 1991 Satsuki Sho and Japanese Derby. Like his sire he entered the classics unbeaten and won both as favorite, seeming the Emperor reborn. But a fracture ended his Triple Crown dream. On his return, his Tenno Sho (Spring) clash with Mejiro McQueen, then the best horse, drew huge attention: Tokai Teio, back in style after winning the Sankei Osaka Hai, against McQueen, flawless after romping in the Hanshin Daishoten. Teio was favorite, but McQueen won easily. Yet that wasn't the end: in autumn he won the Japan Cup, the first Japanese winner in 7 years. Drained by the effort, he was 11th in the Arima Kinen, but after a year off he won the next year's Arima Kinen in a miracle comeback - a great horse who moved fans to the very end. At stud he sired G1 winners Tokai Point (Mile CS) and Yamanin Shukuru (Hanshin JF). Lacking a sire heir, his line seemed in danger, but Leben Steel, out of a Tokai Teio mare, won 3 graded races including the 2023 St. Lite Kinen - hopes rise for the "King's" blood.
295=Until summer at 3 he was winning and losing in low classes, but as he matured in autumn he won the Kikuka Sho in unstoppable form, then won the year-end Arima Kinen with surprise front-running. His first race as an older horse, the Hanshin Daishoten, is still legendary: he fought a fierce duel with year-older Triple Crown winner Narita Brian from the third turn to the line, losing by a head. After a 5th in the Tenno Sho (Spring) he won the Takarazuka Kinen, but in autumn ran poorly - 4th in the All Comers, 7th in the Arima Kinen - and some said he was finished. But he won the next Hanshin Daishoten easily, and in the Tenno Sho (Spring) "Big Three" clash with Sakura Laurel and Marvelous Sunday, unlike his usual forward style, he came with a miraculous late charge to win in world-record time for 3200m. Versatile, fast enough to set records and tough when it counted - a great horse. At stud he sired colorful runners like turf-and-dirt Precise Machine, dirt closer Meisho Tokon and tireless stayer Chakra.
296=An heir of Mr. Prospector and sire of Dubai Millennium. In Japan his offspring Seeking the Pearl and Meiner Love won G1s, proving his suitability to Japanese tracks. He raced well from a mile to middle distances, winning two G1s, the Dwyer S. and the Super Derby. But he often fell short on the big stage, narrowly 2nd in the Breeders' Cup Classic, Haskell Invitational H. and Travers S. After a 7th in the Kentucky Derby, he took the back roads. At stud, his first crop gave 8-time G1 winner Heavenly Prize and his next champion 2yo filly Flanders, making him a hot sire. He went on to produce stars worldwide, like 2000 Dubai World Cup winner Dubai Millennium and Japanese G1 winner Seeking the Pearl, and his fee peaked at $250,000. Dubai Millennium's son Dubawi then firmly established the sire line.
297=A sire who took America by storm from the late 1990s into the 2000s. Leading sire in North America in 1999 and 2000, he was leading 2yo sire a record 7 times, and his fee peaked at $500,000 (about 60 million yen). As a racehorse he showed promise at 2, winning the Young America S. and finishing 2nd in the Breeders' Cup Juvenile, but fractures kept him from racing much and he retired. At stud he showed his true worth: his first crop's November Snow won 2 G1s, and he produced G1 winners almost every year. He sired Tabasco Cat and Hennessy, later imported to Japan as sires, and Giant's Causeway, a hugely successful heir, and his reputation soared. He had no stars in Japan, but as a broodmare sire produced many G1 winners there, including Phalaenopsis, Lord Kanaloa, Kizuna, Loves Only You and Danon Kingly.
298=Won the 1991 Derby, Irish Derby and King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. by wide margins and was rated 139 lbs in the International Classification, the 1990s' highest. Little noticed early at 3, he was a plain 4th as 4th choice in the 2000 Guineas. But in the Derby he suddenly awoke, winning by 5 lengths from Marju, then outdueled Suave Dancer to win the Irish Derby by 3. Facing older horses for the first time in the King George as 1.7 favorite, he won by a race-record 7 lengths. He was a poor 8th in the Arc and retired, but his crushing power earned the 1990s' top rating in both the International Classification and Timeform. At stud he stood 4 years in Britain, then moved to Japan as Caerleon's heir. He sired horses like Erimo Harrier, 3-time Hakodate Kinen winner, but no G1 stars.
