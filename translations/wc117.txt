At 3 he was 3rd in the Shinzan Kinen, then won the Kisaragi Sho as 7th of 8, catching gritty Stone Ridge from 3rd. 7th in the Satsuki Sho and 12th in the Derby, he fractured after; injuries kept him out until June at 5. Two defeats, another break, and winless at 6, he retired.
2806=Winning her debut and the Hiiragi Sho, she won the Fairy S. at 3, leading from a fine start and pulling 2.5 lengths clear. 7th in the Tulip Sho, she was 9th choice in the Oka Sho but hung on to 3rd behind Daring Tact and Resistencia. That summer she won the Yonago S. (L) and was 2nd in the Keisei Hai AH - 2nd in the Summer Mile Series. After a 10th and 2nd, she won the Turquoise S. from up front, holding off Andrasta by a nose. At 4, 14th as favorite in the Lord Derby CT, then 15th and 10th - a sharp decline. All double-figure finishes as an older mare, she retired to breed after the Nakayama Himba S. at 5.
2807=A leading Kizuna colt. He won his debut charging from the back, then the Keisei Hai as 7th choice, closing sharply from the back again and sweeping past gritty favorite Sky Groove. 16th in the Satsuki Sho, a tendon injury meant a long break.
2808=2nd in the Shirayuri S. (L) at 3, outclassed in graded races (9th Kyoto Shimbun Hai, 17th St. Lite Kinen), he matured at 4 and won the Nikkei Shinshun Hai as 2nd choice, pulling clear from a handy spot. 7th in the Tenno Sho (Spring), he was 3rd to Chrono Genesis as 12th choice in the Takarazuka Kinen. At 5, 5th in the AJCC, he beat Triple Crown winner Contrail and sprint queen Gran Alegria for 2nd in the Osaka Hai, then 8th, 13th and 13th. Injured in January at 6, he missed a whole year; back at 7, 10th in the Nakayama Kinen, 4th in the Niigata Daishoten, he retired with a tendon injury after the Takarazuka Kinen.
2809=Winning the 3-win class late at 3, she was 2nd in the Silk Road S. at 4, then won the Keeneland C. on her 3rd sprint graded try, 5th choice behind Hakodate Sprint winner Diatonic and Keihan Hai winner Light On Cue, sweeping wide from last. In the Sprinters S. she finished far back for the first time, then 14th and 15th at 5. She was 2nd defending the Keeneland C. and won the Keihan Hai as 10th choice with a brilliant late run. Unplaced in 5 races at 6, she retired to breed after the Silk Road S. at 7.
2810=A late bloomer from the end of 5, he won the 2022 Takamatsunomiya Kinen at 6 - a rarity among sprinters. (L) in autumn at 5, then won the Tanzanite S. (OP). At 6, 3rd in the Silk Road S. and 2nd in the Ocean S., he won the Takamatsunomiya Kinen as 8th choice, charging from last and edging clear of a 5-horse blanket finish by a neck. 3rd to Gendarme in the Sprinters S., 10th in the Hong Kong Sprint. At 7, 4th in the Takamatsunomiya Kinen, 17th in the Yasuda Kinen and 9th in the Sprinters S., he retired that year.
2811=A late-blooming stayer racing at 2000m+ from his debut. Winning 3 straight in spring at 3, 14th in the St. Lite Kinen, he won the Geishun S. (3-win), then became a long-distance graded regular: 3rd Diamond S., 6th Meguro Kinen. Late at 4 he won the Stayers S. as 7th choice, winning a duel with Tagano Diamante from up front. 9th in the Arima Kinen against the best. At 5 (6th, 13th, 6th), he was withdrawn from the Stayers S. with gait trouble, never returned, and retired to stud at the end of 7.
2812=6th on debut, he won 3 straight incl. the Yayoi Sho Deep Impact Kinen - a classic focus, one of a Big Three with Contrail and Salios in the Satsuki Sho, but faded from midfield to 5th, then 11th as 4th choice in the Derby. 2nd in the St. Lite Kinen and 3rd to Contrail in the Kikuka Sho. Older, 11th AJCC, 7th Kinko Sho, he closed sharply to lose by a neck to Zadar in the Epsom C., then was injured. Back after 1 year 7 months in the Shirafuji S. (L) at 6, he broke down and was pulled up; he went to stud in Argentina.

2817=Placing 2nd, 2nd and 4th on turf, he switched to dirt and won a maiden and 5-million race at once, reaching open class with the Kunpu S. in spring at 4. 3rd as 8th choice in the March S. at 5, he won the Radio Nippon Sho (OP), then the March S. at 6, taking over in the straight from a handy spot. Little else but a 5th in the Elm S.; he retired after the Negishi S. at 7.
2818=The 6th fillies' Triple Crown winner and the only unbeaten one, from new sire Epiphaneia's first crop - a big title at once. She first showed her talent in her 2nd start, the Elfin S. (L): slow away, she alone found an otherworldly kick and won by 4 lengths. Straight into the Oka Sho on rain-soaked ground, she charged from the back past 2yo queen Resistencia and the rest. Oaks favorite at 1.6, she was still near the back at the 4th turn and passed them all in the straight. Straight into the Shuka Sho at 1.4, she moved early and won with ease - an unbeaten Triple Crown in just 5 starts. In the Japan Cup against unbeaten colt Triple Crown winner Contrail and 8-G1 Almond Eye, she was 3rd in a race of the century. At 4, at short odds in the Kinko Sho, she lost by a neck to longshot Gibeon, then was 3rd behind Loves Only You and Glory Vase in Hong Kong's QEII Cup; in May a suspensory injury sidelined her. After a year off she was 6th in the Victoria Mile, 3rd to Titleholder in the Takarazuka Kinen and 4th to Vela Azul in the Japan Cup. Leg trouble led to her retirement in autumn at 6.
2819=2nd in the Arlington C. and 3rd in the NHK Mile C. at 3, then 2nd in the Kyoto Himba S. and 3rd in the Turquoise S. at 4 without winning, she switched to dirt in summer at 5: she won the NST Sho (OP), 2nd in the Green Channel C. (L), then the Musashino S. with a powerful late run - a graded win on her new surface. 2nd to Lemon Pop in the Negishi S. at 6, a February S. hope, she was injured and retired to breed.
2820=She made her name in the 2020 Oaks, half a length behind later Triple Crown filly Daring Tact. Little noticed, she won the Flora S. by a whisker as 4th choice, then as 7th choice in the Oaks tracked in 2nd and held on almost to the line - a fine ride by Norihiro Yokoyama, standing in for his son Takeshi. 15th in the Shuka Sho, she was 4th as 9th choice in the QEII Cup, best of the 3yos. At 4 she won the Nikkei Sho, 5th in the Tenno Sho (Spring), and the All Comers - a force against males at middle-long trips - but 16th as 3rd choice in the QEII Cup. At 5, after a 16th and 7th, she was 3rd in the Sapporo Kinen and 2nd to Geraldina in her 3rd QEII Cup, then won the Hong Kong Vase - the only winner among 13 Japanese runners, her G1 at last, abroad. At 6, unplaced in Dubai, the Sapporo Kinen and All Comers, she held 4th in the BC Filly & Mare Turf, then retired after a 7th in the Arima Kinen to breed.
