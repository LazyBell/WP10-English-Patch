359=Talented but forever falling short: by the Tenno Sho (Spring) in April at 6 he had run 37 times without even an open win, yet was 2nd 7 times and 3rd 7 times in graded races, including four 2nds and two 3rds in G1s. "The best horse never to win a graded race" fit him, and trainer Ikee naturally longed to get him one. In the Meguro Kinen, with Yutaka Take replacing regular jockey Shigefumi Kumazawa, he finally won a graded race in his 38th start. At 7 he won the Nikkei Shinshun Hai, then the Dubai Sheema Classic, beating future BC Turf winner Fantastic Light. In his farewell, the Hong Kong Vase, he won again - his long-sought G1, abroad. "The best never to win a graded race" retired as "a G1 winner unbeaten abroad." At stud he sired brothers Dream Journey and Orfevre, 6-time G1 eccentric Gold Ship, Arc runner-up Nakayama Festa, dirt star Silk Mobius and Hanshin JF winner Red Riviere, establishing himself as a key Sunday Silence heir before dying in 2015.
360=Unbeaten in 8 at local Oi, including the Tokyo Okan Sho, he joined the JRA after winning the Tokyo Daishoten at the end of his 3yo year. He lost his first 2 races, but in the Tenno Sho (Spring), as 4th choice, he beat Mr. Cyclennon by 5 lengths in record time. He then brushed aside rival Yaeno Muteki in the Takarazuka Kinen. In autumn he narrowly lost the Mainichi Okan to Oguri Cap, then ran poorly: 6th in the Tenno Sho and 11th in the Japan Cup. In the Arima Kinen, billed as Oguri Cap vs Super Creek, just as Super Creek caught the tiring Oguri Cap, Inari One swooped wide with a devilish kick to win. He won only 3 races in the JRA, but all were G1s - and fittingly, he was never favorite.
361=At 2 he kept falling short, and the Asahi Hai FS was his 9th start. But as if his narrow losses had never happened, he won in a record 1:33.5, 0.1 seconds faster than Grass Wonder. At 3 he won the Yayoi Sho and ran a good 3rd in the Satsuki Sho. But after a 10th in the Derby, at a distance that didn't suit him, he lost his rhythm, ran poorly and left the JRA without another win. Moved to local Oi, he won the Oi Kinen and was 6th in the JBC Classic, retiring after a 4th in the '07 Sumidagawa Open.
362="The demon of Fuchu," with outstanding aptitude even among Tony Bin's many Tokyo specialists. He was perfect at Tokyo, 3 for 3 including 2 G1s, the Japanese Derby and the Japan Cup. His Japan Cup at 3 was stunning: he ran down reigning champion T.M. Opera O in the straight and caught him right before the line, with a dignity rare in a 3yo. Peslier's ride was superb, but the horse himself seemed to say "no one beats me at Tokyo." Hopes for the first back-to-back Japan Cup were high the next year, but with the race moved to Nakayama, he was 5th. He retired at the end of his 4yo year to stud. As if answering hopes as heir to the late Tony Bin, he sired Kikuka Sho winner Oken Bruce Lee, Tenno Sho winners Jaguar Mail and Tosen Jordan, full sisters Tall Poppy (Oaks) and Aventura (Shuka Sho), and abroad NZ Oaks winner Jungle Rocket.
363=After 2 G1 wins in Italy he raced worldwide, winning G1s in Britain, France, Japan and Hong Kong, and is known for winning the 2002 Japan Cup. Just a graded contender until 3, at 4 he won the G1 Premio Presidente della Repubblica and Gran Premio di Milano. Spreading his wings, he went to the Japan Cup in autumn and beat Symboli Kris S. and others. Shadai then bought half of him, and he was set to stand in Japan after racing. At 5 he moved to Britain, winning the Prix d'Ispahan, Eclipse S., International S. and Queen Elizabeth II S., then was a close 3rd in the Breeders' Cup Turf. He ended his career with the Hong Kong C., his 8th G1. At stud in Japan he sired no superstar, but steady graded winners like Hakodate Kinen winner Transwarp and Fillies' Revue winner One Carat, and as a damsire produced Harp Star and Stelvio.
364=A born sprinter, all his wins at 1400m or less. His first 3 starts on dirt came to nothing, but switched to turf his speed blossomed and he won twice. Wins then dried up, but he reached open class at the end of his 5yo year, and at 6, as 16th choice, last in the betting, he beat Agnes World and others in the Sprinters S. - his first graded win a G1. He also won the Swan S. and Hankyu Hai. A late-blooming top sprinter.
365=With natural speed he set three records, including a Japanese one. At 3 he missed the spring classics, but won his first graded race, the Chunichi Sports Sho Yonsai S., in record time for Kyoto's 1800m. He ran in the Kikuka Sho in autumn but suffered atrial fibrillation mid-race and finished last. The next year, in his prime, he won the Osaka Hai and then the Keihan Hai in record time. He was 5th to Biwa Hayahide in the Takarazuka Kinen, but set a Japanese record for 1800m on turf in the Mainichi Okan in autumn and won his long-sought Tenno Sho (Autumn), cementing his place at middle distances, where his speed was remarkable.
366=He began on turf, but in spring at 3 won a 5-million race and an open special on dirt, first showing his talent. He then ran a close 5th in the Derby, his last turf race before switching to dirt. He won the Japan Dirt Derby and Derby Grand Prix back to back for an early G1 - surprisingly, the first dirt G1 by a Sunday Silence son. 5th in the Japan Cup Dirt, he bounced back to win the Tokyo Daishoten and February S. - 4 G1s in about six months. A Dubai trip was planned, but worsening roaring ended his career early. At stud until his sudden death in 2017, he sired dirt kings Espoir City, Smart Falcon and Copano Rickey, and in 2019 Chrysoberyl won the Champions C. unbeaten in 6, proving his blood's quality again.
367=A top sprinter who emerged as an older horse. He struggled at 3 and 4, but from autumn at 4 began winning, took his first graded race, the CBC Sho, then held on by a neck in the Silk Road S., and won the Takamatsunomiya Kinen with an overwhelming late run to become sprint king. After a 14th in the Yasuda Kinen he won the Sprinters S. in autumn in a record 1:07.0, a spring-autumn sprint G1 double. He never won again and retired at the end of his 6yo year.
368=It took 3 starts to break his maiden, but the next year he won the Kyodo News Hai Yonsai S. and went to the NHK Mile C. Using Tokyo's long straight, he came from the back to beat Toyo de Hair, who used the same tactics, by a nose - a G1 winner. He then slumped, and bold moves like a Dubai trip didn't help; he went over 2 years without a win. But at 5 he won the Tanabata Sho, his first win since the NHK Mile C., and in the Japan Cup Dirt, with a fine ride by Dettori, he shot up the rail in the straight to beat the world's best for another G1. It was his last win, but shedding the "early bloomer" label surely meant a lot to his camp.
369=A colorful character who ran front-running with his head high, mouth open and head tossing, racing steadily from 2 to 5. After a heavy loss in the Spring S. he switched to sprints, and it paid off: he won the Crystal C., his first graded race. The second half of his 3yo year was dull, but the next year he won the Milers C. and came into his own, 2nd in the Yasuda Kinen, winner of the Takamatsunomiya Hai (G2, 2000m), 2nd in the Mainichi Okan, then won his first G1, the Mile CS. Hungry in his prime, he ran a fine 5th in the Arima Kinen. The next year he won the Mainichi Okan and Mile CS in record times and had great duels with Daiichi Ruby. At stud he sired '00 Sprinters S. winner Daitaku Yamato.
370=One of Brian's Time's best. His record falls short of stars like Narita Brian and Mayano Top Gun, but in pure ability he was their equal - true super-G1 class. He narrowly lost his debut over 1000m on dirt, then won easily next time, won the Shinzan Kinen, and with the Arlington C. and Spring S. won 4 straight, becoming the top classic contender. In the Satsuki Sho he had the field's fastest finish but a poor position, finishing 3rd, and was 3rd again in the NHK Mile C. In the Derby, his revenge match, he finally won his G1, topping his crop. But a tendon injury before autumn ended his career before showing his true worth, and he went to stud. His first crop included Vodka, a filly who won the Derby - the first father-daughter Derby winners ever. He also sired Aoba Sho winner Hiraboku Royal and Derby runner-up Smile Jack. As Brian's Time's heir, a star colt is hoped for.
371=10 wins from 12 at local Kasamatsu, he joined the JRA with overwhelming power and won 6 straight graded races from the Pegasus S. to the Mainichi Okan. Then in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) he met Tamamo Cross, the best horse in training, and suffered his first defeat. He trailed him again in the Japan Cup, but avenged himself in Tamamo Cross's farewell Arima Kinen. The next year he started in autumn, winning the All Comers and Mainichi Okan, then narrowly lost the Tenno Sho (Autumn) to Super Creek. Then came the stunning part: the Mile Championship and Japan Cup on back-to-back weekends, defying convention. He won the Mile Championship with a furious finish, then dueled foreign mare Horlicks in the Japan Cup, losing by a neck in a then world record 2:22.2. At 5 he won the Yasuda Kinen in spring, but in autumn slumped - 6th in the Tenno Sho, 11th in the Japan Cup. "Oguri is finished," people said, and he was 4th choice for his farewell Arima Kinen. But first past the post was Oguri Cap. Fans chanted for his miraculous win. With that Arima Kinen, racing became not "gambling" but "drama." The "Oguri Cap story" that thrilled Japan had the happiest possible ending.
372=He debuted in January at 3. As a son of great sire Nureyev he was highly rated, and at 4 he won 3 straight, including his first graded race, the Lord Derby Challenge T. But after just 1 win in the next 18 months, jockey Norihiro Yokoyama suggested "1200m would suit him better." In his first 1200m race, the Sprinters S., he beat Agnes World by a neck for his long-sought G1. But his reign atop the sprint world was brief: he kept falling short, 2nd 5 times in graded races, earning the unwanted nickname "silver collector." At 7 he finally won again in the Yasuda Kinen - as 9th choice, with 15th-choice Break Time 2nd, a wild 120,000-yen quinella. At stud he sired Keeneland C. winner Couverture and Kyoto Himba S. winner Celebrita.
373=A king of the sand who won dirt G1s in spring and autumn. At 3 he was crushed in his first turf race, the Principal S., and from then on raced only on dirt. He won the Shobu S. with a furious charge from 13th at the final turn, then the Nagoya Yushun for his first graded win. He kept rolling, winning 5 straight including 4 graded races - the Grand Chariot C., Unicorn S. and Super Dirt Derby - and became the undisputed dirt king. As an older horse he often lost narrowly, but at 5 won the February S. for a G1, then crushed the foreign raiders by three and a half lengths in the new Japan Cup Dirt - the first to win two JRA dirt G1s in one year. The next year he was 2nd in both races.
374=A great supporting player who fought many epic battles with rivals like Mihono Bourbon and Rice Shower, always livening up G1s. Racing until the end of his 6yo year, he won 8 of 32, including 4 graded races like the American JCC and Meguro Kinen, but in G1s always fell just short, his best a 3rd in the Kikuka Sho. Perhaps that soft finish won sympathy - many fans loved him to the end.
375=Gifted, but lacking a sharp turn of foot, he kept falling short, and tendon injuries forced many breaks. Late in his career, as his leg troubles eased, his running changed, and at 7 he showed remarkable form, winning 2 graded races in a row. In the Tenno Sho (Autumn), amid the drama of front-runner Silence Suzuka's breakdown, he broke clear of the pack and won a duel with Stay Gold.
376=By great sire Sadler's Wells out of Arc winner Urban Sea - a true blueblood, top-class on the track and at stud. Unbeaten in 4, he won the Derby, then romped in the Irish Derby, and brushed aside older horses in the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S., Europe's best by summer at 3. In autumn he tried the Irish Champion S. at middle distance and the Breeders' Cup Classic on dirt, losing both, but retired as Cartier champion 3yo colt. At stud his first crop gave 4 G1 winners including Red Rocks, followed by New Approach, Frankel and more, and he was champion sire in Britain and Ireland 11 years running from 2010 - the most powerful branch of the Sadler's Wells line. His grandchildren include 11-time G1 winner Enable and Derby winner Masar, and in Japan Frankel's Soul Stirring and Mozu Ascot. He died in 2021 at the height of his success, but in 2024 Content's Yorkshire Oaks win made him the sire of 100 G1 winners. With heirs like Frankel, his blood's march goes on.
