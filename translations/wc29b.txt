280=He won the Asahi Hai Sansai S., then the Arlington C. and the NZT Yonsai S. in spring at 3. Hailed as the best miler, he missed the NHK Mile C. through injury. After his break he ran poorly and was written off, but at 4 he won the Yonago S., his first win in a long time, winning the Kitakyushu Kinen and the Mainichi Okan. At year's end he went to Hong Kong and won the Hong Kong Mile, then returned the next April to win the QE2 Cup - and won it again the following year, G1 wins abroad 3 years running. Oddly, he lost all 8 of his older-horse G1s in Japan. At stud, no standout offspring yet.
281=The horse who rewrote Japanese thoroughbred bloodlines from the ground up. He produced many heirs such as Special Week and Deep Impact, nearly turning Japan's bloodlines all Sunday Silence. He was also a superb racehorse, winning the Kentucky Derby and Preakness S. He lost the Belmont S. to rival Easy Goer, but won the Breeders' Cup Classic in autumn and was Eclipse Horse of the Year. After retiring at 4 he was little valued for his pedigree in America, so he was exported to Japan for stud. His first crop gave Fuji Kiseki and Genuine, the next Bubble Gum Fellow and Dance in the Dark - a flood of G1 winners. He kept siring G1 winners every year until his last, sparking a "Sunday Silence storm" that swept the JRA's G1s. His sire sons - Fuji Kiseki, Dance in the Dark, Stay Gold, Special Week, Agnes Tachyon, Heart's Cry, Deep Impact and more - are an elite group, and successful grandchildren and great-grandchildren are countless. His descendants have also spread abroad, and his influence is now worldwide.
283=A leading light of Sunday Silence's first crop, the force that swept Japanese racing. Fickle ("won't run if it's windy"), he was loved as a charming character. Early on, his moods cost him races. In the Wakaba S. he crossed the line 2nd, 5 lengths back, winning only after first-past-the-post Louisiana Boy was demoted, so he was only 3rd choice for the Satsuki Sho - but he held off the charging Tayasu Tsuyoshi, another Sunday Silence son, to win clearly. His explosive power at middle distances and shorter was real: besides the Satsuki Sho, he won the Mile Championship as an older horse. He stood at stud in Japan and Oceania, siring graded winners in the JRA and G1 winners in Oceania.
286=So strong some complained "Taiki Shuttle's races are boring." He debuted late, in spring at 3, then won 3 straight. He was beaten a neck into 2nd in the Bodaiju S., but won the Unicorn S. for his first graded title. His run continued, winning the Mile Championship and the Sprinters S. at 3. Named champion sprinter, he made it clear no one in Japan could match him at short distances. As an older horse he kept rolling, winning the Yasuda Kinen for 3 straight sprint-mile G1s. Then he set off for Europe. With Seeking the Pearl having already won a G1 abroad that year, he was a confident favorite for the Prix Jacques le Marois, and beat the world's fast horses clearly to become the world's mile king in name and fact. Back home he won the Mile Championship again. His farewell, the Sprinters S., ended in a shock 3rd, but he was the undisputed Horse of the Year. At stud he sired G1 winners like Meisho Bowler, Summer Wind and Win Kluger. He retired from stud in 2017 and died of heart failure due to old age in 2022. His stall showed no sign of struggle; a peaceful end.
