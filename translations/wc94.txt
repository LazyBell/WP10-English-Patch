2280=The 1965 Derby winner. Fatally injured in the Hanshin Daishoten in autumn at 6 (old age system), he is remembered for staying beside his fallen jockey Yamamoto. At 3 he won his debut by 10 lengths and 5 straight, then the Yayoi Sho at 4, a classic hope. 2nd to rival Daikoter in the Spring S. and 14th in the Satsuki Sho, he made amends in the Derby: 2nd choice behind Satsuki winner Daikoter, he led all the way and held him off. Daikoter turned the tables in the Kikuka Sho, 2nd. With 7 wins from 10, mostly around a mile, he was champion sprinter. At 5 he won the Kimpai (West), was 5th in the Tenno Sho (Spring) and rested. Back at 6 he won 4 straight, then fell and broke a leg in the Hanshin Daishoten.
2281=Winner of the 1963 Satsuki Sho and Derby. 6th in the Kikuka Sho but 2nd in the Arima Kinen, Horse of the Year. His farm wanted a late developer, so he trained through 3 (old age system) and debuted at 4, winning 3 easily, a classic fancy. Against stablemate and 3yo champion Great Yoruka, he won the Satsuki Sho, then the Derby by 7 lengths over him in record time. A Triple Crown seemed certain; in the Kikuka Sho he was backed at an astonishing 83.2% of win bets, but swept along by the pace he raced 30 lengths clear, then tired to 6th. Top of the fan vote, he was 2nd to Ryu Forel in the Arima Kinen, sharing Horse of the Year with him. At 5, aiming at the Tenno Sho, he was 2nd (Spring) and 8th (Autumn), 3rd in the Arima Kinen, and retired at 6. A home-bred hope at stud, he sired no stars.
2282=Blooming in autumn at 4 (old age system), he won the 1963 Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Arima Kinen at 5 - the first Kansai horse to win the Arima Kinen, in its 8th year. Below top level in spring at 4 (Satsuki Sho 8th, NHK Hai 7th, Derby 8th), he improved after summer: Kobe Hai (now Kobe Shimbun Hai) 1st, Kyoto Hai (now Kyoto Shimbun Hai) 2nd, and a neck 2nd to Hiro Kimi in the Kikuka Sho as 8th of 23. At 5 he was 2nd in the Tenno Sho (Spring), won the Takarazuka Kinen, and as favorite won a fierce duel with Hikaru Polar in the Tenno Sho (Autumn). He then beat younger dual classic winner Meizui in the Arima Kinen, sharing Horse of the Year with him. At 6, with no races at fair weights, he went to the Washington DC International, but tired from the trip finished last of 8 and retired. Little success at stud.
2283=The 1972 Satsuki Sho winner, in a great crop with Long Ace, Ishino Hikaru and Tai Teem. He beat all 3 in the Satsuki Sho, was a narrow 2nd to Long Ace in the Derby and 4th behind Ishino Hikaru and Tai Teem in the Kikuka Sho. Older he faded: 5th and 13th behind Tai Teem in the Milers C. and Tenno Sho (Spring). Unlike Long Ace and Tai Teem, he sired no notable foals.
2284=Winner of the 1972 Kikuka Sho and Arima Kinen, a classic star with Long Ace, Tai Teem and Land Prince. Winning late at 3 (old age system), he won 4 straight into spring at 4. The "Kansai Big Three" were the focus; he was the Kanto ace. 2nd to Land Prince in the Satsuki Sho and 6th to Long Ace in the Derby, he couldn't break the Kansai hold in spring. In autumn, after an open win, he ran again a week later and swept past to win the Kikuka Sho. Favorite over older stars like Mejiro Asama and Mejiro Musashi in the Arima Kinen, he won from midfield - the first 4yo colt (old age system) to win it. Worn out, he rested over 10 months, then was last of 7 on his return and retired. Few foals, no stars.
2285=Blooming when older, he won the 1966 Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Arima Kinen at 5 (old age system). Half-brother of Tenno Sho (Spring) winner Korehisa. Debuting at 4, he won lesser races but flopped in graded ones (Yayoi Sho 7th, Spring S. 10th), and was 17th in the Derby and 8th in the Kikuka Sho. At 5 he won the Tokyo Shimbun Hai and Argentina JCC, then the Tenno Sho (Autumn). 2nd choice in the Arima Kinen, he held off Kabuto Shiro and Speed Symboli - Horse of the Year. At 6, 2nd in the Argentina JCC, he won the Diamond S. for a 5th graded win, then retired injured. No notable foals.
2286=A sprinter who won the 2001 Falcon S. Winning a maiden and 5-million race, she struggled in open class at 2. Back in May at 3, she was 3rd as 15th choice in the Aoi S., then beat favorites like Carston Light O by half a length in the Falcon S. Perhaps her peak; little after, retiring at 5.
2287=Winner of the 2000 Tulip Sho. With 3 wins in Hokkaido at 2, she moved to JRA races at 3. 12th in the Elfin S., she led all the way on heavy ground to win the Tulip Sho as a longshot. Overlooked in the classics, beaten badly in the Oka Sho and Oaks, she raced to 6: 5 wins from 41.
2288=Winner of the 2014 Kyoto Kimpai. Debuting at 3 and reaching open class mid-4, he won the Kyoto Kimpai in January at 5, closing from the back with favorite Osumi Nine and winning by 1.5 lengths. He ran well in mile graded races after.
2289=Winner of the 2014 Chunichi Shimbun Hai and Niigata Kinen. A late bloomer in open class at 5, he loved flat, tight local tracks (3 of 5 wins at Kokura). In his first graded race, the Chunichi Shimbun Hai, he upset as 10th choice, then was 2nd in the Kokura Kinen and won the Niigata Kinen. In the Tenno Sho (Autumn) Tokyo's hill beat him, 13th.
2290=Switched to dirt at 6, he won the 2014 Negishi S. On turf he was 3rd in the Mile CS in his first open race at 3 and 3rd in the Tokyo Shimbun Hai at 4, then slumped. The switch worked: 2 open wins, then the Negishi S., and 4th in Funabashi's Kashiwa Kinen that spring.
2291=A rare graded winner on the flat and over jumps. Struggling on turf, he soared on dirt, reaching open class late at 3; at 4 he won the Pollux S. (OP) and the March S. 2nd in the Heian S. and winner of the Marine S. (OP) at 5, he switched to jumps in autumn at 6, won his jump debut, then the Kokura Summer Jump as 6th choice. Injured in the Hanshin Jump S., he retired.
2292=A late bloomer who won the 2015 and 2016 Victoria Mile - 3 G1s in all. Peaking late at 4, she won the Silk Road S. at 5 in 2014, then was 3rd in the Takamatsunomiya Kinen and Victoria Mile, a rising sprinter. 2nd as 2nd choice in the Sprinters S., she was 3rd in the Hong Kong Sprint. At 6, 13th in the Takamatsunomiya Kinen, she won the Victoria Mile, then the Sprinters S. as favorite. At 7 she beat Mikki Queen, Shonan Pandora and others in the Victoria Mile for a 3rd G1, and retired.
2293=A sprinter at 1200-1800m who won the Ocean S. and Chukyo Kinen. Improving from summer at 3, he won 4 straight classes; in his first open race at 4, the G3 Ocean S., he won from a handy spot as 3rd choice. After poor runs, he won the Chukyo Kinen at 5 by a neck in a 7-horse blanket finish. Winless after, briefly over jumps, he raced until summer at 7, then moved to Macau.
2294=Winner of the 2014 CBC Sho. A miler at first, she soared once switched to sprints mid-3, winning through the classes and taking the CBC Sho at 4 from midfield with the fastest finish. Prized for speed on flat tracks, she was a hope in the Sprinters S. at Niigata but was 14th.
2295=A late-blooming sprinter who won the Ibis Summer Dash at 7. Placing in graded races in open class at 4, he had a tendon injury and over 2 years off. Back at 6, he never gave up: winning at 7, he won the Ibis Summer Dash as favorite that summer. At 8 he was 3rd as 12th choice in the Silk Road S. with the fastest finish.
2296=Blooming at 5 in 2014, he won the Kitakyushu Kinen and Centaur S. Top five in 3 of 4 graded tries, he won the Kitakyushu Kinen by a nose, then the Centaur S. easily. A Sprinters S. fancy, he was scratched with leg trouble; back late in the year, he was 14th in the Hong Kong Sprint.
2297=2nd in the 2014 Takamatsunomiya Kinen and winner of the Sprinters S. Mainly a dirt runner, 2nd in the Capella S., he tried turf graded races at 6 in 2014: 2nd in the Ocean S. and Takamatsunomiya Kinen. Still overlooked, 13th choice in the Sprinters S., he won it, proving himself. After an 8th in the Hong Kong Sprint, leg trouble meant a long break. Back after over a year at 8, he never won again but was 3rd in the Ocean S. and 3rd in the Hokkaido Sprint C. 3 years running. Racing until 11, he retired after a 17th in the Takamatsunomiya Kinen.
2298=Winner of the 2014 Fukushima Himba S. A late bloomer in open class at 5, she was 2nd as a longshot in the Nakayama Himba S., then won the Fukushima Himba S. as 5th choice, leading throughout and just holding on. 6th in the Victoria Mile, at 6 she led all the way in the Kyoto Himba S. and stunned with 2nd from the front in the Victoria Mile.
2299=Winner of 2 dirt graded races in 2014. In open class late at 3, at 4 he won the Heian S. as a longshot, was 2nd in the Elm S., then won the Sirius S. as favorite. He led boldly but was 8th in the Champions C. At 5 he won the Antares S., a steady middle-distance dirt force. At 6 he ran in Korea's G1 Korea Cup, 2nd to fellow Japanese Chrysolite - a Japanese 1-2.
2300=Winner of the 2014 Meguro Kinen. Long stuck in allowances, he reached open class at 6 and won his first graded race, the Meguro Kinen, closing from the back in a 4-way finish. A true stayer, racing mostly at 2400m+.
2301=He won the Epsom C. at 5. Improving mid-4, he won race after race to open class that autumn, placing in graded races (4th Fukushima Kinen, 3rd Nakayama Kimpai). 2nd choice in the Epsom C., he won from midfield. At 6 he won the Chunichi Shimbun Hai and Sapporo Kinen, 2nd in the Mainichi Okan and Kinko Sho, and at 7 the AJCC for a 4th graded win.
2302=A sharp closer who won the Mermaid S., Fuchu Himba S. and Aichi Hai. Blooming at 4 in 2014, she won the Mermaid S. easily as favorite, then beat Smart Layer, Whale Capture and others in the Fuchu Himba S. 3rd in the QEII Cup, she won the Aichi Hai as favorite.
2303=A middle-to-long trip horse, 2nd in the 2014 Meguro Kinen and winner of the Hakodate Kinen. A maiden until the end of 3, he won race after race at 4 and was 4th in Orfevre's farewell Arima Kinen. At 5 he was 2nd as a longshot in the Meguro Kinen and won the Hakodate Kinen, then 4th in the Sapporo Kinen and Tenno Sho (Autumn).
2304=A mare who bloomed at 5 and won the Champions C., by Tenno Sho (Spring) winner Suzuka Mambo out of 2002 Fairy S. winner White Carnival - turf parents, yet she was a dirt horse. 3rd in her first graded race, the Empress Hai, she won the Breeders' Gold C. by passing hot favorite Wild Flapper, then the JBC Ladies' Classic, her first G1. At 6 she won the TCK Jo-o Hai, Marine C. and Ladies' Prelude, then the Champions C.: 12th choice without form against males, she broke clear in the straight to beat Copano Ricky, Hokko Tarumae and others - dirt champion. She retired after it.
2305=Winner of the 2002 Fairy S. Noticed early, she ran 6 times at 2 incl. the Hanshin JF, then won the Fairy S. at 3 and was 3rd in the Queen C. 11th in the Oka Sho, she never won again and retired at 4. She produced Sambista, the first mare to win the Champions C.
2306=Winner of the 2014 Queen S. 2nd in the Aichi Hai late at 4, at 5 she was 2nd in the Nakayama Himba S. and Fukushima Himba S. - 3 straight 2nds - then 5th to Verxina in the Victoria Mile as 13th choice. She won the Queen S. in a 3-way finish, was 5th in the QEII Cup and 2nd again in the Aichi Hai.
2307=A 2014 classic fancy after winning the Spring S., holding off 2yo champion Asia Express by 1 1/4 lengths. 10th in the Satsuki Sho, he went for the NHK Mile C. and, despite a slow start, was 4th. Around a mile he kept falling short, then won the Auro C. (OP) in November at 4, his first win in 1.5 years, and the Hanshin C. At 5, 3rd in the Keio Hai SC, then little.
2308=A 2014 classic runner. After many graded tries, he won the Mainichi Hai under Daichi Shibata. Skipping the Satsuki Sho for the Derby, at 12th choice he closed from midfield for 3rd to One and Only. Rarely beaten badly, he won the Fukushima Minpo Hai (OP) in spring at 4, then went abroad, 4th in the Singapore Airlines International C.
2309=Winner of the 2014 Falcon S. After a 3rd in the Shinzan Kinen and 2nd in the Arlington C., he won the Falcon S. as favorite, charging from the back by a neck. 4th in the Derby, 3rd in the St. Lite Kinen and 4th in the Kikuka Sho, he made news by going from the 3000m Kikuka Sho to the 1600m Mile CS (10th).
2310=Winner of the 2014 Aoba Sho. Stuck in the 5-million class, he stepped up as 10th choice and charged from the back to win. 6th in the Derby closing the same way, 8th in the St. Lite Kinen, he closed sharply for 5th to Toho Jackal in the Kikuka Sho.
2311=Winner of the 2014 Kyoto Shimbun Hai. Struggling in allowances, he won a 5-million race only the week after the Satsuki Sho, then rushed into the Kyoto Shimbun Hai 2 weeks later for a Derby ticket and swept wide from the back to win. Overlooked in the Derby, he was 13th.
2312=Winner of the 2014 Radio Nikkei Sho. A classic hope but keen, he flopped in the Keisei Hai and Spring S., and lacked stamina in the Aoba Sho. In the Radio Nikkei Sho, the "consolation Derby," he held off favorite Clarity City, also out of the classics, for a graded win.
