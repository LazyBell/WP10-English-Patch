1640=Debuting in Hokkaido racing, he won 7 of 17 and moved to the JRA, where he struggled at first. 1644=Improving from autumn at 4, he won the Kabutoyama Kinen and Fukushima Kinen and was 6th in the Arima Kinen. He later won the Kokura Kinen and was 2nd in the Chunichi Shimbun Hai - a local-track star.
1649=After 2 dirt wins he switched to turf in autumn at 3, won 2 allowances and was a nose 2nd in the American JCC, then beat Zenno Rob Roy by a neck in the Nikkei Sho. He then struggled, fractured a leg in the '06 Arima Kinen, was injured again in training, and retired to stud in '08. Blind in his right eye, yet battling on in graded races, he still has many fans.
1650=Won 3 in Southern Kanto, incl. the Kanto Oaks. Dam of Hokkaido Sansai Yushun winner Takino Special.
1653=Plagued by fractures, in only his 2nd graded race, the Asahi Challenge C. at 4, he won when Nishino Raiden, first past the post, was disqualified. 2nd in the Takamatsunomiya Hai (G2), he battled on at 5, then moved to local racing and shone: he won Kanazawa's Hakusan Daishoten, then Nagoya's Tokai Kikkasho and Nagoya Daishoten easily, and won at Kasamatsu too. 1657=Based at Kanazawa. 23 wins incl. the Thoroughbred Challenge C. and Hakusan Daishoten.
1666=Based at Kawasaki. With fierce, explosive runs, he won the Tokyo Okansho and Tokyo Daishoten. Injured before a Hanshin Daishoten bid, he broke down fatally on his return.
1667=Based at Kasamatsu. He won the Tokai Derby, Thoroughbred Challenge C. and the Derby Grand Prix, making his name nationwide. At 4 he was 3rd behind Narita Brian and Mayano Top Gun in the JRA's Hanshin Daishoten.
1672=Based at Kasamatsu. Unbeaten in 5, he tried the JRA's Daily Hai Sansai S., 7th to Seeking the Pearl, but won Kasamatsu's Junior Grand Prix and more, NAR champion 2yo of '96. At 3 he won the JRA-local Nagoya Yushun.
1673=Debuting in Hokkaido racing, he moved to the JRA after a 2nd in the Zen-Nihon Sansai Yushun. In the classics he was 4th in the Kyodo News Hai Yonsai S. (switched to dirt), then crushed in the Spring S. and Crystal C. On dirt he still struggled, and at 4 moved to Southern Kanto, where he won 5 straight from his first race and 3 JRA-local graded races - Urawa Kinen, Kawasaki Kinen and Diolite Kinen - a local star, NAR's best older horse in 2000.
1674=By minor sire Pekin Ryuen. Debuting at Funabashi, he won 21 with powerful runs, above all as an older horse: at 5 he won the Urawa Kinen by 3 lengths from Surprise Power, and at 6 the Teio Sho.
1675=Debuting in Hokkaido racing, he moved to Oi. Quiet at 2, he won the Tokyo Okansho at 3, joining the elite, then starred in JRA-local races, winning the Zen-Nihon Thoroughbred C. and Oguri Cap Kinen.
1676=Based at Iwate. Unbeaten in 9 from his debut, he raced eagerly in JRA-local races, beating Wing Arrow, Toshin Blizzard and others in the Tokyo Daishoten at 5, and winning the Nambu Hai the next year - NAR Horse of the Year in '01 and '02.
1677=Debuting in Hokkaido racing (4 wins from 6), he moved to Oi, won the Wakajishi Tokubetsu and Keihin Hai, then the Haneda Hai, the first classic. 3rd in the Tokyo Okansho, he won the Tokyo Derby for 2 Southern Kanto classics, then the Japan Dirt Derby against the nation's best - NAR champion 4yo of '99. At 4 he was beaten badly in the February S. and Japan Cup Dirt.
1678=Debuting at Funabashi, she won her first 5 incl. the Kanto Oaks, then after a 2nd won 4 more incl. the TCK Jo-o Hai. After winning the JRA-local Marine C. she was injured; back, she had lost her power and was always beaten badly.
1679=Based at Kasamatsu. Strong on dirt, even faster on turf. At 2 he led all the way to win the JRA's Daily Hai Sansai S. by 2.5 lengths over prospect Rugger Regulus, then was favorite in the Asahi Hai Sansai S. despite being a local horse, caught near the line by Eishin Preston for 2nd. Long injury breaks kept graded wins away, but at 6 he won the Zen-Nihon Thoroughbred C. and raced until retiring at 10 at the end of '07, with 26 wins.
1680=Based in Hokkaido. Unbeatable there, he won the Hokkaido Sansai Yushun and was 3rd in the Japan Dirt Derby.
1681=Based at Kasamatsu. After winning a JRA-certified debut, he twice ran in JRA races, beaten badly, then at 3 was 5th in the Wakaba S. NAR champion 4yo of 2000, he boldly kept taking on the JRA, running 3 times in the Hanshin Daishoten and in G1s like the Japan Cup Dirt and Takarazuka Kinen - 17 JRA races in all, without a win, until retiring at 9.
1682=Moved from Aichi to Funabashi. Won the Thoroughbred Challenge C. at 3 and kept trying JRA-local races.
1683=Debuting on turf at Morioka, which has a turf course, he won 3 straight, then tried the JRA: he won the Ivy S. easily, was 2nd by 0.1s in the Keio Hai Sansai S., and 3rd in the Asahi Hai Sansai S., the 2yo championship. The next spring he was 2nd in the NZ Trophy and 4th in the NHK Mile C. Moving to Oi when older, he kept running in the JRA, winning the TV Aichi Open and Ocean S. (OP). Of 52 races, 40 were in the JRA, and as a local horse he ran just 8 on dirt - hopeless there, 8th or worse in 6 - a truly unique local horse. In '06 he won the Ocean S., newly a G3 - his first graded win at 8. Racing on into '08, he was clearly past it and retired in July without running.
1684=Based at Funabashi. Half-brother of Tulip Sho winner Jodi Shiraoki. With 3 wins and a 2nd from 4, he went to the JRA's Unicorn S. and won as 7th choice. After the '03 Hochi Grand Prix C. he never ran again, retiring in April '05.
1685=Debuting in Hokkaido racing, he won the Zen-Nihon 2yo Yushun, NAR champion 2yo. At 3 he moved to Oi, won the Haneda Hai and was 3rd in the Japan Dirt Derby. Winless after moving to the JRA at 4, he went to Saga.
1686=Based at Funabashi. He won the Haneda Hai by 5 lengths and the Tokyo Derby by 5 again, top of his crop. At 4 he won 2 unified G2s, and in '05 was 3rd in the Kashiwa Kinen and Teio Sho. Strong at local level but unable to beat JRA horses in unified graded races, he retired without a G1 in '08 and went to stud.
1687=Debuting at Kaminoyama, he moved to Kasamatsu. He tried the JRA at 3 without winning, but older regained his strength, winning the Diolite Kinen and Oguri Cap Kinen. After a long break he raced on until 9 and went to stud.
1688=A Tokai local star with 14 wins, he ran in the All Comers and broke clear from a good spot to win. In the Japan Cup he was 7th, a bold, creditable run. Moving to the JRA at 5, he made his mark again: in his 2nd race, the Nikkei Sho, well behind winner Mihoshinzan, he beat previous Derby winner Dyna Gulliver by a head in a straight duel for 2nd - a local horse's pride.
1689=Based at Takasaki, winner of many graded races like the Tango Sho and Kaisetsu Kinen. His short life peaked in '88 in the Nambu Hai at Mizusawa, a new East Japan dirt championship: representing Takasaki, he won, "the hero of Takasaki," famous nationwide. The next year, at his peak after winning Takasaki's Kaisetsu Kinen, he was brought down in a fall in the Teio Sho and, with a severe fracture, had to be put down. 22 wins from 58; many fans were captivated by his fighting runs, and he is remembered as "the ill-fated dirt king."
1690=Useless on turf, but after a 2nd in Chukyo's Winter S. to Life Tateyama, then the JRA's best dirt horse, he moved to Kasamatsu, as "the JRA has few dirt races." With Katsumi Ando aboard he was unbeatable at Kasamatsu and Nagoya. In the '88 Zen-Nihon Thoroughbred C. he beat Inari One (then at Oi), next year's JRA Horse of the Year - the top dirt horse. In '89 he went to Oi and won the Teio Sho easily. At home his winning was a given; the only question was by how many lengths. He won Sapporo's Breeders' Gold C., then returned home for the Zen-Nihon Thoroughbred C. - but pulled up after one lap, leaving the crowd stunned. Judged hopeless, he was too great to put down at once; after 3 weeks of treatment he died.
1691=Based at Oi. Apart from a 3rd at 2, 9 wins and three 2nds from 13 - always placed, 90% in the top two. Winning 4 straight - Haneda Hai, Tokyo Derby, Tokyo Hai, Tokyo Okansho - he became the 5th Southern Kanto Triple Crown winner.
1692=Based at Funabashi. Debuting late, in January at 3, he won 7 straight; up to his record win in the Diolite Kinen he had 12 wins and three 2nds from 15 - always top two. But as heavy favorite in the Teio Sho he was 5th, and only 2nd in graded races after.
1693=Based at Oi. Emerging early with the Tokyo Sansai Yushun Himba, she won the first Derby Grand Prix despite being a filly. As a dam she produced decent runners Aquilone (93: Dancing Brave) and Tight Squeeze (95: Tight Spot).
1694=A strong Oi horse who won the Teio Sho twice. Twice in the All Comers, he finished in double figures both times.
1695=A top Oi filly who won the Tokyo Sansai Yushun Himba. In a strong era for local racing, she was consistent and won the Derby Grand Prix, a grand new local event, following Tomi Alco for an Oi double. Moving to the JRA at 4, she never made the top five in 3 starts and retired. As a dam she produced Back Gear (99: Jade Robbery), with 5 local wins incl. the Bluebird C.
1696=Debuting at Utsunomiya, he showed rare talent; he came good at Kaminoyama, then moved to Oi, winning big races like the Tokyo Daishoten, Kawasaki Kinen and Grand Champion 2000. Joining the JRA in '91, he disliked turf - 8th in the Osaka Hai, 16th in the Tenno Sho (Spring) - and returned to Oi after just 2 races.
1697=Debuting at Oi, he won the Tokyo Okansho, Kawasaki Kinen and Derby Grand Prix, then the Diamond Tokubetsu after moving to Kasamatsu. Joining the JRA with 11 wins from 20, his best in 3 open races was second-last.
1698=Based at Oi, he was 2nd in the All Comers at 5 and went to the Japan Cup, where he stayed at the back and finished last. The next year he won the All Comers, and in the Japan Cup led boldly - but was last again.
1699=Starred at Kochi, Kanazawa and Tokai, with 9 wins incl. Kanazawa's Hakusan Daishoten.
1700=5 wins from 24 in Southern Kanto, incl. the Kanto Oaks and Keisei Hai (Funabashi).
1701=The chief rival of Swift Seidai, Tohoku's monster, in fierce battles at Iwate. Long unable to beat him, "the foil," he finally did so in the Kitakamigawa Daishoten at 6.
1702=Debuting at Kasamatsu, he won 24, including the 2nd Oguri Cap Kinen.
