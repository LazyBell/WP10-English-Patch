379=Slow to rise, he missed the spring, but emerged in summer and went to the Kikuka Sho. Outsider Meiner Despot seemed set to win unchallenged in front, but Manhattan Cafe caught him at the line for his first crown. He then beat strong older horses like T.M. Opera O with a powerful finish in the Arima Kinen. The next year he beat crop rival Jungle Pocket to win the Tenno Sho (Spring). That autumn he tried the Arc but was injured during the race and finished 13th. At stud he had no stars at first, but went on to sire NHK Mile C. winner Joe Cappuccino, Tenno Sho winner Hiruno d'Amour, Red Desire, who beat Buena Vista in the Shuka Sho, and 2013 February S. winner Grape Brandy. He was leading sire in 2009.
381=He won his first 2 starts, but a fracture cost him 10 months. Back, he won twice, then a tendon injury cost him another 13 months. Returning at 5, he won a semi-open race and then the Keihan Hai, his first graded win. In the Takarazuka Kinen he was 2nd choice as "the late-arriving star," and won his first G1 in record time. Sadly for him, Rice Shower broke down fatally in that race, leaving the winner little remembered.
382=He ran in all three classics, finishing 6th, 9th and 6th. Improving after the Kikuka Sho, he won the Kimpai (East) early at 4. That autumn he fought a legendary 300m duel with Yamanin Zephyr in the Tenno Sho (Autumn). He lost by a nose, but it was the race where his true ability showed best. The next year he won the Tokyo Shimbun Hai, but in another Tenno Sho (Autumn) he lost to Nehai Caesar, 2nd again.
384=Based at local Iwate. He won 9 straight including the Tohoku Derby, then was crushed in the Derby Grand Prix and Super Dirt Derby. Growing steadily, in autumn at 4 he beat Southern Kanto king Abukuma Poro in the Nambu Hai for a G1. Next year he overpowered Taiki Sherlock, M.I. Blanc and others in the JRA's February S. - the first JRA G1 won by a local horse. After 5 more wins including the Teio Sho, he was 11th in the Tokyo Daishoten, but was still '99 NAR Horse of the Year. Trying to repeat in the February S., he was 4th behind new JRA stars Wing Arrow and Gold Tiara.
387=By European champion Dancing Brave out of Goodbye Halo, a 7-time US G1 winner - a true blueblood. After winning his first 3, a G1 seemed imminent, but his temper held him back, with narrow losses led by a 2nd in the Satsuki Sho. Older, he won the Tokyo Shimbun Hai and Nakayama Kinen, but was crushed in the Yasuda and Takarazuka Kinen, then 2nd in the Mile Championship and 3rd in the Sprinters S. At 5 he finally won the Takamatsunomiya Kinen, on his 11th G1 try. At stud he sired fillies' double winner Kawakami Princess (03: Takano Secretary) and 2-time sprint G1 winner Laurel Guerreiro (04: Big Ten Bee).
388=After a 7th in the Derby he won 3 straight, including the Kobe and Kyoto Shimbun Hai, the year's top late improver. There were doubts about the Kikuka Sho distance, but a slow pace set up a sprint in the straight. His astonishing 33.9 finish outran closers Mejiro Bright, Silk Justice and Tokio Excellent, and he became a classic winner on a 4-race streak. He never won again, retiring after the Takarazuka Kinen at 6.
391=His first win came in March at 3, in his 7th start. He then won the Wakakusa S. and Kyoto Yonsai Tokubetsu and went to the Derby, just failing to catch Sunny Brian but finishing 2nd, ahead of favorite Mejiro Bright. After winning the Kyoto Daishoten, he was 5th in both the Kikuka Sho and Japan Cup, his late charges leaving him short. In the Arima Kinen he moved early from the third turn and powered through from midfield, beating strong older horses clearly - a G1 just 9 months after his first win.
392=A leading classic contender, but 2nd in the Satsuki Sho, 2nd in the Derby, 5th in the Kikuka Sho, then 5th in the Mile Championship - a supporting role. After a 2nd in the next Yasuda Kinen, he finally won his long-sought G1 in the Takarazuka Kinen. A tendon injury the next year seemed to send him to stud, but in autumn '04, 16 months later, he suddenly returned at local Arao. Winless in 4 starts, he retired in June '05.
393=From Fuji Kiseki's first crop, he crushed A Shin Preston by 4 lengths on debut, then won the Shinzan Kinen and Spring S. and was Satsuki Sho favorite. Breaking clear from a good spot, he was outdueled by the charging Air Shakur, a narrow 2nd. After a heavy defeat in the Derby he ran well at a mile, winning 3 graded races including the Naruo Kinen and two Kyoto Kimpai, but lost narrowly to Agnes Digital in the Mile Championship and never won a G1.
394=A rare graded winner by Miho Shinzan. With the "hatchet-sharp" kick of grandsire Shinzan, he romped in the NHK Hai (G2, 2000m) and was 5th in the Derby. Just as he seemed set to rise as his sire's heir, leg trouble forced a long break. He still won the Asahi Challenge C., showing real ability, but leg problems forced retirement at 5. At stud he left no heir... Shinzan's line ended here.
