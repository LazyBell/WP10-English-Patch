1830=Winning 4 straight in summer at 3, he won the Musashino S. over dirt king Kane Hekili, back after 2 years, crop-mate Ubiquitous, 7-length Unicorn S. winner, and G1 winner Sunrise Bacchus. Highly rated, he was still excluded from the Japan Cup Dirt for lack of earnings, causing controversy.
1835=One of the best front-runners in training. Reaching open class in spring at 5, he led and held on for a nose 2nd to Seiun Wonder in the Epsom C. He led all the way in the Final S., Kansai's last race of 2010, then won the Kyoto Kimpai as 7th choice, and the Milers C. as 7th choice too - "never let him lead unchallenged, however unfancied." His next show came in the Tenno Sho (Autumn): 12th choice, drawn gate 1, he blazed through 1000m in 56.5; he finished 16th but set up Tosen Jordan's Japanese record 1:56.1. He passed 1600m in 1:32.2, so as 7th choice in the Mile CS people thought "at that pace he can win," but he ran over 2 seconds slower, 8th. At 7 he won the Milers C. again and led to hold 4th in the Mile CS. In 2013 he held on for a dead-heat-time 3rd in the Nakayama Kinen, then retired to stud.
1836=At 2 he beat even Smart Falcon in a dirt race, then was 8th in the Asahi Hai FS. Racing mainly in dirt sprints, he won 3 JRA-local graded races - Tokyo Sprint, Hokkaido Sprint C., Summer Champion - and made the top five in 2 JBC Sprints, a fixture of the division until 9. He went to stud.
1837=From the first crop of versatile great Agnes Digital. Never in the top five on turf, he emerged on dirt, loving Kyoto's 1800m dirt course with 5 wins there, incl. the Antares S. and Heian S.
1838=By Yamanin Seraphim, known for his Keisei Hai dead heat. Improving in summer at 3, he won 2 allowances, then was 3rd to Oken Bruce Lee in the Kikuka Sho as 9th choice. He ran well in long races like the Hanshin Daishoten and Tenno Sho (Spring), and also middle-distance ones like the Mainichi Okan and Naruo Kinen - versatile, yet graded wins eluded him until he won the Hanshin Daishoten by 3.5 lengths at 6. In the Tenno Sho (Spring), as positions shuffled wildly, he swept from 10th to the lead at the 3rd turn and held on for 3rd behind Hiruno d'Amour. At 7, 3rd in the Hanshin Daishoten, he was 8th to Beat Black in the Tenno Sho (Spring), then moved to local racing.
1839=First foal of Yamanin Paradise, unbeaten Hanshin JF winner. Unbeaten in 2, he met fellow unbeaten Roman Empire in the Keisei Hai - a rare graded-race dead heat. Winless after, he sired Hanshin Daishoten winner Namura Crescent.
1840=Debuting on turf, he found his best on dirt: 4th first time, then 3 straight wins to the Hyogo Championship and the Shirakaba S. (OP) against older horses. More was expected, but injury ended his career at the end of 3.
1841=By Daitaku Riva, a 5-time graded winner and one of Fuji Kiseki's early stars. With early speed, she ran a big 4th as 13th choice in the Shuka Sho and won the Mermaid S. at 5. Then, 8th at best in 6 starts, she retired after the Queen S. at 6 (11th) to breed.
1842=At 2 he was 3rd as 11th choice in the Hagi S., and at 3 3rd as 11th choice in the Kyodo News Hai - suited to middle-distance turf. In summer at 4 he won 3 straight to the Ireland T. 1843=A small filly like her sire Stay Gold, sometimes under 400kg. With a sharp kick she won the Fillies' Revue as 11th choice and the Rose S. as 7th choice. Her best after were a 3rd in the Aichi Hai and 4th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup; winless, she retired to breed after her 5yo season.
1844=At 2 he ran well on dirt, 3rd in the Zen-Nihon 2yo Yushun. In the Kisaragi Sho at 3, winless on turf, he was 8th choice but won with the sharp kick of Agnes Tachyon's runners. Plagued by injuries, at 6 he won the Asuka S. (OP), his first win in 3 years, then the Sekiya Kinen from up front. Mile success was expected, but a fracture sidelined him in autumn at 7.
1845=A late-arriving Deep Impact star. Winning a maiden in May at 3, he won 3 straight to the Radio Nikkei Sho. Unbeaten in 3, he was 3rd behind double classic winner Orfevre and Win Variation in the Kobe Shimbun Hai, then 10th in the Kikuka Sho and 4th behind 3 crop-mates incl. Red Davis in the Naruo Kinen. Stalling, he won only the Nishinomiya S. (16M) at 4. At 5 he was 2nd, 4th and 4th in open races, then broke down in the Chukyo Kinen and was put down.
1846=By King's Best, sire of Derby winner Eishin Flash and Arc winner Workforce. At 3 he won 2 allowances over 2600m on turf, showing stamina. At 4 he won the 3000m Manyo S. (OP), then the Diamond S. with the fastest finish - Cosmo Helenos 2nd, a Cosmo 1-2. 2nd to Namura Crescent in the Hanshin Daishoten, he was expected to star as a stayer, but broke down in the Tenno Sho (Spring) and was put down.
1847=A middle-distance Dance in the Dark colt. Closing from midfield, he won the Edelweiss S. and Radio Nikkei Sho in a row.
1848=A sprinter of 2011-2013 with 3 graded wins. 2nd in the 2011 Sprinters S., in 2012 he won the Ibis Summer Dash and Keeneland C., Summer Sprint Series champion, and in 2013 the Hakodate Sprint S. He went to Hong Kong late in 2011, 14th in the Hong Kong Sprint.
1849=By Miscast, son of mile queen North Flight and a 5-time winner incl. the Principal S. Not a standout as a foal, he improved steadily with racing, and in autumn at 3 defied 13th choice for 3rd in the Kikuka Sho, showing stamina. After a 2nd in the Kyoto Daishoten and more, in his 2nd Tenno Sho (Spring) he raced forward and burst clear in the straight, winning by 4 lengths from Tosen Jordan - his first graded win a big title. He kept running well over long trips, but after the Kyoto Kinen at 6 a tendon injury struck. Back in the 2014 Osaka Hai (8th), he retired after it and became a lead pony at beloved Kyoto.
1850=She won a maiden and a 5-million race on dirt, then her first turf race, the Flower C., with a straight-long charge. With 2yo queen Reve d'Essor out, the Oka Sho was wide open; 4th choice, she closed fastest from last at the final turn for 3rd. Raced mostly at middle distances apart from the Oka Sho, she was expected to shine in the longer Oaks, but injury forced her retirement.
1851=2nd on debut to later Oka Sho winner Marcellina, she won a dirt maiden easily, then beat later Kanto Oaks winner Colorful Days. After an 11th in the Fillies' Revue she focused on dirt, winning the Shoryu S. (OP), then broke clear early in the Unicorn S. to beat Grape Brandy and Boreas, later 1-2 in the Japan Dirt Derby. Favorite against older horses in the Sparking Lady C., she was well beaten by queen Lavelita, 3rd. After dull runs, she retired to breed in summer at 4.
1852=By Deep Impact out of Biwa Heidi, half-brother of Buena Vista - the finest of Japanese bloodlines; he sold for 231 million yen, the top price at the 2008 foal sale. Delayed to a February debut at 3, he won 2 straight on talent alone, but as favorite in the Mainichi Hai and Aoba Sho lacked a finish, 3rd both times. Desperate for a Derby ticket, his camp ran him a week later in the Principal S., and he won, but as 5th choice in the Derby he struggled on heavy ground, 9th behind stablemate Orfevre. Given a gentler schedule after, his highlight was the Epsom C. at 4 - somewhat disappointing. He retired at 9 after 33 races, notable for being ridden mostly by foreign jockeys.
1853=He won the Radio Nikkei Hai 2yo S., a stepping stone to stardom, with the fastest finish - Deep Impact's first graded winner as a sire, with Yutaka Take aboard. A classic hope, he was 9th as favorite in the Kyodo News Hai, then straight into the Satsuki Sho as 8th choice slipped up the inside for 3rd. Hopes of a Derby for Deep Impact's first crop rose, but injury ruled him out. Back in middle-distance graded races, he was 2nd in the Nikkei Shinshun Hai and twice 3rd in the Chunichi Shimbun Hai. At 5 he won the AJCC despite a controversially forceful ride, then raced boldly for 2nd to Gold Ship in the Takarazuka Kinen, ahead of triple crown filly Gentildonna and Tenno Sho winner Fenomeno.
1854=His dam Kei Woman beat colts in the Kyoto Yonsai Tokubetsu (G3). Over 530kg from his debut, he was 3rd in the Aoba Sho at 3 and ran in the Derby (16th). At 4 he won the Diamond S., then was 3rd behind Alnasr Line and Meiner Kitz in the Nikkei Sho - the pair then ran 1-2 in the Tenno Sho (Spring), and the Nikkei Sho 4th was later G1 winner Earnestly, so his potential was clearly high, yet he kept falling short. In spring at 5 he won his first dirt race, the Jinsen S. (OP), as 7th choice, but was 4th in the March S. Growing past 550kg, he ran mostly in long races, closing fastest for 3rd in the Hanshin Daishoten at 6, but never won another graded race.
1855=First foal of great mare Dance in the Mood, winner of the Oka Sho and Victoria Mile. Closing fastest, she won her debut and the Akamatsu Sho (5M). In the Hanshin JF, billed as a 2-horse race, she fought her rider and was 9th to Reve d'Essor. Showing no fatigue, she won the Fairy S. by 2.5 lengths from midfield early the next year. Going for a mother-daughter Oka Sho, she was 7th. She later placed in allowances at double-figure odds, surprising many, but retired winless.
1856=Sister of Maruka Schenk (by Sunday Silence), a Sekiya Kinen winner known for charging late after slow starts. She won the Wasurenagusa Sho (OP) at 3 but was repelled by graded races. She retired to breed after the Nakayama Himba S. at 6 (12th). Her 2nd foal Mickey Olivier (by King Kamehameha) won 4 straight from a maiden.
1857=A Special Week miler. After winning the Mukogawa S. (16M), he led all the way in his first graded race, the Lord Derby CT.
1858=From the first crop of Gold Allure, winner of 4 dirt G1s incl. the February S. After his debut win he was 6th, 2nd, 5th, 4th, 2nd and 3rd in graded races - never badly beaten. In the Satsuki Sho he charged past favorite Meiner Charles but couldn't catch front-running Captain Thule, 2nd. Running well in open races, he was repelled by graded ones until the Lord Derby Challenge T. - where he was favorite at a remarkable 6.7 in a wide-open race. 1859=A foreign-bred sold for 48 million yen at a US sale. Living up to it, he won his debut with an other-worldly kick and was odds-on under 1.5 in his next 4 races. Favorite again in his first graded race, the Falcon S., he closed fastest from last to win. 7th in the NZ Trophy, closing too late, he was 14th choice for the NHK Mile C. amid distance doubts, but charged from behind for 3rd to later double winner Deep Sky.
