1221=Called the last big runner by US Triple Crown winner Secretariat, he drew notice before his debut. His fiery finishing kick thrilled fans and terrified his crop rivals. 1222=Debuting fairly late, in January at 3, she won the Flower C. in her 4th start - "the first star of the East" - and headed to the spring classics, but was no match for heavy favorite Vega: 5th in the Oka Sho, 6th in the Oaks. Winless after the Sapporo Kinen in summer at 4, her camp even tried jump training to find a new path. Then, almost by chance, she ran in the JRA-local Empress Hai at Kawasaki and won by 18 lengths - the start of the "Queen of the Sand" legend. Still struggling on turf, from 6 she switched fully to dirt, winning 9 of 9 dirt graded races, beating every star, JRA and local, and sweeping the major titles. In '97 she went to the Dubai World Cup as Japan's representative. It was the world's top race, but her strength was extraordinary, and fans hoped for a close fight. Having toured all of Japan to reach her dream stage, she was caught in a tragic accident in the race and died on foreign sand. But her role in lighting up JRA-local racing and her record as "the greatest dirt horse" in Japanese history will never fade.
