135=* Kitasan Black vs Satono Diamond\nThis year's Arima Kinen became a clash between last year's Kikuka Sho winner Kitasan Black and this year's, Satono Diamond.\nKitasan Black, owned by enka singer Saburo Kitajima, drew more attention the more he won. Sold for just 3.5 million yen, he won the Kikuka Sho the year before, and this year took the Tenno Sho (Spring) and Japan Cup to reach the top among older horses. His sire Black Tide, full brother of Deep Impact, was a graded winner but lived in the shadow of his brother, hailed as the greatest ever - yet as a sire he produced a huge star.\nSatono Diamond, by Deep Impact out of Malpensa, a 3-time G1 winner in Argentina, was a blueblood sold at the Select Sale for 230 million yen. 3rd and 2nd in the spring classics, he won the Kikuka Sho as favorite by two and a half lengths and was seen as the best of his crop.\nThe clash of two contrasting Kikuka Sho winners drew huge interest, with Satono Diamond 2.6 and Kitasan Black 2.7. Kitasan Black sat in a perfect 2nd, while Satono Diamond moved right behind him down the backstretch, marking him all the way. At the third turn stablemate Satono Noblesse swept forward and the pace quickened. In the straight Kitasan Black led and tried to hold on, but Satono Diamond kept coming and won by a neck. The younger Kikuka Sho winner took the much-anticipated duel.\n\n* Hokko Tarumae, Most G1-Level Wins Ever\nHokko Tarumae, owned by a Tomakomai owner and named after the city's Mt. Tarumae, debuted at 3 and ran steadily on dirt, then from 4 went on a tremendous run, winning 9 G1/JpnI races in three years to 6.\nIn 2016 he raced on at 7. He went for a third straight Kawasaki Kinen (JpnI) and won as favorite - only the second to win it three times running, after Countess Up (1985-87). It was his 10th G1/JpnI win, passing Vermilion and Espoir City's 9 for the most G1-level wins ever. He then tried Dubai again (Dubai WC) but was 9th, and retired at year's end. He has since done well at stud while serving as Tomakomai's tourism ambassador, helping his hometown.
136=* Chasing a Triple Crown: 7 G1 Wins\nKitasan Black lost a thriller to Satono Diamond in the last Arima Kinen, but in 2017 he showed no sign of slowing.\nSpring had only two G1s for older horses at middle-to-long distances (the Tenno Sho (Spring) and Takarazuka Kinen) - one fewer than autumn, and weighted to long races. So the 2000m G2 Sankei Osaka Hai was chosen, renamed the Osaka Hai and made a G1 this year. Kitasan Black took on this new "spring older horses' triple crown." In the Osaka Hai some had doubts, as he was a long-distance horse and had been 2nd the year before. But he didn't chase the runaway leader, sat in a good spot and coolly took the lead in the straight. The others closed but he never tired, becoming the first G1 Osaka Hai champion.\nThe Tenno Sho (Spring) was a two-horse clash with his nemesis Satono Diamond. Kitasan Black was 2.2, Satono Diamond 2.5. But favorites had lost the race 10 years running since Deep Impact in 2006. Runaway leader Yamakatsu Raiden set a blistering pace. As others tired, Kitasan Black led early and built a lead, and held off Satono Diamond and others in the straight. He avenged his defeat and set a record nearly a second faster than Deep Impact's. Having won two spring legs, perhaps tired, he was a dismal 9th in the Takarazuka Kinen as 1.4 favorite, just missing the first spring triple crown.\nIn autumn he started in the Tenno Sho (Autumn). On the first heavy track in 48 years, and racing from behind after a stumbling start, he still beat Satono Crown by a neck, winning both Tenno Sho races. In the Japan Cup, which he won the year before, he tried to lead all the way but was 3rd behind Cheval Grand's late run.\nFor his farewell his camp chose the Arima Kinen, where he had lost twice. His tactic was, of course, his trademark front-running. Setting his own pace, he stayed in front and pulled away in the straight - a textbook front-running win by a length and a half. On his third try he finally won the Arima Kinen, a perfect finale.\nHe missed the triple crowns in spring and autumn, but won 4 G1s that year. His 7 in all tied the record, and his earnings passed T.M. Opera O's to become No. 1 - truly a champion who walked the royal road.\n\n* G1-Level Wins Record Broken Two Years Running\nCopano Rickey was by Gold Allure, a 4-time dirt G1-level winner. He won his first graded race, the Hyogo CS, at 3, but a fracture sidelined him. After his return he kept losing, and squeezed into the February S. through a draw as last choice of 16. But he boldly raced forward and held off Hokko Tarumae and others - a shock win paying 272.1, the second-highest win payout in JRA G1 history. Amid talk of a fluke, he won the Kashiwa Kinen next and declared himself the best dirt horse. He piled up wins, including the first back-to-back February S., winning 8 G1/JpnI races by 6.\nIn 2017 his first race at 7 was the February S. After losses at the end of the previous year he was only 6th choice. He led briefly at the top of the straight but was passed by one horse after another, finishing 14th. He seemed finished, but in the Kashiwa Kinen he won from off the pace instead, surprising fans with a new style at 7 and winning the race for the third time. In autumn he won the Mile CS Nambu Hai again, and chose the Tokyo Daishoten, where he had lost 3 times, as his farewell. He broke well, led and held off everyone in the straight - a record 11th G1-level win, breaking the mark Hokko Tarumae had just set the year before, and a glorious send-off.
137=* Fillies' Triple Crown and a World Record\nAlmond Eye was a blueblood by top sprinter Lord Kanaloa out of Queen Elizabeth II Cup winner Fusaichi Pandora. She lost her debut but won next time, then won her first graded race against colts in this year's Shinzan Kinen.\nThen the first classic, the Oka Sho (from here until retirement she ran only in G1s). Unbeaten 2yo queen Lucky Lilac was favorite. Lucky Lilac raced forward while Almond Eye waited at the back. About 8 lengths behind at the top of the straight, she surged wide and swept past before anyone could react, winning easily. Jockey Christophe Lemaire's comments afterwards sounded like a Triple Crown declaration. In the Oaks she was 1.7 favorite and won by 2 lengths, taking her second crown with ease. In autumn she went straight to the Shuka Sho without a prep. Despite doubts about the schedule, she was 1.3 favorite, more heavily backed than in the Oaks - her power was on another level. In the straight she was about 10 lengths behind the leader, too far even for her believers, but she unleashed an astonishing kick, led with 150m left and won by a length and a half - the 5th fillies' Triple Crown winner.\nBut even that was just a milestone. For her last race of the year she chose not the fillies' Queen Elizabeth II Cup but the Japan Cup, against strong older colts. Ahead of stars like Osaka Hai winner Suave Richard and 2-time G1 winner Satono Diamond, she was again overwhelming 1.4 favorite; her 53.4% of win bets was second in the race's history only to Deep Impact in 2006. She tracked front-runner Kiseki in 2nd, drew level 200m out and pulled away to win by a length and three-quarters - a champion's performance against strong older horses. Her time, 2:20.6, was not just a race record but over a second faster than the world record for 2400m on turf. She became only the second 3yo filly named Horse of the Year, after Gentildonna in 2012.\n\n* Lemaire: JRA Wins Record and First Jockey Quadruple\nChristophe Lemaire, who won the fillies' Triple Crown on Almond Eye this year, also won the Kikuka Sho with Fierement, the Tenno Sho (Autumn) with Rey de Oro and the JBC Sprint with Graceful Leap - the first-ever G1 wins four weeks in a row, starting with the Shuka Sho (he did it again in 2020). He also set a record of 215 wins in a year (8 G1s), and became the first jockey to win four JRA Award categories.\nMeanwhile legend Yutaka Take, at the top since the second racing boom, reached 4,000 JRA wins.
138=* A Great Mare in Full Bloom\nLys Gracieux won her second start in a Hanshin 2yo record for 1800m on turf, then won the Artemis S. True to her name, French for "graceful lily," her career began brilliantly - but she then lost 7 straight G1s (four times 2nd), just short of a title. In autumn at 4, with top mare Almond Eye absent, she finally won her first G1, the Queen Elizabeth II Cup.\nIn 2019, at 5, near misses continued: 2nd in the Kinko Sho, 3rd in Hong Kong's QE2 Cup. She seemed to be back to "good but not quite," but showed her grit in the Takarazuka Kinen. The only filly in the field, she was 3rd choice at 5.4. Her camp planned to come late from behind, but with only favorite Kiseki going forward, she followed him in 2nd. In the straight she easily passed Kiseki and won by 3 lengths for her second G1.\nFrom this year the Takarazuka Kinen winner got priority entry to Australia's G1 Cox Plate, with a bonus for winning. Her camp wanted to go, but her spring Hong Kong trip ran afoul of quarantine rules, and at first she had to give it up. Then Australia's agriculture department and others made the trip possible, and her long-awaited entry was confirmed. Popular there too, she waited at the back and shot up Moonee Valley's straight, just 173m long, to win - her first G1 abroad.\nBack home she chose the Arima Kinen as her last race. Even in her farewell she weighed a career-high 468 kg, showing she was at her peak. Favorite was year-younger fillies' Triple Crown winner Almond Eye, fresh from winning the Tenno Sho (Autumn); Lys Gracieux was a distant 2nd choice. At a fast pace she sat in midfield, swung wide in the straight and unleashed an astonishing finish to win by 5 lengths. Almond Eye was 9th. The first mare to win both Grand Prix races in the same year, she bowed out in style. The "lily" that kept falling short at 3 and 4 bloomed beautifully this year and rose to Horse of the Year.\n\n* Changes to the Race System\nThis year "listed race" status was introduced: important open races ranked just below graded races, marked "(L)" after the name.\nAlso, horses used to have their earned prize money halved in summer at 4 (the "demotion" system), letting them return to a class they had already won. This was abolished this year, and the 5-million, 10-million and 16-million-yen classes were renamed the 1-win, 2-win and 3-win classes.\nNon-graded races' rankings and classes became easier to understand - especially welcome for new fans.
139=* Unbeaten Triple Crowns for Colt and Filly - and a Clash\nThis year, unbeaten Triple Crown winners emerged among both colts and fillies.\nContrail was a son of unbeaten Triple Crown winner Deep Impact. In his second start, the Tokyo Sports Hai 2yo S., he won easily in a 2yo record for 1800m on turf despite not being fully prepared. He won the Hopeful S. clearly too and was named champion 2yo colt by a wide margin over Asahi Hai FS winner Salios. At 3 he boldly went straight to the Satsuki Sho without a prep. As attention focused on his clash with Salios, the coronavirus was raging, and the race was run without spectators. He tracked calmly at the back and passed Salios by half a length in the straight for his first classic. In the Japanese Derby he was 1.4 and Salios 4.4, with the rest in double figures - a two-horse race. But Contrail stretched the gap, winning by 3 lengths and showing his rival the difference in class. Then the last classic, the Kikuka Sho. Despite doubts about the distance, he was 1.1 favorite. Closely marked all the way by Aristoteles under Lemaire, he had a tough race, but never gave up the lead in the straight duel and won by a neck - the third unbeaten Triple Crown winner, and the first father-son unbeaten Triple Crown. A world first, it was covered by racing media around the globe.\nAmong fillies, Daring Tact starred. This year's fillies' classics looked wide open as the expected contenders lost their preps one after another. In her third career start, the Oka Sho, she was 2nd choice behind 2yo queen Resistencia. Morning rain made the going heavy, seemingly bad for a closer. She was about 8 lengths behind into the straight, seemingly too far, but her powerful finish ran down Resistencia and she won by a length and a half - the first to win the Oka Sho in its third start in 40 years, since Hagino Top Lady in 1980, and only the third ever. The Oaks was her first race beyond a mile, but her finish made her 1.6 favorite. As hoped, she swept from the back with a 33.1 final 600m for her second crown. Her camp judged she'd do best without a prep, and sent her straight to the Shuka Sho. Despite good-to-soft ground and the layoff, she was 1.4 favorite. She reared at the start and missed the break, but broke clear cleanly at the end to win by a length and a quarter - the first unbeaten fillies' Triple Crown ever.\nBoth chose the Japan Cup next. Waiting there was the undisputed queen, fellow fillies' Triple Crown winner Almond Eye, with a record 8 G1s, who had announced the Japan Cup as her last race. The first-ever clash of three Triple Crown winners had come. Almond Eye was 2.2, Contrail 2.8 and Daring Tact 3.7. As Kiseki set off in a big lead, the three raced in midfield without chasing. In the straight Almond Eye surged forward; Daring Tact and Contrail swung wide and chased but couldn't catch her. With her 9th G1 she broke her own record, was named Horse of the Year over the two unbeaten Triple Crown winners, and left the turf with the dignity of the senior champion.\n\n* Sodashi, First G1 Win by a White Horse\nIn 2008 Yukichan became the first white horse to win a graded race. Then in 2019 Hayayakko won the Leopard S., and finally Sodashi reached a turf G1. Her granddam Shirayuki Hime (Yukichan's dam and Hayayakko's granddam) and dam Buchiko were both white - a pure white female line.\nIn her second start she raced forward and broke clear in the straight to win the Sapporo 2yo S. (G3). Since Yukichan's graded win was a JRA-local race, Sodashi was the first white horse to win a JRA graded race and a turf graded race. She then won the Artemis S. too, and rode that momentum into the G1 Hanshin JF. Breaking well, she sat in a good spot, broke clear in the straight and won a duel with Satono Reinas by a nose - the first G1 win by a white horse. Beautiful and strong, Sodashi became an undisputed idol horse.\n\n* Gran Alegria Dominates the Sprints\nThe year before, Gran Alegria won the Oka Sho in race-record time. This year she raced only short distances and shone. Her first race as an older horse, the Takamatsunomiya Kinen, ended in a narrow 2nd, and she missed the Victoria Mile with a fever - not a smooth start. But in the Yasuda Kinen she upset overwhelming favorite Almond Eye.\nIn the Sprinters S. she sat far back, then swept past the whole field with a powerful finish to win by 2 lengths, rare in a sprint - the first sprint G1 for Deep Impact's offspring, who mostly raced longer. Her last race of the year was the Mile CS. Blocked in the straight, she switched wide, surged and ran down the leaders for a dramatic win. With 3 JRA sprint-mile G1s in a year, tying Lord Kanaloa's record, she was named champion sprinter.
140=* Efforia: Fighting Back After a Narrow Derby Loss\nBy Epiphaneia (by Symboli Kris S. out of Cesario) out of a Heart's Cry mare, Efforia's pedigree is full of Japan's post-2000 stars. Nothing special at birth, he drew attention after winning his first two starts.\nHe won his first 3yo race, the Kyodo News Hai, as 4th choice, then went to the Satsuki Sho. Favorite was previous champion 2yo colt Danon the Kid, but Efforia broke clear perfectly from a good spot and beat Titleholder by 3 lengths. That made him overwhelming 1.7 favorite for the Japanese Derby, where filly Satono Reinas's entry also drew attention. From gate 1 he sat in midfield and closed in the straight, but Shahryar pounced just before the line, and he missed the double by a nose. By fate, like his sire Epiphaneia, grandsire Symboli Kris S. and broodmare sire Heart's Cry, he finished 2nd in the Derby.\nIn autumn he skipped the Kikuka Sho for the Tenno Sho (Autumn) against older horses. Favorite was 4yo unbeaten Triple Crown winner Contrail, 2nd choice 5yo queen Gran Alegria, going for a middle-distance G1 after sprints and miles. Efforia was 3rd choice. In this clash of three generations, he sat in midfield, passed Gran Alegria 200m out and held off Contrail's charge - the first 3yo to win the Tenno Sho (Autumn) in 19 years, since grandsire Symboli Kris S. in 2002.\nThen the Arima Kinen. He topped the fan vote, the first 3yo to do so since Vodka in 2007. He was favorite, closely followed by 5yo mare Chrono Genesis. She had won three straight Grand Prix races since the previous Takarazuka Kinen, a first for a mare, and was going for an unprecedented fourth in her last race. Efforia tracked right behind her into the straight, dueled with Deep Bond, and held off Chrono Genesis's charge to win. He missed the Triple Crown but won two big G1s against older horses and was named Horse of the Year.\n\n* Loves Only You: 3 G1s Abroad, Proving Her Crop-Mates Wrong\nLoves Only You, a blueblood Deep Impact filly, won the 2019 Oaks in race-record time. But while crop-mates Gran Alegria and Chrono Genesis shone, she lost 6 straight graded races after the Oaks.\nIn 2021, at 5, she won the Kyoto Kinen, her first win in 1 year and 9 months. On a roll, she went to the Dubai Sheema Classic but was 3rd, behind Chrono Genesis (2nd) and others. Undeterred, she flew straight to Hong Kong for the QE2 Cup, won a duel in the straight with favorite Daring Tact, the unbeaten fillies' Triple Crown winner, and took her first G1 in about two years. She traveled again, to the Breeders' Cup, the pinnacle of American racing, in the Filly & Mare Turf. Against top mares from around the world, she raced from a good spot and ran them down. That same day dirt mare Marche Lorraine won the BC Distaff, Japan's first G1 abroad on dirt - two Japanese Breeders' Cup wins, a historic feat.\nShe then returned to Hong Kong and won the Hong Kong Cup at year's end, the first horse with 3 G1 wins abroad in a year. Once left behind by her crop-mates, she soared at the end and retired rated as a great mare to match them.
