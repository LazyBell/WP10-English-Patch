294=Son of "Emperor" Symboli Rudolf and winner of the 1991 Satsuki Sho and Japanese Derby. Like his sire he entered the classics unbeaten and won both as favorite, seeming the Emperor reborn. But a fracture ended his Triple Crown dream. On his return, his Tenno Sho (Spring) clash with Mejiro McQueen, then the best horse, drew huge attention: Tokai Teio, back in style after winning the Sankei Osaka Hai, against McQueen, flawless after romping in the Hanshin Daishoten. Teio was favorite, but McQueen won easily. Yet that wasn't the end: in autumn he won the Japan Cup, the first Japanese winner in 7 years. Drained by the effort, he was 11th in the Arima Kinen, but after a year off he won the next year's Arima Kinen in a miracle comeback. At stud he sired G1 winners Tokai Point (Mile CS) and Yamanin Shukuru (Hanshin JF). Lacking a sire heir, his line seemed in danger, but Leben Steel, out of a Tokai Teio mare, won 3 graded races including the 2023 St. Lite Kinen - hopes rise for the "King's" blood.
295=Until summer at 3 he was winning and losing in low classes, but as he matured in autumn he won the Kikuka Sho, then won the year-end Arima Kinen with surprise front-running. His first race as an older horse, the Hanshin Daishoten, is still legendary: he fought a fierce duel with year-older Triple Crown winner Narita Brian from the third turn to the line, losing by a head. After a 5th in the Tenno Sho (Spring) he won the Takarazuka Kinen, but in autumn ran poorly - 4th in the All Comers, 7th in the Arima Kinen - and some said he was finished. But he won the next Hanshin Daishoten easily, and in the Tenno Sho (Spring) "Big Three" clash with Sakura Laurel and Marvelous Sunday, unlike his usual forward style, he came with a miraculous late charge to win in world-record time for 3200m. Versatile, fast and tough - a great horse. At stud he sired colorful runners like turf-and-dirt Precise Machine, dirt closer Meisho Tokon and tireless stayer Chakra.
