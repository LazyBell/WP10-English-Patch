343=Until spring at 4 he was an unremarkable horse, winning and losing dirt sprints in lower classes. But in his first turf race, the semi-open Dotonbori S., he won as 6th choice, was 3rd in the Silk Road S. (OP) and went to the Yasuda Kinen. Only 10th choice, he won by a length and a half - his first open win, in his 18th start. From then on, with a great duel with Oguri Cap in the Mile Championship and the first Sprinters S. as a G1, he built a proud record as a top sprinter.
344=Won 7 G1s, including two US classics and the Breeders' Cup Classic, and was 1988 Eclipse Horse of the Year. One of Alydar's best, he ranked 42nd in The Blood-Horse's "Top 100 US Racehorses of the 20th Century." Until spring at 3 he kept finishing 2nd and 3rd, but as 6th choice in the Kentucky Derby he finally won a G1 and graded race. Favorite for the Preakness S., he won that too for a double. He was 4th in the Belmont S., but won the Super Derby in autumn for a third G1. At 4 in 1988 he was even better, winning G1 after G1 from the Charles H. Strub S., and took the Breeders' Cup Classic to be Horse of the Year. He retired after that race. At stud he sired Moonlight Dance, winner of France's Prix Saint-Alary (G1), but fell short of expectations.
345=He won his first graded race, the Sapporo Kinen, at 4, then couldn't win and switched to jumps in November. Winning 1 of 2, he returned to the flat and led all the way to win the Niigata Daishoten as 7th choice. He then breezed in front to win the G1 Takarazuka Kinen ahead of Kamino Cresse. Jumping fences just half a year earlier, he became the third G1 winner of the Mejiro crop after McQueen and Ryan. As 15th choice in the Arima Kinen, his famous "second wind" held off Tokai Teio and the rest - a shock spring-and-year-end Grand Prix double, earning champion older male and champion Japanese-sired horse. His sire Mejiro Eagle was also a famous front-runner. A unique late bloomer, truly a Mejiro horse.
346=Based at local Iwate. He won his debut at 2, then developed a tendon injury in his left foreleg. After 19 months off he returned at 4 and won race after race, 18 straight from his debut, then a Japanese record. After one 2nd he won 11 more, including graded races like the Michinoku Daishoten, Tohoku Thoroughbred Daishoten, Kitakamigawa Daishoten, Nambu Hai and Toka Sho. At 7 he suffered his second loss to rival Moriyu Prince in the Michinoku Daishoten, then won 8 more. Leg trouble kept him from racing outside Tohoku, but 39 wins, three 2nds and a 3rd from 43, never out of the top two in 41 straight - a flawless record worthy of "the Iwate monster."
347=Maturing at 5, he won 4 G1s, including the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. and Breeders' Cup Turf, honored on both continents as Cartier Horse of the Year and Eclipse champion turf male. At 3 he ran over a mile, winning the Poule d'Essai des Poulains, but then kept finishing 2nd and 3rd. At 4 he stepped up in distance, winning the 10-furlong Eclipse S. and the Man o' War S. in America. At 5 he won 3 straight G1s: the Coronation C., the King George and the Irish Champion S. He was 9th in the Arc, far behind Montjeu and El Condor Pasa, but won the Breeders' Cup Turf to be Cartier Horse of the Year, and was the first champion of the new World Series Racing Championship. At stud his first crop included Irish Derby winner Grey Swallow, but little followed, and he was exported to South Africa.
348=2nd in his first two maidens, he won in his third start. Then as 5th choice he won the Asahi Hai Sansai S. in record-equaling time. At 3 he led all the way to win the Kyodo News Hai Yonsai S. by 3 lengths and went to the Yayoi Sho as top classic contender, but sank to 4th behind Mejiro Ryan, partly due to the ground. That year's classics were billed a Big Three: "front-running Ines Fujin, stalker Hakutaisei, closer Mejiro Ryan." As Satsuki Sho favorite he was bumped by White Stone at the start and caught near the line by Hakutaisei, losing by a neck. Slightly less fancied in the Derby, he broke well and jockey Nakano set a perfect pace, holding off Mejiro Ryan and White Stone to win in race-record time. For his "wind god" run and the superb ride of the hard-working Eiji Nakano, 200,000 fans roared "Nakano! Nakano!" - the origin of the chants still heard at G1s today. Leg trouble followed, and he retired after the Derby to stud.
349=He debuted early in the year, won 3 straight, then won the Satsuki Sho by 5 lengths. Many fans thought a father-son Triple Crown was certain. But that was as far as it went: a fracture after the Satsuki Sho ruled out the Derby, and on his return he was a dismal 5th in the rain in the St. Lite Kinen - poor heavy-ground form was a Shinzan family trait. Still, he won the Kyoto Shimbun Hai by 2 lengths and was a confident Kikuka Sho favorite, winning clearly with the bottomless stamina of his sire. He was 2nd to Symboli Rudolf in the Arima Kinen, but no one doubted his future. Yet at 4, partly due to a fracture, he went winless all year. Just when he seemed done, at 5 he won 2 graded races and then the Tenno Sho (Spring) by a whisker in a photo - a father-son Tenno Sho double.
350=5th on debut, he won next time out, then won 4 straight including the Yayoi Sho to become the classic favorite. But in the Satsuki Sho he fought his rider, his famous finish misfired, and he was 4th to Narita Taishin. Losing as overwhelming favorite shocked his camp, but it only strengthened their resolve to win the Derby. Favorite again on Derby day, he focused on settling, saving ground inside, and bet everything on the straight. He held off the charging Biwa Hayahide to cross the line first, giving jockey Masato Shibata his long-dreamed title of "Derby jockey." In autumn he was 3rd in both the Kikuka Sho and the Japan Cup, but a dismal 11th in the Arima Kinen. Winless after that, he retired with leg trouble to stud.
351=By great sire Alydar. He won his debut by 8 lengths and, with 2 wins from 3, won the Asahi Hai Sansai S. as favorite in 1:34.0, 0.4 seconds faster than Maruzensky's record - stunning for the time. Jockey Hitoshi Matoba said excitedly, "He may be better than Maruzensky." At 3 he won the Hyacinth S. easily by 4 lengths, but was 2nd to Ibuki Maikagura's late run in the Yayoi Sho. As a foreign-bred barred from the classics, he trained for the Crystal C., but injury forced retirement. With 4 wins and two 2nds from 6, he left the turf with his true worth hidden and went to stud.
352=From 2 to 7, an essential supporting player in the sprint division, winning 4 graded races including the Swan S. and CBC Sho. His Swan S. time of 1:19.3 was then the Japanese record for 1400m on turf. But in G1s his best was 2nd in the Sprinters S. at 4.
353=A French horse well known in Japan for beating El Condor Pasa in the 1999 Arc. As a sire he produced many stars like Hurricane Run and Motivator, greatly boosting the Sadler's Wells line. At 3 he romped in the French and Irish Derbies, then after the Prix Niel went to the Arc. Co-favorite with Japan's El Condor Pasa, he pulled clear of the field in the straight in a two-horse battle, catching the front-running El Condor Pasa by half a length. He then went to the Japan Cup, short of preparation, and was 4th. At 4 he won 4 straight G1s, including the Tattersalls Gold C. and the King George, but lacked sparkle in autumn, 4th in the Arc, and retired at year's end. At stud his first crop gave Hurricane Run and Motivator, making him France's leading sire, and later Derby and Irish Derby winner Camelot. Montjeu died in 2012, but his grandchildren include Motivator's Arc winner Treve, Pour Moi's Derby winner Wings of Eagles and Camelot's Irish Derby winner Latrobe - his blood lives on.
354=By great sire Sunday Silence out of Oka Sho winner Agnes Flora - a true blueblood. Slow to mature, he missed 2yo racing, but won his debut at 3. He lost next time, then won the Wakakusa S. and Kyoto Shimbun Hai to earn a Derby ticket. In the Japanese Derby, as 3rd choice, he chose his usual late charge - risky in a big G1 field - but surged sharply, dueling with Air Shakur, who also came from the back. Slightly behind entering the straight, he won the fierce battle by a nose to take the Derby. It was jockey Hiroshi Kawachi's first Derby, on his 17th try. Winning with the son of a horse he once rode to a G1 win, with the same owner and trainer, felt like a strange bond of fate.
355=Sunday Silence sired countless G1 winners, but only one was ever called "the genius": Agnes Tachyon. Full brother to Derby winner Agnes Flight, he won his first 4 starts, always with ease, becoming an unbeaten Satsuki Sho winner - a true genius. Trainer Hiroyuki Nagahama even declared a Triple Crown, but just before the Derby a severe tendon injury struck, and he retired to stud without waiting to recover. "Tachyon" is a hypothetical particle faster than light. True to his name, he flashed through his career, and the brothers' Derby double and the Triple Crown stayed hypothetical as he left the turf. A superb sire, he produced Daiwa Scarlet (Oka Sho, Shuka Sho, Queen Elizabeth II Cup, Arima Kinen), double winner Deep Sky, Satsuki Sho winner Captain Thule and Hanshin JF winner Reve d'Essor. A leading heir to the late Sunday Silence, he died suddenly of heart failure in June '09.
356=The greatest rival of seven-G1 winner T.M. Opera O. They first met in the Takarazuka Kinen at 4: Doto came in after winning the Kinko Sho, Opera O on 3 straight graded wins including the Tenno Sho (Spring). In his first G1, Doto was 2nd by a neck. In autumn he won the All Comers and went into the Tenno Sho (Autumn) fully prepared, but lost to Opera O again in 2nd - and the same in the Japan Cup and Arima Kinen: 4 straight G1 2nds, a perfect foil to Opera O's march. At 5 he won the Nikkei Sho, then took on Opera O in the Tenno Sho (Spring), only to trail him yet again - a strange record of the same 1-2 in 5 straight G1s. After a full year of chasing him, in the Takarazuka Kinen he raced aggressively, with sheer tenacity, and finally beat Opera O. But the win didn't reverse their roles; both declined from there.
357=Called Northern Taste's last star. Injury-prone, he had many long breaks, but at 5 he won 4 straight to reach open class, and at 7 won his first graded race, the Fuji S., giving his sire a record 20 straight crops with a graded winner.
358="The grey monster" who flashed through just 10 races with explosive speed and power on turf or dirt. He won the NHK Mile C. in spring at 3 for his first crown, then ran in the Japanese Derby, the first foreign-bred ever to do so, finishing a fine 5th. In autumn, after a 3rd in the Kobe Shimbun Hai, he was excluded from the Tenno Sho (Autumn), and instead ran in the Musashino S., beating Eagle Cafe by 9 lengths - stunning for his first dirt race - in an astonishing 1:33.3, 1.2 seconds under the record. In the Japan Cup Dirt he treated the best from home and abroad like children, winning easily in another record. Many hoped he'd go abroad, but a tendon injury ended that dream. At stud he sired many G1 winners, led by 2-time sprint G1 winner Curren Chan, plus Sleepless Night, Whale Capture and Fusaichi Richard. Recently his filly Aerolithe won the NHK Mile C. leading at a fast pace and ran well in many G1s against colts. He also sired Sodashi, the first white turf G1 winner, and white graded winner Yukichan. He retired from stud in 2019; his last crop's Mamakocha won the Sprinters S., giving him JRA graded winners 19 years running (the streak ended in 2024). He died of old age at 23 in 2021.
