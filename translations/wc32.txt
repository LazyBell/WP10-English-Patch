313=Seen as Storm Cat's leading heir, he sired many G1 winners like Shamardal and Aragorn and greatly boosted the Storm Cat line. Based in Ireland, he won 6 G1s. At 2 he won the Prix de la Salamandre (G1) unbeaten. At 3 he was 2nd in both the 2000 Guineas and Irish 2000 Guineas, then reeled off 5 G1s: the St. James's Palace S., Eclipse S., Sussex S., International S. and Irish Champion S. In autumn he was 2nd in the Breeders' Cup Classic, showing he could handle dirt. That year he lost Cartier champion 3yo colt to Sinndar but was Cartier Horse of the Year - a split decision. At stud his first crop included French Derby winner Shamardal, and he sired G1 winners every year; in Japan, A Shin Apollon won the Mile CS. He is damsire of Lemon Pop, 6-time G1 winner and champion dirt horse of 2023 and 2024.
314=He debuted late, in July at 3 at local Urawa, and won 5 straight with overwhelming power. 2nd in his 6th start, he won twice more, but then suffered a fracture that nearly ended his career. Back after 15 months, in May at 5, he won, and with 8 wins and a 2nd from 9 he moved to the JRA. Puzzled by turf at first, he was 2nd, then won in the 9-million class and in semi-open class, proving his Urawa record was real. In open class he lost 3 graded races, but romped in the Ireland T. over a mile, then won the Mile Championship with a late charge - his first graded win, a G1. At 7 he won the Tokyo Shimbun Hai and, after the Keio Hai Spring C., the Yasuda Kinen - autumn and spring mile G1s in a row, making him 8 for 8 at a mile including Urawa. But after the Yasuda Kinen, a dispute over the ownership name forced his retirement while still at his best - an ill-fated star whose fate was decided by people's affairs.
315=Switched to jockey Mirco Demuro, he won the Spring S., a Satsuki Sho trial, clearly. With Demuro again he ran down Sakura President to win the Satsuki Sho, then in the Derby caught front-running Zenno Rob Roy by half a length, horse and rider as one - the first Japanese Derby won by a foreign jockey. He then ran 4th in the Takarazuka Kinen, unusual for a Derby winner. Triple Crown hopes rested on the Kikuka Sho, but Demuro had used up his short-term license days. The JRA, however, made a special rule allowing a foreign jockey who had won 2 G1s on the same horse that year to ride it. Even so, he was only 3rd. The next spring he was a dismal 10th in the Tenno Sho (Spring), then developed a tendon injury and retired before settling scores with older horses. At stud his first crop gave Derby winner Logi Universe and Satsuki Sho winner Unrivaled, and his second Dubai World Cup winner Victoire Pisa - many big-name offspring.
316=At about 430 kg, slim and small like a filly, he formed a Big Three with Winning Ticket and Biwa Hayahide to light up the classics. His razor-sharp late charge from that small frame overpowered both rivals in an instant to win the Satsuki Sho. After a 3rd in the Derby he took on older horses in the Takamatsunomiya Hai (G2, 2000m), finishing 2nd. Then illness hit and he went straight to the Kikuka Sho; 3rd choice on his record, he was a dismal 17th. In his first race at 4 he regained his old finish to win the Meguro Kinen, and was 2nd to Biwa Hayahide's flawless run in the Tenno Sho (Spring). But injury forced a long break, and back after a year he was 16th in the Takarazuka Kinen, his last race. At stud he left no notable offspring.
317=He won his debut and the Sumire S., then made the Derby his third start - an unheard-of schedule, chosen because he was frail and needed the shortest route. In the Derby straight, favorite Dance in the Dark surged from a good spot; just as everyone was sure he'd win, Fusaichi Concorde unleashed a "supersonic" kick and ran him down wide. Fans called the 3-for-3 Derby winner "Japan's Lammtarra." In autumn he took another odd route, the Cassiopeia S. then the Kikuka Sho, running well but losing both. He then developed periostitis and retired after just 5 races. At stud he sired stars like Blue Concorde, Balance of Game and Osumi Haruka.
318=He won the Asahi Hai Sansai S. easily to top his generation, then the Spring S. at 3. Just as he set off on the royal road, a fracture wiped out his spring. Back in autumn, he ignored the Kikuka Sho and stunned everyone by aiming for the Tenno Sho (Autumn). After a 3rd in the Mainichi Okan on his return, he won the big one against stars like Mayano Top Gun and Sakura Laurel - the first 3yo ever to win the Tenno Sho. He sank to 13th in the Japan Cup, but that was his only finish worse than 3rd in his career. At 4 he kept finishing a narrow 2nd and won no G1, but all agreed he was the best of his crop. At stud he sired many dirt runners, like Appare Appare, Meiner Bownot and Ono Yu. He died of pneumonia in April 2010.
319=Danzig's most successful heir. As a racehorse he did little beyond winning Britain's Sprint C., but at stud he blossomed into one of the top sires. A rare "shuttle sire" then, he covered mares in Ireland in the northern spring and Australia in the southern spring, siring Irish Derby winner Desert King and Australian champion 2yo Flying Spur and spreading his blood worldwide. Heirs like Danehill Dancer and Dansili also succeeded, forming the mighty Danehill line. From 2005 he broke Sadler's Wells's grip to be champion sire in Britain and Ireland 3 years running, and was champion sire 9 times in Australia. He stood in Japan for just one year, 1996, siring graded winners like Fusaichi Sonic and Break Time.
320=Won 4 G1s in America, including the Haskell Invitational H. and Travers S., and as a sire produced many G1 winners in America and Japan - a notable heir of Mr. Prospector. At 2 he won two G1s, the Futurity S. and Champagne S., and was Eclipse champion 2yo colt. At 3 he was narrowly 2nd to the filly Winning Colors in the Kentucky Derby and 7th in the Preakness S., but won the Haskell and Travers in summer for 4 G1s. In autumn he took on older star Alysheba but fell short, 2nd in the Woodward S. and 4th in the Breeders' Cup Classic, and retired at the end of his 3yo year. At stud he sired G1 winners from his first crop and was North America's leading sire in 1996. He was sold to Japan before that, where he sired many local stars like Meiner Select and Utopia. His American sons End Sweep and Coronado's Quest were also imported to Japan as sires, spreading his line there.
321=By great sire Mr. Prospector out of 10-time G1 winner Miesque, a blueblood and hugely successful heir to Mr. Prospector. He won the Poule d'Essai des Poulains, the St. James's Palace S. and the Prix du Moulin de Longchamp, a speedster typical of his sire. At stud his first crop included El Condor Pasa, and he sired many stars like Belmont S. winner Lemon Drop Kid, future top sire King's Best and Alkaased, who won the Japan Cup in 2005. His 2001 son King Kamehameha succeeded hugely as both racehorse and sire, and his descendants flourish in Japan, where his line is sometimes treated as a separate "Kingmambo line." Due to a chronic illness he retired from stud in 2010 and died at 26 in 2016.
322=From Sunday Silence's first crop, he missed the classics through injury. Returning in spring at 4, he won 6 straight from a 9-million special, including 4 graded races: the Epsom C., Sapporo Kinen, Asahi Challenge C. and Kyoto Daishoten - a true "late-arriving star." He was 4th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and 2nd in the Arima Kinen, winning no crown at 4, but the next spring he was rated in a Big Three with Sakura Laurel and Mayano Top Gun. In the Takarazuka Kinen he shut out Bubble Gum Fellow, Dance Partner, Taiki Blizzard and others to win a G1. At stud he sired Silk Famous, a "late-arriving star" just like him who won 5 straight at 4 including 2 graded races and was 2nd in the Takarazuka Kinen, and Nikkei Sho winner Never Bouchon.
323=Winner of the 1997 Arc, called one of Europe's best of the 1990s. He was 3rd in his first graded race, the Prix des Chenes at 2, but at 3 won the Prix Greffulhe, the French Derby and the Grand Prix de Paris, then after a 2nd in the Prix Niel went to the Arc. Against stars like last year's winner Helissio, Pilsudski, Swain and Oscar Schindler, he was a clear favorite. He burst clear powerfully in the straight and won by 5 lengths from Pilsudski, in a stunning record 1.7 seconds faster than before. He was named Cartier Horse of the Year and rated 137 lbs in the International Classification, second only to Dancing Brave. A tendon injury then ended his career, but at stud he had some success, siring 2004 Santa Anita Derby winner Castledale, 2006 Arc runner-up Pride and German Derby winner Dai Jin.
324=He won his first graded race, the Arlington C., then was 8th in the Mainichi Hai, partly due to the distance. Giving up on the classics, he went to the NHK Mile C. As 9th choice, low due to his erratic record, he raced boldly forward and won. After that he slumped badly, winless after the NHK Mile C. With no sign of revival, in '06 he had surgery for colic and a long recovery. He returned in the '07 Lord Derby Challenge T. but lacked sparkle, finishing in double figures in open races. In July he switched to jumping and won a maiden hurdle, his first win in 4 years. He was a fine 4th in the Hanshin Jump S., showing promise over fences, but retired in November. At stud he sired Ever Rose, who won 3 straight 1000m straight-course races in lower classes.
325=The late trainer Tameo Toyama trained his horses with Spartan rigor, believing "you build the best horse through training." Mihono Bourbon was the ultimate masterpiece of that belief. With speed honed on the hill course, he won his debut, his powerful late burst making light of a slow start - a brilliant opening to a legend. He then led all the way in a 5-million race, and held off Yamanin Miracle by a nose in the Asahi Hai Sansai S., unbeaten 2yo champion. From 3, doubts about distance, jockey and more swirled before every race, but his power never wavered: never letting rivals near, he won the Satsuki Sho and the Derby, an unbeaten double. Triple Crown hopes rested on the Kikuka Sho, but he was 2nd to Rice Shower, who emerged as a stayer. Leg trouble then forced a break, and he never returned to the turf.
326=In his debut at Hakodate over 1800m on good turf, he was 6th choice of 6, yet came from last to win in a remarkable 2:01.6. He won his first graded race, the Radio Tampa Hai Sansai S., but in the classics, always well backed, he stuck to late charges and kept losing narrowly. Then at year's end, with jockey Hiroshi Kawachi aboard for the Stayers S., he raced from a good spot and won by a distance. Having learned to win, he kept rolling the next year, winning the American JCC and Hanshin Daishoten, then the Tenno Sho (Spring). Crop-mate Silk Justice, last year's Arima Kinen winner, was favorite, but Bright won with a champion's authority - 4 straight graded wins, his long-sought Tenno Sho, and three generations of G1 winners after grandsire Amber Shadai and sire Mejiro Ryan. With the year-younger "greatest generation" starring, he won just one more G2. He raced until 6 and went to stud, but died of a heart attack in spring '04.
