597=A highly rated foreign-bred by Mr. Prospector. 7th in the Fairy S. in her 2nd start, she won the Kobushi Sho and Elfin S. to reach open class. Two races later came the NZT Yonsai S. Against Yamanin Paradise, unbeaten in 3 in record times through the Hanshin Sansai Himba S., graded winner Go Go Nakayama and Kokuto Julian, she held firm and beat the charging Presto Symboli by a neck.
598=She won her first 2 and drew attention, but was 10th in the Oka Sho. A romp in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East) kept her Oaks dream alive, but injury ended it. In autumn she was 4th in the Rose S. and 9th in the Shuka Sho, and retired to breed after 3 more races.
599=After winning her debut she was expected to star in the classics but couldn't win. In summer she won again, then 3 straight including the Sapphire S., and was 2nd to Sakura Candle by three-quarters of a length in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. Back after 7 months in local summer racing, she won the Hakodate Kinen. In autumn she ran in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, newly open to older mares, but finished 12th and retired.
600=Her granddam Talented Girl denied Max Beauty the 1987 fillies' Triple Crown in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. She debuted on dirt at Hanshin in June at 2, 4th as favorite, then won on turf next time and was 2nd in the Phoenix Sho. She stalled in 3 graded races, best 7th, but in the Falcon S., as 4th choice, she waited at the back and powered home to win. On the classic sprint route she was 11th in the NZ Trophy and 6th in the NHK Mile C., and a fracture of her right radius followed. Back in sprints she won the Shunrai S. (OP), and at 7 switched to jumping, winning graded races like the Niigata Jump S.
601=She won both the Oka Sho and Oaks trials, and in the Oaks ran a fine 2nd, three-quarters of a length behind Chokai Carol. A one-paced closer, she still reliably finished near the top whatever the race, winning many fans. Retiring in spring at 6, her first foal Sidewinder (98: Tony Bin) won 3 graded races including the Keihan Hai - a decent record as a dam.
602=Not top class, but blessed with a sharp kick, tough speed, a cool head in traffic and versatility. 5th in the Oka Sho and Oaks and 6th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, she was no classic star, but won 4 graded races, 3 against colts, 2 as an older mare - no ordinary filly. Her Kimpai (West) at 4 became a legend of handicapping: 9 horses abreast at the line. She won by three-quarters of a length with her grit, but 3rd was a dead heat by a nose, and every place down to 8th was decided by a nose - an unprecedented blanket finish.
603=She won the Hanshin Sansai Himba S. as 9th choice, part of a 120,000-yen quinella, but did nothing afterwards and retired after the next Queen Elizabeth II Cup. As a dam she produced '99 Kokura Daishoten and Naruo Kinen winner Suehiro Commander (95: Commander in Chief).
604=Winning a semi-open race at 5, she went to the Epsom C. as 3rd choice and won, jockey Tsunoda's first graded win. In autumn she was 3rd in the Mile Championship. As a dam she produced Tabatha Tosho (93: Dancing Brave), dam of Sweep Tosho.
605=In the fillies' classics she was beaten every time: 11th in the Oka Sho, 14th in the Oaks, 7th in the Shuka Sho. As an older mare she raced around a mile, winning the Sekiya Kinen in summer at 4, then the Nakayama Himba S. and Fukushima Himba S. for 3 graded wins.
606=She won the Queen C. at 3, then was 3rd in the Oka Sho and 5th in the Oaks - real talent. Her form dipped from autumn at 3, but she won a semi-open race in spring '04 and the Oro C. open special that autumn. After a heavy loss in the Hanshin Himba S. at year's end she slumped and retired without results.
607=She won her debut and was never out of the top two in 1 win and three 2nds before the Oka Sho. Her speed was rare, especially early, and she often ended up leading on superior ability. Her temperament played a part: as when she was crushed from an inside draw in the Oka Sho, she often couldn't show her ability in big fields. She won the Centaur S. in a record 1:22.1 at the end of her 4yo year and retired. Passing her speed on, she produced 3 graded winners: '00 Yayoi Sho winner Fusaichi Zenon (97: Sunday Silence), '01 Spring S. and Kisaragi Sho winner Agnes Gold (98: Sunday Silence) and 7-time dirt graded winner Limitless Bid (99: Sunday Silence).
608=Crushed in the Yayoi Sho and NZ Trophy, he stalled at 3 despite high hopes. Maturing at 4, he won the Amethyst S. (semi-open) and Chukyo Kinen, but just as things took off, he went on a year-long break. Back, he ran well in open specials for a long career, his last race the Miyakooji S. (3rd) in May '09.
609=She won in her 2nd start but was crushed in the TV Tokyo Sho Sansai Himba S. Slow to rise, she won a 4-million race in April, then via the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East) went to the Oaks. 2nd choice in a field without a standout, she was 5th. In autumn she won the Queen S., her first graded race, but was 7th as 2nd choice in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. As an older mare her talent bloomed, beating colts in 3 graded races: the Kimpai (East), Argentina Kyowakoku Hai and American JCC.
610=Half-sister of Katsuragi Ace. Steady in allowance races with 6 wins, three 2nds and three 3rds, she won an open race, the Fukushima Minyu C., in November at 5. She struggled at first - 10th in the Fukushima Kinen, 11th in the Hanshin Himba Tokubetsu - but at 6 was 2nd in the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu, then won the Nakayama Himba S., her first graded race. As a dam she left no notable offspring.
611=She won her debut and a 5-million race and aimed for the classics at 3, but lost momentum, 5th in both the Queen C. and Flora S., and missed the spring classics. She then won the JRA-local Kanto Oaks. After a summer break she was even better: 3rd in the Queen S., then 2nd to Fine Motion in both the Rose S. and Shuka Sho. Big things were hoped for, but after a 6th in the next Kyoto Himba S. she retired to breed.
612=She won her debut by over 3 lengths, then the Niigata Sansai S. and Keisei Hai Sansai S., 2 graded wins unbeaten and high hopes. But she was 3rd to Elpus in the TV Tokyo Sho Sansai Himba S., her first loss, and a fracture found afterwards cost her about a year. She ran well in graded races - 2nd in the Kitakyushu Kinen and Keio Hai Autumn H., 3rd in the Kinko Sho and Niigata Daishoten - without winning, until the Tanabata Sho at 5, her first win in 2 years 8 months.
613=He debuted in autumn at 2 over 1600m at Tokyo and won in a decent 1:35.4, then in the Tokyo Sports Hai 2yo S. closed in 33.7 for a close 3rd behind Nakayama Festa and Break Run Out. As favorite in the Hiiragi Sho and Junior C. his late charges fell short, 3rd both times. Prone to lugging in, he ran with his tongue tied in the Crocus S., his 5th start, and won well. 4th to Unrivaled in the Spring S., he went to the NZ Trophy, prep for his spring goal, the NHK Mile C., and powered past Falcon S. winner Joe Cappuccino and the rest to win - a new sprint star. But in the NHK Mile C. he wobbled in the straight and hampered others; finishing 8th, he was demoted to 18th. As an older horse he was 4th, 2nd, 2nd and 9th in the Takamatsunomiya Kinen and 3rd, 7th, 6th and 8th in the Sprinters S. - just short of a G1 - but ran in the Hanshin C. 6 years running, winning it in '11 and '12, lighting up recent sprinting.
614=She won 1 of 3 on dirt with two 2nds, then in her first turf race, the Flower C., won by two and a half lengths as 8th choice. She ran above her odds in the spring classics: 3rd in the Oka Sho, 4th in the Oaks. After a summer break she won the Queen S. as favorite in autumn, but was injured. As a Real Shadai filly her best was still to come, but she had to retire. As a dam she produced Sing Like Talk (92: Northern Taste).
615=Rated a speedster, she was favorite for the Hanshin Sansai Himba S. but 3rd behind Biwa Heidi and Air Groove. She won the Queen C. at 3 and sought revenge in the Oka Sho, but lost to Fight Gulliver by half a length. She never won again.
616=Tough, she raced until the end of her 6yo year, winning 6 of 43. Her steady form, never badly beaten in any conditions, was her strength, and at 5 she won her long-sought first graded race, the Kokura Kinen. As a dam she produced '03 Nikkei Shinshun Hai winner Bamboo Juventus (99: Mayano Top Gun).
617=His gleaming black coat recalled his sire Symboli Kris S. On debut in autumn at 2 at Tokyo, he swooped from the back to beat future Tenno Sho winner Hiruno d'Amour. In the Hopeful S. he pulled away from a good spot like a star, 2 for 2 and the East's top classic hope. In his first graded race, the Kyodo News Hai, he was a close 3rd to Hansode Band and Danon Chantilly, his first loss, but in the Spring S. he led alone and shut out 2yo champion Rose Kingdom and the rest. 3rd choice for the Satsuki Sho, he lacked a final kick and was 5th. Down to 6th choice for the Derby, he led at a slow pace but couldn't match the astonishing finishes, 13th. He later won the Mainichi Okan, among the best of his crop, but leg trouble led to retirement in autumn at 5.
618=She beat Dance Partner by a nose in the Tulip Sho to become a classic contender. In spring she was 5th in the Oka Sho and 2nd in the Oaks. 13th in the Rose S. in autumn, she was less fancied for the Queen Elizabeth II Cup but boldly raced forward and held on for 5th.
619=Just 2 wins, her debut and the Daily Hai Sansai S., but she finished outside the top five only in her last race, the Queen Elizabeth II Cup - a 3rd in the Shuka Sho among her steady runs. Her first foal Rose Bud (98: Sunday Silence) outdid her, 2nd in the Oaks, Shuka Sho and Queen Elizabeth II Cup, and Rosenkreuz (02: Sunday Silence) won 3 middle-distance graded races including the Kinko Sho - like her dam Rosa Nay, a great broodmare in the making.
620=In a chaotic fillies' classic year, Arahoutoku was 2nd in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West), then as 5th choice won the Oka Sho from a good spot. She won the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East) too, but as Oaks favorite was 7th behind outsider Cosmo Dream, and retired after a 4th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup.
621=Her sire Buzen Dai O was registered as a stallion but used as a teaser, with just 2 foals that year. Yet in the Oaks, which even her connections didn't expect, she won with apprentice Shigefumi Kumazawa. She later ran well in older graded races like the Takamatsunomiya Hai and Kyoto Daishoten.
622=Tamamo Cross's sister. With Oaks winner Cosmo Dream fractured and Oka Sho winner Arahoutoku off form, she won a Queen Elizabeth II Cup lacking a standout, by a nose from Shiyono Roman. Two weeks earlier her brother had won the Tenno Sho (Autumn) - a brother-sister G1 double.
623=She beat the colts in the Shinzan Kinen and was 3rd in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West). 7th in the Oka Sho, she won the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East) and was a fine 4th in the Oaks. Perhaps due to her plain pedigree, she was never favorite in 25 starts despite her record - a long-shot fans' idol. Tough and injury-free, she kept livening up graded races, winning the Asahi Challenge C. at Chukyo at 4. As a dam she produced 8-time winner Fundori Ryoma (93: Mogami).
624=Winning her debut by 9 lengths, she was hailed as a big foreign-bred star. After some stumbles she teamed with Yutaka Take to lead all the way in the Kyoto Yonsai Tokubetsu - her 2nd win, a graded one. Brushing aside colts in what was then the last big Derby trial was impressive, but she then stopped improving, her form slipping until her later years brought heavy defeats far from her star image. As a dam she produced Diamond S. winner Monte Kris S (05: Symboli Kris S.).
625=One-paced, needing the race to suit, she still won 2 local graded races at 5, the Niigata Daishoten and Niigata Kinen. As a broodmare, her 5th foal Heart's Cry (01: Sunday Silence) won the '05 Arima Kinen and '06 Dubai Sheema Classic.
626=Debuting late, she missed the fillies' classics, winning 3 of 11 at 3. She reached open class at 4 but couldn't win. Maturing at 5, she was 2nd in the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu, won the Kinko Sho for her first graded win, then the Fuchu Himba S. The year-end Hanshin Himba Tokubetsu drew four G1 winners: Hokuto Vega, Dance Partner, Wonder Perfume and Sakura Candle. Having walked the back roads, Samani Beppin was only 6th choice. But she unleashed a 34.7 finish, swooping widest of all to cut down the glamorous G1 winners together - surely a win worth a G1 to her and her connections.
