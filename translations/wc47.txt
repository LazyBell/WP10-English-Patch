690=The second unbeaten Triple Crown winner ever, with 7 G1 wins, then equal most; leading sire 11 years running, with classic winners in all 13 crops - a modern legend who rewrote Japanese racing history. He won his debut with an astonishing 33.1 finish and romped by 5 lengths in the Wakakoma S. with a furious kick, quickly becoming the classic favorite. In the Yayoi Sho he had trouble catching the forward-running contenders, but caught Admire Japan by a neck at the line for his first graded win; his ability was now beyond doubt. In the Satsuki Sho he stumbled right after the start and lost a lot of ground, but powered home through the worst of situations, two and a half lengths clear at the line. From that moment the Derby was all but assured: he won it by 5 lengths in record-equaling time, then ran down front-running Admire Japan in the Kikuka Sho for an unbeaten Triple Crown. In his last race at 3, the Arima Kinen, he was 2nd to Heart's Cry, ending his unbeaten run, but at 4 he won the Hanshin Daishoten, Tenno Sho (Spring) and Takarazuka Kinen in a class of his own. In autumn he went straight to the Arc, but his kick misfired, a disappointing 3rd. Then a mistaken use of a banned substance came to light and he was disqualified from the Arc, casting a dark cloud. It was Deep Impact himself who blew it away, winning the Japan Cup and Arima Kinen with overwhelming power - truly "the best run," silencing all noise with ability. With 7 G1s, equal with Symboli Rudolf and T.M. Opera O for the JRA record, he retired at the end of 2006 to stud. His first crop gave Yasuda Kinen winner Real Impact and Oka Sho winner Marcellina. In 2012 his second crop's Deep Brillante won the Derby and Gentildonna the fillies' Triple Crown; after just 3 crops he was leading sire, and he reigned unchallenged, siring many G1 winners every year. Then in July 2019 he died young, at 17, news reported not only in Japan but abroad. In honor of his great achievements, the Yayoi Sho was renamed the "Hochi Hai Yayoi Sho Deep Impact Kinen." His JRA wins as a sire passed his sire Sunday Silence's for No. 1 all-time, and his 71 JRA G1 wins (end of 2024) tie Sunday Silence's record. He sired 7 Japanese Derby winners, and his last crop even produced Irish-born Auguste Rodin, winner of the Derby and Irish Derby. His "impact" will carry on to the next generation and beyond.
