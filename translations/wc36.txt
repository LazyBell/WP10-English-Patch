377=Based at local Funabashi. Unbeaten in his first 8, he won the Southern Kanto triple crown and the national G1 Japan Dirt Derby - a superhorse. The first triple crown winner since the great mare Rosita was big news for dirt racing, but after the Japan Dirt Derby a fracture was found and he rested 5 months. On his return his old dominance was gone, but he still ran 2nd to Agnes Digital in the February S. and won the Kashiwa Kinen. From 5 he ran well only now and then, finishing 12th in another February S., showing decline. He raced until 7 but never regained his shine.
378=A versatile star, top-class on both turf and dirt, with a multi-surface career beyond all convention. At 2 he won the Zen-Nihon Sansai Yushun. At 3 he went the turf mile route in spring without winning, then won 2 graded races on dirt. Seen as a future dirt leader, he ran in the turf Mile Championship instead. With all his wins on dirt, he was 13th choice, but a powerful late charge from the back beat the older horses - his first turf win, a G1. The next autumn he returned to dirt to win the Nambu Hai, then back on turf beat seven-G1 winner T.M. Opera O in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), and at year's end won the Hong Kong Cup. He then won the February S., 4 straight G1s across local, JRA turf, overseas and JRA dirt. Trips to the Dubai World Cup and Hong Kong's QE2 Cup brought no wins, and injury cost him a year, but the legend wasn't over: after a poor comeback run, he won the Yasuda Kinen in race-record time. There may never be another like him. At stud he sired a varied group: dirt runners Daishin Orange, Ubiquitous and Kaze no Ko, turf middle-distance Yamanin Kingly and turf sprinter Grand Prix Angel.
379=Slow to rise, he missed the spring, but emerged in summer and went to the Kikuka Sho. Outsider Meiner Despot seemed set to win unchallenged in front, but Manhattan Cafe caught him right at the line for his first crown. His momentum carried into the Arima Kinen, where his powerful finish beat strong older horses like T.M. Opera O. The next year he beat crop rival Jungle Pocket to win the Tenno Sho (Spring). That autumn he tried the Arc but was injured during the race and finished 13th. At stud he had no stars at first, but went on to sire NHK Mile C. winner Joe Cappuccino, Tenno Sho winner Hiruno d'Amour, Red Desire, who beat Buena Vista in the Shuka Sho, and 2013 February S. winner Grape Brandy. He was leading sire in 2009.
380=A leading figure of the In Reality line. He was the first to win the Breeders' Cup Classic twice, and as a sire produced many stars like Colonel John. He debuted late, in April at 3, and won his first graded race only after the classics ended. In autumn he won his first G1, the Super Derby, then won the Breeders' Cup Classic after a fierce duel with Giant's Causeway, earning Eclipse Horse of the Year. At 4 he won the Santa Anita H. for a third G1, but then kept losing narrowly and was less fancied when he returned to the Breeders' Cup Classic. Against stars like Sakhee and Galileo, he won a duel with Sakhee in the straight by a nose - the first to win the race twice. He retired after it. At stud his first crop included Dubai World Cup winner Well Armed, and G1 winners kept coming - a central force of Man o' War's direct line.
381=He won his first 2 starts, but a fracture cost him 10 months. Back, he won twice, then a tendon injury cost him another 13 months. Returning in March at 5, he won a semi-open race and then the Keihan Hai, his first graded win. In the Takarazuka Kinen he was 2nd choice as "the late-arriving star," and lived up to it, winning his first G1 in record time. Sadly for him, Rice Shower broke down fatally in that race, leaving the winner little remembered.
382=He ran in all three classics, finishing 6th, 9th and 6th. Improving after the Kikuka Sho, he won his long-sought graded race, the Kimpai (East), early at 4. That autumn in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) he fought a legendary 300m duel with Yamanin Zephyr. He lost by a nose, but it was the race where his true ability showed best. The next year he won the Tokyo Shimbun Hai for a second graded win, but in another Tenno Sho (Autumn) he lost to Nehai Caesar, 2nd again.
383=Little as a racehorse, but a huge success at stud, champion sire in Argentina 10 times - called "the Sunday Silence of the Southern Hemisphere" in Japan. Racing in America he won no graded race; his best were 2nds in the G1 Swaps S. and Super Derby. But valued for his Halo bloodline, then booming, he went to stud in Argentina, where he sired G1 winners one after another and was champion sire 8 seasons running from 94/95, plus 03/04 and 07/08 - 10 in all. Hailed there as greater than the historic sire Congreve, his line now dominates more than Sunday Silence's does in Japan. As a shuttle sire he also produced G1 winners in North America like More Than Ready. He was leased to Japan for a year as a relative of Sunday Silence, but sired no stars there.
384=Based at local Iwate. He won 9 straight including the Tohoku Derby, then was beaten badly in the Derby Grand Prix and Super Dirt Derby. But he grew steadily, and in autumn at 4 beat Southern Kanto king Abukuma Poro in the Nambu Hai for his first G1. The next year he took on the JRA's February S., overpowering Taiki Sherlock, M.I. Blanc and others like a champion - the first JRA G1 won by a local-based horse. After 5 more wins including the Teio Sho, he tried for his long-sought Tokyo Daishoten but was crushed in 11th. Still, he was overwhelmingly voted '99 NAR Horse of the Year. The next year he went for another February S. but was 4th behind new JRA stars like Wing Arrow and Gold Tiara.
385=He showed ability at 3, finishing 4th in the NHK Hai (G2, 2000m turf), but then stalled, reaching open class only in spring at 5 - a late bloomer. At 6 he won the Nakayama Kimpai on his 8th graded try, then, as if defying his low 6th-choice rating, beat Taiki Sherlock and others by 4 lengths in the February S. for a G1. He went to stud but sired no stars.
386=The best son of top sprinter Sakura Bakushin O, in whom his sire's genes truly bloomed. Raced only on dirt early, he was erratic and slow to rise, but switched to turf as an older horse he showed overwhelming speed, as if his past were a lie, leading all the way to win the Yamashiro S. and Ocean S. with ease. Riding that momentum into the sprint G1 Takamatsunomiya Kinen, his superior speed let him lead all the way, never letting anyone near - a G1 winner. He went to Hong Kong at year's end but, perhaps unsettled by travel and tired from a hard year, ran unlike himself and was 10th. At stud he sired NZ Trophy winner Shonan Achieve.
387=By European champion Dancing Brave out of Goodbye Halo, a 7-time G1 winner in America - a true blueblood. After winning his first 3, a G1 seemed imminent, but his temper held him back, with narrow losses led by a 2nd in the Satsuki Sho. As an older horse he won the Tokyo Shimbun Hai and Nakayama Kinen, but was crushed in the Yasuda Kinen and Takarazuka Kinen, and in autumn was 2nd in the Mile Championship and 3rd in the Sprinters S. - always short of a G1. At 5 he finally won the Takamatsunomiya Kinen, on his 11th G1 try. A good sire too, he produced fillies' double winner Kawakami Princess (03: Takano Secretary) and 2-time sprint G1 winner Laurel Guerreiro (04: Big Ten Bee).
388=After a 7th in the Derby he won 3 straight - an allowance race, the Kobe Shimbun Hai and Kyoto Shimbun Hai - drawing attention as the year's top late improver. Doubts about the Kikuka Sho distance were raised, but the slow pace set up a sprint in the straight, ideal for closers. His astonishing 33.9 finish far outran fellow closers Mejiro Bright, Silk Justice and Tokio Excellent, and he became a classic winner on a 4-race streak. But he never won again, retiring after the Takarazuka Kinen at 6.
389=Often mistaken for a son of Lyphard because of his name, his sire was actually Relaunch, of the Man o' War line. He won 3 graded races on turf and dirt, but fell just short in G1s. He went to stud, but his offspring did poorly.
390=Repeated fractures cost him chance after chance. When he overcame his third, he was already in autumn at 5, late in a racehorse's career, but in his comeback, the Mile Championship, he ran as well as ever, then beat sprint queen Flower Park in the CBC Sho in record time. Then came the Sprinters S.: after a long photo, he lost to Flower Park by just 1 cm, missing his long-sought G1 - an epic struggle for the history books.
391=His first win came in mid-March at 3, in his 7th start. He seemed too late for the spring classics, but won the Wakakusa S. and Kyoto Yonsai Tokubetsu and went to the Derby, just failing to catch front-runner Sunny Brian but finishing 2nd, ahead of favorite Mejiro Bright - among the best of his crop. Then, after winning the Kyoto Daishoten, he was 5th in both the Kikuka Sho and Japan Cup, his late-charging style leaving him just short. In the Arima Kinen he abandoned the late charge, moving early from the third turn and powering through the straight from midfield. Less sharp than usual, but with a superb burst, he beat strong older horses clearly - a G1 just 9 months after his first win.
392=A leading classic contender, but 2nd in the Satsuki Sho, 2nd in the Derby, 5th in the Kikuka Sho - narrow losses. He was 5th in the Mile Championship too, settling into a supporting role. But after a 2nd in the next Yasuda Kinen, he finally won his long-sought G1 in the Takarazuka Kinen. The next year a tendon injury forced him to retire to stud - or so it seemed, until in autumn '04, 16 months later, he suddenly returned at local Arao. He ran 4 times without a win and retired in June '05.
393=Watched as one of Fuji Kiseki's first crop, he crushed A Shin Preston by 4 lengths on debut. The next year he won the Shinzan Kinen and Spring S. and was Satsuki Sho favorite. Breaking clear from a good spot, he had the crown in sight but was outdueled by the charging Air Shakur, a narrow 2nd. After a heavy defeat in the Derby he ran well mostly at a mile, winning 3 graded races including the Naruo Kinen and two Kyoto Kimpai, but lost narrowly to Agnes Digital in the Mile Championship and never won a G1.
394=A rare graded winner by Miho Shinzan. With a powerful kick from his grandsire Shinzan, famed for his "hatchet-sharp" finish, he romped in the NHK Hai (G2, 2000m turf) and ran a fine 5th in the Derby. Just as he seemed set to rise as his sire's heir, leg trouble forced a long break. More mishaps followed, though he still won the Asahi Challenge C., showing real ability. But he couldn't beat his leg problems and retired at 5, to much regret. At stud he left no heir and was retired... Shinzan's line ended here.
