129=* Apapane: A Triple Crown Won by "Studying at Ritto"\nApapane is named after a beautiful red Hawaiian bird, a nod to her sire King Kamehameha, named for the king who united Hawaii. She was 3rd on debut, then after over three months off gained a whopping 24 kg and won her maiden with a powerful run that belied her cute name. She won the Akamatsu Sho in a 2yo course record, then the Hanshin JF, and was unanimously champion 2yo filly.\nIn 2010, at 3, the Miho-based Apapane moved to Ritto in mid-February for training. Her trainer Kunieda devised this to spare horses the stress of shipping right before a race - a method called "studying at Ritto," first used with her dam Salty Bid. She was 2nd in the Tulip Sho at Hanshin but stayed at Ritto for the Oka Sho, which she won with a sharp run from up front in race-record time.\nFor the Oaks there were doubts about the distance: she had never run beyond 1800m and often fought her rider. She raced in midfield and surged wide in the straight together with Flora S. (Oaks trial) winner Saint Emilion. Apapane got clear about 200m out, but Saint Emilion fought back on the inside and drew level at the line. After a 12-minute photo, it was a dead heat - a first in JRA G1 history. Winning her second classic this way, she was the first Kanto-based fillies' double winner in 24 years, since Mejiro Ramonu in 1986.\nIn autumn she went back to Ritto for the Rose S. (Shuka Sho trial), having grown another 24 kg. She led briefly but was caught, a shock 4th. Still she was 2.3 favorite for the Shuka Sho. She broke well, sat at the back of midfield, and came wide with a powerful finish to win - only the third fillies' Triple Crown winner ever.\n\n* Oguri Cap Dies\nIdol horse Oguri Cap, who led the second racing boom, stood at stud at the Yushun Stallion Station in Niikappu, Hokkaido, after retiring in 1990. He was open to the public until 2002, a tourist spot visited by many fans.\nIn July 2010, after public viewing resumed, he was found lying in the viewing paddock. He had apparently slipped in the mud and suffered a compound fracture of his right hind leg, and was put down. About 800 racing people and fans from across Japan attended his farewell - a reminder of how deeply he moved racing.
130=* Orfevre Wins the Triple Crown\nFull brother to Grand Prix double winner Dream Journey, Orfevre was watched from before his debut. He won his debut easily, showing rare power, but also a fiery temper, throwing jockey Kenichi Ikezoe after the line. Perhaps because of that he sometimes fought his rider, and was a heavy-beaten 10th in the Keio Hai 2yo S.\nHis second win came in 2011, in his third 3yo start, the Spring S., earning priority entry to the Satsuki Sho. Due to the March Great East Japan Earthquake, the Satsuki Sho, usually at Nakayama, was moved to Tokyo. Despite winning the prep, doubts about his temper left him 4th choice at 10.8 (favorite was Yayoi Sho winner Sadamu Patek). But he settled at the back of midfield, burst out on the inside in the straight and won by 3 lengths from Sadamu Patek.\nHis rout made him 3.0 favorite for the Japanese Derby. A typhoon left Tokyo's track in terrible shape, but he powered through the heavy going to win the double. Ikezoe said, "Riding him all along is our strength," showing the bond of a pair who rose from that heavy defeat at 2.\nIn the Kobe Shimbun Hai he settled well, raced forward and broke clear early to win in commanding style. Then the Kikuka Sho, for the Triple Crown, with 68,000 fans at Kyoto. Unfazed when the horse next to him acted up in the gate, he broke well, sat in a good midfield spot, took the lead early at the top of the straight and ran away, beating Win Variation by two and a half lengths - the 7th Triple Crown winner ever. His running showed further growth, but after the line he again threw Ikezoe - his temper unchanged.\nWith no rivals left in his crop, he met older horses for the first time in the Arima Kinen. Against a superb field of 9 G1 winners, including double classic filly Buena Vista in her final race, he won the Grand Prix for his fourth crown and was named Horse of the Year.\n\n* JRA Direct and WIN5 Begin\nThis year ways to bet and enjoy racing widened greatly.\nOne was JRA Direct: with a JRA-designated credit card, anyone could easily bet online, including on smartphones, which had spread from the late 2000s. Betting became easier than ever, and it's still widely used.\nThe other was a new bet type, the "five-race pick," known as WIN5: picking the winners of 5 designated races. It's very hard to hit, but the big payouts make it a popular bet.
131=* The "Lady's" Triple Crown, Beating the Colts Too\nGentildonna, a filly by Deep Impact, was full sister to 2-time graded winner Donau Blue. She broke her maiden at the end of the previous year, then chose the G3 Shinzan Kinen against colts for her first 2012 race, her camp believing she could match the colts. As hoped, she won by over a length for her first graded win.\nShe was 4th in the Tulip Sho, partly due to illness, and 2nd choice for the Oka Sho behind previous 2yo queen Joie de Vivre. Waiting at the back of midfield, she came wide in the straight and ran down Verxina and I'm Yours, who had broken clear first, for her first classic.\nIn the Oaks she was only 3rd choice despite her Oka Sho win (favorite was Flora S. winner Midsummer Fair). Her dam Donna Blini was a sprinter, sister Donau Blue a miler, and she had only run at 1600m in 5 starts, raising doubts. But she closed from behind and won by 5 lengths from Verxina, breaking the race record by 1.7 seconds. With the distance doubts gone, she was overwhelming 1.3 favorite for the Shuka Sho. In the warm-up she was startled by the roar of fans who had come to see the Triple Crown and threw her jockey, but no harm was done. Her obstacle in the final battle was again Verxina, 2nd in both earlier classics. Under 100m out the two dueled, and Gentildonna won by a nose - 7 cm - becoming the 4th fillies' Triple Crown winner.\nThe best filly of her crop then took on the Japan Cup. Waiting was last year's Triple Crown winner Orfevre. This year he won the Takarazuka Kinen and was 2nd in the Arc, but in spring went berserk in the Hanshin Daishoten and had to retake a training exam (for horses judged insufficiently trained) - a true maverick, "the golden tyrant." It was the first clash of Triple Crown winners in the Japan Cup in 28 years, since 1984 (Symboli Rudolf vs Mr. C.B.). Again it came down to the straight. Under 200m out Gentildonna broke clear on the inside and Orfevre on the outside, fighting fiercely. Gentildonna held on by a nose - the first 3yo filly ever to win the Japan Cup, earning her Horse of the Year.\n\n* Gold Ship, the Great Eccentric, Wins Two Classics\nGold Ship came from the same golden cross as Triple Crown winner Orfevre and Grand Prix double winner Dream Journey: by Stay Gold out of a Mejiro McQueen mare. He was 4th choice for the Satsuki Sho (favorite was Spring S. winner Grandezza). He left the gate slowly and sat last, but while the others avoided the rough inside, he alone charged up it, quickly gaining ground. He burst clear in the straight and beat World Ace by two and a half lengths. His unorthodox long run and deliberate choice of the rough inside stunned fans.\nIn the Derby he couldn't catch the leaders from the back and was 5th, but he romped in the Kobe Shimbun Hai and went to the Kikuka Sho. Again at the back, he launched a long run down the backstretch on the second lap, led at the top of the straight and won with no one near. A double of the Satsuki Sho and Kikuka Sho, 1000m apart, is rare - the first since Air Shakur in 2000. He then won the Arima Kinen at 3 as well.\nWith 3 G1 wins that year he was unanimously champion 3yo colt. He was clearly among the best in training, but from the next year people said whether he showed his power depended on his mood. Yet fans came to love him precisely for his eccentricity.
132=* Sprint King Lord Kanaloa\nLord Kanaloa inherited top-class speed from his sire King Kamehameha and won his debut by 6 lengths. But he was 2nd in his second start, the Junior C. over a mile, and from then on his camp kept him to sprints. It paid off: at 4 the year before, he won the Sprinters S. in record time, then became the first Japanese horse to win the Hong Kong Sprint.\nIn 2013 he was invited to the Dubai Golden Shaheen, a sprint G1, but declined to try again for the Takamatsunomiya Kinen, which he had lost to Curren Chan the year before. He was overwhelming 1.3 favorite and, despite a slightly slow start, won in record time in a class of his own - the first Japanese horse to win three straight sprint G1s. Next was the Yasuda Kinen, his first mile since the Junior C. 2 years and 5 months earlier, and he won with a powerful late run, as if to say "I'm not the horse I was."\nIn autumn he won the Sprinters S. again and chose the Hong Kong Sprint as his farewell. He was 1.8 favorite in Hong Kong too. Tracking the leaders, he took the lead mid-straight and kept pulling away, winning by 5 lengths, rare for a sprint - the biggest margin in Hong Kong Sprint history, a parting record.\nHe was named Horse of the Year, the first sprinter since Taiki Shuttle 15 years earlier. Never out of the top three in his career, he went to stud and sired nine-G1 winner Almond Eye in his first crop, achieving great things as a sire too.\n\n* Orfevre's Second Arc Challenge\nThe year before, Triple Crown winner Orfevre, "the golden tyrant," went to France, won the Prix Foy prep, but just missed in the Arc, finishing 2nd.\nThis year, with the Arc still the goal, he focused on Japan in spring. Careful training aimed to fix his habit of veering, which cost him the Arc, and to help him settle. It worked: in the Sankei Osaka Hai (then a G2) he settled perfectly and won easily, though by just half a length. He targeted the Takarazuka Kinen, a clash with stars like fillies' Triple Crown winner Gentildonna and the eccentric double winner Gold Ship that drew huge attention, but he bled from the lungs after his final workout a week before and was withdrawn.\nLuckily it was mild, and the French trip went ahead. In the Prix Foy he settled perfectly and won so easily his jockey looked back in the straight, winning it again. Hopes for Japan's first Arc win soared, and he was favorite. With that year's Derby winner Kizuna also running, about 5,800 Japanese fans came to Longchamp. He tracked at the back of midfield. In the straight 2nd choice Treve, a French 3yo filly, burst clear, and Orfevre chased, but against her powerful finish the gap only grew, and he was 2nd, beaten 5 lengths.\nHe missed the Arc again, but two straight 2nds are something to be proud of, and he was warmly welcomed home. And he wasn't done: in his farewell, the Arima Kinen, he won by 8 lengths from Win Variation. Shining a dazzling golden light for the over 120,000 fans who came to see him one last time, Orfevre left the turf.
133=* Just a Way: To the Top of the World\nJust a Way, by Heart's Cry, ran in the classics but kept falling just short. But in the previous Tenno Sho (Autumn), as 5th choice, he beat fillies' Triple Crown winner Gentildonna by 4 lengths for his first G1 in his 4th try - a stunning leap.\nIn 2014, at 5, he announced a trip to Dubai. He won the Nakayama Kinen, his prep and send-off, proving his Tenno Sho was no fluke. The big race, the Dubai Duty Free, drew a stellar field with 7 G1-level winners. He missed the break and sat at the back, but in the straight unleashed a stunning finish, passed the whole field and kept going, winning by six and a quarter lengths - the biggest margin in the race's history, in a course record a full 2.41 seconds faster than the old one. The rout was hailed worldwide, earning him 130 in the international ratings (World's Best Racehorse Rankings) - far above the 125 of Deep Impact, called Japan's greatest ever, and second among Japan-trained horses only to El Condor Pasa's 134. Just a Way became the first Japan-trained horse ranked world No. 1. And No. 2 that year was Epiphaneia, rated 129 after winning the Japan Cup - Japan-trained horses 1st and 2nd in the world.\n\n* Back-to-Back G1 Feats\nGold Ship, loved as "the grey idol horse," was crushed by Triple Crown winner Orfevre, by the same sire, in the previous Arima Kinen. Verxina set an odd record, 2nd to Gentildonna in all three fillies' classics. These two, humbled by champions, both shone this year.\nGold Ship, who won the Takarazuka Kinen the year before, topped both the fan vote and the betting this year. He dropped to last after the start, but moved up to about 4th before the first turn. He took the lead in the straight and pulled away to win by 3 lengths - the first to win the Takarazuka Kinen twice, and in a row. With 5 G1s he passed Oguri Cap and Mejiro McQueen's 4 to become the grey with the most G1 wins. But in his first race abroad, the Arc, he did nothing from the back and was 14th, and going for a third Takarazuka Kinen the next year he reared up in the gate at the start and was crushed - truly a horse "beyond common sense."\nVerxina, a Deep Impact filly, won her long-sought G1 in the previous Victoria Mile. But she then lost 6 straight without making the top five, and despite being the defending champ was only 11th choice for this year's Victoria Mile. Her camp, believing her poor runs came from a lack of fighting spirit, decided to send her to the front to light a fire. As planned, once in front her fighting spirit blazed and she led all the way - the first to win the Victoria Mile twice in a row.
134=* Father-Son Derby Winners, and a Kikuka Sho Without Its Star\nDuramente, by King Kamehameha out of Admire Groove, was one of Japan's finest bluebloods. Named after a musical term meaning "harshly," he stormed this year's classics with fierce running.\nIn the Satsuki Sho he was 3rd choice behind Yayoi Sho winner Satono Crown and Kyodo News Hai winner Real Steel. Racing from behind, he botched the final turn and veered from inside to the far outside. But he regained his balance in the straight and unleashed an explosive finish, sweeping past Real Steel, Kitasan Black and others before they could react, and won with ease. The win completed an extraordinary four generations of G1 winners: his dam Admire Groove, granddam Air Groove and great-granddam Dyna Carl. Jockey Mirco Demuro was suspended 4 days for the interference.\nIn the Japanese Derby, the big race his sire had won, his brilliant win made him overwhelming 1.9 favorite. This time he settled well, led early in the straight and held on, winning the father-son Derby double and breaking his sire's race record by 0.1 seconds.\nEveryone expected the Triple Crown after such wins, but while resting after the Derby he was found to have fractures in both forelegs and missed the Kikuka Sho. In the Kikuka Sho without its star, the winner was Kitasan Black, who later won many G1s.\n\n* Maurice's Triumphant Run\nMaurice was by Screen Hero (Japan Cup winner) out of a mare by Carnegie (Arc winner) - not a pedigree that drew attention. Still, he won his debut in a 2yo course record. But he could barely make the top five in graded races after that, and went on a long break before summer at 3, during which his painful back was carefully treated.\nIn 2015, at 4, he took off. After winning two allowance races, he took on the G3 Lord Derby Challenge Trophy. Despite missing the break in a mile race, he burst clear in the straight to win by three and a half lengths in race-record time, his first graded win.\nNext was his first G1, the Yasuda Kinen. The field was far stronger, with 5 G1 winners, but his last run made him favorite. This time he broke sharply, sat in a good spot, led in the straight and held off Vincennes by a neck for his first G1. After summer he went straight to the Mile CS, five months on, a schedule that raised doubts and left him 4th choice. But he won anyway - only the 6th horse to win both the spring and autumn mile G1s.\nWith no rivals left among Japan's milers, he ran in the Hong Kong Mile at year's end. Favorite was Able Friend, "Hong Kong's hero," hugely popular at home; Maurice was 2nd choice. Maurice tracked in midfield with Able Friend right behind. In the straight he was passed once, but fought back to cross the line first, adding the Hong Kong Mile to his spring and autumn mile G1s. After losing all 4 starts the year before, he won all 6 this year and became only the third sprinter-miler named Horse of the Year, after Taiki Shuttle (1998) and Lord Kanaloa (2013).
