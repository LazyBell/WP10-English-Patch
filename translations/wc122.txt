2890=A late-blooming sprinter who won the 2023 Takamatsunomiya Kinen at 7. His only graded win before was the CBC Sho, and he had flopped in every G1 - then came the race of his life. Losing his first 6, he was deregistered in summer at 3 and moved to Hokkaido racing, won 3, and returned to the JRA in summer at 4. As a sprinter he won a 1-win race and the Seibu Sponichi Sho at once, then the CBC Sho at 5 off a break as 8th choice, leading all the way, and was 2nd to Yokayoka in the Kitakyushu Kinen - Summer Sprint champion. Then 15th in the Sprinters S., 7th in the Hanshin C., 9th in the Takamatsunomiya Kinen at 6. At 7 he was 2nd as 10th choice in the Silk Road S., then won the Takamatsunomiya Kinen as 12th choice, breaking clear from a handy spot as the leaders collapsed in a hot pace - a superb ride by Taisei Danno. He retired to stud.
2891=A 2021-2022 dirt star with 3 G1s: Teio Sho, Champions C., JBC Classic. Winning a 1-win race early at 3 and 2 more classes in autumn, he was 2nd in the Betelgeuse S. (L), then 6th but only 0.2s behind Omega Perfume in the Tokyo Daishoten a week later. At 4 he won the Nagoyajo S. (OP), the Antares S. and the Teio Sho by 3 lengths - 3 straight and a G1. Favorite in the JBC Classic, slow away, 4th. Favorite again in the Champions C. over Chuwa Wizard and Inti, he won by 6 lengths from a handy spot. At 5, 8th in the Saudi Cup, he won the Heian S. and JBC Classic, then was 4th at 1.5 defending the Champions C. At 6, no G1 win but always close: 2nd Kawasaki Kinen, 4th Dubai World Cup, 3rd Teio Sho and JBC Classic, 4th Champions C. - his farewell before stud.
2892=Winning a maiden in spring at 3 and a 1-win race in summer, she blossomed in autumn, stepping up to win the Rose S. as 4th choice from a handy spot. 3rd choice in the Shuka Sho behind Sodashi and Fine Rouge, she drew level in the straight and was 3rd behind Akaitorino Musume and Fine Rouge. At 4, 2nd in the Hanshin Himba S., 3rd in the Fuchu Himba S., but 14th in the Victoria Mile and 17th in the QEII Cup.
2893=Winless in 13 dirt starts, he switched to turf and won a 1-win race in his 3rd try, then another and the Sarakurayama Tokubetsu - 3 straight. Unable to win the 3-win class, he stepped up for the Kokura Kinen in summer at 4 and, 6th of 9, charged thrillingly from last to win. After a 4th as 14th choice in the Niigata Daishoten at 5, he switched to jumps after the Kokura Kinen at 6.
2894=Beaten badly at first, he won a maiden in his 8th start, then was unplaced in almost every race but a 1-win victory in June at 4. Late that year he won the 2-win class, the Wakashio S. early at 5, then the Tokyo Shimbun Hai - 3 straight. 13th after a slow start in the Yasuda Kinen, he was 2nd in the Sekiya Kinen and 5th in the Keisei Hai AH. At 6 he won the New Year S. (L), 3rd in the Tokyo Shimbun Hai, 2nd in the Nakayama Kinen; 16th in the Yasuda Kinen, he stepped up to 2000m and won the Niigata Kinen. 6th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), 8th in the Japan Cup. At 7 he won the Niigata Daishoten, then 9th in the Naruo Kinen and 16th in the Takarazuka Kinen.
2895=3rd on debut, she won a maiden easily, then was 2nd to Fine Rouge as 8th choice in the Fairy S. at 3, and won the Flower C. as 5th choice, breaking clear early. Losing her sparkle, 9th to Sodashi in the Oka Sho, she missed the Oaks unwell, then was 13th in the Shion S. and 16th in the Shuka Sho after a slow start. At 4, 5th in the Kan'etsu S. (OP), then 2nd to stablemate Hoh O Emeuse in the Niigata Himba S. (OP) - a 1-2 for one owner.
2896=A great mare who won 6 G1s in a row in 2021-2022, ending with an Arc at 5. A debut winner, she won only 1 of 3 at 2; at 3, 2nd in her first G1, the Yorkshire Oaks, but just 1 listed win from 4. Blooming at 4, she won a listed race, the Lancashire Oaks, then the Grosser Preis von Berlin and 2 more German G1s - unbeaten in 5, 3 G1s. At 5 she won the Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud and Yorkshire Oaks - 5 straight G1s - and went to the Arc as 2nd choice against Japan's Titleholder and Irish Champion winner Luxembourg. Racing 4th-5th on rain-soaked ground, she passed Titleholder 300m out and held off French Derby winner Vadeni and defending champion Torquator Tasso - 6 straight G1s, and the first 5yo mare to win the Arc in 85 years. A Japan Cup farewell was rumored, to Japanese fans' delight, but injury in training ended her career; she retired to breed.
2897=Rated the best gelding in US history: 34 wins from 57, 14 G1s, racing until 8, and 8th in The Blood-Horse's top 100 US racehorses of the 20th century. As crop-mate Secretariat drew the spotlight, he bloomed from autumn at 3. His first G1, the Widener H. at 4, was followed by the Woodward S., Jockey Club Gold C. and more - Horse of the Year. He won from 7 furlongs (Vosburgh H.) to 12 (Woodward S.) and even 16 (Jockey Club Gold C.) - truly versatile. Carrying huge weights from then on, he still won the Brooklyn H. and Suburban H., Horse of the Year 3 years running to 6, and won the Woodward S. 4 years in a row (4-7). He died in 1997 at 27; the G1 Forego S. has honored him since 2001.
2898=Winner of the 1976 Tenno Sho (Autumn). Last of 8 in the Yayoi Sho at 4 (old age system), he raced mostly in allowances, nearly always top three, rising slowly and blooming at 6. He won the Kimpai (East) over Arima Kinen winner Ishino Arashi, was 2nd in the Nakayama Kinen and 3rd in the Keio Hai SH, and won the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai. That autumn, after an open win, he won the Tenno Sho (Autumn), a Big Eight race, breaking clear from midfield by 1 3/4 lengths over Harbor Young. In the Arima Kinen, behind 4yos Tosho Boy and Ten Point, he was 3rd, best of the older horses. At 7 he won the Nakayama Kinen and Argentina Kyowakoku Hai, retiring after a 4th in the Takarazuka Kinen. At stud just 3 years; no notable foals.
2899=Dam of Tenno Sho (Autumn) winners Fujino Perseus and Three Giants. She won only a maiden in 7 starts, retiring in March at 4 (old age system) to breed at once. She had 11 foals by 22. Her first, Western Pearl, won 3 and produced graded winner Higashi Majorca. Her 2nd, Fujino Perseus, won the 1975 Tenno Sho (Autumn), Takarazuka Kinen and Takamatsunomiya Hai (4 graded wins); her 4th, Three Giants, won the 1979 Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Diamond S. - only the 3rd brothers to win the Tenno Sho, raising her standing. No other stars; daughters like Western Young and Sengoku Rose bred on without notable grandchildren.
2900=Winner of the 1975 Tenno Sho (Autumn) and 1976 Takarazuka Kinen. In allowances through 4 (old age system), he won the Tokyo Shimbun Hai at 5 and became a force: 2nd in the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai and Nihon Keizai Sho, 2 open wins, then the Tenno Sho (Autumn) as 2nd choice by 1 3/4 lengths over Colonel Symboli. Arima Kinen favorite, he was caught by longshot Ishino Arashi, 2nd, but at 6, after the Diamond S., won the Takarazuka Kinen as favorite over Ishino Arashi and others. He also won the Takamatsunomiya Hai, then was 6th in America's Washington DC International. 8th behind 4yos Tosho Boy and Ten Point in the Arima Kinen, he retired. No notable foals.
2901=One of Ireland's greatest stayers, winning 5 G1s in the 1970s incl. 3 Gold Cups, dominant at 3000m+. Skipping the classics at 3, he won the Prix de l'Esperance (G3, 3000m), then the Grand Prix de Paris (then 3100m), his first G1, and was 11th to Allez France in the Arc. At 4 he won the 4000m Gold Cup; at 5 the Prix du Cadran and Gold Cup, both 4000m - king of the stayers. At 6, 2nd in the Prix Jean Prat and Cadran, he won a 3rd straight Gold Cup and retired. At stud he passed on too much stamina, perhaps, and sired no stars.
2902=Dam of Kaburaya O, who won the Satsuki Sho and Derby with astonishing front-running, and QEII Cup winner Miss Kaburaya - full siblings by Pharamond, the best offspring of both mare and sire. She won 6 of 32, debuting at 4 (old age system), winning her 6th start and 5 allowances by the end of 5, then retired after 2 heavy defeats over jumps. Her 2nd foal Kaburaya O won the 1975 Satsuki Sho and Derby; bred to Pharamond again that year, she produced her 4th foal, Miss Kaburaya. Miss Kaburaya failed as a broodmare, ending the line. Others incl. Derby runner My Horse (4 wins) and Silver Kaburaya (3 wins).
2903=Dam of 1975 2-classic filly Tesco Gaby. Her 6th dam was Keen Dra, a foundation mare imported by Koiwai Farm in the Meiji era - a line with fewer stars than others, of which she was the most notable. She won only a maiden in 25 starts, retiring at 5 (old age system). Her 2nd foal Tesco Gaby won the Oka Sho and Oaks, making her name - a star from an unproven mating, as sire Tesco Boy was then new. Tesco Gaby was put down at 5, ending that line. Of 13 other foals, the best was Todoroki Hiryu, an Oi star who won the Tokyo Daishoten and Tokyo Hai.
2904=Winner of the 1975 Oka Sho and Oaks, a shock to Japanese racing with her blazing speed - called "the greatest filly ever." She ran away with her debut, the 3yo S. (old age system) and her first graded race, the Keisei Hai 3yo S., then won the Keisei Hai at 4 and sought tougher rivals in the Tokyo 4yo S. (now Kyodo News Hai), losing by a neck to later Satsuki and Derby winner Kaburaya O - impressive in defeat. After an easy Hanshin 4yo Himba Tokubetsu as hot favorite, she led in the Oka Sho and kept drawing away to win by a huge margin - unheard of in the race. 3rd in the 4yo Himba Tokubetsu, perhaps tired, she led to win the Oaks by 8 lengths, still a JRA record margin. Injuries followed, and in January at 6 she died of a heart attack, leaving no foals - a great pity.
2905=Dam of Green Grass; herself winner of 2 graded races, the Tanabata Sho and Fukushima Daishoten. In allowances until summer at 4 (old age system), she was 2nd in her first graded race, the Fukushima Daishoten. Blooming at 5, she won it as favorite, then the Tanabata Sho as 2nd choice, beating Hakusen Sho by a head, but lost the Fukushima Kinen to him by a head. Strong in summer local races, she was called a "summer girl"; winless after, she placed in local graded races until autumn at 6. Her 3rd foal Green Grass formed the "TTG Big Three" with Tosho Boy and Ten Point, raising her standing; she also produced Queen S. winner Hazama First (by Barbare).
