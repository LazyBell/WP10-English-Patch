1455=Half-brother of NHK Mile C. winner Taiki Fortune. Won the NZ Trophy but ran poorly after, tried jumps and moved to Iwate in '07.
1460=He won the Radio Tampa Hai 2yo S., but at 3 was 6th in the Yayoi Sho and 8th in the Satsuki Sho. Over further he was 3rd in the Derby and 5th in the Kobe Shimbun Hai. In the 3000m Kikuka Sho his sire Dance in the Dark's blood awoke: he powered on from the final turn to win, denying Neo Universe the Triple Crown. 2nd in the Japan Cup, older he managed only a 2nd in the Hanshin Daishoten, and a tendon injury ended his career.
1461=He won the Shinzan Kinen but struggled in the classics and the Takarazuka Kinen, so switched to dirt, winning the Musashino S. and running 7th in the Japan Cup Dirt. The next year he was a close 2nd to Admire Don in the February S., but 12th in the Dubai World Cup. After a long tendon-injury break, he won 2 open races, was 3rd in the '06 Teio Sho, and won the '07 Saga Kinen. He retired to stud in '08, drawing interest as full brother of G1 winner To the Victory, but has sired no graded winner.
1463=A local graded regular, as his Kokura Daishoten win shows. Racing until 9, in his 51st race, the Epsom C., he had run in graded races at all 10 JRA tracks. He retired after the Tanabata Sho.
1475=He won his debut but rested a year with a fracture, then won 3 straight to open class. In autumn '07 he was 2nd 3 times running - Sapporo Kinen, Mainichi Okan, Tenno Sho (Autumn) - and 4th in the Mile CS, then a fracture was found. Back in the '09 Kinko Sho he was 16th and went to stud.
1483=In the Yaezakura Sho at 3 he beat Running Free, later a graded star, by 3/4 of a length. Dull after, he came good at the end of 3 and won the Sprinters S. (G2) at 4.
1484=A fast horse at short-to-middle trips on dirt or turf. A late bloomer, he reached open class at 5 and won the Sprinters S. (G2) at 6, but was crushed in the Yasuda Kinen (7th) and Mile CS (15th), retiring after the next June's CBC Sho.
1485=Until late at 4 he couldn't even win in the 4-million class. Winning his 3rd race at year's end, he ran above his class for 2nd in the Naruo Kinen - a fluke, it seemed, as he then couldn't win even in his own class, never placing in long races. He tried the Tenno Sho (Spring) hoping for a repeat, beaten badly. But in his last race, the Kyoto Daishoten, again above his class, he beat Cosmo Dream by 3 lengths - a graded winner at last.
1486=After winning the Manyo S. at 4, he dead-heated with champion Tamamo Cross in the Hanshin Daishoten. He won the Kyoto Kinen and was 4th in the Takarazuka Kinen, but oddly flopped in the Tenno Sho (Spring), finishing in double figures twice.
1487=Right after winning a maiden at 3, she was 2nd to later double classic winner Max Beauty in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East), earning an Oaks spot. In the Oaks, as 10th choice, she tracked only Max Beauty and finished 2nd, ahead of Kosei, Mejiro Fulmar and others. After poor runs, at 5 she was 3rd in the Kimpai (East) and 2nd in the Nakayama Himba S., then won the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai.
1488=He just couldn't win. At 5 he won the Kimpai (East) as 6th choice, but then was 3rd in the Nakayama Kinen and Nikkei Sho - six 2nds and five 3rds from 25. No match in G1s, but a fine supporting player always running well in graded races.
1489=The last graded winner sired by great sire Partholon. He won a maiden in May at 3, then often fell short until September, when he won 3 straight to open class. 3rd in the American JCC at 4, he then won the Meguro Kinen, his only graded win.
1490=A supporting player who ran 47 times at short-to-middle trips. With 2 wins at 2-3, he was 2nd in the NZ Trophy Yonsai S. and Radio Tampa Sho. At 4-5 he won the Keio Hai Spring C. and was 2nd in the Epsom C. and Tokyo Shimbun Hai. With a sharp 34-second kick, he was 3rd to Bamboo Memory in the Sprinters S. From 6 a regular in sprint-mile graded races, he declined, rarely making the top five, and retired after finishing last in the Sprinters S. at 7.
1491=A tough horse who raced twice a month almost without a break, building strength steadily in allowances. In the All Comers at 5, as 11th choice, he beat Mejiro Ardan, Doctor Spurt and others - a graded win in his 32nd start.
1492=His temper was so bad that, despite real ability, he was never consistent - a pure closer. He had a sharp 34-second kick but often misfired, rarely running to the betting. Valued as a son of Tosho Boy, he went to stud.
1493=A flashy chestnut with 3 white socks, a blaze and a flaxen mane - and a stylish front-runner who glided at the head of the field, his golden mane flowing. He had many fans even in the lower classes. At first they came for his looks, but late in his career he bloomed, winning the Kimpai (East) and American JCC at 6. Then his chronic hoof cracks worsened, forcing a long break, and he retired after a 15th in that November's Tenno Sho (Autumn). Valued for his looks, he became a lead pony at Tokyo Racecourse.
1494=A speedy sprinter. He won the Spring S. and went to the Satsuki Sho, but the longer trip and heavy ground left him 9th, so he gave up the classics for sprints. He won the Shobu S. but ran badly in autumn. Struggling to win, he took the Swan S. at 4 and Tokyo Shimbun Hai at 6, and at the end of 5 ran 2nd in the Sprinters S. as 11th choice.
1495=He won his 2nd start and the Momiji S., then was 3rd in the Hanshin Sansai S., Kansai's 2yo championship, and won the Kisaragi Sho, a classic contender. But then injury cost him a year. Back at the end of 3, he lost twice, then won the Kyoto Kinen as 3rd choice. He kept running well in graded races without winning, raced until 6, and became a lead pony.
1496=Raced mainly on dirt early at 3 without success, he switched to turf and won 3 straight 5-million races, then 3 more to open class, a regular in turf sprint graded races. His first graded win, the Centaur S. at 6, came in record time. Then a long losing streak: at 8, 19 losses in a row, he was 13th choice, second-last, in the Sekiya Kinen - and with a furious charge won by a nose, setting up a 40,000-yen quinella.
1497=He won the Spring S. by 5 lengths and was 5th choice in a strong Satsuki Sho, but 11th. After an injury break, he was 5th in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai and 9th in the Kikuka Sho, then won the December S. (OP) by 3.5 lengths. Leg trouble forced another long break; he returned in the Yasuda Kinen 2 years later, 17th, and retired to stud. His first crop drew attention with debut wins, but little followed.
1498=Unlike his sire Green Grass's image, a sprint closer. At 5 he won a 9-million race, was 2nd in an open special, then won the Keio Hai Autumn H. In his farewell, the CBC Sho, he won in a then-astonishing record 1:07.9.
1499=He beat White Stone and others in the NHK Hai. After a 9th in the Derby he never won again in the JRA, but won 4 at Saga and Arao.
1500=He won the Wakaba S. at 3 but was crushed in the Aoba Sho and Derby. Racing mainly at middle distances, he ran in 31 graded races, winning 3 local ones: Fukushima Kinen, Kabutoyama Kinen, Niigata Daishoten - rarely placing otherwise.
1501=He won his debut by a stunning 9 lengths, then the Niigata Sansai S. and Keisei Hai Sansai S. - seemingly invincible, a star overnight. But he was 2nd to Lindo Shaver, who set a record, in the Asahi Hai Sansai S., 2nd again in the Keisei Hai, then 14th in the Satsuki Sho and 15th in the Derby. Focusing on a mile or less, he struggled to regain form, taking over 3 years to win a 4th race.
1502=A bit slow to win a maiden, he then used his speed to win the Kisaragi Sho and Spring S., a leading classic contender. With genius Yutaka Take aboard he ran in the Satsuki Sho and Derby, but was nowhere near Tokai Teio, crushed in double figures. Little after; at 6 he switched to jumps and, showing a flat graded winner's class, won a jump graded race in only his 2nd try.
1503=He starred before dirt racing was organized and before unified JRA-local graded races. 3rd in the Keisei Hai at 3, a classic hope, he was 6th in the Kyodo News Hai, then injury cost him the spring classics. Back, he won steadily, taking the Fuji S. and Christmas S. (OP) in autumn at 3. From the Ginrei S. at 5 he raced more on dirt, and the next year, after a 2nd in the March S., beat Calypso Song, Tomishino Porunga and others in the Teio Sho for a big title. He didn't win again even on dirt and retired at 7. Seen as a dirt horse, but half his 8 wins came on turf.
1504=He beat Leo Durban in the St. Lite Kinen, but not nominated for the classics, couldn't run in the Kikuka Sho. After a long injury break he did little and moved to local Niigata.
1505=Brilliantly fast, he won the Crystal C., but leg trouble ended his career early. As a son of great mare Star Roch's daughter, he went to stud; his best was '06 Ibis Summer Dash winner Sachino Sweetie (03: Per Favore).
1506=A modest pedigree and a thin, small, frail-looking frame - yet she ran on without rest as a leading filly: 2nd in the Hanshin Sansai Himba S., 7th in the Oka Sho, 2nd in the Oaks, 5th in the Queen Elizabeth Cup. Amid fans' criticism that she was overraced, she ran in the Arima Kinen, her 10th graded race that year, and broke down just before the line. At her connections' insistence she was spared euthanasia, but never recovered and died 22 months later.
1507=He won his debut at Niigata in May at 3, as the spring classics heated up. Apart from one 1800m race, he ran only at a mile or less, all 10 wins at 1400m or less, and never made the top five at 1600m - a pure sprinter. Perhaps due to temper, he was impossible to read: winning streaks, then heavy defeats, then sudden wins. At 5 he won the Hankyu Hai, then after a 3rd in the CBC Sho won the Sapporo Sprint S.
1508=A late bloomer who reached open class late at 4. He won the CBC Sho at 5, then ran in many mile graded races, winning the Milers C. at 7. At stud he sired no stars.
1509=In the Kikuka Sho, where Mihono Bourbon sought the Triple Crown, he fought for the lead and set a forceful pace - called "the horse who wrecked Bourbon's Triple Crown." It failed, but it was more truly Bogan than the Kyoto Shimbun Hai, where he let Bourbon lead. In his 4 races after, he never led again; tracking in 2nd as if out of deference, he never won again.
1510=Just 2 wins up to 3. Effective leading or racing forward, he grew much stronger as an older horse. 1511=Slow to win, he stalled after reaching open class. At 7 he tried jumps, winning 2 of 6, then oddly returned to the flat for the Nikkei Sho. 1512=A flagship of Masaru Sayama's stable, called "Sayama of the sprints" with stablemates Gold Mountain, Yuki Top Run and Hishi Akebono. 1513=Steady in the spring classics, he finally won a graded race, the St. Lite Kinen, in autumn. He then plateaued and moved to Kochi at 5, winning 15 and becoming a star there.
