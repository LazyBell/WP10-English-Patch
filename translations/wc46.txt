628=She was crushed in the Oka Sho, 14th, and only 17th choice for the NHK Mile C. But from the back she unleashed a powerful finish, crushing colts like Laurel Guerreiro to win a G1. She was a fine 5th in the Oaks, but after a 14th in the Shuka Sho slumped for a long time. Switching to forward tactics at 5, she was 2nd in the Nakayama Himba S. and 4th in the Fukushima Himba S., but retired that August to breed.
629=He won a dirt maiden in his 4th start. In his first turf race, the Crocus Sho, heavy going blunted his speed and he was 7th, but on good-to-soft he led all the way in the Moegi Sho. In his first graded race, the Falcon S., he missed the break but charged to win, becoming a top turf sprint contender. Over 200m more in the NZ Trophy he was 3rd behind Sun Carlo's finish, and as 10th choice in the NHK Mile C. he tracked in 2nd and pulled 2 lengths clear. His 1:32.4 beat King Kamehameha's 2004 race record of 1:32.5 and gave his sire his first G1. Following Deep Sky, he tried the NHK Mile-Derby double, but blasted off in front on heavy going and collapsed, last of 18. After over a year off he returned to win the Silk Road S. and light up the sprint division.
630=From a great family, full sister of Scarlet Bouquet. She beat Arahoutoku in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West) and was Oka Sho favorite, but finished 12th, and injury cost her the classics. She later ran well over sprints and miles without winning.
631=In his second graded try, the Winter S. at the end of his 3yo year, he won a shock victory, brushing aside top runners like last year's champion dirt horse Narita Hayabusa. But after a 2nd in the Garnet S. he did little.
632=He won his turf debut over 1600m, then finished 4th or worse in his next 4. In his 6th start, his first on dirt, he won with the field's fastest 34.8 finish, then beat older horses in a special for 2 straight, and went to the Japan Dirt Derby for a third. Despite stepping up 800m from 1200m, he beat Silk Mobius and the rest. He did nothing in 2 autumn races, then rested for throat surgery. Back with a good 3rd in the Kawasaki Kinen and 2nd in the February S., he showed his class, but a fracture was found. It was mild, and he won the March S. as 7th choice. At 6, in the February S. as 7th choice, jockey Iwata's strong hands drove him to a second G1. He kept running well in JRA-local races, winning the Sakitama Hai, and went to stud in Korea.
633=She won her debut in June at 2 and the Cosmos Sho, raising classic hopes. Quiet for a few races, she won the Oka Sho decisively, breaking clear from up front. Hopes rose for the Oaks, but Silk Prima Donna just ran her down, denying her a double. Off form in autumn, she was 4th in the Shuka Sho, then leg trouble led to retirement. Her daughter Cheers Goddess (03: French Deputy) won in '05.
634=He won his debut over 1800m at Hanshin from up front. In the Kyoto 2yo S. two months later he tried the same but, perhaps short of experience, was caught in the straight, 4th. In the Kantake Sho at Nakayama he switched to waiting tactics and won by 2 lengths, showing versatility. In his first graded race, the Kisaragi Sho, he was no match for the speedy Reach the Crown, 4th, but he won the Wakaba S., outdueling Triumph March for a Satsuki Sho spot. He closed for a fair 5th behind Unrivaled, then won the Kyoto Shimbun Hai from up front, holding off Delphoi for his first graded win. He became a Derby contender - only for fractures of both forelegs to be found after the race. He ran well in graded races later, but repeated long breaks followed, and he retired without racing at 5.
635=Half-sister of Pilsudski, who beat top mare Air Groove by a neck in the 1997 Japan Cup. By great sire Danehill, she carried big hopes, and once she raced she far exceeded them, winning her first 5 including the Shuka Sho. She then won the Queen Elizabeth II Cup with ease, 6 straight, reigning as champion filly. But as an older mare her perfect running began to fade, with heavy defeats in big races against colts. She won 2 graded races, the Hanshin Himba S. and Sapporo Kinen, but without her old fire, and retired after the Mile Championship at the end of her 5yo year.
636=She shone only briefly, from her debut at 3 to the Oaks. Winning her first 2, she was 2nd in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West) and 3rd in the Oka Sho, then Oaks favorite. Living up to it, she surged in the straight and caught front-running Oka Sho winner Cheers Grace by a neck - a perfect race for the Oaks crown. But she seemed spent: 10th in the Shuka Sho, 5th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, then never placed again.
637=On debut at Chukyo in January at 3, he raced forward and pulled away to win by 9 lengths, then won the Viola Sho by three and a half lengths 3 weeks later, races of real scale. In the Spring S. against classic contenders, his inexperience showed: 4th as 3rd choice, his first loss. But in the NZ Trophy, despite a slow start, he pushed up to lead at the final turn and pulled away - a stunning performance. Fancied for the NHK Mile C., he raced forward at a fast pace and faded but still held 4th. A tendon injury in his right foreleg ended his Derby plans; after over 2 years off, he retired after a 3rd in the 2013 New Year S. (OP).
638=His sire Admire Boss, full brother of Derby winner Admire Vega, won the St. Lite Kinen; his dam Luca Dance was by 1991 classic star Dance Dance Dance - hardly fashionable. But he made a flashy debut at Kokura in February at 3, winning by 7 lengths. With Yutaka Take aboard in the Arlington C., he missed the break but closed for a close 4th, then in the Mainichi Hai swooped to beat Golden Ticket and the rest, with future Aoba Sho winner Apres Un Reve 3rd - proof his ability was real. As 2nd choice in the NHK Mile C. he was hampered in the straight, 8th, and in the Derby the sodden heavy going drained him to 17th of 18. A tendon injury then forced a long break, and he never regained his kick before retiring.
639=Out of French G1 winner Reve d'Oscar, sister of Hanshin JF runner-up Reve d'Amour - a blueblood whose name is French for "after a dream." 4th on debut at the end of his 2yo year, he won his next 2, then closed for 3rd behind Iron Look in the Mainichi Hai, his first graded race. Short of earnings, he couldn't get into the Satsuki Sho. Aiming for his big goal, the Derby, he won the Aoba Sho as favorite, bursting clear in the straight. In the Derby, on heavy going that hurt closers, he gradually came on for 5th. But in the Kobe Shimbun Hai, as 4th choice, he was 9th, and a tendon injury led to his retirement.
640=3 wins from 42. At 3 she was 2nd to Mejiro Ramonu by two and a half lengths in the Oaks, but was crushed even in open specials after. In autumn at 4 she finally won a graded race, the Kabutoyama Kinen, then retired without another win. As a dam she produced great jumper Gokai (93: Judge Angelucci).
641=Unlike most of Amber Shadai's late bloomers, she showed full talent from the start and livened up the classics. She won the Fuyo S. at 2 to reach open class, then the Flower C. the next spring, beating future Oaks winner Chokai Carol.
642=Half-sister of Fuji Kiseki. Very consistent, but with a soft finish, she lost many close races and rose slowly. Her first graded title, the Mermaid S., came more than a year after her brother retired; already past her peak, it was her only graded win. As a dam she produced '02 Queen C. winner Shinin' Ruby (99: Sunday Silence).
643=After a string of 2nds she broke her maiden, then won the Flower C. In the Oka Sho she came from behind to beat Admire Kiss and others. Going for a double in the Oaks she was 6th behind Kawakami Princess. In autumn she ran 5th against colts in the St. Lite Kinen, but was 6th in the Shuka Sho and 10th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. She kept trying G1s and even went to America without results, and seemed finished, but won the '08 Keisei Hai AH, her first win in 2 years 5 months, then her farewell, the '09 Nakayama Himba S., a fine send-off to breeding.
644=Out of the great mare Dyna Actress, she won her debut, then the Sapporo Sansai S. in her second start - Sunday Silence's first graded winner. She won the Fairy S. too and was the top classic contender, but at 3 her temper showed: she couldn't win the Queen C. or Tulip Sho, and was 3rd in the Oka Sho and 5th in the Oaks. Her temper kept her erratic, and she never won after 3. As a dam she produced 2-time mile graded winner Absolute (by Tanino Gimlet).
645=A fillies' classic star. She won in her 2nd start, then the Kobai Sho, and as favorite in her first graded race, the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West). 9th in the Oka Sho, she showed her ability with a 3rd in the Oaks.
646=She won her debut by 9 lengths, then a 4-million special and the Tulip Sho by 2, 3 straight. 4th choice for the Oka Sho, she ran perfectly, breaking clear from a good spot with plenty left, but stablemate Arahoutoku's sharp finish beat her into 2nd. After a 5th in the Oaks a fracture was found, but she came back to win the Rose S. Favorite for the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, she lost by a nose to outsider Miyama Poppy's late run. G1 class - she just needed a little more luck.
647=She broke her maiden in her 3rd start and won once more before the Oaks, finishing 7th. After summer she won the Queen S. and met Max Beauty in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, 5th behind outsider Talented Girl, and became a regular in fillies' graded races.
648=In the Sapphire S., moved to Chukyo, she led at her own pace and shut out good horses like Chokai Carol and Golden Jack. She later led all the way in the Aichi Hai over the same 2000m at Chukyo too - a perfect match for that course.
649=Half-sister of Satsuki Sho winner Narita Taishin. After her 3rd allowance win she was a fine 5th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, then won the Golden Whip T. and ran 3rd and 2nd in the Hanshin and Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu. After a slump she won the Hanshin Himba Tokubetsu in winter at 5, her first graded win.
650=A front-runner suited to speed duels at middle distances. Leading in her first races, she was 2nd 3 times, unable to hold on before she matured. In summer she rose fast, leading all the way to win the Sapphire S., her first graded try. 4th choice for the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, she lost the lead to Kei Woman and finished 16th. She kept leading after that, but lacked the skill to vary her pace, and stalled.
651=Unusually for a Sakura Yutaka O runner, she rose on dirt, 5 of her 8 wins coming there. Late in her career she switched to turf, 3rd in the Epsom C. and Tanabata Sho and 2nd in the Sekiya Kinen. In her farewell, the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu, she won as favorite - her first graded win, a perfect finale. As a dam she produced '02 Nakayama Kimpai runner-up Tough Grace (96: Tony Bin).

As a broodmare she produced steady stars, led by '04 Yasuda Kinen winner Turu Maru Boy (98: Dance in the Dark), plus Turu Maru Fighter (99: Forty Niner) and Turu Maru Sister (01: Dance in the Dark). 653=She won her debut and the Kokura Sansai S. at Kokura, then went winless until 5. Switched to front-running, typical of "A Shin" horses, she suddenly steadied, winning 2 allowance races in a row and running consistently, even leading for 3rd in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup.
654=She ran in the Oka Sho and Oaks, 6th and 11th, but bloomed in winter, winning 3 fillies' graded races in a row: the Hanshin Himba Tokubetsu, then the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu and Nakayama Himba S. The next year she won the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu again and was 2nd in the Nakayama Himba S. - a different horse against fillies.
655=She won her debut by 7 lengths and 4 straight, including the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West). Heavy Oka Sho favorite, she sank to 4th, then 6th in the Oaks. Back after 15 months, she was crushed in the Queen S., then a tendon injury ended her career.
656=In his 5th start he led all the way to win a JRA maiden exchange race over 1600m on dirt at local Funabashi. As 12th choice in the Mainichi Hai he raced forward and held on for 2nd behind Iron Look. In the Satsuki Sho the classic proved too much: he led and finished 11th.
