598=She won her first 2 but was 10th in the Oka Sho. A romp in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East) kept her Oaks dream alive, but injury ended it. 4th in the Rose S. and 9th in the Shuka Sho, she retired to breed.
603=She won the Hanshin Sansai Himba S. as 9th choice, part of a 120,000-yen quinella, but did nothing after and retired after the next Queen Elizabeth II Cup. As a dam she produced Suehiro Commander (95: Commander in Chief), '99 Kokura Daishoten and Naruo Kinen winner.
605=Beaten in every fillies' classic - 11th, 14th and 7th - she later raced around a mile, winning the Sekiya Kinen at 4, then the Nakayama Himba S. and Fukushima Himba S. for 3 graded wins.
609=She won in her 2nd start but was crushed in the TV Tokyo Sho Sansai Himba S. After winning a 4-million race in April and the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East), she was 5th as 2nd choice in the Oaks. In autumn she won the Queen S. but was 7th as 2nd choice in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. Older, she bloomed, beating colts in 3 graded races: the Kimpai (East), Argentina Kyowakoku Hai and American JCC.
610=Half-sister of Katsuragi Ace. Steady in allowance races, she won an open race at 5. After early struggles she was 2nd in the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu at 6, then won the Nakayama Himba S. for her first graded win. No notable offspring.
612=She won her debut by over 3 lengths, then the Niigata Sansai S. and Keisei Hai Sansai S. unbeaten. 3rd to Elpus in the TV Tokyo Sho Sansai Himba S., she was then found to have a fracture and lost a year. She ran well in graded races without winning, until the Tanabata Sho at 5, her first win in 2 years 8 months.
616=Tough, she raced until 6, winning 6 of 43, never badly beaten in any conditions, and at 5 won her first graded race, the Kokura Kinen. As a dam she produced '03 Nikkei Shinshun Hai winner Bamboo Juventus (99: Mayano Top Gun).
618=She beat Dance Partner by a nose in the Tulip Sho, then was 5th in the Oka Sho and 2nd in the Oaks. 13th in the Rose S., she was less fancied in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup but boldly raced forward for 5th.
619=Just 2 wins, her debut and the Daily Hai Sansai S., but out of the top five only in her last race, with a 3rd in the Shuka Sho. Her first foal Rose Bud (98: Sunday Silence) was 2nd in the Oaks, Shuka Sho and Queen Elizabeth II Cup, and Rosenkreuz (02: Sunday Silence) won 3 graded races including the Kinko Sho - a great broodmare like her dam Rosa Nay.
620=In a chaotic year, Arahoutoku was 2nd in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West), then won the Oka Sho as 5th choice from a good spot, and the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East). Oaks favorite, she was 7th behind Cosmo Dream, and retired after a 4th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup.
621=Her sire Buzen Dai O was a teaser stallion with just 2 foals that year. Yet in the Oaks, unexpected even by her connections, she won with apprentice Shigefumi Kumazawa, and later ran well in older graded races like the Kyoto Daishoten.
622=Tamamo Cross's sister. With Oaks winner Cosmo Dream injured and Oka Sho winner Arahoutoku off form, she won the Queen Elizabeth II Cup by a nose from Shiyono Roman, two weeks after her brother won the Tenno Sho (Autumn).
623=She beat the colts in the Shinzan Kinen and was 3rd in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West). 7th in the Oka Sho, she won the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East) and was 4th in the Oaks. With a plain pedigree, she was never favorite in 25 starts - a long-shot fans' idol. Tough and injury-free, she livened up graded races, winning the Asahi Challenge C. at Chukyo at 4. As a dam she produced 8-time winner Fundori Ryoma (93: Mogami).
