401=He trailed Fusaichi Zenon in the Yayoi Sho, but as 2nd choice in the Satsuki Sho his late charge from the back made him a classic winner. Going for a double in the Derby, he lost to Agnes Flight by a nose, then boldly went to Britain's King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S., 5th against Montjeu. 3rd in the Kobe Shimbun Hai, he still won the Kikuka Sho narrowly as 2nd choice - two classics plus a nose 2nd in the Derby, a "near Triple Crown." Winless after, he went to stud at the end of '02. But the next spring, out at pasture, he kicked the paddock fence, fractured a leg and was put down.
402=US double classic winner of 2002. After retiring he went to stud in Japan as a hoped-for successor to Sunday Silence, but his extreme pickiness limited his matings, and he didn't greatly succeed. As a racehorse he was erratic: early at 3 he was crushed when intimidated by the pack, then won his next 2 easily at his own pace. In the Kentucky Derby, only 9th choice, he romped again, then won a tight Preakness for a double. But a slow start in the Belmont left him a heavy-beaten 8th, no Triple Crown. He won the Haskell Invitational H. easily, but was crushed by older horses in the Pacific Classic S. and Breeders' Cup Classic. Bought by the Shadai Group, looking for Sunday Silence's successor, he went to stud in Japan. But he showed interest only in certain mares, so covering was a struggle; in 12 years he left under 100 offspring. Still, they included Shuka Sho winner Black Emblem and other graded winners, hinting at his quality as a sire.
403=16 starts, 9 wins, his only G1 the Takarazuka Kinen. By the numbers, not enough to be called great. But his fearsome ability to win in record time just by running his own race made him a true monster, and his career is full of legends: ducking under the gate in the Yayoi Sho; an unprecedented 6 straight wins, 5 graded, after Yutaka Take took over at 4; winning the Kinko Sho by a distance; the switch to jockey Minai in the Takarazuka Kinen because Take rode Air Groove; toying with year-younger El Condor Pasa and Grass Wonder in the Mainichi Okan; and the Tenno Sho (Autumn) that ended the legend... Ending unfinished through a fatal breakdown, his reputation is not settled; some say "tragedy has glamorized him." On records, perhaps. But no one can deny the impact of his running or the thrill it brought. That makes him a legend.
404=Called "the black assassin from Kanto" and "the last stayer," he was loved by fans. He debuted early, in August at 2, and won the Fuyo S. in his third start. Then injury cost him 6 months; back in the Spring S. he was 4th, then 8th in both the Satsuki Sho and NHK Hai (G2, 2000m turf), and only 16th choice for the Derby. But perhaps helped by the longer trip, he held on for 2nd to Mihono Bourbon. In autumn he was 2nd in both Kikuka Sho trials, the St. Lite Kinen and Kyoto Shimbun Hai. In the Kikuka Sho itself, his stayer's power finally beat Mihono Bourbon, shattering his Triple Crown dream for his first crown. The next year he denied Mejiro McQueen a third straight Tenno Sho (Spring) and Yutaka Take a fifth, beating the champion by two and a half lengths. A long slump followed, but at 6 he won the Tenno Sho (Spring) again, his first win in two years. Voted first for the Takarazuka Kinen, he he broke down in the race and had to be put down. By fate, that Takarazuka Kinen was held at Kyoto. The greatest stayer bloomed at Yodo, and fell at Yodo.
406=Half-brother to Tenno Sho winner Biwa Hayahide, the best horse of the 1990s, "the shadow-roll monster." He ran some poor races early, but from the Kyoto Sansai S., when the shadow roll went on, he won race after race. He won the Satsuki Sho by three and a half lengths, the Derby by 5 and the Kikuka Sho by 7 - a Triple Crown by huge margins. He then beat Hishi Amazon and Rice Shower by 3 lengths in the Arima Kinen. But after winning the next spring's Hanshin Daishoten, things darkened. A hip inflammation cost him 7 months; back in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), the old sparkle was gone and he was a dismal 12th. Things never came right, and he retired after the Takamatsunomiya Hai at 5. He went to stud but died young after just 2 crops - the greatest Triple Crown winner, dogged by tragedy at the end.
407=Debuted at local Oi. Ordinary at 3, he came into his own after moving to Funabashi at 4, winning 7 straight including the Oi Kinen. At 5 he won the JRA's Tokai Winter S. and kept improving, and from 6 he was a monster, with 10 wins and a 3rd from 11. In JRA-local races packed with stars like Kyoto City, Meisei Opera, Taiki Sherlock and M.I. Blanc, he showed overwhelming power, winning 9 graded races (4 G1s), and was NAR Horse of the Year in 1997 and 1998. In his prime, among Japan's best ever on dirt. A Dubai World Cup trip was naturally hoped for, but it never happened for various reasons - still a cause for regret.
408=Brother of Sakura Chiyono O, by Tosho Boy - a blueblood watched from the start. As hoped he won 3 straight, including the Asahi Hai Sansai S., but as favorite in the Yayoi Sho he sank to 12th, then a dismal 19th in the Satsuki Sho. Many blamed the heavy ground, but he was 9th in the Derby on good going too. In autumn he won the St. Lite Kinen and went all in on the Kikuka Sho. But at the final turn he ran wildly off course. Into the straight he charged boldly along the outside rail with a tremendous kick, but the lost ground was too much and he could only finish 5th. Gifted, he ended the classics uncrowned.
409=Losing his dam soon after birth, Sakura Star O was raised by hand - an unusual start. He won the Yayoi Sho and became a classic favorite. 2nd choice behind Material in the Satsuki Sho, he won with overwhelming power, but leg trouble forced a long break and he missed the Derby. His preparation fell further behind, and he returned after six months in the Kikuka Sho itself - an unheard-of route. But he blew away all doubts, winning to announcer Kiyoshi Sugimoto's famous call, "Cherry blossoms in full bloom in chrysanthemum season!" - "the miracle double winner." Then the Arima Kinen, where he topped the fan vote, brought tragedy: he caught a leg in a hollow in the track, fractured it and was pulled up. Devoted treatment followed, but in vain; 4.5 months later he was put down.
412=He won twice at 3, was 2nd in the Chunichi Sports Sho Yonsai S. and ran in the Kikuka Sho, finishing 11th. At 4 he won his first graded race, the Kokura Daishoten, then the Chukyo Kinen. At 5 he won the Tanigawadake S., was 2nd in the Kitakyushu and Kokura Kinen, won the Fukushima Minpo Hai and went to the Tenno Sho (Autumn). With Tokai Teio a clear favorite, Let's Go Turkin was 11th choice. Held up, he won with a sharp late kick.
416=His temper was extraordinary (he'd bite horses alongside him) so despite his ability he lost many close races. Still, in autumn at 3 he won his first graded race, the Unicorn S., and the next year the Heian S. Then in the February S., newly a G1 that year, he ran a good time on Fuchu's dirt, turned to mud by rain, and won the JRA's first-ever dirt G1. Then long injury breaks and a worsening temper kept him from winning again.
