1071=A fast grey known as sire of Tamamo Cross, called "the White Lightning" for his sharp late charge. At 4 (old age system) he was 5th in the Satsuki Sho and 7th in the Derby. In autumn he was 2nd in the Lord Derby Challenge T. but 10th in the Arima Kinen. At 5 he won the Kimpai (East), was 3rd to Kashu Chikara in the Tenno Sho (Spring), and in autumn won the Mainichi Okan and Meguro Kinen. Retired to stud at 8, he sired 3-time G1 winner Tamamo Cross, White Stone, Inazuma Cross and more.
1087=The horse with the greatest influence on modern Thoroughbred pedigrees - his blood runs in most horses worldwide. With Northern Dancer's arrival, a landscape divided among the Nasrullah, Nearctic, Hyperion and St. Simon lines was repainted in Northern Dancer colors. He sired great sires who became champions - Nijinsky, Northern Taste, Lyphard, Nureyev, Sadler's Wells, Danzig, Storm Bird and more - each founding its own line, building a vast Northern Dancer empire. Beyond his male line, many fine daughters spread his blood through dams too. Northern Dancer was also top class on the track: the first Canadian-bred to win the Kentucky Derby and Preakness S., and winner of Canada's great Queen's Plate.
1088=His talent was spotted before his debut, and he received elite schooling from Wada of Symboli Farm, trainer Yuji Nohira and jockey Yukio Okabe. He debuted over 1000m at Niigata and won 3 low-key races, the Icho Tokubetsu and an open race; through these Okabe is said to have taught Rudolf how to race. After a break, his first race at 3 was the Yayoi Sho. Untested against top rivals, he was 2nd choice behind Bizen Nishiki, but by crushing that highly rated favorite he cemented his status. In the Satsuki Sho, the first classic, he drifted out in the straight and bumped Bizen Nishiki, still green, yet won by a length and a quarter. Already sure of the Triple Crown, Okabe famously raised one finger - "the first of three." By the Derby he was already a complete racehorse: ignoring his rider's urging to go early, he raced his own way and won comfortably. He won the St. Lite Kinen easily in autumn and the Kikuka Sho with ease - Japan's first unbeaten Triple Crown winner. For his steady dominance he was called "the Emperor." In the Japan Cup he lost for the first time to Katsuragi Ace's bold runaway, but won the year-end Arima Kinen by a wide margin - 4 G1s at 3 and Horse of the Year. The next year he was a shock 2nd to Gallop Dyna's surprise charge in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), but won the Tenno Sho (Spring), Japan Cup and Arima Kinen - a monumental 7 G1s. The following year he went to America, but was injured and beaten badly, and retired. At stud he sired Tokai Teio, 2-time Stayers S. winner Airton Symboli and unfulfilled talent Tsurumaru Tsuyoshi, and died in autumn 2011.
1089=Winner of the 1980 Oka Sho and Queen Elizabeth Cup, half-sister of Hagino Kamui O and dam of Daiichi Ruby. She won her debut by a mile in record time, was 3rd in an open race, and in only her 3rd start won the Oka Sho, leading at a fast pace and holding on. 17th in the Oaks on heavy ground, she won the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu in autumn, then the Queen Elizabeth Cup, champion 4yo filly (old age system). A trip abroad at 5 was planned but dropped after a 4th in the Takarazuka Kinen. In summer she won the Takamatsunomiya Hai, then fought a fierce match race with Brocade, Oka Sho winner a year younger, in the Tomoe Sho - a race fans remember. She retired after an 8th in the Mainichi Okan. As a dam she produced Yasuda Kinen and Sprinters S. winner Daiichi Ruby.
1090=The JRA's 3rd Triple Crown winner, after St. Lite and Shinzan. His sire Tosho Boy and dam C.B. Queen had met in their debut (Tosho Boy 1st, C.B. Queen 5th) - a match made by fate. Fans loved his bold style: sweeping forward from near last and charging home in the straight. In the Kikuka Sho he launched a long run up the 3rd-turn hill and swept forward on the downhill, then held on - breaking Kyoto's outer-course golden rule - to win the Triple Crown. At 4 he missed the spring with injury, returned with a 2nd in the Mainichi Okan, then won the Tenno Sho (Autumn) in record time from last with a fierce kick, his 4th big title. But his glory faded as Triple Crown winner Symboli Rudolf emerged a year behind him. He was 10th to Katsuragi Ace in the Japan Cup (Rudolf 3rd), 3rd to Rudolf in the Arima Kinen and 5th to Rudolf in his last race, the Tenno Sho (Spring) at 5. Never beating Rudolf, he retired to stud. He sired Yamanin Global (Meguro Kinen) and Shako Grade (2nd, Satsuki Sho), but none to match himself.
1091=53 races until 7 (old age system), 8 wins. Her only graded win was the Niigata Kinen at 7, but she was 2nd in the Mainichi Okan and Himba Tokyo Times Hai (now Fuchu Himba S.) - truly talented. At 4 she ran in the classics: 11th in the Oka Sho, but a fine 5th to Toko Elsa as 16th choice in the Oaks, and 2nd to Toko Elsa in the Victoria C. (forerunner of the Queen Elizabeth Cup) in autumn. Also known as dam of Tenno Sho winner Pre Classny.
1092=She won 2 graded races, the Niigata Kinen and Himba Tokyo Times Hai (now Fuchu Himba S.), in 1984, the first year of graded races. At 4 (old age system) she won 3 straight allowance races to reach open class, then kept falling short; dropped back in class the next year, she won again, then the Niigata Kinen and Himba Tokyo Times Hai in a row. As a dam she produced Broad Mind, JRA champion jumper 2 years running. Her full sister Dyna Shoot is known as granddam of Rhein Kraft.
1093=A great French mare who won the 2008 French fillies' Triple Crown and the Arc, unbeaten in 7. She won her first G1, the Prix Marcel Boussac for 2yo fillies, and at 3, after a G3 prep, won the French 1000 Guineas in record time. She won the French Oaks easily as favorite, then went straight to the Prix Vermeille in autumn to complete the Triple Crown unbeaten - after missing the break badly, swinging wide and sweeping past them all. Then the Arc: she was favorite over Duke of Marmalade, on a 5-G1 streak, and previous runner-up Youmzain. Waiting in midfield, she split the field powerfully in the straight to beat Youmzain by 2 lengths. She retired unbeaten after it. As a broodmare she produced Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud winner Zarak and others, retiring from breeding in 2024.
1094=Unbeaten in 8 in 2009 in America, including 5 straight G1s like the Kentucky Oaks and Preakness S., she was Eclipse Horse of the Year. Up and down at 2, she changed after a stakes win at year's end, won 4 straight and took the Kentucky Oaks by over 20 lengths. In the Preakness she beat colts, the first filly to win it in 85 years. Instead of the Belmont she ran in the fillies' Mother Goose S., winning by 19 lengths, then won the Haskell Invitational, the summer's top 3yo race, by 6 lengths from Belmont winner Summer Bird - the best of her crop, colts included. She then beat older males narrowly in the Woodward S. - 5 straight G1s - and became the first 3yo filly named Eclipse Horse of the Year. At 4 a clash with unbeaten Zenyatta, 2 years older, was awaited, but she lost form with two early 2nds and they never met. After a graded and a Listed win, she retired after a 2nd in the Personal Ensign H. She produced G1 winner Rachel's Valentina, but after being badly hurt foaling she was no longer bred.
1095=Known as dam of Hishi Amazon, plus graded winners Hishi Nile and Hishi Pinnacle - a great broodmare. An Irish racehorse, she won the Irish 1000 Guineas, then the Coronation S. (G2), and retired after graded 2nds. She had 3 foals in America and, in foal with her 4th, was bought by Japanese owner Masaichiro Abe; her later foals raced in Japan under the "Hishi" prefix. Her 5th foal, Hishi Amazon, starred in races like the Queen Elizabeth Cup and Hanshin Sansai Himba S., making her name, followed by Fairy S. winner Hishi Nile and Rose S. winner Hishi Pinnacle. Their descendants didn't flourish, but her first, American-born foal Katies First was imported as a broodmare, producing grandsons Admire Moon and Efforia. Her 3rd foal What Katy Did was imported too, producing Sleepless Night - her family thrives in Japan.
1096=A blueblood by champion sire Northern Taste, she won her debut by 5 lengths as favorite and won 3 straight at 2, incl. the Sansai Himba S., champion 2yo filly. In spring at 3 she was 5th and 2nd in the preps, then favorite for the Oka Sho, but on heavy ground and slow away was 3rd to Shadai Sophia. In the Oaks, 2nd choice, she drew level in the straight; with 50m left five horses were in a line, and she won a blanket finish with the first five on the same time. Skipping the Queen Elizabeth Cup, she won the Turquoise S. (OP) and was 4th in the Arima Kinen as 9th choice, champion again that year. She ran well but never won another big race and retired injured at the end of 6. A superb dam, she produced Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Oaks winner Air Groove (93: Tony Bin) and Curly Angel (90: Judge Angelucci). Air Groove produced Admire Groove and many G1 winners, and in 2015 great-grandson Duramente won 2 classics. Curly Angel produced graded winner Egao wo Misete and Takamatsunomiya Kinen winner Ore wa Matteruze. Her family is now one of Japan's great families.
1097=Just 1 G1 win on the track, but a huge success at stud: North American champion sire 2014-2016, with a fee once reaching $300,000 - a great modern sire. He won his first 2 impressively and was a Kentucky Derby fancy at 3, but with an infection was 6th in the Florida Derby. He won the Wood Memorial S., a G1, but did nothing in the Kentucky Derby, 9th. Aiming for the Belmont, he hadn't fully recovered and needed a long break. Back in autumn, he was 9th in the Pennsylvania Derby and retired to stud. His first crop gave Eclipse champion 2yo filly Stardom Bound, and G1 winners came almost every year. In 2014 his runners earned a record $16.8 million, and he led North America for 3 years. In Japan his son Testa Matta won the February S.
1098=The 1982 Derby winner. A close 6th in the NHK Hai trial, he went to the Derby and won in 2:26.5, then a race record. Injured after the Kobe Shimbun Hai that autumn, he retired after just 8 starts. At stud he sired Kikuka Sho winner Bamboo Begin, El Casa River, Agnes Parade and many graded winners.
1099=A late-maturing stayer carrying Northern Taste's blood. Long in the lower classes, he bloomed in autumn at 4, winning the Meguro Kinen (Autumn) and Arima Kinen to join the elite. The next year he fell short - 2nd in the Tenno Sho (Spring), 5th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), 2nd in the Arima Kinen - but in spring at 6 he won the Tenno Sho (Spring), proving his class. His greatness showed even more at stud: son Mejiro Ryan, and grandchildren Mejiro Bright and Mejiro Dober, built a G1 dynasty - a precious hope for Japan-bred sires as imports grew stronger.
1100=Unbeaten in 4, he won the Kyodo News Hai Yonsai S.; his margins totaled over 10 lengths. A clash with fellow unbeaten Rudolf was keenly awaited. In their first meeting, the Yayoi Sho, the margin was small, but everyone saw it as "Rudolf by a mile." He regained his confidence in the Spring S., then sought revenge in the Satsuki Sho, but bowed to the Emperor again. After winning the NHK Hai he was beaten badly in the Derby, injured in autumn, and retired that year. Called an early bloomer, he seemed to collapse against the wall that was Rudolf. Either way, he was born in the wrong era.
1101=Winner of the 1982 Tenno Sho (Autumn), making a father-son Tenno Sho double. Emerging with a St. Lite Kinen win in autumn at 3, he won the Nikkei Sho by 10 lengths the next year to join the top older horses. But he often either won big or lost big, rated a fickle horse. Even on his biggest day, the Tenno Sho (Autumn), he was only 4th choice, yet won clearly in record time. Retired to stud at 5, he sired 4-time G1 winner Mejiro McQueen, who also won the Tenno Sho - 3 generations of Tenno Sho winners: Mejiro Asama, Mejiro Titan, Mejiro McQueen.
1102=A great miler unbeatable at a mile or less. He won the Mile Championship twice and the Yasuda Kinen, and was champion sprinter 3 years running, 1983-85. Unbeaten in 3 to the Daily Hai Sansai S., he was narrowly 2nd to Daizen King in the Hanshin Sansai S. He won the Kisaragi Sho at 3 and was a classic contender, but after a 6th in the Spring S. he was 20th of 20 in the Satsuki Sho, hurt by the trip or the heavy ground (the winner, Mr. C.B., went on to the Triple Crown). He skipped the Derby and gave up the classics, but in 1984 graded races arrived with a proper sprint division, and his run began: from '84 he won 7 and was 2nd once in 8 races at 1600m or less. Many fans wanted to see him meet 1984 Triple Crown winner Rudolf, "the Emperor," and it happened only once, in the 92nd Tenno Sho (Autumn), where he finished behind him (Rudolf 2nd, Winner 3rd, outsider Gallop Dyna winning). Many said at a mile Winner would have had the edge. At stud he sired speedsters like Flower Park and Towa Winner, while Yamanin Zephyr won the Tenno Sho (Autumn), overcoming the distance barrier his sire never could.
1103=2nd on debut, he won a debut race and the Hakodate Sansai S., a classic hope, but a chipped left knee sidelined him. Back in the Spring S. at 3, he charged into 2nd off the layoff, belatedly raising the "beat Rudolf" flag. But leg trouble struck again, costing 6 months. In autumn he was 8th in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai and 9th in the Kikuka Sho. He then focused on middle distances, but with repeated breaks ran just once at 4, and retired at 5 after his only graded win, the Tanabata Sho. At stud he sired fast Tenno Sho winner Nehai Caesar (90: Nehai Tesco) and others.
1104=One of Mr. Prospector's leading sons. He sired world-class mare Urban Sea and BC Classic winner Black Tie Affair, and as a broodmare sire produced Galileo, Sea the Stars and many more - his blood spreading worldwide. Not a standout racehorse, his main results were a win in the Prix de la Salamandre (G1) and a 2nd in the Prix Morny (G1) at 2. At 3 he raced on dirt in America, winning no stakes race, and retired. At stud his first crop gave G1 winner Papal Power, followed by Black Tie Affair, Misil and Japanese star Marvelous Crown. His daughter Urban Sea produced world-beating sires Galileo and Sea the Stars, making him a great broodmare sire, leading in Britain and Ireland in 1999 and 2001. In Japan, Silence Suzuka and That's the Plenty carry Miswaki as broodmare sire.
1105=At 3 (old age system) he won the Fuchu Sansai S. (now Tokyo Sports Hai 2yo S.) and was 2nd in the Asahi Hai Sansai S. He slumped at 4 and had a long break. 2nd in an allowance race at 5, he climbed steadily at 6 and won the Lord Derby Challenge T. in its first year as a graded race. At 7 he won the Nakayama Kinen and was 2nd in the Mile CS, a new G1 the year before. At stud he sired February S. winner Gourmet Frontier and 1992 2yo queen Suehiro Jo-o.
1106=At 2 he won the Asahi Hai Sansai S., 3 wins and a 2nd from 4, but Sakkah Boy took the champion 2yo colt title. He meant to avenge it in the classics, but was 4th in the Kyodo News Hai. In the Yayoi Sho he beat old foe Sakkah Boy. 2nd choice in the Satsuki Sho, he was 3rd to Yaeno Muteki - the top was within reach. 3rd choice for the Derby, he won a tight duel with Mejiro Ardan - top of his crop at last. But leg trouble then cost him a year. Back, he was 16th in both the Yasuda Kinen and Takarazuka Kinen, and as crop-mate Oguri Cap ruled and fueled the racing boom, he quietly retired.
1107=The 47th Derby winner in 1980, but then lost 32 straight until retiring - "the weakest Derby winner ever." At 2 he won 2 of 4, 4th to Laughon Tace in the Hanshin Sansai S. At 3, 5th in the Mainichi Hai, he won the Nanohana Sho, then was narrowly 2nd to Hawaiian Image in the Satsuki Sho. After a 2nd in an open race, in the Derby he won a duel with Monte Prince near the line to claim the title. But from then their fortunes split: Derby loser Monte Prince won the Tenno Sho (Spring) and Takarazuka Kinen at 5, while Opec Horse's best was a 2nd in the Asahi Challenge C.; barely making the top five in graded races, he couldn't win even in plain open races or on dirt, and retired without another taste of victory.
1108=A leading contender for the 1981 fillies' classics. She won the Flower C. the day before the Oka Sho, was 5th in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (old age system) and 4th in the Oaks. She won the Radio Tampa Sho in summer and was 4th in both the Himba Tokyo Times Hai (now Fuchu Himba S.) and Queen Elizabeth Cup in autumn. At 5 she more than held her own against colts, winning the Kimpai (East) and Nakayama Kinen. After 10 months off injured, she won the Nakayama Kinen again at 6. Apart from a 10th in the Arima Kinen, she was always in the top five - superbly consistent. As a dam she produced Queen C. and Hakodate Sansai S. winner Mother Tosho.
1109=At 2-3 he won 3, all on dirt, but also ran well on turf, 3rd in the Keisei Hai and Spring S. At 4, after a turf allowance win, he set a record in a Nakayama 1800m dirt open race. At 5 he lowered his own record by 0.5s in his first race, then won the Niigata Daishoten, Yasuda Kinen and Keio Hai Autumn H. - a big year. From 6 he did little and retired after an 11th in the Sprinters S. at 7. His 1:48.5 for 1800m on dirt at 5 stood as the Japanese record until Premium Thunder beat it by 0.1s in 1997 - a truly valuable mark.
1110=He won his debut, the Daily Hai Sansai S. and Hanshin Sansai S., champion 2yo colt. Hopes were high for the classics, but injury kept him out of the Satsuki Sho and Derby. Back in autumn, he was 13th in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai and 17th in the Kikuka Sho. A middle-distance graded regular after, he won just one open race and retired at the end of 6. Named after a perfume (chypre), his kick charmed fans at 2, but his fragrance may have faded during his injury absence.
