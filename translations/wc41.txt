485=Half-sister of the monster Oguri Cap. She debuted at local Kasamatsu like her brother, won 6 of 7 with a 2nd, and moved to the JRA. Puzzled by turf, she was 9th in the Elfin S., but 2nd in the Tulip Sho earned an Oka Sho spot - the classic road her brother never took. Ridden by Yutaka Take, who rode her brother's farewell, she saved ground in midfield and unleashed an Oguri-like kick to win by a nose. Brother and sister, both local-bred, G1 winners - simply superb. But she was crushed in the Oaks and slumped as if her Oka Sho never happened; racing to the end of her 3yo year, the Oka Sho was her only JRA win.
486=She won her debut, then ran poorly 4 times. But in the Tulip Sho (OP) she beat 2yo queen Nishino Flower by three and a half lengths and was 2nd in the Oka Sho, living up to early hopes. In the Oaks she held off Saneisankyu by half a length to be crowned. With leg trouble in autumn she went straight to the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, a solid 4th, having raced in all three legs of a chaotic fillies' classic year. She retired after it, producing '99 Kyoto Kinen winner Emocion (95: Tony Bin) and Sumire S. winner Salire (96: Tony Bin).
487=2nd in his first 2 starts, he then won 3 straight - a maiden, the Junior C. and the Kyodo News Hai. His first graded win beat future NHK Mile C. winner Danon Chantilly and Spring S. winner Arrivee, and big things were expected. But he was last (18th) in the Satsuki Sho and 16th in the Derby. He returned the next year but couldn't regain his early-3yo shine and retired. Close relatives include great sires Woodman and New England.
488=JRA-raised, he sold for 27.3 million yen, the top price at the JRA Breeze-Up Sale, and was highly rated. 2nd on debut, he won his maiden by 6 lengths, then the Niigata 2yo S. by a length despite racing wide on heavy ground. Skipping the Tokyo Sports Hai 2yo S. with a hoof inflammation, he went straight to the Asahi Hai FS and won it like his sire - 2yo champion. At 3 he was 8th in the Yayoi Sho after the break, then 3rd in the Satsuki Sho under Hiroyuki Uchida. 3rd choice for the Derby, he was 13th on heavy ground. In autumn he was 3rd in both the Kobe Shimbun Hai and Kikuka Sho, showing G1 quality. At 4 he won the Epsom C., his first win in 18 months, but was 16th in the Takarazuka Kinen, then went on a long break and retired without returning.
489=She won her debut in January at 3, was 2nd in the Tulip Sho and Oka Sho, then won the Oaks. In autumn she went to France: 2nd in the G3 Prix de la Nonette, she was favorite for the G1 Prix Vermeille but, hampered in the straight, finished 6th behind Carling. Back home she tried the Kikuka Sho, again favorite (5th). As an older mare she won the first Queen Elizabeth II Cup under its new conditions - a great mare who tried everything. Her broodmare career brought steady runners but few graded winners, until her 6th foal Federalist won the Nakayama Kimpai and Nakayama Kinen, proving her quality.
490=Named after a red Hawaiian bird. 3rd on debut, she won her next 2, then, as 2nd choice from the widest draw, slipped up the inside in the straight to win the Hanshin JF - 2yo queen and her sire King Kamehameha's first G1 winner. At 3 she fought her rider and was 2nd in the Tulip Sho, but won the Oka Sho from up front in race-record time. Doubts about the Oaks distance, her dam Salty Bid being a sprinter, were dispelled: she dead-heated for the win with trial winner Saint Emilion. After summer and a 4th in the Rose S., she won the Shuka Sho, passing northern assassin Apricot Fizz and holding off trial winner Animate Bio - the first fillies' Triple Crown in 7 years, since Still in Love. Older, she won the Victoria Mile against Buena Vista. One more, the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, would have swept the fillies' G1s, but British mare Snow Fairy beat her at 3 and 4. As a broodmare, crossed with Deep Impact - two Triple Crown winners, 12 crowns combined - she produced Jinambo and Akaitorino Musume.
491=She won her debut, then injury cost her six months. 2nd on her return, she won 3 straight, and in her 6th start, her first graded try, beat colts by 2 lengths in the Nikkei Shinshun Hai. She kept running well in graded races and won the Nakayama Himba S. at 5.
492=Mejiro Ramonu won the TV Tokyo Sho Sansai Himba S. with 2 wins from 3. 4th in the Queen S. at 3, she then won 6 straight to take the fillies' Triple Crown. From the start of the streak, the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West), she was ridden by Hiroshi Kawachi, later famed as "Kawachi of the fillies." Winning that by a neck, she broke clear to win the Oka Sho by a length and three-quarters. After the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East), people said "no filly can beat Ramonu now," and she won the Oaks as heavy favorite. But in autumn she won the Rose S. by just a neck, and in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup Super Shot closed steadily, held off by half a length - the first fillies' Triple Crown ever. Mr. C.B. and Symboli Rudolf had won Triple Crowns 3 and 2 years before; how good was a fillies' Triple Crown winner? The year-end Arima Kinen was the stage to test it - beat the older horses and colts and the title "best" was hers. But as 2nd choice she was a dismal 9th. Retiring after it, she is remembered as the first fillies' Triple Crown winner, but without the fame of Air Groove, who matched the colts. Her title stayed "best filly."
493=Ridden by "Tsunoda of the fillies," she won her first 3 with overwhelming power, beating Scarlet Bouquet by two and a half lengths in the Tulip Sho. But that year's fillies' classics were a wide-open, unusually strong field. Unbeaten Isono Roubles (5 for 5) was Oka Sho favorite, Northern Driver, who beat colts in the Pegasus S. (G3, 1700m), 2nd choice, and Sister Tosho only 4th choice behind Scarlet Bouquet, whom she'd beaten. Isono Roubles lost a shoe just before the start in a strange atmosphere. Sister Tosho tracked the leaders, led at the final turn and burst clear, winning by 2 lengths - an unbeaten Oka Sho winner. Isono Roubles got revenge in the Oaks, but Sister Tosho dispelled doubts about the distance in 2nd, among the best of her crop, and was named champion 3yo filly. Injury followed, and she retired without another win.
494=She won the Elfin S. and Pegasus S. and went to the Oka Sho, but drew the widest gate, 18 - seen as a big disadvantage. Some say jockey Yutaka Take missed the break on purpose to overcome it; no one knows. Slow away, she headed for the rail and reached 6th by the final turn, then unleashed an otherworldly kick to catch Hokuto Venus by a head for a G1. In the Oaks she lost to outsider Light Color in 2nd. She won the Rose S. in autumn, but leg trouble struck; nursed through training, she tried the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, long announced as her farewell. Favorite, she broke down fatally mid-race. Take sensed it and tried to pull her up, but her fighting spirit wouldn't let her stop; dragging her injured leg, she finished last by a distance, but finished.
495=Along with A Shin Washington, a year older, this speedster cemented the image "A Shin = foreign-bred, sprinter, front-runner." She won her debut by a distance in record time, and at 3 won two G3s, the Queen C. and Arlington C. After an 18-month break she slumped, but at 5 won the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu and Silk Road S. She was 2nd in the Takamatsunomiya Hai (now Kinen), never winning on the big stage. As a broodmare she retired without producing a star with her speed.
496=Whether for his pedigree - by great sire Mr. Prospector out of Sinezita, a daughter of US Triple Crown winner Secretariat - or his lucky name, he was always heavily backed: favorite in a remarkable 14 of 24 starts, given his record. He had ability, chasing Genuine to half a length in the Mile Championship at 5, and won 7 races. But his graded wins were just two G3s against fillies and mares - a little underwhelming.
497=After a 3rd in the Anemone S., she was only 10th choice for the Oka Sho, without a graded win. But with hot favorite Air Groove out, any horse had a chance, and she cleanly ran down the field to become a G1 winner. In the Oaks she ran a fine 2nd to favorite Air Groove, proving her Oka Sho was no fluke. But as if she'd used it all up, after a 7th in the Rose S. she was injured, never won again and retired after another injury. As a dam, Pegasus Fight (02: El Condor Pasa), a 6-time winner, is her best so far.
498=She beat Daiichi Ruby by a neck in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East) trial, then was 6th in the Oaks, and ran poorly in the Queen S. and Himba Tokyo Times Hai in autumn. But in a wide-open Queen Elizabeth II Cup, she was 8th choice at 13.6, a fancied outsider. Waiting at the back, she closed in the straight and crossed the line to Kiyoshi Sugimoto's famous call: "Number 18, one week late!" The week before, her jockey Norihiro Yokoyama had lost the Kikuka Sho as favorite on Mejiro Ryan - also number 18.
499=3rd in the Oaks and 3rd as favorite in the Queen S. in autumn, she was consistent. In the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, with the betting focused on fillies' double winner Max Beauty, she was 4th choice, but swooped on Max Beauty in the straight and won by 2 lengths. She was put down in '08 due to old age.
500=From the famous Star Roch family; her dam Shiba Sky was a sister of Machikane Tannhauser. With a razor-sharp finish she won 9 of 20, including 4 graded races - all against colts, beating stars like Taiki Blizzard, Rice Shower and Nice Nature. She also gave Olivier Peslier, now a G1 regular, his first graded win in Japan. She became a broodmare but produced no standout.
501=She ran a gutsy 3rd in a debut hailed as "the strongest maiden race ever." Her jockey said "she'll win a G1," and she did in her 3rd start, the Hanshin JF. With amazing finishes she won the Oka Sho and Oaks, earning the nickname "the female Deep Impact." An Arc trip was planned, but after a 2nd in the Sapporo Kinen she stayed home. Going for the fillies' Triple Crown in the Shuka Sho, she trailed rival Red Desire and was demoted to 3rd for interference. In the Queen Elizabeth II Cup lightly-fancied Queen Spumante and T.M. Precure held on in front, leaving her 3rd, and in the Arima Kinen she was 2nd to Dream Journey's sharp finish. At 4 she proved herself world-class with a 2nd in the Dubai Sheema Classic, won the Victoria Mile with a devilish kick for her 4th G1, was 2nd to outsider Nakayama Festa in the Takarazuka Kinen, then won the Tenno Sho (Autumn) by 2 lengths - her long-sought G1 against colts. In the Japan Cup she crossed the line first but was demoted to 2nd behind Rose Kingdom for interference, and in the Arima Kinen she just failed to catch Victoire Pisa, 2nd by a nose - frustrating defeats. Still she was named Horse of the Year, the 4th female ever, after Vodka the year before. At 5 she was 8th in the Dubai World Cup, her first heavy defeat. In the Victoria Mile she met year-younger fillies' Triple Crown winner Apapane; Apapane perhaps suited the mile better, and she was 2nd. She was 2nd again to Earnestly's record run in the Takarazuka Kinen, and 4th to Tosen Jordan trying to repeat in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) - over a year without a win. In the Japan Cup, for the first time not favorite in Japan (Arc winner Danedream was), she raced a little closer than usual, unleashed a 33.9 finish and won, avenging last year. In the Arima Kinen, a summit clash with Triple Crown winner Orfevre, she couldn't quicken from midfield and was 7th. Always calm before races, as if she understood when to fight, she kept delivering in top races. If Vodka was a queen with the strength to keep challenging, Buena Vista was a queen with the strength to keep reigning. Her unprecedented footprints are surely etched deep in people's memories.
502=Debuting late, in April at 3, she needed 7 starts to win. Then she won 4 straight, and even her first graded race, the Heian S. Her form then dipped, and after a winless spell and long break, her retirement to stud was announced in '08.
503=She led all the way to win her debut by 5 lengths and a 5-million race by 6, then won the Fantasy S. from a good spot with a 34.0 finish - speed on another level. But leg trouble ruled out the Hanshin Sansai Himba S. Back at 3, she raced forward and faded to 4th in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West), and was caught by Phalaenopsis at the end of the Oka Sho, 2nd. After a 10th in the Oaks, which didn't suit her, a tendon injury ended her career. On paper, 3 wins from 6 and one G3 - but a speedster who starred in the classics. As a broodmare she produced Oaks winner Daiwa El Cielo (01: Sunday Silence), her daughter fulfilling her classic dream, then 2-time graded winner Big Planet (02: Brian's Time), and Greater London (12: Deep Impact), born when she was 17, who was 4th in the Yasuda Kinen after 6 wins from 7 - proof of her quality as a broodmare.
504=Debuting in January at 3, she missed the Oka Sho. She won the Wasurenagusa Sho and, with 2 wins from 4, won the Oaks with a sharp run from midfield. She did little in the Shuka Sho and Queen Elizabeth II Cup, but kept starring at middle distances, winning 3 graded races at 4.
505=Trainer Hashiguchi, who developed many greats, hailed him as "on the level of Dance in the Dark or Heart's Cry." 2nd in a strong debut in October at 2, he won his maiden by a distance 3 weeks later. After an easy win in the Senryo Sho, some said "this is the classic horse," but he was beaten 4 lengths by unbeaten Logi Universe in the Radio Nikkei Hai 2yo S. and lost the lead role. He won the Kisaragi Sho easily at 3, but the pace didn't suit him in the Satsuki Sho, 13th. In the Derby he shrugged off heavy going, raced forward and held on for 2nd to Logi Universe. 2nd in the Kobe Shimbun Hai in autumn, he led by a mile in the Kikuka Sho but was 5th, then flopped in the Japan Cup and Arima Kinen. At 4 he switched to the mile: 10th in the February S. on dirt, he won the Milers C. next, becoming a top miler. But in the Yasuda Kinen he was jostled and finished 15th; a fracture followed, and back he managed little beyond a 3rd in the Nakayama Kinen. Undeniably first-class, but strength and fragility lived side by side.
506=He improved rapidly late at 4, but graded races were still too much, and he kept losing. At 6, though, he powered past future Takamatsunomiya Kinen winner Ore wa Matteruze to win the Tokyo Shimbun Hai, a graded winner at last. Poor runs followed, and he retired after a 13th in the '09 CBC Sho.
507=A filly with beautiful lines, but as tough as any colt, she won the Sapporo Sansai S. by three and a half lengths. In the Hanshin Sansai Himba S. she led all the way at a perfect pace, beating Air Groove, a future Tenno Sho winner, and was unbeaten champion 2yo filly. But at 3 Air Groove beat her by 5 lengths in the Tulip Sho, she was 15th in the Oka Sho, and then boldly ran in the Derby, a filly against colts (13th). After a long injury break she won the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu at 5. As a broodmare she was a huge success, producing 6 graded winners: Keisei Hai winner Admire Japan, Yayoi Sho winner Admire Aura, Horse of the Year Buena Vista, Epsom C. winner Tosen Reve, Hanshin JF winner Joie de Vivre and Flora S. winner Sangreal. She retired from breeding in 2015 with 12 foals and died of old age at 29 in 2022.
508=A star of the 1991 fillies' classics. Unbeaten in 5, she was Oka Sho favorite, but lost a shoe just before the race and finished 5th. In the Oaks she led all the way, as if venting her frustration, holding off Oka Sho winner Sister Tosho by a nose. In autumn she went for the third leg, the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, but wasn't at her best and finished 16th behind Linden Lily. An injury followed and she retired. As a broodmare she produced Isono Winner, 2nd in the 1999 Garnet S.
509=Led by a 2nd in the Oka Sho, she had 8 seconds and 5 thirds - many near misses - and won little after her debut. But at the end of her 3yo year she won the CBC Sho from a good spot, her first graded race. The next year she was 2nd by a head to Sunningdale in the Hankyu Hai, a fine 5th in the Takamatsunomiya Kinen, then won the Hakodate Sprint S. In '05 she won it again, and at 6 in '06 won the CBC Sho and Centaur S., becoming the first champion of the new Summer Sprint Series.
510=Leading in nimble style, she won the Flower C., her first graded race. 2nd choice in the Oka Sho, she was hampered and finished 14th, and her injuries kept her out of the Oaks. 17th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup on her return, she then won the Turquoise S. The next year she won the Nakayama Himba S., then beat hot favorite Mejiro Ryan in the Nakayama Kinen. She ran steadily until retiring at 4, producing '98 Queen C. winner Eidai Queen (95: Mejiro McQueen).
