461=Winner of the 1993 Arc and dam of 4 G1 winners including Galileo and Sea the Stars - a historic great mare of unmatched success. On the track she drew little attention: 6th in the Prix de Diane, 3rd in the Prix Vermeille, and just 2 G3 wins until midway through her 4yo year. Then in the Arc, as 11th choice, she beat stars like Hernando, Opera House and User Friendly, the first mare to win in 10 years. Perhaps seen as a fluke, she was only 10th choice for the Japan Cup, the lowest ever for an Arc winner, and finished 8th. She raced until 5, winning one G2. As a broodmare she produced future greats Galileo and Sea the Stars, plus 2 more G1 winners and 2 graded winners - unprecedented success, her blood now spread worldwide.
462=By double classic winner Neo Universe, brother of Derby winner Fusaichi Concorde - a blueblood. In a much-watched debut in October at 2 he beat Reach the Crown and Buena Vista. In the Kyoto 2yo S. bold tactics backfired and he was 3rd as favorite. At 3 he won the Wakakoma S. with a sharp run, then in his first graded race, the Spring S., beat Asahi Hai FS runner-up Fifth Petal and future NHK Mile C. runner-up Red Spada, becoming a classic contender. In the Satsuki Sho, billed as a Big Three with Logi Universe and Reach the Crown, his powerful late charge won it, making him the best of his crop. But in the Derby the heavy going blunted his kick and he was a shock 12th. Back from a break, he was 4th as favorite in the Kobe Shimbun Hai, then crushed in the Kikuka Sho and Arima Kinen, and developed a tendon injury. Back after 18 months, he was a good 5th to Rulership in the Kinko Sho as 9th choice, but the injury recurred and he retired to stud.
463=She won her debut and the Fukushima Sansai S. easily. 3rd to Nishino Flower and Saneisankyu in the Hanshin Sansai Himba S., her outstanding ability was still clear. But as a foreign-bred she couldn't run in the classics, racing mainly at a mile. Never flashy but always efficient and consistent, she won 10 of 15. Of her 5 losses, all but a 6th on her first comeback in the Carnation C. were top-three finishes, and many fans loved her steady strength. Surprisingly, her first G1 came only in her farewell, the Mile Championship at 4 - finishing a career with a win is hard enough, yet she did it with her first crown. It was also the first G1 for "the great trainer of Heisei," Kazuo Fujisawa. As a dam she produced graded-class runners like '01 Chukyo Kinen winner Lord Cronos (95: Tony Bin) and Treasure (98: Brian's Time).
464=Unbeaten in her first 3, including the NZT Yonsai S., she was overwhelming NHK Mile C. favorite but finished 14th. After a break she went straight to the Shuka Sho and held off Erimo Chic for a G1. In the Japan Cup, a 3yo filly against world stars like Singspiel, she was a superb 2nd and highly rated. But she was 10th in the Arima Kinen and retired after just 7 races. As a dam she produced Hanshin Daishoten winner Gustave Cry (08: Heart's Cry).
465=Frail from birth, she took time to debut, finally running at 3 and winning by a distance. 2nd in the St. Paulia Sho and 3rd in the Flower C., she missed the Oka Sho. But she won the Wasurenagusa Sho by 4 lengths, was a confident 2nd choice for the Oaks and won clearly, though by three-quarters of a length - the first fillies' G1 for Brian's Time. In the Queen Elizabeth II Cup she lost a nose-to-nose battle with top mare Hishi Amazon. As an older mare she won the Chukyo Kinen and was 2nd in the Keihan Hai, holding her own against colts. She retired in spring at 4 but left no notable offspring.
466=She won 7 G1s in America, including the Breeders' Cup Juvenile Fillies, losing only twice, both 2nds. So consistent she was called "the second Ruffian," she broke down fatally in her last race, the Breeders' Cup Distaff. Champion 2yo filly after winning the Juvenile Fillies, at 3 she won the Ashland S. by 5 lengths. She was 2nd to front-running Seaside Attraction in the Kentucky Oaks, then reeled off 5 straight G1s including the Mother Goose S. and Test S. In the big Breeders' Cup Distaff she was co-favorite with Bayakoa, an Argentine import with 11 US G1 wins. They dueled into the straight, but in the final furlong she fell, was found beyond saving and put down - remembered as the Breeders' Cup's greatest tragedy.
467=She beat Nippo Teio in the Keisei Hai and was 2nd choice for the Oka Sho, but finished 17th, then a narrow 4th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. At 4 she won 3 graded races against colts - the Epsom C., Niigata Kinen and All Comers - proving her talent. As a broodmare she produced Aoba Sho winner Summer Suspicion (92: Sunday Silence) and Rosenkavalier (93: Sunday Silence), winner of the St. Lite Kinen, American JCC, Nikkei Sho and Meguro Kinen.
468=Half-sister to Sakura Chitose O. A late improver, she won a 5-million race and the Queen S., then came to the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. Only 10th choice, she outdueled Bright Sandy at the end to take the crown, and later won the Fuchu Himba S. and ran well at middle distances.
469=Just 11 starts, but 8 wins, two 2nds and a 5th - nearly perfect, top two in over 90%. At a mile she never lost, whatever the grade, winning both mile G1s, the Yasuda Kinen and Mile Championship - the only mare to do so. She also ran 2nd to Hokuto Vega over 2400m in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. Truly a modern great, "the Queen of the Mile." Retiring at the end of her 4yo year, she produced Fukushima Kinen runner-up Miscast (98: Sunday Silence), who went to stud despite no graded win and sired Tenno Sho winner Beat Black.
470=He struggled to get out of the lower classes, but his camp believed in him, stepping him up into graded races early, even the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai as a 2-time winner at 3. Reaching his prime with 5 wins, he ran 2nd to Admire Fuji in the Nikkei Shinshun Hai at 5, his third graded try against older horses. After a break he won the Kokura Kinen in record time and was 2nd in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), his peak. From '07 his best was a 2nd in the Kinko Sho, and he retired to stud in May '09.
471=A New Zealand mare who won the 1989 Japan Cup in a world record for 2400m on turf after a fierce duel with Oguri Cap. By Three Legs out of a Moss Trooper mare, a typically local pedigree, she won 3 G1s in New Zealand and Australia, then came to the Japan Cup at 6 in 1989. The field was stellar - Arc winner Carroll House, world-record holder Hawkster, and Japan's best: Oguri Cap, Super Creek, Inari One - and she was overlooked as 9th choice. But she tracked the fast pace from a good spot, broke clear in the straight and held off Oguri Cap's charge by a neck in an astonishing world-record 2:22.2, burning her image into Japanese fans' minds. Back home she won 2 more G1s and retired. As a broodmare she produced G1 winner Brew and more down to her grandchildren, and is in her country's Hall of Fame.
472=She romped in the Tulip Sho (OP) in her third start, then won the Oka Sho with more in hand than the margin showed. In the Oaks she stayed rock solid for a double. Hopes rose for the fillies' Triple Crown, but leg trouble struck just before, and she went straight to the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, finishing 3rd. She retired after a 13th in the next Takarazuka Kinen. As a dam she produced three sires: Derby winner Admire Vega (96: Sunday Silence), dirt king Admire Don (99: Timber Country) and Admire Boss (97: Sunday Silence). Her only daughter and last foal, Historic Star, never raced due to frailty, but produced Harp Star, winner of the Hanshin JF and Oka Sho. Queen Vega was top-class as a broodmare too.
473=At 3 she won the Sweet Pea S. and went to the Oaks, then was 3rd in the Shuka Sho and won the Turquoise S. In '05 she won her first graded race, the Nakayama Himba S. After a slump she was 2nd in the '07 Kyoto Himba S. and 2nd again in her farewell, the Nakayama Himba S. - no win, but back-to-back graded 2nds as a proud exit.
474=By Pegasus Tosho Boy out of the lady Hagino Top Lady, she sold for 100 million yen and drew attention from the start. Debuting late, in February at 3, she was excluded from the Oka Sho trial, won the Anemone Sho but lost the Oka Sho entry draw, and was a shock 2nd in the Wasurenagusa Sho instead. In the Oaks a costly slow start left her 5th - a 3yo year of unmet hopes. But at 4 she found her path in sprints, fighting many great duels with her "eternal rival" Daitaku Helios and winning two G1s, the Yasuda Kinen and Sprinters S. She seemed a pampered blueblood, but reportedly had more grit than the colts.
475=At 3 she was 6th in the Oaks, 2nd in the Shuka Sho and 8th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. A sharp closer, but at the mercy of the pace, she fell just short of a crown. At 4 she started in spring, improved through summer and was 2nd to Air Groove in the Sapporo Kinen. In her second Queen Elizabeth II Cup, her powerful kick caught favorite Dance Partner near the line for her long-sought G1.
476=He won in his 4th start, then as 12th choice ran a good 4th in the Asahi Hai FS. At 3 he won the Shinzan Kinen clearly by 3 lengths, breaking clear from up front. He was to run in the Spring S. but was slow to recover condition, going straight to the Satsuki Sho, where he was 13th, far behind Victoire Pisa, then 15th in the NHK Mile C., and gave up the Derby. He later won the Tokyo Shimbun Hai and Lord Derby CT, was 5th in the Yasuda Kinen, and as 14th choice 2nd in the Hanshin C. at 5 - steady around a mile. At 7 he moved to sprints, and in the Hakodate Sprint S., with Straight Girl, 3rd in consecutive G1s, a heavy favorite, he surged from midfield for his first graded win in about 2 years. He met crop-mate Cosmo Sensor in over 10 races, and in 2012 they finished 1-2 in both the New Year S. and Tokyo Shimbun Hai.
477=Unbeaten in 4 after winning her debut by 10 lengths, Agnes Flora went to the Oka Sho. Her dam was Oaks winner Agnes Lady. Wanting to "win the Oka Sho her dam couldn't," the owner bred Lady to Royal Ski, proven at middle distances, and Flora was born. She won the Oka Sho as hoped. Going for a dam-daughter double in the Oaks, she was 2nd to A Shin Sunny, then developed leg trouble and retired. But Flora had a bigger role: passing her talent on. Her 4th foal was an elegant chestnut by Sunday Silence - Agnes Flight, who gave jockey Kawachi, rider of her and her dam, his long-sought Derby. The next year she produced unbeaten Satsuki Sho winner Agnes Tachyon, mother of two classic winners.
478=The first and best of Mejiro Ryan's offspring, a great mare who starred in the fillies' division from debut to retirement. Outstanding from the start, she won the Hanshin Sansai Himba S. with 3 wins from 4. Her temper showed then, sometimes fighting her rider and losing. In the classics she battled Kyoei March: beaten by 4 lengths in the Oka Sho, she got revenge in the Oaks, then won the All Comers against older colts and beat Kyoei March clearly in the Shuka Sho, earning champion 3yo filly. Unlike Air Groove she didn't battle the top colts, but she won a G1 every year: the Hanshin Sansai Himba S. at 2, the Oaks and Shuka Sho at 3, and two Queen Elizabeth II Cups at 4 and 5 - 5 G1s, then a record for a mare - and JRA Awards 4 years running, 1996-99. In fillies' races she was beaten only 3 times (two 2nds, one 3rd), by just 4 horses in all, and beat Air Groove head-to-head in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup at 4 - truly "the greatest of great mares."
479=Debuting late in October at 3, she steadily won races and the next May took the Takamatsunomiya Hai (now Kinen), newly a sprint G1 - the fastest older-horse G1 win ever, 6 months after her debut. In that year's Sprinters S. she chased down front-runner A Shin Washington in a thrilling finish. With no result for a long time after the photo, Nakayama buzzed, "The first G1 dead heat?" She won by just 1 cm. In Japan's ever-changing sprint scene, she is the only filly or mare to win both sprint G1s in one year. She retired after a 4th in the Sprinters S. at 5. Her best offspring are 5-time winner Firenze (01: Sunday Silence) and Vincennes (09: Deep Impact), Tokyo Shimbun Hai winner and Yasuda Kinen runner-up.
480=A steady runner rarely out of the top five, she won the Hanshin Himba Tokubetsu at 4, her first graded win, and ran in the Japan Cup and Arima Kinen before retiring. As a dam she produced classic runner She's a Chance (93: Sunday Silence).
481=She won her debut by 2 lengths, then as 8th choice was 2nd by half a length in the Sapporo 2yo S., going on to the Hanshin JF. At 3 she was 2nd in both the Tulip Sho and Oka Sho. She kept running well without winning until her second win, the Kyoto Himba S. Winless after that, she retired after a 6th in the '07 Oro C. to become a broodmare.
482=She won her debut, the Sapporo Sansai S. and Daily Hai Sansai S., all by three and a half lengths or more. In the Hanshin Sansai Himba S. she beat talented rivals like Saneisankyu and Shinko Lovely, and was champion 2yo filly. The next year, with the authority of a 2yo queen, she won the Oka Sho by three and a half lengths from future Oaks winner Adorable - a true "child prodigy." But in the Oaks and the Queen Elizabeth II Cup the distance stopped her, 7th and 3rd, and she lost her crown. Yet that wasn't the end. Targeting sprints to use her abundant speed, she beat strong older horses like Daitaku Helios and Yamanin Zephyr in the Sprinters S. as a 3yo filly - a comeback win, her second G1 that year, and champion 3yo filly. JRA Awards two years running - simply superb. She retired after a 3rd in the next Sprinters S., with 7 wins from 16. As a broodmare she produced Satsuki Sho and Derby runner Nishino Seiryu (96: Brian's Time) and Aichi Hai runner-up Nishino Manamusume (04: Agnes Tachyon).
483=She romped in the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West) trial by 7 lengths and went to the Oka Sho with 4 wins and a 3rd from 5. Letting Mini Skirt lead, she tracked in 2nd and pulled away to beat Mejiro Dober by 4 lengths - a perfect race. Going for a double in the Oaks, she was 11th, partly due to the distance, but won the Rose S. in autumn. In the Shuka Sho, though, she lost again to Mejiro Dober. Focusing on shorter races, she was 2nd to Taiki Shuttle in the Mile Championship. As an older mare she won the Hankyu Hai and Kyoto Kimpai but never an older-horse G1. She also tried dirt, finishing 2nd in the JRA-local G1 Nambu Hai, and raced until 6. As a broodmare she produced Satsuki Sho runner-up Triumph March (06: Special Week). She died in spring 2007, but her granddaughter Sanbure Muse (10: Daiwa Major) won the Fuyo S. (OP) - her speed lives on.
484=A bargain under 10 million yen, she won her debut and the Phoenix Sho. But in the classics she was 11th in the Oka Sho, 9th in the Oaks and 4th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, though she ran well in trials: 3rd in the Sapphire S., 2nd in the Rose S. At 4 she won the Coral S. (OP) for her first win in a while, then the Keihan Hai, her first graded race, and was 2nd in races like the Kitakyushu Kinen and Asahi Challenge C. At 5 she won the Emerald S. and Kinko Sho in a row, plus the Kokura Kinen and All Comers. The next year she was 2nd to Yamanin Zephyr in the Yasuda Kinen as 14th choice and 2nd to Mejiro McQueen in the Takarazuka Kinen, becoming the top-earning mare. Running 51 races safely from 2 to 6, she won 4 graded races and was twice 2nd in 11 G1s - a tough mare called "the Iron Lady."
