364=A born sprinter, all his wins at 1400m or less. His first 3 starts on dirt came to nothing, but switched to turf his speed blossomed and he won twice. Wins then dried up, but he reached open class at the end of his 5yo year, and at 6, as 16th choice, last in the betting, he beat Agnes World and others in the Sprinters S. - his first graded win a G1. He also won the Swan S. and Hankyu Hai. A late bloomer.
365=With natural speed he set three records, including a Japanese one. At 3 he missed the spring classics, but won his first graded race, the Chunichi Sports Sho Yonsai S., in record time for Kyoto's 1800m. He ran in the Kikuka Sho in autumn but suffered atrial fibrillation mid-race and finished last. The next year, in his prime, he won the Osaka Hai and then the Keihan Hai in record time. He was 5th to Biwa Hayahide in the Takarazuka Kinen, but set a Japanese record for 1800m on turf in the Mainichi Okan in autumn and won his long-sought Tenno Sho (Autumn), a middle-distance speedster.
367=A sprinter who bloomed late. He struggled at 3 and 4, but from autumn at 4 began winning, took his first graded race, the CBC Sho, then held on by a neck in the Silk Road S., and won the Takamatsunomiya Kinen with an overwhelming late run to become sprint king. After a 14th in the Yasuda Kinen he won the Sprinters S. in autumn in a record 1:07.0, a spring-autumn sprint G1 double. He never won again and retired at the end of his 6yo year.
368=It took 3 starts to break his maiden, but the next year he won the Kyodo News Hai Yonsai S. and went to the NHK Mile C. Using Tokyo's long straight, he came from the back to beat Toyo de Hair, who used the same tactics, by a nose - a G1 winner. He then slumped, and even a Dubai trip didn't help; he went over 2 years without a win. But at 5 he won the Tanabata Sho, his first win since the NHK Mile C., and in the Japan Cup Dirt, with a fine ride by Dettori, he shot up the rail in the straight to beat the world's best for another G1. It was his last win, but shedding the "early bloomer" label meant a lot to his camp.
369=A character who led with head high, mouth open and tossing, racing steadily from 2 to 5. After a heavy loss in the Spring S. he switched to sprints: he won the Crystal C., his first graded race. The second half of his 3yo year was dull, but the next year he won the Milers C. and came into his own, 2nd in the Yasuda Kinen, winner of the Takamatsunomiya Hai (G2, 2000m), 2nd in the Mainichi Okan, then won his first G1, the Mile CS. He was a fine 5th in the Arima Kinen. The next year he won the Mainichi Okan and Mile CS in record times and had great duels with Daiichi Ruby. At stud he sired '00 Sprinters S. winner Daitaku Yamato.
371=10 wins from 12 at local Kasamatsu, he joined the JRA with overwhelming power and won 6 straight graded races from the Pegasus S. to the Mainichi Okan. Then in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) he met Tamamo Cross, the best horse in training, and suffered his first defeat. He trailed him again in the Japan Cup, but avenged himself in Tamamo Cross's farewell Arima Kinen. The next year he started in autumn, winning the All Comers and Mainichi Okan, then narrowly lost the Tenno Sho (Autumn) to Super Creek. Then the Mile Championship and Japan Cup on back-to-back weekends. He won the Mile Championship with a furious finish, then dueled foreign mare Horlicks in the Japan Cup, losing by a neck in a then world record 2:22.2. At 5 he won the Yasuda Kinen in spring, but in autumn slumped - 6th in the Tenno Sho, 11th in the Japan Cup. "Oguri is finished," people said, and he was 4th choice for his farewell Arima Kinen. But first past the post was Oguri Cap. With that Arima Kinen, racing became not "gambling" but "drama." The "Oguri Cap story" had the happiest ending.
373=A king of the sand who won dirt G1s in spring and autumn. At 3 he was crushed in his first turf race, the Principal S., and from then on raced only on dirt. He won the Shobu S. with a charge from 13th at the final turn, then the Nagoya Yushun for his first graded win. He kept rolling, winning 5 straight including 4 graded races - the Grand Chariot C., Unicorn S. and Super Dirt Derby -. As an older horse he often lost narrowly, but at 5 won the February S. for a G1, then crushed the foreign raiders by three and a half lengths in the new Japan Cup Dirt, the first JRA dirt G1 double in a year. The next year he was 2nd in both races.
