417=Debuting in Hokkaido local racing at 2, he emerged in Southern Kanto, 2nd in all three legs of its triple crown: the Haneda Hai, Tokyo Derby and Tokyo Okan Sho. He then won the Hochi Grand Prix C., Kinpai and Mile Grand Prix. Best of all was the 20th Teio Sho, the first as a national G1, where he held off stars like Abukuma Poro and JRA dirt regular Battle Line by a neck.
421=His maiden win came on turf, but all 12 other wins were on dirt - a sand specialist. Running in many JRA-local races, he won the Hokkaido Sprint C. at 5, then 5 graded races at 6 including the Garnet S. and Kashiwa Kinen, and 8 dirt graded races by retiring at 9.
422=He emerged at 5, stepping up from the 10-million class to finish 3rd in the Nikkei Shinshun Hai; until then he had won 2 of 15. After 2 more wins he was a close 2nd in the Nikkei Sho. That autumn he won the Asahi Challenge C. in record time and was 2nd in the Arima Kinen with a bold forward run. The next year he won the Kinko Sho, was 3rd in the Takarazuka Kinen and won the Kyoto Daishoten. In the Japan Cup, against the best in training, Symboli Kris S., he won a legendary wire-to-wire race by 9 lengths for his first G1. After a heavy loss in the Arima Kinen, he romped in the '04 Takarazuka Kinen and announced an Arc bid. But his plane broke down and he flew in on a replacement just before the race; the punishing schedule left him 17th. He was 2nd to rival Zenno Rob Roy in the Arima Kinen. Racing on at 8 in '05, he won a third straight Kinko Sho but was 7th in the Takarazuka Kinen, and after more heavy losses retired that year.
428=A late-blooming middle-distance dirt horse who changed the image of his sire Soccer Boy. Lacking punch early, he was 2nd 3 times before winning his 5th start, and only took off in summer at 4. He then won the Winter S. and local Nagoya's Nagoya Daishoten, and in JRA-local races battled stars like Hokuto Vega, Lively Mount and Amazon Opera, showing fans the fun of dirt racing. At 5 he won the Tokyo Daishoten, and was 2nd in it the next year as a G1. At 7 he was 6th in the Dubai World Cup, and ran tough on dirt until 8, winning 13 races.
429=5th in the Asahi Hai Sansai S., 2nd in the Keisei Hai, 3rd in the Yayoi Sho, 8th in the Satsuki Sho, 3rd in the NHK Hai (G2) and, as 12th choice, 3rd in the Derby - always running well, never quite winning. In autumn he won the St. Lite Kinen, his first graded race. 2nd in the Kikuka Sho, he was 4th in the Japan Cup, best of the Japanese ahead of Oguri Cap, and 3rd as favorite in Oguri's farewell Arima Kinen. He ran in 28 straight graded races from the Asahi Hai Sansai S., slipping year by year, yet remained an essential supporting player.
430=The monster of local Utsunomiya, whose 43 wins set a local thoroughbred record. At 9 he was entered in the All Comers as "North Kanto's best," but in his first turf race was 7th. He went to stud but retired in '07 for poor results, and has since lived quietly at Araki Training Farm in Hokkaido.
431=He won 3 straight from the Hiiragi Sho, including the Keisei Hai, but leg trouble ruled out the NHK Mile C. Coming back in the Yasuda Kinen, he was a fine 3rd at 3 behind Taiki Blizzard and Genuine, and 3rd to Bubble Gum Fellow in the Mainichi Okan, but then ran poorly as if burned out.
432=He debuted late, in September at 3, and was 15th in his hyped debut, but improved steadily, reaching open class with 3 straight wins by the end of his 3yo year. At 5 he won the Aichi Hai, Kitakyushu Kinen and Mainichi Okan in a row, then was a fine 3rd behind T.M. Opera O and Meisho Doto in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), ahead of Narita Top Road and Stay Gold. But an injury to his right foreleg followed, and despite long treatment he never returned.
433="The demon of Kyoto": all 8 of his wins came at Kyoto. He won his debut and the Shiragiku Sho, but injury cost him the spring classics; he was 3rd in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai and 10th in the Kikuka Sho. At 5 he won the Subaru S., then the Kyoto Kinen from the front for his first graded win, and the Kyoto Daishoten in autumn. In the Takarazuka Kinen at 6, as others swung wide off the torn-up inside, he alone dove to the rail for a thrilling 3rd.
434=Crushed in the Spring S., he missed the classics. After 6 months off he won 3 straight in autumn, and just 3 months after returning won the Nakayama Kimpai. At 5 he won the Nakayama Kimpai and Tokyo Shimbun Hai, but a tendon injury forced him to retire to stud. His daughter Gina Fontaine (Empress Hai winner) is the dam of Funabashi star Casino Fontaine, who led all the way to beat JRA horses in the 2021 Kawasaki Kinen.
435=At 3 he was a fine 3rd against older horses in the Takarazuka Kinen. The next year he won the Nakayama Kinen, his first graded race, then the Milers C. in record time, and in France was 2nd by half a length in the Prix du Moulin de Longchamp. Erratic like many front-runners, he also had many heavy defeats. Winless in autumn at 5, he tried dirt again in the Japan Cup Dirt but blasted off in front and collapsed. At 6 he won the Milers C., his first win in 2 years, and in his first race of '07 led to win the Nakayama Kinen easily. When it worked, he was formidable. At stud his second crop's Logotype won the Asahi Hai FS and Satsuki Sho, with fellow son Gottfried 3rd in that Asahi Hai FS.
437=Favorite for the Asahi Hai FS, he lost by a neck to A Shin Champ's record 1:33.5. A close 2nd to Neo Universe in the Satsuki Sho, he was crushed in the Derby and Kikuka Sho. But he won the Sapporo Kinen against older horses like Air Eminem, and the next year crushed Lohengrin and others in the Nakayama Kinen in record time - hard to beat at middle distances. In autumn at 4 a tendon injury forced retirement. At stud he sired Ocean S. winner Sakura Gospel and Flower C. winner Sakura Plaisir.
