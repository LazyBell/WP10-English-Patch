76=* American Pharoah's Triumphant Run\nAmerican racing had gone without a Triple Crown winner since Affirmed in 1978. Twelve horses won two legs - Spectacular Bid, Pleasant Colony, Alysheba, Sunday Silence and more - but all fell in the final leg, the Belmont S. Then this year, at last, a Triple Crown winner appeared: American Pharoah.\nHis Kentucky Derby was a close win, but he won the Preakness S. by 7 lengths and the Belmont S. by five and a half - beyond dispute. On Belmont day an overflowing crowd packed Belmont Park to see the first Triple Crown winner in 37 years and witnessed the historic moment in a frenzy.\nHe went on to win the Breeders' Cup Classic too, completing the "Grand Slam." When Affirmed won the Triple Crown 37 years earlier the Breeders' Cup didn't exist yet, so American Pharoah became the first Grand Slam winner in US racing history.\nThat year's Breeders' Cup Classic also promised a clash with Beholder, then called the best mare in training - unbeaten in 5 that year, with 9 G1s in all and invincible even against colts. Excitement ran high, but Beholder fell ill just before the race, and the showdown never happened.\nWith his great rival absent, American Pharoah won the Classic easily by six and a half lengths. For this stunning run he was unanimously named Eclipse Horse of the Year and rated world No. 1 at 134 lbs in the World's Best Racehorse Rankings.\nBy the way, "Pharoah" was meant to be "Pharaoh," after the kings of ancient Egypt, but was misspelled when registered. Thanks to his success the story became widely known, and the misspelled name is simply pronounced "Pharaoh" by commentators and in Japanese.\n\n* Treve Goes for a Third Arc\nIn Europe all eyes were on Treve's attempt at an unprecedented third straight Arc. She had become famous in Japan two years earlier by beating Orfevre in the Arc. This year she won three straight from the start of the season and went into the Arc as overwhelming favorite. But she was beaten by the new 3yo star, Golden Horn, and the hat-trick was not to be.\nGolden Horn had won 6 of 7, including three G1s: the Derby, the Eclipse S. and the Irish Champion S. He won the Arc clearly, holding off Treve's charge, and more was expected, but after a 2nd in the Breeders' Cup Turf he retired.\nWith both stars, Treve and Golden Horn, gone from the turf, European racing entered a wide-open era the next year.
77=* The West Coast Hero, Horse of the Year Again\nIn 2015 California Chrome ran a fine 2nd in the Dubai World Cup, but injuries kept him sidelined most of the year. He returned in January 2016 in the San Pasqual S. As favorite he won safely, and after a handicap at Meydan he headed for the Dubai World Cup, the race he had missed out on the year before.\nHe sat in a good spot, came wide into the final turn, accelerated to the front, pulled clear from 300m out and won by over 3 lengths from Mubtaahij.\nBehind the win was a mishap: his saddle slipped badly during the race. Jockey V. Espinoza said, "It was all I could do to keep my balance." Yet it was a stunning run, as if nothing had happened - the West Coast hero showing his class. The win made him North America's all-time leading money earner.\nHis run continued. He won two more G1s, including the Awesome Again S. In the BC Classic he was beaten half a length by 3yo Arrogate's fierce charge, but no one in the world doubted his strength anymore.\nThe California-born hero won Eclipse Horse of the Year for 2016, his second after 2014, carving his name into American racing history.\n\n* The Pegasus World Cup Is Born\nIn 2016 the world's richest race was the Dubai World Cup at $10 million (total purse). But in May, the Stronach Group, an American racing business, announced the Pegasus World Cup, with an even bigger $12 million purse - for 4yos and up, 9 furlongs (1800m) on dirt. Its name comes from the Pegasus statue at the host track, Gulfstream Park.\nIn January 2017 the first Pegasus World Cup was held. Among many stars, including Dubai World Cup winner California Chrome, the winner was Arrogate, who had beaten California Chrome in the BC Classic. Setting a track record of 1:46.83, he dazzled the world with a champion's run.\nIn 2018 the purse rose to $16.3 million. But in 2019, with the new Pegasus World Cup Turf ($7 million) added, it fell to $9 million, and from 2020, with entry fees abolished, to $3 million.\nThough the prize is smaller than at first, the winner earns priority entry to the Saudi Cup in February, so it remains a big race drawing many top horses. As a showcase for American racing, its future is worth watching.
78=* Fated Rivals\nThe horse who dominated this year's BC Classic was Gun Runner, named Eclipse Horse of the Year. A blueblood, he was out of a mare by Giant's Causeway, "the Iron Horse," and his uncle was BC Classic winner Saint Liam.\nHis rival was Arrogate, from the same crop, who beat Horse of the Year California Chrome in the 2016 BC Classic and was top-rated at 134 lbs. Comparing their 3yo records, Arrogate was far ahead. Gun Runner ran consistently well but often fell short, and in their meeting in the Travers S. he was crushed by 15 lengths.\nThen came their 4yo season. They met again in March's Dubai World Cup, and Arrogate won by two and a quarter lengths. Gun Runner was again shown the gap between them.\nBut after this race things changed. Gun Runner won G1 after G1, as if his sleeping talent had awoken, while Arrogate's form faltered.\nThey met once more in the BC Classic. Gun Runner, the challenger trying to stop Arrogate repeating, made the running and pulled away in the straight to win, finally getting revenge on his rival. After the race his owner said, "I was confident we could beat Arrogate, but sitting there I started getting nervous." Taking on the champion clearly brought enormous pressure.\nArrogate retired after this race. The next January Gun Runner won the Pegasus World Cup, which Arrogate had won in 2017, and also ended his career. So ended the era built by the two rivals.\n\n* Enable, First British-Trained Filly to Win the Arc\nThe British filly Enable was from the first crop of Nathaniel, winner of the 2011 King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. and more. When she debuted at 2 she wasn't highly rated, nor did she show eye-catching power.\nIt was the 2017 Cheshire Oaks and her meeting with jockey L. Dettori that led her to the big stage. She won safely and went on to the Oaks, winning by 5 lengths in record time.\nHer winning run kept going. She romped in the Irish Oaks to complete the Anglo-Irish Oaks double, then ran in the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S., once won by her sire Nathaniel, beating Eclipse winner Ulysses by four and a half lengths. She added the Yorkshire Oaks and moved on to the Arc.\nThe 96th Arc was held under cloudy skies at Chantilly. Among 18 runners from many countries, Enable was overwhelming favorite. The pace was surprisingly slow - 1:27.10 for the first 1400m - but she raced calmly in 3rd or 4th. In the straight she broke clear and pulled away from runner-up Cloth of Stars, showing the world her power and brilliance.\nDettori called her "a superstar." She was the first British-trained 3yo filly ever to win the Arc.
79=* Winx Sets New Records\n43 starts, 37 wins, 25 G1s - an unprecedented, unbreakable record held by one great mare: Winx, an Australian-bred by Dubai World Cup winner Street Cry.\nFrom May of her 3yo year, when she won the Sunshine Coast Guineas and the Queensland Oaks, no one could stop her. In her first Cox Plate she won by four and three-quarter lengths. Her trainer C. Waller said after the race, "I never imagined she'd become a horse like this." She went on to win graded races including G1s, one after another - the Chipping Norton S., the George Ryder S. and the Doncaster Mile - reigning over the turf with crushing power, and was named Horse of the Year.\nIn 2016, at 5, the Cox Plate was hers alone again. Racing with the leaders, she hit the front at the final turn and pulled away to win by 8 lengths.\nIn 2017 she won the Cox Plate in record time, the first to win it three times running since Kingston Town in 1982. She kept winning, and her Chipping Norton S. win broke Black Caviar's Australian record of 15 G1 wins.\nThen came 2018. At 7, with retirement in sight, she showed no sign of slowing. Besides extending her winning streak every time she ran, she set records like a third straight George Main S. and back-to-back Turnbull S. wins. Then she went for an unprecedented fourth straight Cox Plate.\nShe faced many strong rivals, including Dubai Turf winner Benbatl, but brushed them aside. Moving up from midfield, she took the lead with ease and won by 2 lengths, and was named Horse of the Year for the fourth straight year.\n\n* An Unbeaten US Triple Crown, But...\nJustify. His name means "to show to be right." 6 starts, 6 wins, an unbeaten US Triple Crown - who could have guessed that after such a brilliant career his own name would turn ironic?\nAfter debuting at 3, Justify aimed for the US Triple Crown: the Kentucky Derby, the Preakness S. and the Belmont S. Earning enough qualifying points by winning the Santa Anita Derby, he went for the Triple Crown fully prepared.\nHe won the Kentucky Derby from the front in commanding style. Two weeks later he won the Preakness S., then went to the Belmont S. unbeaten.\nHe led and set a fast pace. In the straight no one could go with him, and he drew away to the line. He was the 13th US Triple Crown winner - only the second unbeaten one, after Seattle Slew in 1977, and the first ever to debut at 3. Just 112 days after his debut, the fastest Triple Crown ever.\nHe was then prepared for the BC Classic in autumn, but an injury forced his retirement, to much regret. After shining like a brilliant shooting star, he went to stud.\nBut in 2019 a scandal emerged: after his Santa Anita Derby win, a banned substance had been detected in his sample. Had he not won the Santa Anita Derby, he could not have run in the Kentucky Derby. The scandal grew into a huge affair involving many people.\nHis camp denied using any drug, claiming it came from a plant growing wild. The legal battle dragged on, and in March 2024 he was officially disqualified. Even so, his Triple Crown that followed was not taken away. His record and achievement will remain.\n\n* The Stayers' Million Is Founded\nWith British racing shifting toward shorter races, the BHA (British Horseracing Authority) founded the Stayers' Million this year, a long-distance race series to protect the breeding of stayers. A horse that won one qualifier, such as the Sagaro S. or the Yorkshire C., and then won all three main races - the Gold C., the Goodwood C. and the Lonsdale C. - earned a GBP 1 million bonus, a huge sum for British racing then. Before the series began, no horse had ever won all three main races in the same year, and a winner was expected to be rare. But a monster stayer, Stradivarius, not only did it with ease but did it two years running. Whether that was the direct cause is unclear, but the series was scrapped after just two years.
80=* Australia's Great Mare Leaves With One More Feat\nIn April 2019 the great mare Winx, who had left so many legends on the world's turf, was about to run her final race, the Queen Elizabeth S. at Royal Randwick.\nSix months after her unprecedented fourth Cox Plate, she had also won the Chipping Norton S. and the George Ryder S. for the fourth straight time, stretching her winning streak to 32 and her G1 tally to 24 - truly unbreakable records.\nIn the Queen Elizabeth S. she was overwhelming favorite at 1.1. After the start she waited in midfield to the rear, saving her energy, then came wide at the final turn and swept past the whole field. Japan's Kluger charged hard up the inside, but couldn't match her explosive finish. She ran out the last furlongs of her career and won by a length and a half.\n33 straight wins, 25 G1s. And with this race's prize money she passed Arrogate, the previous record holder, to become the world's all-time leading earner: A$26,451,174 (about 2.124 billion yen). No one threatened her record until Hong Kong's Golden Sixty broke it in 2023.\nHaving finished her last race, Australia's hero and undisputed queen left the turf - with a win so dominant some said she shouldn't retire yet. Perhaps no one, her camp included, ever found the limit of her ability.\nHer regular jockey H. Bowman later said, "She has nothing left to prove."\n\n* A Shock Arc Finish\nHaving won two Arcs, Enable ran in the 2019 Arc going for a third. As people hoped for a historic record, an assassin stood in her way: Waldgeist, a colt who had been 4th in the 2018 Arc.\nUnremarkable early in his career, he came into his own as an older horse, winning G1s like the Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud and the Prix Ganay. In the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. he trailed stars like Enable and Crystal Ocean, but still ran a fine 3rd. He then won the Prix Foy, the Arc prep, and went into Arc day on a roll.\nOn the day, Longchamp was all about Enable. Fans were handed boards to cheer her on, and of course she was favorite. Everyone hoped for an unprecedented third win. Her expected rivals were Prix Niel winner Sottsass and Grand Prix de Paris winner Japan. Waldgeist was only 5th choice.\nRain since the day before had left the ground soft. The 12 runners ploughed through the heavy going. Enable, racing just off the leaders, burst out of the pack after the false straight. But perhaps drained by the tough ground, she faltered just short of the line. Pouncing was Waldgeist, who had been biding his time in the pack. Unleashing his kick in the final furlong, he caught the queen 50m out and pulled away to the line.\nIt was the first Arc win by a colt aged 5 or older in 17 years, and the first by a Prix Foy winner in 35 years. Having set new records, Waldgeist left the turf after this race.
