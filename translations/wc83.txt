1822=A Symboli Kris S dirt horse; won 3 straight, then the Hakusan Daishoten by 6 lengths on heavy ground.
1894=Debuting at Sapporo at 2, he won a maiden in late October and was 4th in the Tokyo Sports Hai 2yo S., but missed the classics. He reached open class with the Spica S. (16M) at 5, was 3rd as 12th choice in the Fukushima Kinen, and the next year moved from the 3rd turn to win the Nakayama Kinen as 13th choice, then 3rd as 7th choice in the Nikkei Sho, retiring at 7. In open class his placings came at 12th, 8th, 13th and 7th choice, ridden by longshot specialist Teruo Eda - longshot fans owed him plenty.
1906=Champion 2yo filly in Gentildonna's crop, from a superb family with many graded winners incl. Buena Vista and Admire Aura. She won her debut as favorite and got into the Hanshin JF by ballot. Only 4th choice, she won with something to spare - the 3rd Hanshin JF winner from one family after her dam Biwa Heidi and sister Buena Vista. Oka Sho favorite at 3, she closed too late, 6th, then a fracture kept her out for the year. The next year she was 4th to Verxina in the Victoria Mile, but broke a leg in training and was put down.
1917=A German mare who won 5 G1s, incl. the 2011 Arc and 2012 King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. She came to the 2011 Japan Cup as favorite but was 6th to Buena Vista. Emerging at 3, she won 2 German G1s that summer, the Grosser Preis von Berlin and Grosser Preis von Baden, then went to France and won the Arc - only the 2nd German-trained winner, the first in 36 years. In the Japan Cup she closed well from the back but only to 6th. Cartier champion 3yo filly. At 4 she won the King George - the first female to win both it and the Arc. She won the Grosser Preis von Baden again and was preparing to defend the Arc, but an infectious disease at her training center banned travel, and she retired. A broodmare in England, then in Japan from 2021, she has had no notable foals.
1918=Champion dirt horse of 2012 at 5, the year he won the Japan Cup Dirt. That year he won the Nagoya Daishoten (Nagoya) and Hakusan Daishoten (Kanazawa), and was 2nd in the JRA's Tokai S. and Miyako S. - at the forefront of dirt racing. In the Japan Cup Dirt, as 6th choice, he sat handy throughout and broke clear early in the straight to win easily. With superb early speed he added graded titles like the Heian S., Tokai S. and Diolite Kinen, but no more G1s: 2nd in the Teio Sho, 3rd in the Tokyo Daishoten. At 8 in 2015 his best was a 2nd in the Nagoya Grand Prix.
1919=By King Kamehameha out of a Mejiro McQueen mare, he raced at middle-to-long distances on turf at first, without success, then switched to dirt sprints - with success: open class in autumn at 4. In the Cluster C. at 5 he led and pulled away in the straight to win by 6 lengths. Unable to lead in the Tokyo Hai, he was 2nd to Love Me Chan. In the JBC Sprint he boldly took the lead and beat Sei Crimson, Suni and others by 3 lengths for his first G1. He also tried the Dubai Golden Shaheen (12th), and kept starring in dirt sprints.
1920=A mare with a gritty but slow-building finish, who won the 2012 Queen Elizabeth II Cup on soft ground. 8th in the Shuka Sho and 5th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup at 4, she reached open class only late at 5. In her 2nd Queen Elizabeth II Cup, helped by the soft going, she kept on to beat favorite Verxina. She ran in it a 3rd straight year at 6, then retired.
1921=Unbeaten in 3, he won the 2011 Asahi Hai FS. Expected to be open, it was his show: breaking well, he waited, then burst clear in the straight to win easily. 12th by a wide margin on soft ground in the Spring S., he skipped the Satsuki Sho for the NHK Mile C., beaten clearly by Curren Black Hill but holding 2nd, keeping his 2yo champion's pride. In the Derby, perhaps beyond his trip, he was 13th, then a tendon injury at pasture cost him nearly 2 years. Back at 5, he ran gamely in graded races, 2nd in the Tokyo Shimbun Hai and 3rd in the Niigata Daishoten. He was Symboli Kris S's first turf G1 winner.
1922=Winner of 4 graded races in 2013 incl. the Lord Derby Challenge T. and Sapporo Kinen. Unremarkable until 3, he stayed in allowances, then came good at 4: winning a 16-million race, then the Lord Derby Challenge T. In summer he won the Naruo Kinen, Hakodate Kinen and Sapporo Kinen in a row, joining the elite. 10th as 2nd choice in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), he went to Hong Kong and was a narrow 2nd in the Hong Kong Cup. In 2014 he raced abroad eagerly: Dubai Duty Free (7th), Singapore International C. (4th).
1923=By Heart's Cry, who beat Deep Impact in the Arima Kinen, out of Fabulous la Fouine, Shuka Sho winner and close 2nd in the Japan Cup. Winning his 4th start and a 5-million race, he tried the Yayoi Sho (6th) and Aoba Sho (4th) and gave up the classics. Winning steadily at middle-to-long trips, he was 2nd as favorite in the Diamond S. at 4, then beat Triple Crown winner Orfevre, Tenno Sho winner Hiruno d'Amour and others in the Hanshin Daishoten, and was 5th in the Tenno Sho (Spring) despite trouble. Autumn hopes ended when, after a 6th in the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai, he partially ruptured a tendon and retired. With Sunday Silence sires dominant, stud was hard even for a blueblood, and he became a riding horse.
1924=By Deep Impact out of Horse of the Year Air Groove - the ultimate blueblood, much noticed before her debut. She won it as 1.5 favorite, was 3rd to later Oka Sho winner Marcellina in the Elfin S., then won a 5-million special and ran in the Oaks: 3rd choice but 14th to Erin Court. Often 2nd in her own class, she was 14th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. In her 2nd race at 4, the Murasakino Tokubetsu, she won for the first time in a year, then the Hong Kong JCT with a superb run and the Mermaid S. easily. Autumn G1 success was expected, but a tendon injury struck, and she retired to breed at her birthplace.
1925=Half-brother of Miracle Legend (by Fuji Kiseki), queen of the dirt with 2 JBC Ladies' Classics. After 2 turf races he switched to dirt in his 3rd start and was transformed, leading all the way to win by 6 lengths, then kept winning on dirt. In his first graded race, the Elm S., he overpowered 6-time G1 winner Espoir City, proving himself, then won the Miyako S. after a duel with Nihon Pillow Ours and Hokko Tarumae. Favorite for the Japan Cup Dirt, he disappointed, 4th, but in the Tokyo Daishoten won a duel with Hatano Vainqueur and Wonder Acute for his first G1. In 2013, favorite in the Kashiwa Kinen, a mile perhaps too quick, he couldn't catch Hokko Tarumae and Espoir City, 3rd, then 6th in the Teio Sho, and went winless all year. At 6 in 2014 he won the Elm S. off a break, then was 3rd in the new G1 Champions C., and in 2015 3rd in the Heian S. and Miyako S.
1926=Half-brother of Tenno Sho (Autumn) winner Tosen Jordan (by Jungle Pocket); he sold for 162.75 million yen, 2nd-highest at the 2009 foal sale. Debuting at the end of 2, he was 2nd twice as a fancy before winning his 3rd start. After the Daikanzakura Sho he raced forward instead of holding back in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai and beat Veiled Impact and others for a Derby ticket. In the Derby he tracked in 2nd and held on doggedly for 3rd, raising autumn hopes, but a tendon injury while resting ended his career, and he went to stud.
1927=By Osumi Haruka, 4-time graded winner incl. 2 Queen S. and twice 2nd in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. Beaten badly in 2 turf races, he switched to dirt and won a maiden by a wide margin in his 2nd dirt race, then a 5-million race and the Hyogo Championship. 2nd to Straw Hat in the Unicorn S. and 4th to Hatano Vainqueur in the Japan Dirt Derby, older he led all the way to win the Diolite Kinen as 6th choice.
1928=From Deep Impact's 2nd crop; his broodmare sire Singspiel was a British great who won the Dubai World Cup and Japan Cup. He won his debut by 2.5 lengths from 2nd. In the Radio Nikkei Hai 2yo S., with a Big Three of Erica Sho winner Trip and Sapporo 2yo S. 1-2 Grandezza and Gold Ship, he was 4th choice; saving ground among the leaders, he slipped up the inside of Grandezza in the straight to win by 1.5 lengths. Unbeaten in 2, a leading 2012 classic fancy, he then fell away: 8th in the Yayoi Sho, last (18th) in the Satsuki Sho. A tendon injury sidelined him; back in 2014, he won an allowance, his first win in 2.5 years.
1929=From the first crop of autumn triple crown winner Zenno Rob Roy. Like his sire, he bloomed when older; still in the 16-million class, he ran in the Takarazuka Kinen as last choice, 8th. After a 2nd in the Koto S. (16M), he held off Kikuka Sho winner Oken Bruce Lee in the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai, 3 lengths clear of 3rd. In the Japan Cup, as 11th choice, he was 4th, best of his crop ahead of Derby winner Eishin Flash. Just 14 days later he was 6th of 13 in the Hong Kong Vase. At 5 he beat Dark Shadow and others in the Kyoto Kinen and eyed Dubai, but nosebleeds ended that plan; after a 9th in the Meguro Kinen and another nosebleed, he went to America, where Lasix is allowed. 2nd against West Coast's top milers in the Arroyo Seco Mile, he was a fine 4th in the BC Turf. Back home, tired and against stronger rivals, he was 13th in the Arima Kinen, and kept racing abroad. From summer at 6 he raced only in Japan, without success, retiring after a 15th in the Nikkei Sho. He became a riding horse in Minamisoma.
1930=Brother of 5-time dirt graded winner Wonder Speed, by US dual classic winner Charismatic, imported to Japan. Debuting in January at 3, he won 3 in 6 months, all at 7th choice or longer. 5th in the Japan Dirt Derby, he teamed up with Ryuji Wada that autumn, winning the Auckland RCT (16M), the Sirius S. and the Musashino S. 3rd choice for the Japan Cup Dirt, he was 6th. At 4 he was scratched from the Antares S. and rested; back after a year, he was 6th in the Miyako S., won the Betelgeuse S. (OP) and was 10th in the Tokyo Daishoten behind Smart Falcon's crushing win. Often falling short after, he won the Tokai S. in May at 5, then split the Transcend-Espoir City duel for 2nd in the Japan Cup Dirt and chased Smart Falcon to 3.5cm in the Tokyo Daishoten - 2 straight G1 2nds. At 6 he was 3rd in the February S., then rested after 2 poor runs. Refreshed, after 6 months off he led and drew away to win the JBC Classic by 5 lengths, his long-sought G1. He was 2nd to Nihon Pillow Ours in the Japan Cup Dirt and 3rd to Roman Legend in the Tokyo Daishoten. In 2014 he was 6th as Copano Ricky led all the way in the February S. and 3rd when Copano Ricky ran him down in the Kashiwa Kinen, then in the Teio Sho tracked Copano Ricky and passed him in the straight to win by 2 lengths - a 2nd G1. Racing on, he won the Kashiwa Kinen at 9, and in his farewell, the Tokyo Daishoten, was 3rd to Sound True.
1931=Debuting late, in spring at 3, she beat older horses in a 5-million race in autumn and ran in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, outclassed while still developing, 15th to Snow Fairy. She then won the Sakaiminato Tokubetsu and Asuka S. to reach open class, was 2nd with the fastest finish in the Nakayama Himba S., and won the Fukushima Himba S. with a burst from midfield. Her name comes from a classic film that won the Palme d'Or at Cannes.
1932=A Deep Impact colt hyped before his debut. He won his debut in December at 2, then as 1.2 favorite in the Wakagoma S. was caught out by runaway leader Zeros, 2nd. He then won the Kisaragi Sho and Wakaba S., the leading classic contender. In the Satsuki Sho he clipped heels and stumbled after the start, yet finished 2nd to Gold Ship - impressive in defeat. In the Derby he closed fastest but the slow pace didn't suit him, 4th to Deep Brillante. Hoping for autumn revenge, he suffered a tendon injury and a long break. Back after 20 months, 5th in the Shirafuji S. (OP), he won the Milers C. over fellow Deep Impact stars like improver Fiero, on 3 straight, and Kyoto Kimpai winner Extra End. He showed his class around a mile - 5th in the Yasuda Kinen, 4th in the Hong Kong Mile - and went to Australia at 6, but didn't win again.
1933=At 3 he held his own against older horses in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) and Japan Cup, and at 4 in 2013 won the Tenno Sho (Spring). A classic hope in spring at 3, he disliked Nakayama, so skipped the Satsuki Sho for the Derby, losing by a nose to Deep Brillante. Ignoring the Kikuka Sho, he took on older horses in autumn: 2nd in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), 5th in the Japan Cup. At 4 he won the Nikkei Sho, then beat favorite Gold Ship in the Tenno Sho (Spring) for a G1. Gold Ship turned the tables in the Takarazuka Kinen (4th). Missing the autumn injured, he was 5th as 2nd choice in the next Nikkei Sho, then in the Tenno Sho (Spring) took the lead early and held off Win Variation and others by a neck for back-to-back wins - a "monster," as his name says. Winless after, he retired injured to stud.
1934=Steady on turf, winning the Falcon S., he switched to dirt at 4 and reached the top. 3rd in the Elm S., he won the Miyako S. as 2nd choice, saving ground from an inside draw and holding on by a nose. He was 4th in the Japan Cup Dirt, 5th in the February S. and 5th in Dubai's Godolphin Mile, but didn't win again.
1935=Owned by Sheikh Mohammed, ruler of Dubai. With 1 win from 6 by spring at 3, she won the Kunshiran Sho by 3.5 lengths, an Oaks hope, then the Flora S. from midfield with the fastest finish, and was Oaks favorite. Closing as usual, she didn't quicken at all, 13th, far behind Gentildonna. Losing her rhythm, she was 3rd in the Queen S. on a light weight but never won again.
1936=Her dam Omega Island was a half-sister of Arima Kinen winner Heart's Cry, a year younger. Debuting in September at 2, she closed fastest in 3 straight races, with 2 wins and a 3rd. Favorite in the Fairy S., she was 4th, then 9th in a sprint-finish Queen C., but won the Flower C. on heavy ground with the fastest finish. In the fillies' classics she was 12th, 12th and 14th, seemingly an early bloomer, but in her last race at 3, the Turquoise S., she ran 2nd as last choice.
1937=A 2012-2013 dirt star who won the JRA-local Japan Dirt Derby and Kawasaki Kinen. JRA-based but a dirt specialist, at 3 he aimed for Oi's Japan Dirt Derby and won it as favorite with a fierce finish. 2nd in the year-end Tokyo Daishoten, at 4 he beat favored Wonder Acute in the Kawasaki Kinen. 2nd in the Diolite Kinen and Hakusan Daishoten, he had a tendon injury and retired at the end of 2013.
1938=Beaten badly in a turf debut, he won a dirt maiden by 6 lengths, then fought later G1 winner Hokko Tarumae to a neck in the Leopard S. in summer at 3. He won the Brazil C. by 5 lengths, but was caught in a collapse of the leaders in the Musashino S. After a long break, he won the Betelgeuse S. in his 3rd race back. Teamed with Koshiro Komaki in the Jinsen S. at 5, he won by 4 lengths, then swept forward from behind to win a blanket finish (first 4 on the same time) in the G3 Antares S. Winless after, he was 8th choice in the G1 Champions C. but charged to 2nd behind Hokko Tarumae, then kept closing too late.
1939=Related to Colorful Days, who starred in JRA-local races incl. the Kanto Oaks. After 2 dirt wins she tried the Fillies' Revue, an Oka Sho trial (7th). Giving up turf, she beat colts in the Shoryu S., but faded from the front to 11th in the Unicorn S. Against top older mares Miracle Legend, Clave Secreta and others in the Sparking Lady C., she was a good 4th. In the Queen Sho, her first race in 5 months, her rider let her set her own pace up front and she beat Clave Secreta by 4 lengths. She has been a steady force among dirt fillies.
1940=Full sister of fillies' Triple Crown winner Gentildonna, a year older. Compact but talented, she won her first 2. Favorite in the Shinzan Kinen over later classic winners Orfevre and Marcellina, she lost for the first time, 5th. Often closing too late after, she missed the spring classics. But in the Kyoto Himba S. she moved early to win, showing versatility. In her first G1, the Victoria Mile, she raced forward and held on for 2nd, won the Sekiya Kinen in summer, and was 3rd in the Mile CS with a sharp finish. Winless after, she was 5th in the next Mile CS, running well around a mile.
1941=A great mare of many G1s, hailed as the best in years. In 2012 she became the 4th fillies' Triple Crown winner, then won the Japan Cup in a clash of Triple Crown winners with previous colt Triple Crown winner Orfevre. Most favored the older colt, but after a fierce duel she won by a nose - the first 3yo filly named Horse of the Year. At 4, aiming for the Arc, she was 2nd in the Dubai Sheema Classic and 3rd in the Takarazuka Kinen, and the Arc plan was dropped. Venting her frustration, she won the Japan Cup as favorite in autumn - the first to win it twice. In 2014 she returned to the Dubai Sheema Classic for revenge: blocked in the straight, she regained balance and surged to win a G1 abroad. Back home, one of a Big Three with Gold Ship and Win Variation in the Takarazuka Kinen, she was 9th. In the Tenno Sho (Autumn) she won a duel with 3yo Isla Bonita but was beaten by Spielberg, bursting down the middle, 2nd. 4th going for a 3rd Japan Cup, her camp at once named the Arima Kinen as her last race. Amid doubts - "is she declining?" "does right-handed Nakayama suit her?" - she was 4th choice. But guided by jockey Tosaki, she raced boldly, pulled clear of Japan Cup winner Epiphaneia and held off Gold Ship and Just a Way to win her farewell. Her 7 turf G1s equaled her sire Deep Impact's record (as of 2014), and her earnings of over 1.7 billion yen were 2nd only to T.M. Opera O, ahead of Orfevre (as of 2014). A retirement ceremony followed, and the great mare left the turf to the cheers of a big crowd. As a broodmare, her 3rd foal Geraldina won the 2022 Queen Elizabeth II Cup - the "lady's" blood has crowned a new queen.
