1024=She won the Flora S. and, with 3 wins from 4, was Oaks favorite without Vodka and Daiwa Scarlet, but lost by a nose to Robe Decollete. 2nd in the Rose S. and 4th in the Shuka Sho, at 4 she was 3rd in the Fuchu Himba S. and Queen Elizabeth II Cup. With a powerful kick, always among the fastest finishers yet falling short, she was frustrating. In her last race, the '09 Fuchu Himba S., Mood Indigo outkicked her. Her G1 dream passes to her foals.
1043=He won his debut, the Daily Hai 2yo S. and Kyoto 2yo S. impressively, but injury kept him out of the Satsuki Sho. He made the Derby but was 4th. After that his best was a 2nd in the Naruo Kinen, below expectations. After a stable move he suffered a twisted intestine; surgery succeeded but meant a long break. Back after about a year, he led all the way in an open special, then won the Sekiya Kinen in summer. He later thrilled mile fans with last-to-first charges after slow starts, but never won another graded race, retiring to stud at the end of 7.
1047=4th in the Oka Sho and 3rd in the Oaks, she was 2nd in the American Oaks. In autumn she was 2nd in the Shuka Sho and 4th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. Older, she was 2nd in the Victoria Mile, then won the Queen S., and was 2nd in the Fuchu Himba S. After nearly a year off, she was last in the '08 Fuchu Himba S., a fracture was found, and she retired to breed.
1065=Winner of the 1975 Satsuki Sho and Derby and Horse of the Year. His 9 straight wins, from his debut at 3 (old age system) to an open race in spring at 5, are the JRA record. But more than records, Kaburaya O is remembered for his wild front-running, called "killer laps" and "insane pace." After winning the Tokyo Yonsai S. (now Kyodo News Hai) and Yayoi Sho, he led the Satsuki Sho at a blistering 58.9 for the first 1000m and pulled away in the straight for a record win. Lake Sprinter, who dueled him, broke a leg in the race and was put down, giving rise to the feared names. After the NHK Hai, he led the Derby even faster, 58.6 for 1000m. Surely he couldn't last 2400m - yet, mouth open and wandering in the straight, he held everyone off and won, amazing fans. No horse has since won the Derby leading at such a pace. Injury ruled out the Kikuka Sho; back in spring at 5 he won 2 dirt races and 1 on turf, but another injury forced his retirement. At stud he sired Queen Elizabeth Cup winner Miyama Poppy and Derby runner-up Grandpa's Dream. His camp later revealed his front-running was a tactic born of his timid nature and rare heart-lung capacity.
1066=Winner of 6 G1s incl. the English and Irish Oaks, and familiar in Japan for winning the Queen Elizabeth II Cup in 2010 and 2011. Little rated at first, at 2 she won just a maiden from 6 starts. At 3 she won a Listed race easily, then the Oaks for her first stakes win - a G1. She won the Irish Oaks by 8 lengths, top of her crop. In autumn she came to Japan and won the Queen Elizabeth II Cup by 4 lengths from Meisho Beluga, then won the Hong Kong Cup, Cartier champion 3yo filly. At 4 she suffered near misses - 2nd in the Irish Champion S. and 3rd in the Arc - but came back to Japan to win the Queen Elizabeth II Cup again. At 5 she won the Irish Champion S. for her 6th G1, then retired with leg trouble. Her 3rd foal, John Leeper by Frankel, made headlines running in the Derby.
1067=The best son of Takeshiba O. Debuting at Oi, he won the 1978 Southern Kanto Triple Crown: Haneda Hai, Tokyo Derby and Tokyo Okansho. After 5 straight wins from autumn at 3 (old age system), he won the Haneda Hai by 2.5 lengths, then the Tokyo Derby by 6 for 7 straight. 3rd in the Tokyo Hai in autumn, he won the Tokyo Kinen, then the Tokyo Okansho by 5 lengths for the Triple Crown, and the year-end Tokyo Daishoten too. At 5, after a 2nd in the Teio Sho, he moved to the JRA, but was 12th in the Arima Kinen, his first big defeat, and retired. His best foal was Asahi Challenge C. winner Hatsushiba Ace.
1068=A star of the 1965 classics: after 2nds in the Satsuki Sho and Derby, he won the Kikuka Sho, in a fierce rivalry with Derby winner Keystone. Emerging late at 3 (old age system), he won the Kisaragi Sho at 4 and beat Keystone, on a 6-race streak, in their first meeting in the Spring S. But outsider Chitose O beat him in the Satsuki Sho, and after winning the NHK Hai he lost the Derby duel to Keystone - no spring title. Owner Ueda, obsessed with winning the Derby, had bought him just before the race, and his narrow defeat popularized the saying "the Derby can't be bought." In autumn he won the Kobe Hai (now Kobe Shimbun Hai) and an open race, then beat Keystone in the Kikuka Sho for his classic title. After a 4th in the Hanshin Daishoten, he slumped from 5, switched to jumps at 7, won once and retired. At stud he sired many graded winners like Nishino Raiden, Kitano Rikio and Hoshu Missile, leading Japan-bred sires in hard times and ranking 4th on the sire list in 1981.
1069=The great heir who developed Mill Reef's blood in Japan. He sired many local stars like Rocky Tiger, Inari One and Rosita, and JRA G1 winners like Osaichi George and Linden Lily, and in 1989 was champion sire of JRA and local racing combined, ahead of Northern Taste. An American racehorse, he won his 3rd start, then a 9-furlong dirt allowance in a good 1:50.8, showing promise, but a fracture soon after ended his career. Standing in Japan, his first crop gave local star Rocky Tiger, followed by Inari One - who starred at Oi, then won 3 JRA G1s - Takarazuka Kinen winner Osaichi George and Southern Kanto star Rosita. Many excelled on dirt, and he was local racing's champion sire from 1987 to 1989. But he lacked successful sons, and his male line died out.
1070=The 1978 Derby winner, who also won the Takarazuka Kinen at 5 (old age system). Emerging early, he won the Tokyo Yonsai S. (now Kyodo News Hai) and was 2nd choice for the Satsuki Sho, 3rd to Fantast. He gained revenge in the Derby, breaking clear from a good spot and holding on by half a length. In autumn he won the St. Lite Kinen and was 2nd in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai, then favorite for the Kikuka Sho, but 5th to Inter Gushiken. Still, he was a respectable 5th in the Arima Kinen for his crop. Older, he won the American JCC and Meguro Kinen (Spring), was 2nd in the Tenno Sho (Spring), and won the Takarazuka Kinen as favorite, a Grand Prix winner. Winless after, he retired after the Meguro Kinen in February at 6. At stud he succeeded too, siring double classic winner Sakura Star O.
1071=A fast grey known as sire of Tamamo Cross, called "the White Lightning" for his sharp late charge. At 4 (old age system) he ran in the classics, 5th in the Satsuki Sho and 7th in the Derby. In autumn he was 2nd in the Lord Derby Challenge T. but 10th in the Arima Kinen. At 5 he won the Kimpai (East), was 3rd to Kashu Chikara in the Tenno Sho (Spring), and in autumn won the Mainichi Okan and Meguro Kinen. Retired to stud at 8, he sired 3-time G1 winner Tamamo Cross, White Stone, Inazuma Cross and many more.
1072=A sire who took Japan by storm from the late 1980s to early '90s. As Northern Taste's blood spread, the Shadai group bought him in America as a new line to cross with it. Carrying the "Shadai" name, he raced in France until 3, winning the Grand Prix de Deauville (G2), 2nd in the French Derby and 5th in the Arc, then went to stud in Japan from 1984. His first crop gave graded winners Muguet Royal and Free Talk, his 2nd Oka Sho winner Shadai Kagura, then Hanshin Sansai S. winner Ibuki Maikagura and Rice Shower, 3-time G1 winner incl. the Kikuka Sho and Tenno Sho (Spring). In 1993 he beat Northern Taste to be champion sire. Other graded winners include Monsieur Shekel, Stage Champ and Hagino Real King. His runners were mostly strong stayers, and as a broodmare sire he produced Tenno Sho (Spring) winners Ingrandire and Admire Jupiter - a renowned source of stamina.
1073=His name means "blushing groom," inspired by his dam Runaway Bride. He won 5 G1s incl. the French 2000 Guineas and sired many heirs like Nashwan and Rainbow Quest. His 2yo season was brilliant: from his 2nd start he won 5 straight, sweeping all 4 French 2yo G1s, an undisputed champion. The streak continued at 3: after a G3 prep he won the French 2000 Guineas by 3 lengths. In England he was 3rd in the Derby, and after a 2nd in the Jacques le Marois that summer he retired. At stud in America he sired 24 G1 winners, incl. Derby winner Nashwan, Arc winner Rainbow Quest, and Crystal Glitters and Arazi, later imported to Japan as sires. As a broodmare sire he produced Lammtarra, T.M. Opera O, Goldikova and many more, leading broodmare sire in Britain and Ireland in 1988 and 1995.
1074=Famous as the great rival of crop-mate and US Triple Crown winner Affirmed. He was 2nd to Affirmed in all 3 legs, and in the Belmont S. the two fought a fierce match race, 13 lengths clear of the rest. In 10 meetings he won only 3, but his ability was highly rated: 27th in The Blood-Horse's "Top 100 US Racehorses of the 20th Century." He won 14 of 26, incl. 6 G1s. At stud he sired G1 winners Alysheba, Easy Goer, Strike the Gold and many more, and was US champion sire in 1990, far surpassing Affirmed, who disappointed at stud. As a broodmare sire his blood reached Lure, Peintre Celebre and others, a strong branch of the Raise a Native line, though behind Mr. Prospector's.
1075=With Sadler's Wells and Nijinsky, one of Northern Dancer's most successful sire sons. Leg trouble limited him to 3 races, a maiden and allowances, but at stud he showed his worth. His first crop gave 8-time G1 winner Chief's Crown, followed by Danehill, Green Desert, Dayjur, Adjudicating and more. US champion sire 3 years running from 1991, he also sired Japanese stars like Agnes World and Yamanin Paradise. Many sons succeeded at stud, especially Chief's Crown, Danehill and Green Desert, with many G1 winners. At one point his branch made up about half of the Northern Dancer line, and it is now seen as an independent line: the Danzig line.
1076=America's 9th Triple Crown winner, all 3 legs in record time. In the Belmont S. he won by 31 lengths in a legendary world record 2:24.0. Not only unbroken, no horse has since even run 2:25 - called an unbeatable time. He raced until the end of 3, winning 16 of 21, Eclipse Horse of the Year at 2 and 3, and 2nd only to Man o' War in The Blood-Horse's "Top 100 US Racehorses of the 20th Century." At stud he sired 11-time G1 winner Lady's Secret, incl. the BC Distaff, and dual classic winner Risen Star, but few others; his male line is fading. As a broodmare sire, though, he produced great sires like Storm Cat, A.P. Indy and Gone West, deeply influencing today's pedigrees.
1077=An Irish horse who, like his great-grandsire Ribot, won the Arc twice. Those were his only G1s, but trainer O'Brien called him "on a par with Nijinsky, or better." At 2 he ran once, winning his debut. At 3 he won graded races steadily aiming for the St. Leger, where he was 2nd, then won the Arc as favorite, a star. At 4 his preparation was disrupted, winning just 2 graded races before the Arc, which he won again as favorite, retiring with 2 Arcs. 9 wins from 10, his only loss a 2nd in the St. Leger. At stud in America he sired many G1 winners like BC Turf winner Miss Alleged and Irish Derby winner Law Society; son Fair Judgement was imported to Japan as a sire.
1078=From Northern Dancer's 2nd crop, he was outstanding as both racehorse and sire, and did much to establish the Northern Dancer line. Unbeaten, he won the English Triple Crown and crushed older horses in the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. He was 2nd in the Arc, missing the European Triple Crown, but no horse has won the English Triple Crown since him, the first in 35 years. His dominance led to a then-record $5.44 million syndication, and he went to stud in America the next year. His first crop gave Green Dancer, then Maruzensky, Ile de Bourbon, Yamanin Ski and others who became successful sires. With his most successful son Caerleon and Lammtarra too, he built the great Nijinsky line. His male line is becoming a minority, but through dams his blood runs widely in stars like Dance in the Dark, Special Week, Taiki Shuttle and Buena Vista.
1079=He established the Mr. Prospector line through heirs like Miswaki, Woodman and Kingmambo - with Northern Dancer, one of the two giants of modern pedigrees. On the track he was an ordinary sprinter, never winning a stakes race, just setting 2 records over 6 furlongs. But at stud he sired Fappiano in his 2nd crop, Miswaki in his 3rd and Conquistador Cielo in his 4th, and with Gulch, Gone West and Seeking the Gold starring he was champion sire in 1987-88. His sons succeeded at stud too, building a great empire. In Japan, Kingmambo's son King Kamehameha was champion sire, and his son Lord Kanaloa sired Almond Eye and many G1 winners - a force rivaling the Sunday Silence line.
1080=A Northern Dancer sire son who produced many stars: 6-time US G1 winner Theatrical, Miesque with 10 G1s in Europe and America, and 5-time European G1 winner Soviet Star. A miler who won 2 G3s, in his 3rd start he crossed the line first in the 2000 Guineas but was disqualified for interference. Then an infection ended his career with little to show. At stud his first crop gave Theatrical and his 3rd Miesque and Soviet Star, plus Arc winner Peintre Celebre and Japanese G1 winners Heart Lake and Black Hawk - a big success. Champion sire in France in 1987 and 1997. He never founded his own line, but through Miesque, dam of Kingmambo, he is a major broodmare sire.
1081=From Northern Dancer's 4th crop, he excelled mainly at sprint-mile trips, and at stud sired Dancing Brave, Mogami and others, a key branch of the Northern Dancer line. He ran in the classics, incl. the English and Irish Derbies, without success, then switched to shorter races: he won the G1 Jacques le Marois, was 2nd in the Moulin and won the Prix de la Foret, showing great aptitude. He retired at the end of 3 with 6 wins from 12. At stud he was French champion sire 2 years running from 1978, when his first crop turned 3, and US champion sire in 1986. With Dancing Brave and Mogami succeeding as sires, he founded the Lyphard line. His male line is fading, but his son Alzao sired Wind in Her Hair, dam of Deep Impact, spreading his blood widely.
1082=The first unbeaten US Triple Crown winner, also a great success at stud. Through his heir A.P. Indy he carries on the Bold Ruler line, still a strong force. Brilliant at 2, he won the G1 Champagne S. easily, Eclipse champion 2yo. At 3 he won 2 more G1s and, unbeaten in 6, won the Kentucky Derby, Preakness S. and Belmont S. with ease - the first unbeaten Triple Crown winner. The next year he met Affirmed, the Triple Crown winner a year younger. He beat him decisively in the Marlboro Cup H., but in their 2nd meeting, the Jockey Club Gold C., though ahead of Affirmed, he was beaten a nose by outsider Exceller. 14 wins from 17, 8 G1s. At stud he sired A.P. Indy, Slew o' Gold, Capote and others, and in Japan Taiki Blizzard and Dantsu Seattle; he is also broodmare sire of Kawakami Princess, Seeking the Pearl, Hishi Akebono and Agnes World.
1083=America's 11th Triple Crown winner. With Seattle Slew, a Triple Crown winner a year older, their rare clashes made headlines. At 2 he fought rival Alydar fiercely, the two running 1-2 almost every time; Affirmed won 4 of 6 and was Eclipse champion 2yo colt. At 3 they met in every Triple Crown leg, and he won all 3 narrowly, Alydar 2nd each time. He then met Seattle Slew in the Marlboro Cup H., the focus of the nation, but was beaten 3 lengths, 2nd. Still, he was Eclipse Horse of the Year, and the next year won 6 G1s to repeat the title. He retired with 22 wins from 29, 14 G1s, 12th in The Blood-Horse's "Top 100 US Racehorses of the 20th Century." At stud he sired Arlington Million winner The Tin Man and Canadian Triple Crown winner Peteski, but lacking successful heirs, his line is in decline.
1084=Winner of the European Triple Crown - the Derby, King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. and Arc - and 8th in Timeform's "100 Greatest Racehorses." At stud he founded the Mill Reef line through many heirs - one of the greats. At 2 he was rated 2nd, behind My Swallow, but at 3 became the first European Triple Crown winner and Horse of the Year. A clash at 4 with crop-mate Brigadier Gerard, the unbeatable miler, was eagerly awaited, but equine flu prevented it. He then suffered a severe fracture, survived and retired safely, with 12 wins and two 2nds from 14. At stud he sired Shirley Heights, Mill George, Magnitude and more, and was twice champion sire in Britain - the most successful sire of the Never Bend line, forming the Mill Reef line. From the mid-2010s it has faded, with only a few European successes like Shakeel, winner of the 2017 Grand Prix de Paris.
1085=Sire of Real Shadai, Brian's Time, Kris S. and more, he did much to establish the Hail to Reason line - and as a racehorse was resented as "the villain of the century." First, he won the Derby after a ruthless jockey switch: regular rider Williamson was replaced just before the race by master Lester Piggott, and he won amid loud boos. Second, he beat British hero Brigadier Gerard, ending his bid for a European-record 16th straight win. That great upset was not welcome at all, and he lost his spark afterward and retired. At stud he was a huge success, also siring Sunshine Forever, Silver Hawk, Dynaformer, Red Ransom and many other successful sires. In Japan today, his sire lines are firmly rooted: Kris S. to Symboli Kris S to Epiphaneia, and Silver Hawk to Grass Wonder to Screen Hero.
1086=An American horse, US champion sire in 1983 and 1989, who sired Sunday Silence and transformed Japanese pedigrees. Not a standout racehorse, he struggled to win a stakes race until the Lawrence Realization on turf in autumn at 3. Late maturing, at 5 he won the turf United Nations H., a G1 title. At stud his first crop gave Canadian Horse of the Year Glorious Song. In 1983 Sunny's Halo won the Kentucky Derby and Devil's Bag was champion 2yo, and he was champion sire. He later sired 7-time G1-winning mare Goodbye Halo and Sunday Silence, exported to Japan, deeply influencing pedigrees. He had a fierce temper to match his sire Hail to Reason's, and his habit of biting people is famous.
