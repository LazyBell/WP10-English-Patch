475=At 3 she was 6th in the Oaks, 2nd in the Shuka Sho and 8th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. A sharp closer, but at the mercy of the pace, she fell just short of a crown. At 4 she started in spring, improved through summer and was 2nd to Air Groove in the Sapporo Kinen. In her second Queen Elizabeth II Cup, her powerful kick caught favorite Dance Partner near the line for a G1.
479=Debuting late in October at 3, she steadily won races and the next May took the Takamatsunomiya Hai (now Kinen), newly a sprint G1 - the fastest older-horse G1 win ever, 6 months after her debut. In that year's Sprinters S. she chased down front-runner A Shin Washington in a thrilling finish. With no result for a long time after the photo, Nakayama buzzed, "The first G1 dead heat?" She won by just 1 cm. In Japan's sprint scene, she is the only filly or mare to win both sprint G1s in one year. She retired after a 4th in the Sprinters S. at 5. Her best offspring are 5-time winner Firenze (01: Sunday Silence) and Vincennes (09: Deep Impact), Tokyo Shimbun Hai winner and Yasuda Kinen runner-up.
484=A bargain under 10 million yen, she won her debut and the Phoenix Sho. But in the classics she was 11th in the Oka Sho, 9th in the Oaks and 4th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup At 4 she won the Coral S. (OP) for her first win in a while, then the Keihan Hai, her first graded race At 5 she won the Emerald S. and Kinko Sho in a row, plus the Kokura Kinen and All Comers. The next year she was 2nd to Yamanin Zephyr in the Yasuda Kinen as 14th choice and 2nd to Mejiro McQueen in the Takarazuka Kinen, becoming the top-earning mare. Running 51 races safely from 2 to 6, she won 4 graded races and was twice 2nd in 11 G1s - a tough mare called "the Iron Lady."
468=Half-sister to Sakura Chitose O. A late improver, she won a 5-million race and the Queen S. In the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, as 10th choice, she outdueled Bright Sandy to win, and later won the Fuchu Himba S.
480=Steady and rarely out of the top five, she won the Hanshin Himba Tokubetsu at 4 and ran in the Japan Cup and Arima Kinen before retiring. As a dam she produced classic runner She's a Chance (93: Sunday Silence).
