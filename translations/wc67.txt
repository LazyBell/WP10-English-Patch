1289=From Dandy Route's first crop, he gave his sire his first graded title. In spring at 4 (old age system) he was 2nd in the Keisei Hai and Tokyo Yonsai S., won the Yayoi Sho and was Satsuki Sho favorite, but broke down in the race and rested long. After struggling, he revived to win the Lord Derby Challenge T. at 6 and Meguro Kinen (Spring) at 7, racing until 8. He left few foals, but they included Nuevo Tosho, winner of 5 graded races like the Asahi Challenge C. and Aichi Hai.
1305=Everything on a fierce straight-long charge - fans loved his style. He won the Hakodate Sansai S. and Keisei Hai Sansai S. and was 2nd in the Asahi Hai Sansai S., but finished in double figures in the Satsuki Sho and Derby. Racing mainly at 2000m or less, he often closed too late: four 2nds and eight 3rds. He won the Keio Hai Autumn H. at 4 and the Tokyo Shimbun Hai and Nakayama Kinen at 6.
1307=A speedster who held the Hakodate 1200m record until One Carat broke it in July 2010. She won her debut at 3 as 9th choice, but injury delayed her return to autumn. Winning a 4-million race, she went straight to the Queen Elizabeth Cup, beaten badly, then kept adding wins, ridden by top jockeys like Yutaka Take and Katsumi Minai. Racing until 7, she won 2 G2s, the Swan S. and Keio Hai Spring C., and ran well in the Asahi Challenge C. and Takamatsunomiya Hai (G2: 2000m) - more than a sprinter.
1318=He needed 10 starts to win a maiden and 13 to leave the 4-million class, but switching to long turf races in September at 3 unlocked his ability. After a 16-million win, he beat Inari One, Sururi O Dyna and others in the Hanshin Daishoten, but was 15th in the Tenno Sho (Spring). He then switched to jumps and won the Kyoto Daishogai (Spring) at 6.
1320=2nd as 5th choice in the Kyodo News Hai, he won the Yayoi Sho on muddy ground over Sakura Hokuto O. 2nd in the Kikuka Sho, he retired with leg trouble.
1324=A local-track king, with 6 of 9 wins at Chukyo and Kokura, often by wide margins thanks to a sharp kick. At 5 he won the Kinko Sho and Kita-Kyushu Kinen. At 6 he won the Chukyo Kinen and was 3rd in the Yasuda Kinen, then closed fiercely for 2nd to Let's Go Tarquin in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), part of an upset. At 7 he swept past Sister Tosho, Yamanin Zephyr and others with a 34-second finish to win the Nakayama Kinen.
1325=He won his debut by 3 lengths, then the Kigiku Sho and Daily Hai Sansai S., the top classic favorite - until a fracture was found. Over a year off cost him the classics; his rider Yutaka Take lamented, "We've lost next year's classics." He returned in January at 4 with a bolt still in his leg and won the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai and Meguro Kinen. Everyone mourned what might have been had he stayed sound.
1326=A son of great sire Nijinsky. As a foreign-bred he couldn't run in the classics, but won his first graded race, the NZ Trophy Yonsai S. A fracture then cost him a year. Back, he won the December S. (OP), but was 13th in the Arima Kinen and never won again. At stud he sired Surprise Power (94: Shinobu Mochizuri), winner of the '97 Tokyo Derby and Tokyo Okansho and the '99 Nippon TV Hai.
1327=He won a debut race and 3 straight, drawing notice with a 2.5-length win in the Wakakusa S. A Derby dark horse, 5th choice without graded experience, he was 11th against a strong crop. After a 4th in the Chunichi Sports Sho Yonsai S. and a win in the Seaside S., he was 3rd in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai and 4th in the Kikuka Sho. The next year he beat Mejiro Ardan and others in the Nikkei Shinshun Hai - but in his next race, the Osaka Hai, he broke down and had to be put down.
1328=A gifted sprinter who came within a step of the top before his fragile end. Debuting late, in April at 3, he rose quickly, and 6 months later won the Swan S., beating sprint queen Daiichi Ruby in a Japanese record 1:20.6 - a superb win. But as favorite in the Sprinters S., on the verge of the top, he broke down in the race and was put down.
1329=He won the Kyoto Yonsai Tokubetsu with a swoop from behind, was 4th to Tokai Teio in the Derby, and won the Chunichi Sports Sho Yonsai S. easily, then fractured a leg. Back at 4, he hurt a ligament and retired after one race.
1330=A fine miler with G1 talent, kept from his best by repeated leg trouble. A fracture cost him the classics, but back he won the Nakayama Kinen and Keio Hai Spring C., heading to the Yasuda Kinen as the mile leader, where he was below par, 5th. Leg trouble then forced another long break; after 2.5 years he finally returned, a shadow of himself, and retired after one race.
1331=In the shock '92 Takarazuka Kinen he was 3rd behind Mejiro Palmer and Kamino Cresse, then beat strong rivals in the Takamatsunomiya Hai (G2: 2000m). Strong in form, but inconsistent.
1332=Running above his class in the February S., he beat the established stars to win - a new dirt star. Leg trouble then plagued him, and he retired after winning the Tokai S., his first win in 18 months.
1333=Debuting late, in May at 3, he won the Andromeda S. and Winter S. the next winter - his first graded win and a father-son Winter S. double with sire Andrea Mon. Injuries followed, and he moved to local Kaminoyama.
1334=He reached open class only at 6, after 30 races. He then ran mainly in dirt graded races, also trying long turf races, 5th in the Hanshin Daishoten. Truly "stronger with age," he won his first graded race, the Musashino S., at 7. At 6 he was 2nd to Lively Mount in the Breeders' Gold C. and to Admire Bosatsu in the Tokyo Daishoten; at 7, 3rd to Hokuto Vega in the Teio Sho; and at 8 he beat dirt star Kyoto City in the Oguri Cap Kinen - a fixture of dirt racing's transition era, battling its successive kings.
1335=He won the Wakana Sho and Wakagoma S., was 3rd in the Wakaba S., then 2nd to Mihono Bourbon in the Satsuki Sho. He won the NHK Hai (G2: 2000m) and went to the Derby primed, but did nothing, 7th. In autumn he was 10th in the Kyoto Shimbun Hai, then injury cost him over a year. Back, he was 14th in the Sirius S. and quietly retired.
1336=He won the Momiji S. and Keisei Hai Sansai S., then met Mihono Bourbon in the Asahi Hai Sansai S. Bourbon was 1.5 favorite, Yamanin Miracle a distant 2nd choice. Against expectations it became one of the closest finishes ever: as Bourbon tried to break clear, Miracle clung on desperately. They hit the line together; Bourbon won by a nose, but it was Miracle's finest hour.
1337=Running above his class, he was crushed in the Kikuka Sho. After a year off, a switch to dirt unlocked his talent: 3 straight wins to open class, then the March S. Big things were expected, but he died young in a training accident.
1338=With a sharp kick he won the Chukyo Sansai S. and Shinzan Kinen, but in the classics was 5th in the Satsuki Sho and 3rd in the Derby. Injured after the Derby, he retired and became a lead pony at Kyoto Racecourse.
1339=At 2 he won 1 of 4, an ordinary 6th in the Icho S. But at 3 he improved rapidly, winning the Hyacinth S. and the Yayoi Sho with speed and a sharp kick. 2nd choice for the Satsuki Sho, he charged into 3rd behind Mihono Bourbon, the leading Kanto hope against strong Kansai horses for the Derby. But he broke a leg in his final workout before the Derby - 2 bolts were needed - and had to retire.
1340=Gifted with speed, he often weakened late when young. At 4 he matured, learning to settle and race from behind. In open class he won the Kinko Sho and Asahi Challenge C. and was 3rd to Yamanin Zephyr in the Tenno Sho (Autumn).
1341=6 wins from 30, racing until 6. In the JRA-local Breeders' Gold C. he beat stars like Heisei Silver and Laurier Andre for his first graded title. At 4 he set a 1:42.5 record over 1700m on Sapporo dirt.
1342=He won a debut race in January at 3 but stalled, a late-blooming stayer who emerged when older. At 5 he swept past later Tenno Sho winner Sakura Laurel to win the Meguro Kinen. That year he was 3rd behind Rice Shower and Stage Champ in the Tenno Sho (Spring), part of a rare Real Shadai 1-2-3. He won the Nikkei Shinshun Hai at 6 and raced tough until 8.
1343=Despite chronic leg trouble, he lived up to his name with steady results. Spending over half his career resting, he ran just 21 times though racing until 7. Still, he won 10 - surely G1 class at heart. At 7 he won the Kyoto Kinen, his long-sought graded title, and that autumn won the Cassiopeia S. under 60kg. Valued for his talent, he went to stud.
1344=He won his debut in February at 3 easily, then the Mainichi Hai in only his 2nd start. 4th choice behind the Big Three in the Satsuki Sho, he was 3rd, ahead of Winning Ticket. 14th in the Derby, he rested long with leg trouble and retired after just 6 starts.
1345=The best son of Tamamo Cross, giving his sire his first graded title. From a slow-maturing family, he steadily built strength in allowances away from the classics, winning the Epsom C. in spring at 4. Then that winter he won the Naruo Kinen and American JCC, for a time the leader of the Kanto older horses. But in 7 G1 tries he never made the top five.
1346=At 3 he was 3rd in the Satsuki Sho, 4th in the Derby, and 3rd against older horses in the Mile CS. Versatile on dirt too, he won the Negishi S. at 3, champion dirt horse that year. Racing mainly at a mile on turf and dirt, incl. JRA-local races, he won 2 graded races on turf and 1 on dirt. He moved to Iwate at 7, winless, then to Arao, racing until 9.
1347=One of Derby winner Dyna Gulliver's best runners. A powerful runner unfazed by soft ground, at 4 he used heavy going to beat Tenno Sho winner Rice Shower in the Nikkei Sho. A middle-to-long distance regular after, he couldn't win, declined with age, and left the JRA at 6. At Kochi he found a new stage, winning 8.
1348=Half-brother of English and Irish Derby winner Generous. With leg trouble he worked steadily through the classes, reaching open class late at 4, but graded wins were hard. In March at 6, he won the Milers C. under rookie Koshiro Take, who had debuted the day before - his first win, and a graded win on just his 2nd day, a record. With Koshiro Take aboard he also won the Centaur S.
1349=Debuting late and needing 7 starts to win, he then rose quickly to open class and won the Kyoto Kimpai easily, then crushed Oaks winner Dance Partner in the Kyoto Kinen. His brother Shin Chest also won the Kyoto Kinen. But he fought his rider in the Tenno Sho (Spring), 15th, then lost form and retired at 6 without another win.
1350=Of his 26 races, he was worse than 3rd choice only 4 times - remarkably well backed, fitting his over-50% top-two rate, though he only became consistent from 5. At 6, his 3 straight graded wins (the Diolite Kinen before unified grading) hinted at a new dirt king, but a long injury break meant he never quite reshaped the field.
1351=Known as dam of Soul Stirring. A French racehorse, she won her first 6, incl. 3 G1s: the Prix Saint-Alary, French Oaks and Prix Vermeille. In the Arc she was 3rd choice but 7th to Sea the Stars. At 4 she won a G3, then the Prix Jean Romanet, her 4th G1, but then stopped winning, and at 5 moved to America. She won her first race there, the Beverly D. S., then the Flower Bowl Invitational S. - 2 straight G1s. 10th in the BC Filly & Mare Turf, she was still Eclipse champion turf female, and retired that year. Bought by the Shadai group, she had her first foal in Britain, then came to Japan in foal to Frankel - that foal became Oaks winner Soul Stirring. She also produced Artemis S. winner Schon Glanz, and her first foal, Southern Stars, became a broodmare in Japan, producing double classic filly Stars on Earth - her family is flourishing in Japan.
1352=Full brother of BC Turf winner Prized, much hoped for. But he was 2nd on debut against 3 other blueblood imports, and slow to get going. His 2nd to Hishi Amazon in the NZ Trophy Yonsai S. sparked a rise worthy of his pedigree, and he beat veterans like Airton Symboli and Stage Champ in the Argentina Kyowakoku Hai. After a 10th in the Arima Kinen he developed leg trouble and retired, his limits unknown. At stud his runners disappointed; exported to Korea, he died in an accident in transit.
1353=His first win came late at 4, but he made up for lost time, rising quickly to open class. He raced eagerly in JRA-local races, winning the Breeders' Gold C. twice among other successes.
1354=Steady in Hokkaido racing, he moved to Oi at 3. 2nd in the Keihin Hai and Haneda Hai, he won the Tokyo Derby from a good position and was 2nd to Furioso in the Japan Dirt Derby.
1355=After winning his 2nd start, he was 2nd in 3 straight graded/open races, then won the Kyodo News Hai Yonsai S. by 3 lengths from Tayasu Tsuyoshi and the Spring S., Satsuki Sho favorite. Scratched lame in his right shoulder, his luck turned: straight into the Derby he was 11th. In autumn he won the Kyoto Shimbun Hai trial but was 7th in the Kikuka Sho. Still short on big days, he moved to local Funabashi.
1356=Winless in 3 on turf, he won 7 on dirt: the Antares S. at 4, then the JRA-local Sakitama Hai and Kurofune Sho easily as favorite. Outclassed in deep dirt G1s, his best was 5th in the Nambu Hai.
1357=At 2 he was 3rd to Fuji Kiseki and Ski Captain in the Asahi Hai Sansai S., then used his speed in sprints, winning the Crystal C. at 3. Short of class in older graded races (best 3rd in the CBC Sho), he ran well on flat local tracks, winning the Marine S. and Seikan S. at 4 and running two close 2nds at 6. Never top class, he raced steadily until 7 - a popular supporting player.
1358=By Shin Chest out of Shin Miss Brown - the culmination of the "Shin" prefix. He won 2 graded races, the Asahi Challenge C. and Chunichi Shimbun Hai, at middle distances on turf. 8 wins from 27 but never 2nd - win or lose, nothing in between. After 4 straight double-figure finishes from late 7 to the Kyoto Kimpai at 8, he tried jumps, but broke down in his first race and was put down.
1359=He won his first 3, incl. the Shinzan Kinen, but was last in the Derby. In autumn at 3 he was 2nd in the Mile CS, setting up a huge payout - a one-off fluke, as he never made the top five again.
1360=With just 1 win, a 2nd in the Anemone S. got her into the Oka Sho. Favorite was Kasamatsu's Raiden Reader, in a strong field, but from 8th at the final turn she unleashed a fierce kick to beat Dance Partner. 3rd in the Oaks proved it no fluke. But she slumped in autumn, 16th in the Queen Elizabeth Cup, and in her comeback race, the Kyoto Himba Tokubetsu at 4, she fractured a leg and was put down.
1361=With 2 wins from 3, he was narrowly 2nd to Bubble Gum Fellow in the Asahi Hai Sansai S. and 8th in the NHK Mile C. He then won the Bodaiju S. and Sekiya Kinen, and the next year's Sekiya Kinen with a 33.7 finish.
1362=Despite injuries he ran steadily at a mile to middle distances, finally winning a graded race, the Tokyo Shimbun Hai, in his 23rd start. At 7 he won the Sapporo Kinen and Fuji S., but never won again.
1363=4 wins from 26, the Epsom C. his best, but with eight 2nds he was known for consistency. He never won a G1, but had near misses, above all the NHK Mile C., reborn that year as the spring 3yo mile championship: 2nd choice behind Fabulous la Fouine, he closed fastest in 34.7 but fell 3/4 of a length short. His half-brother Yeats (01: Sadler's Wells) was Europe's champion stayer 4 years running.
1364=A sharp miler. He won the Lord Derby Challenge T. at 4, was 10th in the Yasuda Kinen, then 3rd in the Swan S. and 4th in the Mile CS. At 5 he ran down front-running Eishin Berlin to win the Swan S.
