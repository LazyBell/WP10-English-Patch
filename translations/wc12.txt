61=* Two Contenders for Cartier Horse of the Year\nThis year Europe had two worthy candidates for Cartier Horse of the Year: Giant's Causeway, a 3yo star over short-to-middle distances, and Sinndar, also 3, the best over middle-to-long distances.\nGiant's Causeway won 5 straight G1s over about 8-10 furlongs (1600-2000m), including the St. James's Palace S., the Eclipse S. and the International S., then traveled to America for a fine 2nd in the Breeders' Cup Classic. Sinndar won the Derby and Irish Derby plus the Arc, utterly dominant at 12 furlongs (2400m). It was hard to choose between them: Giant's Causeway was named Cartier Horse of the Year, but Sinndar won champion 3yo colt - a split that had never happened before in the awards' history.\nBeyond these two, many stars emerged in 2000. Montjeu, who beat El Condor Pasa in the previous year's Arc, won 3 straight G1s including the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. Kalanisi, 2nd to Giant's Causeway in races like the Eclipse S., won the Champion S. and the Breeders' Cup Turf. And Dubai Millennium, named to be "the millennium's Dubai World Cup winner," won the 2000 Dubai World Cup just as his name promised. A match race between Dubai Millennium and Montjeu was proposed and drew great interest, but Dubai Millennium fractured a leg and retired, and the dream clash never happened.\n\n* The US and European Horses of the Year Clash\nIn America, Breeders' Cup Classic winner Tiznow was at the center of racing this year. He only broke his maiden at the end of May and missed the classics, but he improved rapidly through summer and autumn and won 3 straight, capped by the Breeders' Cup Classic. That remarkable run earned him Eclipse Horse of the Year. That Breeders' Cup Classic also featured Cartier Horse of the Year Giant's Causeway from Europe, so the future Horses of the Year of America and Europe met head-on in a star-studded race (Giant's Causeway ran a superb 2nd in his first dirt race).\nAnother headline: Fusaichi Pegasus became the first son of Mr. Prospector to win the Kentucky Derby. Mr. Prospector had sired many G1 winners and won the Preakness S. and Belmont S. early on, but the Kentucky Derby had always eluded him until his 22nd crop, when Fusaichi Pegasus finally fulfilled the long-held dream.\nAlso, Yutaka Take was on a long stint in America this year and rode 2yo Point Given on his debut. Point Given would be Eclipse Horse of the Year the next year, but back then he was an unknown, still developing 6th choice of 11, and Take's skill shone in bringing him home 2nd.
62=* Hong Kong Horse of the Year Two Years Running\nWith its international races established, Hong Kong racing was booming. But Hong Kong-trained horses long failed to win outside Hong Kong, Japan included, with near misses like Oriental Express's 2nd in the 1998 Yasuda Kinen and Indigenous's 2nd in the 1999 Japan Cup. Fairy King Prawn broke through. In the 2000 Yasuda Kinen he was only 10th choice of 18, but he burst through wide in the straight to win, defying the odds to become the first Hong Kong-trained winner of a Japanese G1. With his Hong Kong Sprint win also counted, he was named Hong Kong Horse of the Year for the 1999/2000 season (Hong Kong's season runs from September to early July).\nThe next season he won two Hong Kong G1s, then traveled to the Dubai Duty Free, running a superb 2nd by a neck to French horse Jim and Tonic and showing the world Hong Kong's strength. Back home he won another G1. He was 9th in the Yasuda Kinen and couldn't repeat, but with 3 G1 wins that year he was named Hong Kong Horse of the Year for the second straight season.\n\n* First-Ever Back-to-Back Breeders' Cup Classic\nThe year before, Tiznow beat Giant's Causeway and Fusaichi Pegasus to win the Breeders' Cup Classic and was named Eclipse Horse of the Year. In 2001 he opened with a win in the G2 San Fernando BC S. He was 2nd in his next G2, but won the big Santa Anita H. by 5 lengths for his 3rd G1.\nThen he was injured in training and went on a long break. The injury was serious enough that many expected him to retire, but his camp set a second Breeders' Cup Classic as the goal and kept him in training. Soon after his return, an event shook the world: the September 11 attacks.\nWith New York's World Trade Center devastated, the Breeders' Cup a month and a half later was, by fate, held at Belmont Park in New York State. The track was under tight security with soldiers and police dogs, an eerie atmosphere. With European stars like Sakhee and Galileo in the field, Tiznow, 3rd in both races since his return, was only 4th choice despite being the defending champion. In the straight he, Sakhee and Albert the Great fought fiercely. Sakhee went ahead, but as if showing American pride, Tiznow fought back to win by a nose - the first horse ever to win the Breeders' Cup Classic twice, lighting a spark of hope for American fans in their darkest hour. The commentator's cry as he crossed the line, "Tiznow wins it for America!", is still remembered as a classic call.
63=* Rock of Gibraltar's World Record\nRock of Gibraltar was named after the great rock on the Strait of Gibraltar, which links the Mediterranean and the Atlantic. True to his name, his running was rock solid. He stumbled to 6th in a G3 in his second start, but that was the only time in his life he finished out of the top two, and he ended his 2yo year with two straight G1 wins.\nAfter six months off, his first race of 2002 was the 2000 Guineas itself. Stablemate Hawk Wing was favorite; Rock of Gibraltar was only 4th choice, perhaps due to a jockey change and his first mile without a prep. Sitting in midfield, he led a furlong (200m) out, but Hawk Wing burst out of the pack and drew level at the line. After a photo, Rock of Gibraltar won by a short head. From then on he stuck to the mile division, winning the Irish 2000 Guineas and the St. James's Palace S. easily. Winning both 2000 Guineas was a first in 10 years, and adding the St. James's Palace S. a first in 33.\nNow Europe's best miler, no one could stop him. He won the Sussex S., his first race against older horses, without a scare. Then in the Prix du Moulin de Longchamp he won a fierce battle with French star filly Banks Hill for his 7th straight G1, breaking Mill Reef's record. The streak ended in the Breeders' Cup Mile, where he was 2nd after being hampered by an injured horse, and he retired. His reputation stood firm, and he was named Cartier Horse of the Year.\n\n* German-Bred Kazzia Wins the British Fillies' Double\nMeanwhile in Britain the German-born filly Kazzia was shining. After debuting in Germany in 2001 and winning her first graded race in her second start, she was bought by Godolphin (the racing operation led by Dubai's Sheikh Mohammed) and moved to Dubai.\nIn 2002 her first 3yo race was the 1000 Guineas. She was only 6th choice at 15, but she led a furlong out, held on and won by a neck - the first British G1 win by a German-bred. She kept rolling and won the Oaks too, completing the British fillies' double.\nHer camp planned the St. Leger for the Triple Crown, but a leg abscess forced her out. The Triple Crown dream was over, but she won one more G1, retired with 3 G1 wins, and was a great success as a broodmare too.
64=* "A Spear Through the Pack" High Chaparral\nThe year before, High Chaparral won the Derby and the Irish Derby with a spear-sharp finish. He was 3rd in the Arc, but won the Breeders' Cup Turf and was Eclipse champion turf male.\nHorses with records like that often retire early, but his camp kept him in training in 2003. With a shoulder injury, his first 4yo race was the G2 Royal Whip S. in August, which he just won by three-quarters of a length. In the Irish Champion S. he was only 3rd choice behind King George winner Alamshar and 3-time G1 winner that year Falbrav. He won by a neck from Falbrav, showing his power was intact.\nNext he took on the Arc. The year before a viral illness had ruined his summer preparation, so this time, fully prepared, he carried high hopes and was favorite. But unluckily the going was heavy. A closer by nature, he couldn't quicken in the straight and sank to 3rd.\nHe then traveled to America again for the Breeders' Cup Turf. Despite being the defending champ, perhaps because of the Arc loss, he was only 4th choice of 9. High Chaparral and Falbrav, sitting in good spots in midfield, surged up together in the straight. It looked like a two-horse fight, but Johar came charging from behind and all three hit the line together. After a 12-minute photo, High Chaparral and Johar dead-heated for the win, with Falbrav 3rd by a head - a thrilling finish, and High Chaparral became the first horse to win the Breeders' Cup Turf twice.\nHe retired after this race. With 3 wins from 4 that year, he was Eclipse champion turf male for the second straight year. At stud he succeeded in both Europe and Oceania, spreading the blood of his sire Sadler's Wells around the world.\n\n* Great Horsemen Retire, and a New Record\nThis year two great jockeys of Britain and America retired: P. Eddery and L. Pincay Jr.\nP. Eddery won 4,632 races, 2nd most in British history, and was champion jockey 11 times. He was especially famous for partnering Dancing Brave, whose 1986 Arc is called "the greatest Arc ever."\nL. Pincay Jr. was a great American jockey who broke the world record for career wins in 1999, with achievements such as three straight Belmont S. wins and 7 Breeders' Cup wins. He retired with 9,530 wins.\nMeanwhile, a trainer set a new record: America's R. Frankel won 25 G1s this year, including the Belmont S. with Empire Maker - a new world record, breaking the 23 set in 2001 by himself and Ireland's A. O'Brien.\n\n* The BHB Summer Triple Crown and Grand Slam\nThis year the BHB (The British Horseracing Board), Britain's racing authority, created bonus series called the BHB Summer Triple Crown and Grand Slam, aiming to boost British flat racing's most prestigious races. The first leg was the Oaks or Derby for 3yos, or the Coronation C. or Prince of Wales's S. for older horses; the second the Eclipse S.; the third the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. Winning all three earned the Summer Triple Crown and a GBP 1 million bonus, and also winning the fourth leg, the International S., completed the Grand Slam, raising the prize to GBP 5 million. The next year the rules changed: a horse that won the first leg needed only 2 wins from legs 2-4 for the Summer Triple Crown (the Grand Slam was scrapped after trainers said it put too much pressure on top horses). Under these rules, past horses like Nashwan and Opera House would have qualified, so it was expected to be achievable, but no horse did it in either year, and the series was dropped after 2004 to cut costs. Had it continued, four horses would have won the Summer Triple Crown: Duke of Marmalade in 2008, Sea the Stars in 2009, Ghaiyyath in 2020 and City of Troy in 2024 - but three wins in just over two months is a tall order.
65=* The Indomitable Late Bloomer\nThe American mare Azeri debuted very late, in November of her 3yo year. She shrugged off long odds of 18.3 to win, and by the end of her 4yo year had won 10 of 11, including 5 G1s like the Breeders' Cup Distaff - a dominant record that made her 2002 Eclipse champion older mare and Horse of the Year. She was only the third filly or mare to be Horse of the Year, and the first in 16 years since Lady's Secret in 1986.\nShe kept rolling in 2003. Aiming for the Breeders' Cup, she followed the same path as the year before, winning 4 races again including the Apple Blossom H., then ran in the G2 Lady's Secret H., which she had also won before. But perhaps due to her 128 lb (about 58 kg) weight she was 2nd, and afterwards she was found to have a tendon injury. She had to skip the Breeders' Cup, and her trainer even announced her retirement. But owner M. Paulson didn't give up: he consulted several vets, found a path back, and moved her to a new stable. Named Eclipse champion older mare for the second time while recovering, Azeri finally returned in April 2004 at 6 in her third Apple Blossom H. After a long layoff and as top weight with 128 lbs, she was only 3rd choice, but she ran like her old self to win the race for an unprecedented third time. She didn't always win after that, but added 2 more G1s before retiring, and was Eclipse champion older mare for the third straight year - a perfect finale.\n\n\n* A Historic Record After 200 Years\nSadler's Wells, heir to the great sire Northern Dancer, dominated European and indeed world racing. After first becoming champion sire in Britain and Ireland in 1990, he lost the title to Caerleon the next year, but then held it 13 years running from 1992 to 2004. That broke the record of 13 British sire titles set by Highflyer, the great horse of the 18th century, in 1798 - unbeaten for over 200 years.\nSadler's Wells stood at stud until 26, retiring in 2008 due to falling fertility. But many heirs, such as Galileo, were already succeeding, and his blood remains strong in racing.
