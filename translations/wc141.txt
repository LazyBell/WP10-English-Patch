3227=Debuting with the JRA in May at 3, winless in 3, he moved to Nagoya, won 2 straight, then returned to the JRA and passed allowances in autumn at 4. In open class at 5 he won the Brilliant S. (L), then the Urawa Kinen as 5th choice, moving early from the back, leading in the straight and drawing clear. He won the Nagoya GP, breaking the course record by over a second. At 6, 4th Kawasaki Kinen, 3rd Teio Sho, then he won the Hakusan Daishoten. Moving to Oi at 7, he stalled, then won Korea's Korea Cup (G3) and beat the JRA runners in the Tokyo Daishoten (G1) - a G1 horse.
3228=An Oi horse who, at 3 in 2023, went to the US and was 2nd in the Santa Anita Derby. Unbeaten in 4 at 2 incl. the local Haiseiko Kinen, 2nd in the Kumotori Sho at 3, he lost the Santa Anita Derby by a nose, then became the first local-racing horse to run in the Kentucky Derby, 12th. Back home, 2nd in the Kuroshio Hai, Totsuka Kinen and Derby Grand Prix - so close.
3229=A globetrotter who raced in the UAE, US, Saudi Arabia and Korea, winning the UAE Derby (G2) and Korea C. (G3). Unbeaten in 2 from October at 2, he announced an overseas campaign and won the UAE Derby. 13th in the Kentucky Derby, back home he was 2nd in the JBC Classic and Champions C. At 4, 5th in the Saudi Cup and Dubai World Cup, 2nd in the Teio Sho, he won the Korea C. Unplaced for a while, he won the Mercury C. at 5 and the Korea C. again.
3230=The 1948 US Triple Crown winner, who set many milestones incl. the first $1 million in earnings and 16 straight wins. 3rd in The Blood-Horse's top 100 of the 20th century, behind Man o' War and Secretariat. He won the Kentucky Derby by 3.5 lengths, the Preakness by 5.5 and the Belmont by 8. 19 wins from 20 that year; almost unanimous Horse of the Year and champion 3yo. Seen as the greatest since Man o' War ("Big Red"), he was nicknamed "Big Cy." Injury cost him the next year; back at 5, he won his return for 16 straight, then often missed. Racing on for the first $1 million, at 6 he won 3 straight incl. the Hollywood Gold C., passed $1 million and retired. At stud his first crop included Preakness winner Fabius, but he fell short of his sire Bull Lea, and his male line faded.
3231=A great sire who founded the Tom Fool line through Buckpasser and Tim Tam. His male descendants (Buckaroo, Egg Toss, and Japan's imports Meiwa Passer and Silver Charm) are minor, but Buckpasser's huge success as a broodmare sire spread his blood worldwide; horses out of Buckpasser mares include Maruzensky, Woodman, Miswaki and Seeking the Gold. A top racehorse too: in 1951 at 2 he won 5 of 7 with 2 seconds, US champion 2yo. Unwell, he missed the classics at 3, winning 6 from summer but no title. At 4 he shone again, winning all 10 starts from a handicap opener, incl. the New York Handicap Triple (Metropolitan H., Suburban H., Brooklyn H.) - only the 2nd ever - and was Horse of the Year over dominant 3yo dual classic winner Native Dancer.
3232=Hyperion's best son, who himself sired St Crespin and Vienna (sire of Vaguely Noble) - a key link keeping the Hampton line alive. His name, meaning the halo of a saint or icon, nods to his sire Hyperion (a god) and dam Angelola (angel). English-bred and raised, he ran in the English classics: 5th in the 2000 Guineas, 2nd in the Derby, 3rd in the St Leger, unplaced but talented, also 2nd in the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. He blossomed at 4, winning 4 straight to the King George and retired after it. Temperamental, before the King George he went wild and threw his rider, then started slowly. At stud his first crop included Arc winner St Crespin; he was champion sire in Britain and Ireland in 1960 and 1961. Vienna, from his 2nd crop, was modest on the track but sired Vaguely Noble, spreading Aureole's blood worldwide.
3233=A great sire who influenced pedigrees worldwide through Hail to Reason and Sir Gaylord. Hail to Reason sired Halo (sire of Sunday Silence and others) and Roberto (sire of Brian's Time and others), so in Japan it's hard to find a horse without them. Sir Gaylord sired Sir Ivor and Habitat, forming the Japanese line Habitat - Steel Heart - Nihon Pillow Winner. Though he founded an empire, he sired only 25 stakes winners and was not then seen as special; Hail to Reason and Sir Gaylord made him a great ancestor. He was a fine racehorse too: at 2 he won 3 incl. the Saratoga Special S., joint top of the US Free Handicap; unbeaten in 3 at 3, he fractured just before the Kentucky Derby and retired.
3234=One of Nasrullah's best sons, he sired Never Beat and Die Hard, whose imports made an era in Japan. American-bred, he raced in England and won the Derby and St Leger - Nasrullah's flagbearer in Europe. At stud in England, he was champion sire in 1962 with Derby winner Larkspur. Even his modest runners were exported for their blood; in Japan Never Beat, Die Hard and Cipriani did very well. Never Beat sired Mahchis, Lupinus, Riki Eikan and more, was champion sire 3 times, and sparked a "Never Say Die line boom." But in England his male line had nearly died out by the 1980s, and in Japan it faded with the rise of Northern Taste and others. Today it survives only in female lines.
3235=One of Nasrullah's leading sons. His best son, Tesco Boy, was a huge success in Japan, founding the great "Princely Gift line." A mid-level English sprinter, he was hoped to succeed Nasrullah at stud and steadily sired early-maturing sprinters. Tesco Boy, imported to Japan, sired Kitano Kachidoki, Tosho Boy, Sakura Yutaka O and many more, making his name. Then nearly all his sons, like Faberge, So Blessed and Sun Prince, were imported, so his male line died out elsewhere and developed uniquely in Japan; in the 1970s Tesco Boy was champion sire year after year. It faded with Northern Taste's arrival, and only Tesco Boy's line survives, carried on by Sakura Bakushin O, Air Jihad and others.
3236=A Nasrullah son who excelled both racing and at stud. Debuting in the US in 1954, he won 6 of 8 with 2 seconds, champion 2yo. At 3, 2nd to Swaps in the Kentucky Derby, he won the Preakness and Belmont and was Horse of the Year. Winning 22 of 30 incl. 2 Jockey Club Gold Cups, he retired to stud in autumn at 4 with US record earnings, later passed by Round Table. At stud he sired New York Filly Triple Crown winner Shuvee and many stars - 77 stakes winners. His best were mostly fillies, notably Gold Digger, dam of Mr. Prospector, and Bramalea, dam of Roberto. His male line struggled, but Good Manners, exported to Argentina, became champion sire there and carries it on.
3237=An American-bred 1955 Kentucky Derby winner, whose rivalry with crop-mate Nashua, winner of the Preakness and Belmont, was a sensation. Unremarkable at 2, at 3 he won 4 straight to the Kentucky Derby, beating favored Nashua. Back home in California he kept winning, and a rematch with Nashua was demanded; in their summer match race Nashua won by 6.5 lengths and took Horse of the Year. At 4 he won 8 of 10 and was Horse of the Year over Nashua, then retired to stud that autumn. In The Blood-Horse's top 100 of the 20th century he ranked 20th, above Nashua's 24th. At stud he sired Kentucky Derby winner Chateaugay and Hall of Famer Affectionately; Chateaugay, imported to Japan, sired Asahi Hai 3yo S. winner Hokuto Flag and is broodmare sire of Tamamo Cross and Mery Nice.
3238=A key sire keeping the Herod line alive today, spreading this minor line worldwide through Luthier and Lorenzaccio. French-bred and raised, he excelled at a mile, winning the Poule d'Essai des Poulains and Prix Jacques le Marois. Racing until 5, he retired to stud in France. His first crop included Oaks and Prix Vermeille winner Monade, and from his 7th came Luthier and Lorenzaccio, both successful sires. Luthier sired Sagace, No Lute and others, champion sire in France 4 times; another son, Dandy Lute, went to Japan and sired Bizen Nishiki. Lorenzaccio sired Ahonoora, leading on to Indian Ridge and Dr Devious. That line still carries Klairon's blood today.
3239=A hugely successful Nasrullah son who founded the Bold Ruler line, dominant in the US. His best include US Triple Crown winner Secretariat, Bold Lad, Bold Bidder, Lamb Chop and Gamely; he was US champion sire 8 times between 1963 and 1973. From his son Boldnesian's line came Seattle Slew and A.P. Indy, great sires who built the Bold Ruler empire. A top racehorse too, he won 23 of 32 incl. the Preakness and Trenton H. In 1957 at 3 he was US Horse of the Year over strong crop-mates Gallant Man and Round Table. 19th in The Blood-Horse's top 100 of the 20th century.
3240=One of the US "golden crop" of 1957 classic horses, battling Bold Ruler and Gallant Man. 3rd in the Kentucky Derby, he then won 11 straight incl. the Hollywood Gold C., forming the "big three" with Preakness winner Bold Ruler and Belmont winner Gallant Man. Beaten by them head to head, at 4 he won 14 of 20 and was Horse of the Year. Racing until 5, he retired with world-record earnings of $1.74 million. Versatile on turf and dirt, he set 16 track records. At stud he was US champion sire in 1972; sons Bold Lick and Targowice, imported to Japan, sired Kyoei Promise and Let's Go Tarquin. His male line faded, but as broodmare sire of Caerleon and in Seattle Slew's pedigree, his blood lives on.
3241=One of the US "golden crop" of 1957 classic horses with Bold Ruler and Round Table. Blossoming at 3, he had the Kentucky Derby won in a big-three showdown, but his jockey misjudged the finish line and he lost by a nose. He made amends in the Belmont, winning by 8 lengths in record time. Favorite in an autumn rematch of just the big three, he was 2nd, 2 1/4 lengths behind Bold Ruler, who took Horse of the Year. He raced until autumn at 4. At stud he trailed Bold Ruler and Round Table but sired 52 stakes winners; in Japan in the 1970s his foals included 2-time Sprinters S. winner Meiwa Kimiko and Asahi Hai 3yo S. winner Gallant Dancer.
