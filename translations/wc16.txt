81=* Enable Leaves the Turf\nThe year before, Enable narrowly missed a third straight Arc. All eyes were on her future, but her camp kept her racing at 6, aiming for a third Arc win even though her streak had ended.\nAfter more than six months off, she returned in the Eclipse S. in July 2020. Because of the coronavirus pandemic, Britain's flat program was partly irregular, and for the first time the Eclipse was closed to 3yos, limited to 4yos and up. Hopes for her comeback made her favorite, but she was beaten into 2nd by Ghaiyyath, who had won the Coronation C. in record time.\nNext she ran in the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth S. Perhaps to avoid facing a two-time winner of the race, rivals pulled out one after another, leaving just 3 runners, the smallest field ever. She easily ran down front-runner Sovereign in the straight and won by five and a half lengths, the first horse ever to win the King George three times. In autumn, as planned, she tried a fourth Arc but sank to 6th, her first and only finish out of the top three. It was her last race. Her dream of a third Arc went unfulfilled, but her running surely left its mark on racing fans around the world.\n\n* A Solo on the Turf\nStradivarius was a British colt named after the world's finest violins. After winning the 16-furlong (3200m) G1 Goodwood C. at 3, he made long distances his domain. At 4 he won all 5 of his long-distance graded races, including the 20-furlong (4000m) Gold C., and was named 2018 Cartier champion stayer. At 5 he won the Gold C., the Goodwood C. and the Doncaster C., the first stayers' triple crown in 24 years, and was champion stayer again.\nIn 2020, at 6, he won his third straight Gold C. and a record fourth straight Goodwood C., proving he was still the best and taking champion stayer for the third straight year. He kept running long races until 8, winning 18 graded races in a long career.\n\n* Love's British Fillies' Double\nThe year before, Love won the G1 Moyglare Stud S. and became a leading contender for the British fillies' classics. After over six months off, she went straight into the 1000 Guineas for her first 3yo race. She was 2nd choice behind unbeaten 2yo queen Quadrilateral, but won by four and a quarter lengths. As favorite in the Oaks she lived up to it, winning by 9 lengths in a race-record 2:34.06 to complete the British fillies' double. She then beat older horses easily in the Yorkshire Oaks, ending the year unbeaten in 3, and was named Cartier champion 3yo filly.
82=* Snowfall Wins the English and Irish Oaks\nSnowfall was a filly by Japanese Triple Crown winner Deep Impact, born in Japan and trained in Ireland, out of a Galileo mare - a blueblood. But at 2 she won just 1 of 7. In her last 2yo race, the G1 Fillies' Mile, there was a bizarre mix-up: Snowfall and stablemate Mother Earth ran in each other's colors without anyone noticing. It came to light after the race, and the correct result was announced: Mother Earth 3rd, Snowfall 8th.\nAfter drawing attention for that odd incident, she seemed like a different horse at 3 in 2021. In her first race, the G3 Musidora S., she was 4th choice at 15 but won by three and three-quarter lengths. Her stock rose to 3rd choice for the Oaks. On good-to-soft ground she waited at the back of midfield, surged when a gap opened, took the lead and kept pulling away, winning by 16 lengths - the biggest margin in the Oaks' 200-plus-year history. Jockey L. Dettori, a 20-time classic winner, said, "I've won many Classics but not one as easy as that." In the Irish Oaks she was overwhelming favorite at 1.3 and won by eight and a half lengths. She then won the Yorkshire Oaks for 3 straight G1 wins.\nShe tried races like the Arc afterwards but didn't win again. Still, her dominant wins that year earned her Cartier champion 3yo filly. But early the next year came sad news: while on a break she was found to have a fractured pelvis, and despite treatment her condition worsened until she had to be put down. The snow that fell so heavily in summer melted away in the cold winter sky, mourned by all.\n\n* Arlington Park Closes\nThe Arlington Million S., founded in 1981 as the world's first race worth $1 million, was held at Arlington Park, opened in 1972 in Illinois. It suffered a fire in 1985 and changed owners repeatedly from the 1990s, and in 2021 the site was sold and the track closed. It was also known for being among the first to introduce technologies now taken for granted, like the odds board and photo-finish camera - a track that contributed greatly to racing.
83=* 6 Starts, 71 Lengths: A Shock\nFlightline, by top American sire Tapit, stood out even as a foal and sold for $1 million at the 2019 Saratoga yearling sale, the top price on day one. No real rival ever emerged in his career - his greatest enemy was injury. Before his debut he was startled at the farm and slammed his hindquarters into a stall door, badly injuring himself. Then the coronavirus pandemic delayed his development, and he didn't debut until April at 3. Though he was never asked, he won his 6-furlong (1200m) debut by thirteen and a quarter lengths, drawing great attention. Then he injured a hoof and had to rest through summer. But as if layoffs meant nothing to him, he won his comeback and then the G1 Malibu S. by over 10 lengths each, ending his 3yo year unbeaten in 3.\nIn 2022, at 4, all eyes were on him, but another training injury held him back. He finally ran in June's Metropolitan H. and, despite a slow start, won easily by 6 lengths. Next came the 10-furlong (2000m) Pacific Classic S. Some wondered whether the longer trip would trouble a horse whose weapon seemed to be speed, but such logic meant nothing to him. He took the lead down the backstretch and simply kept pulling away. Even easing up over the last 100m, he beat Dubai World Cup winner Country Grammer by an astonishing 19-plus lengths. Trainer J. Sadler said, "This is an exceptional horse. He has speed and keeps going."\nThen, at last, the Breeders' Cup Classic. In a star field where all 8 runners were G1 winners, he was a clear favorite at 1.44. So sure was everyone of his win that his show odds (top 2) of 1.46 were actually higher than his win odds - a rare oddity. He sat right on Life Is Good, who set a blistering pace, passed him easily at the final turn and ran away from the field, winning by a race-record eight and a quarter lengths.\nPlagued by leg trouble all along, Flightline retired unbeaten, having won his 6 races by a combined 71 lengths. After his stunning Pacific Classic his rating was 139 lbs, raised to 140 the next January - level with Frankel at the top, and far above Cigar's 135, by far the highest ever for a North American-trained horse. Despite just 3 races that year he was named Eclipse Horse of the Year almost unanimously. A great horse who carved his name into American racing history, he will be talked about for years to come.
84=* Golden Sixty Becomes the All-Time Money Earner\nThe big news this year was Hong Kong's Golden Sixty setting a world earnings record. In recent years the record had been about $17.1 million, set by America's Arrogate at the end of 2017, until Australia's Winx broke it in April 2019 with about A$26.45 million (about $18.6 million then). Then in April of this year, Golden Sixty reached about HK$167.17 million (about $21.27 million then) to become the new world leader.\nGolden Sixty raced only in Hong Kong, winning 10 international G1s, including three Hong Kong Miles and three Champions Miles. He kept running through about five tough years, from late in his 3yo season to late at 8, to earn that much. He was Hong Kong Horse of the Year three years running from 5 to 7 - a great horse of modern Hong Kong racing.\nBut the next year his record was broken by fellow Hong Kong horse Romantic Warrior. Romantic Warrior also won G1s abroad, like Australia's Cox Plate and Japan's Yasuda Kinen, and at the end of 2024 won his third straight Hong Kong Cup to pass Golden Sixty in career earnings.\nAlso in the news: jockey Z. Purton, who has also ridden Romantic Warrior, won 173 races in a season, a new Hong Kong record.\nBy the way, Japanese racing has high prize money overall, and several Japanese horses, like Equinox and Almond Eye, rank near the top of the world earnings list.\n\n* Ace Impact Wins the Arc Unbeaten\nIn Europe, Ace Impact won the Arc unbeaten from his debut, and attention turned to the birth of a new young star. Ace Impact debuted in January this year and won the French Derby just four and a half months later. Unbeaten in 5, he went into the Arc as a well-deserved favorite and won. His seemingly limitless power shocked European racing, but this was his last race: after a career of just 9 months he went to stud, stepping onto a new stage to pass on the great blood of his grandsire Frankel and sire Cracksman.\nA star of Ace Impact's crop was Ireland's Auguste Rodin. From Deep Impact's final crop, he won 4 G1s: the Derby, the Irish Derby, the Irish Champion S. and the Breeders' Cup Turf. He never met Ace Impact, but the two competed as candidates for Cartier Horse of the Year.\nIn the end Ace Impact won both Horse of the Year and champion 3yo colt, the No. 1 horse of the year.
85=* City of Troy, Europe's Horse of the Year\nCity of Troy is by US Triple Crown winner Justify, out of a mare by European great Galileo. Living up to his blood, he won 3 straight at 2, capped by the G1 Dewhurst S. In 2024 his first 3yo race was the 2000 Guineas, where he was overwhelming favorite at 1.6. He raced up front but was easily passed and finished a well-beaten 9th. Still, trainer A. O'Brien announced he would go straight to the Derby. In the big race, favorite again, he slipped out from the back of midfield and won clearly by two and three-quarter lengths - the first American-bred Derby winner in 21 years. Having overcome that heavy defeat, he beat older horses clearly in the Eclipse S., then won the International S. in course-record time. He was 8th in his last race, the Breeders' Cup Classic, but his 3 G1 wins earned him Cartier Horse of the Year.\nCity of Troy went to stud after the Breeders' Cup Classic. Even O'Brien, who has trained so many greats, said, "He's definitely the best we've ever trained, no doubt about it." His future at stud is eagerly awaited.\nHis stablemate Auguste Rodin also grew after a heavy defeat. He debuted in 2022 and soon won a G1, but in his first 3yo race, the 2000 Guineas, he sank to 12th as favorite. He then won the Derby and the Irish Derby, and even the Breeders' Cup Turf. In 2024, at 4, he wasn't as dominant, but won the G1 Prince of Wales's S. For his last race his camp chose the Japan Cup. He finished 8th, but at the retirement ceremony afterwards Japanese fans gave a huge ovation to Auguste Rodin, from Deep Impact's last crop.\n\n* A New World Earnings Record\nBy the previous year Hong Kong's Romantic Warrior had won 6 Hong Kong G1s and even Australia's G1 Cox Plate. In 2024 he traveled to Japan for the Yasuda Kinen and won, beating Namur and others. He ended the 2023/2024 season (Hong Kong's season runs from September to early July) with 5 straight G1 wins and was named Hong Kong Horse of the Year. In December he ran in the Hong Kong Cup, which he had won the two previous years. Beating Japan's fillies' Triple Crown winner Liberty Island and Derby winner Tastiera, he became the first to win the Hong Kong Cup three times running. With this win his career earnings reached $22.8 million, passing fellow Hong Kong horse Golden Sixty to become the world's all-time leader.
