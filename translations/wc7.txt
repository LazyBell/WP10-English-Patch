41=* Only the Second Filly to Win the Kentucky Derby\nGenuine Risk was a filly born in Kentucky. She joined the stable of L. Jolley, who had trained Kentucky Derby winner Foolish Pleasure. She was very stubborn and hard to train, but her trainer, thrown off again and again, patiently built her trust. It paid off: debuting in 1979, she won 4 straight unbeaten, beating even Smart Angle, that year's champion 2yo filly, in her last race, the Demoiselle S.\nIn 1980, at 3, her winning run continued to 6. Jockey J. Vasquez, convinced she was as good as any colt, proposed taking on the colts' classics, but Jolley was reluctant. In 1975 he had watched his colt Foolish Pleasure run a match race against Ruffian, the best filly of the time, in which Ruffian broke down and had to be put down. Vasquez still believed in her and persuaded Jolley, and Genuine Risk stepped onto the colts' battlefield.\nFirst she ran in the Wood Memorial S., a prep full of colts. Sitting 3rd and waiting for a gap, she was blocked by a horse who veered badly and finished 3rd. After this Jolley suggested switching to the Kentucky Oaks, but the owner, impressed that she still held 3rd despite the interference, chose the Kentucky Derby. In fact, a filly even placing in the Wood Memorial was only the 4th time ever, and the first in 33 years.\nThen came the Kentucky Derby. Genuine Risk was the first filly to run in 21 years. Against 12 colts and geldings, including Wood Memorial winner Plugged Nickle, she was a respectable 6th choice of 13. She broke well and tracked the leaders. The pace was slow, and as the front-runners struggled, she made her move around the third turn, passed horse after horse to take the lead, and held on. She was the first filly to win the Kentucky Derby in 65 years.\nShe went on to the Preakness S. and the Belmont S., but was 2nd in both. Still, Genuine Risk remains the only filly ever to place in all three US Triple Crown races.\n\n* Spectacular Bid's World Record\nThe year before, Spectacular Bid had just missed the US Triple Crown and lost Horse of the Year to Affirmed. But this year he was in peak form.\nHe won his first two races, then in the Charles H. Strub S. ran 10 furlongs (2000m) on dirt in 1:57.80, breaking the world record for the first time in 30 years. The record still stands. He kept winning and retired unbeaten in 9 starts that year. He also broke the career earnings world record set by Affirmed the year before and was the undisputed Horse of the Year.
42=* That Finishing Kick: Pure Delight\nPleasant Colony was born in Virginia. Debuting in 1980, he finished 6th, 16 lengths behind winner Summing. A poor start, but he ended his 2yo year with a decent 2 wins from 5.\nIn 1981, on his 3yo debut in the Fountain of Youth S., he was beaten a nose into 2nd. In the Florida Derby he was a distant 5th, 13 lengths back. After this the owner decided to move him to Italian-born trainer John P. Campo. From then on he showed his true worth.\nHis first race for the new stable was the Wood Memorial S. In a strong field he won by 3 lengths. Unnoticed until then, Pleasant Colony suddenly emerged as a leading classic contender. In the Kentucky Derby the favorite was Proud Appeal, 8 wins from 9, with Pleasant Colony 2nd choice. Proud Appeal and others battled for the lead at a blistering pace. When it slowed down the backstretch, Pleasant Colony, waiting at the back, swept past the tiring leaders in delightful fashion and won by three-quarters of a length. In the Preakness S. he was favorite at 2.6 and lived up to it, winning by a length for his second classic.\nThen the last classic, the Belmont S. With hopes of a Triple Crown his support grew, and he was favorite at 2.0. He waited at the very back, made up ground fast down the backstretch and was 3rd into the straight, but couldn't reach the leader and finished 3rd. The front-runner who held on was 3rd choice Summing. Afterwards Campo blamed the waiting tactics, calling it "the worst ride in history" to the media. Pleasant Colony had to settle for two legs of the US Triple Crown.\nHe ran three more times and retired. At stud, despite few matings, he sired Irish Derby winner St. Jovite and Pleasantly Perfect, winner of the Breeders' Cup Classic and Dubai World Cup, becoming the leading sire of the direct line of Ribot, one of the great horses of the 20th century.\n\n* South Africa Adopts Graded Races\nIn modern racing, races have been ranked since 1970, when Britain, Ireland and France introduced the "Group system," ranking some races by level as Group 1, Group 2 and Group 3. Italy and Germany followed, and race programs took shape.\nThe "graded" system, familiar to Japanese fans, began in the US and Canada in 1973. It works like the Group system, but the ranks are called Grade I, Grade II and Grade III. Most countries followed Europe with Group systems, but in 1981 South Africa adopted grades. The reason is unclear, but South Africa had long imported mostly British-bred thoroughbreds, and by then imports were mainly North American, which may have played a part. Today the only Part I countries (the top level set by the International Cataloguing Standards Committee) using graded races are the US, Canada, South Africa and Japan.
43=* An Unbeaten Derby Win in Just 4 Starts\nGolden Fleece was by British Triple Crown winner Nijinsky. Born in America, he was owned by famous British owner Robert Sangster and, like Nijinsky, trained in Ireland. On his debut in September at 2 he met Assert, another Sangster horse, and won by 6 lengths. But he didn't race again that year and went on a long break.\nHe finally returned in April 1982 in the Ballymoss S. (now the Tattersalls Gold C.). Against a field including future Japan Cup winner Stanerra, he won easily by 3 lengths. Then in the Nijinsky S. (later the Derrinstown Derby Trial S.), named after his sire, he met Assert again and won by two and a half lengths.\nHe went into the Derby unbeaten. Far back in the straight, he swept past the field with a tremendous kick and won by 3 lengths, a Derby winner unbeaten in 4 starts. Jockey P. Eddery called him "the best horse I've ever ridden."\nBut Golden Fleece was injured in training and had to retire, leaving the turf after just 4 races despite his enormous talent. His strength is clear from the fact that Assert, the rival he beat twice, became the first horse to win both the French and Irish Derbies.\n\n* A 14-Length Belmont Romp\nConquistador Cielo was an American colt whose name means "conqueror of the sky" in Spanish. True to it, he ran as if flying, leaving the earthbound field behind.\nInjured the year before, he was 4th on his 3yo comeback, but then bounced back with a 4-length win. A recurring fracture ruled out the Kentucky Derby, so he went for the Metropolitan H., America's championship mile. He won an allowance race on the same course by 11 lengths and was ready. The Metropolitan included Star Gallant, who had beaten him in his 3yo debut, but Conquistador Cielo was favorite. He went straight to the lead, set his own pace, and by the straight was running alone, winning by over 7 lengths in course-record time. It was the first win by a 3yo in this race against older horses in 13 years.\nAfter this, his camp announced he would run in the Belmont S., the last classic, stunning racing people and fans. The Belmont was just 6 days after the Metropolitan, and the distance jumped from 8 to 12 furlongs (1600m to 2400m) - an unheard-of schedule. On a day of heavy rain, Linkage, 8 wins from 11, was favorite, with Conquistador Cielo, doubted for the quick turnaround and the distance, 2nd choice. Right after the start he again went to the front. He dropped to 2nd once, but as the sloppy track sapped the others' stamina, he never weakened and kept extending his lead, winning by a record 14 lengths. Winning the impossible on an impossible schedule, he was named Horse of the Year.
