141=* Equinox: An Autumn Charge Erasing Spring's Frustration\nEquinox was from the first crop of 7-time G1 winner Kitasan Black. A handsome horse with a gleaming black coat, he won his second start, the Tokyo Sports Hai 2yo S., with an astonishing finish and became a leading classic contender.\nAt 3 he went straight to the Satsuki Sho without a prep, 3rd choice behind previous champion 2yo colt Do Deuce and unbeaten Kyodo News Hai winner Danon Beluga. He moved from midfield to a good spot and pulled clear in the straight, but was caught at the end by 5th-choice stablemate Geoglyph. The Japanese Derby looked like a four-way battle with Geoglyph, Do Deuce and Danon Beluga. He raced at the back and produced the fastest finish, but fell a neck short of Do Deuce under Yutaka Take - 2nd again.\nNext, following Efforia's path from the year before, he went to the Tenno Sho (Autumn). Panthalassa, a dramatic Dubai Turf winner in spring, set off in his trademark huge lead, with Equinox tracking in midfield. As Panthalassa turned into the straight over 10 lengths clear, the stands roared, "He's going to hold on!" But Equinox unleashed an astonishing kick, closed fast and ran him down. The win also gave his sire Kitasan Black a G1 winner from his first crop. He then won the Arima Kinen in just his 6th career start, the fewest ever, completing a father-son double. Like Efforia the year before, he turned the frustration of narrow classic defeats into triumph among the older horses.\n\n\n* Stars on Earth's Double\nStars on Earth was from the second crop of double classic winner Duramente. She broke her maiden in her second start, then was favorite three times running but kept just missing.\nGiven that record she was only 7th choice for the Oka Sho. But she surged from midfield and drew level with Water Navillera right at the line. After a photo, Stars on Earth was crowned queen of the cherry blossoms. In the Oaks she broke well, settled in midfield and swung widest of all in the straight, powering down the middle to win clearly by a length and a quarter - the fillies' double.\nBut after the race a chip fracture in her right leg was found. After a break she made it to the Shuka Sho, but just missed in 3rd, falling short of the Triple Crown.
142=* Liberty Island Wins the Fillies' Triple Crown\nLiberty Island (by Duramente) showed outstanding speed from her very first race. Her final 600m of 31.4 seconds on debut tied the fastest in JRA history, and was naturally the fastest ever for a debut. She narrowly lost the Artemis S. after finding no room in the straight, but won the Hanshin JF clearly in her third start and was unanimously champion 2yo filly.\nShe went straight to the Oka Sho for her first 3yo race, overwhelming 1.6 favorite over the horses who came through the trials. She was a bit slow away, but unleashed a tremendous finish widest of all in the straight to win. The new jockey camera, introduced that spring, captured her astonishing run and jockey Yuga Kawada calling her "young lady" after the race, which became a big talking point among fans. Next was the Oaks. Racing much further forward than before, she left the field behind in the straight for the double, winning by 6 lengths - beyond Gentildonna's 5 (2012), the biggest Oaks margin since graded races began (1984).\nShe went straight to the last leg, the Shuka Sho. Her dominant wins made her an incredible 1.1 favorite. She broke well, sat in midfield, broke clear early at the final turn and shut out the late chargers - 4 straight G1 wins and the 7th fillies' Triple Crown ever.\n\n* Equinox Retires After 6 Straight G1s\nThe year before, Equinox dominated the older horses in autumn at 3. This year he tried the Dubai Sheema Classic, his first race abroad. All eyes were on how he'd do against the world's best - and he broke well and, for the first time, led. It was less front-running than simply pulling away on superior ability, and he never gave up the lead, winning easily. Lemaire never even used the whip, yet he broke the record by a second. This made him world No. 1 at 129 lbs in the international ratings (World's Best Racehorse Rankings) - the world's best horse in name and fact.\nBack home he ran in the Takarazuka Kinen, which his sire never won, holding off late-improving Through Seven Seas by a neck to avenge his sire. Then he went for a second Tenno Sho (Autumn). Like Panthalassa the year before, Jack d'Or set a fast pace in front. But where Equinox had closed from midfield the year before, this time he raced near the front. As Jack d'Or and others tired, he kept going and pulled away to win easily, showing his versatility and again setting a record time - the overwhelming power of the world's best horse.\nHe went straight to the Japan Cup, where the entry of this year's fillies' Triple Crown winner Liberty Island made it a summit clash. With Panthalassa again in a huge lead, he raced from a good spot, shook off Liberty Island 200m out to lead with ease and won by 4 lengths. Liberty Island's jockey Kawada called Equinox "the answer of modern Japanese racing, the greatest horse in Japanese racing history." The win took his earnings past 2.2 billion yen, passing Almond Eye as the all-time leader.\nAfterwards his camp announced his retirement. Having moved past his classic disappointments, he left the turf with an astonishing 6 straight G1 wins.\n\n* Big Feats on Dirt\nPanthalassa, who thrilled racing with his runaway style the year before, tried a dirt G1 for the first time in the Saudi Cup. Leading at a fast pace, he couldn't pull clear as usual, but showed tremendous grit in the straight to win - the first Japanese horse to win the Saudi Cup, and, with his Dubai Turf win, the first Japan-trained horse to win G1s abroad on both turf and dirt. He was 10th in the Dubai WC, won by Japan's Ushba Tesoro, who until autumn at 5 had won just 3 races on turf, but rose to G1 level after switching to dirt and showed his power abroad.\nLemon Pop, ridden by Ryusei Sakai, won both the February S. and the Champions C. - only the 4th horse ever to do so.\nWith the 3yo dirt triple crown to start the next year, a star also emerged among the 3yos: Mick Fire, who won the Haneda Hai, Tokyo Derby and Japan Dirt Derby for the Southern Kanto triple crown. He was the first to win it since these three races became the triple crown in 2002, and as it was reorganized into the dirt triple crown from 2024, he was both the first and only winner.\nMany dirt heroes were born this year, raising great hopes for dirt racing's future.
143=* Do Deuce's Shock, Regaleira's Brilliant Run\nIn 2024, after the great Equinox left the turf, 13 straight JRA G1s from the February S. to the Sprinters S. in September were won by different jockeys, and 11 of the winners were first-time G1 winners - a wide-open year. But the star was still Do Deuce, Equinox's great rival and the previous Arima Kinen winner. Unlucky with pace and going in his two spring races, he won his first autumn race, the Tenno Sho (Autumn), clearly by over a length from last year's Derby winner Tastiera. In the Japan Cup he moved up wide and won a fierce battle near the line by a neck, one step from the autumn older horses' triple crown, only the third ever and first in 20 years since Zenno Rob Roy in 2004. With the Arima Kinen his last race, hopes were sky-high, but two days before it he was scratched with lameness in his right foreleg, sending shockwaves through racing.\nThe Arima Kinen, a little flat without him, was turned into a historic occasion by Regaleira, winless since the previous year's Hopeful S. Unlike before she raced from a good spot, and in the straight fought a fierce duel with Shahryar. They hit the line together, and the photo went to Regaleira - the first 3yo filly to win the Arima Kinen in 64 years, since Star Roch in 1960, and only the second ever.\n\n* Year One of the 3yo Dirt Triple Crown: Forever Young Shines\nThis year the 3yo dirt triple crown began. The first leg, the Haneda Hai, went to Amante Bianco, whose pure white coat stood out on the sand; the second, the Tokyo Derby, to Ramjet, who came up through the Unicorn S. trial; and the third, the Japan Dirt Classic, to Forever Young - three different winners in its first year.\nForever Young was especially brilliant abroad, winning the Saudi Derby and UAE Derby and running 3rd in both the Kentucky Derby and the Breeders' Cup Classic, America's greatest races. At year's end he beat Japan's top older horses in the Tokyo Daishoten.
144=* The Long-Awaited Breeders' Cup Classic Win\nAfter running well abroad and winning races like the Tokyo Daishoten at home the year before, Forever Young went even further this year. He opened abroad in the Saudi Cup, where Hong Kong's Romantic Warrior, the world's top earner, also ran. Romantic Warrior raced skillfully for a horse trying dirt for the first time and led at the final turn, but Forever Young hung on. Seeing no room inside, jockey Sakai deftly switched him out, and he closed with every powerful stride, catching him by a neck just before the line - a first G1 abroad for both horse and rider.\nHe then went to Dubai for the Dubai World Cup. Overwhelming favorite and expected to win, he couldn't quicken in the straight and was 3rd. Right after, trainer Yahagi said they had been "given the away-team treatment," revealing trouble in a pre-race check that left the horse keyed up.\nBut his camp didn't give up, aiming for their goal from the start: the Breeders' Cup Classic, where he'd been 3rd the year before. In his prep, the Nippon TV Hai, he won easily by two and a half lengths, staying unbeaten in Japan, and set off for America. The field included last year's winner Sierra Leone and runner-up Fierceness, both of whom had beaten him. He sat near the front and held his spot, led at the final turn and tried to pull away, but Sierra Leone charged wide. Answering his rider's urging, he held on by half a length at the line. It was the first Japanese win in the race in 29 years since Taiki Blizzard first tried it in 1996 - the moment Japanese racing's long dream came true. The win took his earnings past 2.9 billion yen, passing Ushba Tesoro as the all-time leader.\n\n* A Japanese Whirlwind in Dubai\nDubai World Cup night, a racing festival with 9 G1 and G2 races in one day, saw many Japanese horses shine this year. It began with Win Carnelian's fine 2nd in race 4, the Al Quoz Sprint on turf; then Admire Daytona won race 5, the UAE Derby. In race 7, the Dubai Turf, Soul Rush beat favorite Romantic Warrior, and in the Dubai Sheema Classic Danon Decile won with Durezza 3rd. In the final race, the Dubai World Cup, Forever Young just missed in 3rd, but it was a historic day that showed the world the strength of Japanese horses.\nIn Europe too, a horse made history. Satono Reve won his first G1, the Takamatsunomiya Kinen, at 6 this year, then went to Britain for the Queen Elizabeth II Jubilee S., part of the royal "Royal Ascot" meeting. Breaking from the widest gate, 16, he closed wide on Lazzat, who had broken clear, but was half a length short in 2nd. Still, it was the first top-two finish by a Japanese horse at Royal Ascot in 25 years, since Agnes World was 2nd in the King's Stand S. (now the King Charles III S., then a G2) in 2000 - and the first ever in a G1.
