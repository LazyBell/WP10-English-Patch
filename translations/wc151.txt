3370=An Australian sprint gelding with 2 G1s, whose A$16.44 million in earnings were then 2nd only to great mare Winx. At 2 in 2014/15 he won his debut from 2 starts; at 3 he won a listed race and a handicap late in the season. Blossoming at 4 in 2016/17, he won the Regimax S. (G3), was 2nd in The Galaxy (G1), and won the Doomben 10,000 - a G1. At 5 he won 6 straight incl. the new The Everest and the Darley Classic. 2nd in the Black Caviar Lightning and T J Smith S. (both G1). At 6 he won The Everest again but only a G3 besides; at 7, just a G3. 3rd in his farewell T J Smith S., he retired.
3371=Winner of 3 mile G1s in 2015: the 2000 Guineas, Irish 2000 Guineas and St. James's Palace S. Aidan O'Brien called him "the best miler I've had." Debuting in June at 2, he won his 2nd start and 4 straight to the National S. (G1); first past the post in the Prix Jean-Luc Lagardere, he was demoted to 3rd for interference, but with 4 wins from 6 was Cartier champion 2yo colt. At 3 he won the 2000 Guineas easily, the Irish 2000 Guineas in a duel with Endless Drama, and the St. James's Palace S. over Poulains winner Make Believe - top of Europe's 3yo milers. Kept out by ground conditions, he was 6th in the QEII S. in autumn, then last in the BC Classic on his first dirt start at a new trip, and retired. At stud from 2016 he has sired G1 winners Loving Dream and Highland Chief; in Japan, Shock Action won the Niigata 2yo S.
3372=Imported to Japan from the US, at stud from 2018. A son of Storm Bird-line Gio Ponti, he was prized in Japan as free of Sunday Silence blood. His first crop produced Sapporo 2yo S. and Satsuki Sho winner Geoglyph, making him top first-season sire - a sire to watch. He won 3 US sprint G1s in 2016-2017. Debuting in October at 2, he won a maiden in his 2nd start, then 3 straight at 3 incl. the King's Bishop S. (G1), and the BC Sprint - Eclipse champion sprinter. At 4 he unseated his rider in the Bing Crosby S., then won the Forego S. for a 3rd G1. Bidding to repeat in the BC Sprint, he found nothing in the straight, 6th, and retired to stud in Japan.
3373=An Australian sprint star who won 9 G1s incl. 3 straight T J Smith S. Debuting at 3 in 2017/18, he won 7 of 10, mostly handicaps and listed races. At 4 he won the Mitty's McEwen S. for his first stakes, then The Galaxy S. for his first G1. At 5 he won 3 G1s: A J Moir S., Sprint Classic and T J Smith S. At 6, the Black Caviar Lightning and T J Smith S.; at 7, the Sprint Classic, T J Smith S. and England's King's Stand S. - 9 G1s. 3 T J Smith S. in a row; in the Sprint Classic 2 wins and a 2nd in 3 years. At 8 he won only a G2, and retired after a 6th in a G3 at 9.
3374=A British stayer at the top from 2017 to 2022, named after the famous string instruments made by the Stradivari father and sons in Italy. Winning a maiden from 3 at 2, he won the Goodwood C. in summer at 3 for his first G1. At 4 he won all 5 starts incl. the Ascot Gold C. and Goodwood C. At 5 he won 5 more, a 2nd Gold Cup and 3rd Goodwood C., before losing a G2 by a nose - 10 straight ended. At 6 he won a 3rd Gold Cup and 4th Goodwood C., 7th in the Arc - Cartier champion stayer 3 years running. At 7, 4th bidding for a 4th Gold Cup and 2nd in the Prix du Cadran - no G1. At 8, 3rd in the Gold Cup and 2nd in the Goodwood C., then he retired.
3375=A British horse who won G1s in 3 countries in 2018: the UAE, Germany and Australia. Debuting late in April at 3, he was 5th in the Derby and King George, and won the Hampton Court S. (G3). Based in Dubai early at 4, he won the Dubai Turf for his first G1, then Germany's Grosser Dallmayr-Preis, and in Australia the Ladbrokes S. - 3 G1s. In the Cox Plate, Australia's biggest race, he was 2nd to queen Winx. After that hard year he rested until autumn at 5, winning a G2 in 2 runs. At 6 he won 2 G2s in Dubai and was 3rd in Saudi Arabia's new Saudi Cup. In his final year, at 7, he won another G2 in England and retired. Imported to Japan, he has stood at stud since 2022.
3376=The 2020 French Derby winner, who ran in the world's richest race, the Saudi Cup, twice, winning it in 2021, the year before it became an international G1. Debuting in October at 2, he won a maiden in his 3rd start. At 3 he won the listed Newmarket S., then the French Derby, surging from a handy spot. At 4 he won the 2nd Saudi Cup and its huge purse, then the Dubai Sheema Classic; 2nd to Adayar in the King George, he won the International S. by 6 lengths - 3 G1s. At 5, last in the Saudi Cup, now an international G1; then 2nd Eclipse S., 3rd King George, 2nd International S., and after a 4th in the BC Turf he retired to stud in France.
3377=The 2021 Arc winner. As 13th choice of 14, he became only the 3rd German-trained winner. Debuting late in May at 3, he won his 2nd start and was 2nd in the German Derby in his 3rd. In autumn he won the Grosser Preis von Berlin, his first G1. At 4, 2nd defending the Berlin race, he won the Grosser Preis von Baden. The Arc field was stacked - Frankel's Hurricane Lane and Adayar, Shamardal's Tarnawa - and he was ignored, but he surged from midfield to beat all the fancies. At 5, no G1 win, but 2nd in the King George and Grosser Preis von Baden; in the Arc, from a wide draw in 18 of 20, he was 3rd, 3/4 length behind Alpinista. He retired to stud in Germany.
3378=A gelding who ruled Hong Kong racing in the 2020s, winning 4 straight Hong Kong Cups (G1) and 3 straight QEII Cups (G1). At the end of 2024 he passed Hong Kong's Golden Sixty as the world's top earner. Debuting in 2021/22, he won 2 legs of the local Triple Crown, the Classic Mile and Hong Kong Derby, then won the QEII Cup 3 seasons running and the Hong Kong Cup 3 straight from 2022/23. In 2023/24 he became the first Hong Kong-trained Cox Plate winner, then went to Japan for the Yasuda Kinen as favorite over top milers Soul Rush, Serifos and Namur, and won - the first foreign-trained winner in 18 years. He was Hong Kong Horse of the Year that season over 3-time champion Golden Sixty. In 2024/25 his 3rd straight Hong Kong Cup took his earnings to a world-record $22.8 million. 2nd in the Saudi Cup and Dubai Turf, he fractured and rested long, but returned in 2025/26 to win a 4th Hong Kong Cup - still the champion.
3379=Winner of the 2023 Derby and Irish Derby, an Irish-bred son of Deep Impact and the best of his final crop of just 12 worldwide. Debuting in June at 2 and winning his 2nd start, he won the Champions Juvenile S. (G2) and Vertem Futurity T. (G1). At 3, 12th in the 2000 Guineas, he won the Derby with a brilliant late run - a historic first for a son of a Japanese horse - then the Irish Derby as favorite. 10th in the King George, he won the Irish Champion S. for a 4th G1, then the BC Turf in America as favorite, beating Japan's Shahryar. At 4 he won the Prince of Wales's S., 2nd in the Tattersalls Gold C. and Irish Champion S. His last race was the Japan Cup, 8th, and he left the turf with a retirement ceremony in Japan, his sire's homeland.
3380=A Nagoya star who moved to the JRA at 5 (old age system) and won 3 graded races. At Nagoya he won the Shinshun Grand Prix and Tokai King Sho, with 14 wins, 2 seconds and a 3rd from 18. Adapting to turf, he won the Chunichi Shimbun Hai in autumn at 5, then the Hanshin Daishoten and Diamond S., and was 2nd to Tomei in the Tenno Sho (Autumn) at 6. Leg trouble kept him out about a year; 4th in an open race at 7, he rested again, and after a 4th trying jumps at 9 he retired.
3381=The 1975 Southern Kanto Triple Crown winner, only the 2nd after Hikaru Takai. Debuting in September at 3 (old age system), he won 2 straight. At 4, after near-misses, he won the Haneda Hai, then the Tokyo Derby, 3rd in the Tokyo Hai, and the Tokyo Okan Sho - the Triple Crown. Moving to the JRA at 5, he was winless but 2nd in the Tomoe Sho at 6.
3382=He won the 1976 Tokyo Daishoten easily in record time, a lasting memory for Southern Kanto fans. Debuting in November at 3 (old age system), at 4 he ran in the Southern Kanto Triple Crown: 4th Haneda Hai, 5th Tokyo Derby, 2nd Tokyo Okan Sho. He then beat JRA stars in the JRA Invitational and was fancied as a 4yo in the Tokyo Daishoten; leading all the way, he won in 3:08.6, smashing the record by 1.4 sec. He raced until 6. At stud he sired Urawa Oka Sho winner Grace Taizan and Niigata 3yo S. winner Meiner Mut.
3383=A durable Oi horse who raced until 7, winning the Tokyo Daishoten in 1979 at 7. Few wins but always racing: 2 from 9 at 3 (old age system), 3 from 14 at 4, 4 from 11 at 5, none from 6 at 6. At 7, 1 win from 9, then he won a tight finish in his final race, the Tokyo Daishoten - a big title at last. Moving to the JRA at 8, he was unplaced in the Nakayama Kinen and Nikkei Sho, ran once over jumps, and retired.
3384=Winner of the 1977 Tokyo Daishoten. Debuting in September at 3 (old age system), he won 5 straight to January at 4. In the Southern Kanto Triple Crown he was 2nd, 4th and 3rd. Favorite in the Tokyo Daishoten, he beat later winner Ebi Chikara. Older, he won only the Tokyo Hai; moving to the JRA at 6, he was winless and retired.
3385=Battling leg trouble, he raced in Southern Kanto, Nagoya and Hokkaido, and in Hokkaido was called the strongest ever. Debuting at Kawasaki in September at 3 (old age system), he was a Triple Crown hope at 4, but tendon trouble led to heavy losses in the Haneda Hai and Tokyo Derby. In Nagoya leg trouble limited him to 4 runs in 1.5 years. Moving to Hokkaido at 6, he won 9 straight to summer at 7, finally showing his class: 16 wins from 18 there to the end of 8, incl. a Japanese record for 1800m on dirt.
