38=* Seattle Slew, Carrying Dreams of Freedom\nA cheaply bought horse with an unremarkable pedigree runs like a champion and sets a historic first - there is no more thrilling American dream in racing. Seattle Slew was its embodiment.\nWhen he was born, he was not much to look at. He was entered in the yearling sale at Keeneland in Kentucky, America's famous breeding region, but his pedigree and looks kept him out of the select sale, so he went to the general sale in Lexington and sold for only $17,500 (about 5.18 million yen then). His trainer even nicknamed him "the ugly duckling," but once he debuted he ran brilliantly, quickly winning twice. He then won the G1 Champagne S. easily in 2yo record time and was named champion 2yo colt.\nIn 1977, at 3, his camp believed in him and chose the classic path. They expected the Belmont S., the longest leg at 12 furlongs (2400m), to be the hurdle to the Triple Crown. Thinking he needed to calm down mentally to handle the extra distance, they rested him through the winter in warm Florida. The plan seemed to work: he won two G1s and went into the Kentucky Derby unbeaten. He hit his head on the gate and lost about 2 lengths at the start, but quickly recovered, took the lead around the final turn and won with ease. He won the Preakness S. safely too, and went to the last classic, the Belmont S. His camp had always worried about the distance, and more doubts piled up: the horse van arrived 20 minutes late, and it was his first race on heavy going. But as if shrugging it all off, he shot to the front from the start and, never letting anyone near, won by 4 lengths. It was the moment the first unbeaten US Triple Crown winner was born.\n\n* British Fillies' Double Winner Dunfermline\nThe British royal family founded Ascot, Britain's flagship racecourse, and still owns it - royalty and racing are inseparable. Of all the royals, Elizabeth II was said to be the most passionate about racing.\nThe Queen owned many racehorses, and one of the most remarkable was Dunfermline, a filly named after a beautiful Scottish town. At 2 she was twice 2nd in G3s, a fair record, and she won her first race in the Pretty Polly S. on her 3yo debut. After that she went the classic route and took on the Oaks. Before the race the favorite Durtal bolted and was withdrawn; in that chaotic mood, 4th choice Dunfermline showed a sharp turn of foot in the straight to win a classic.\nIn the St. Leger S., Alleged, unbeaten in 5, was a heavy favorite, with Dunfermline only 3rd choice. Alleged went to the front early and tried to hold on. But Dunfermline, biding her time behind, surged up alongside. After a duel she pulled clear a furlong out and won by a length and a half. She completed the British fillies' double and became the only horse ever to beat Alleged, who would go on to win the Arc twice.
39=* Two US Triple Crown Winners Collide\nAt this time America had Triple Crown winners two years in a row, and racing was booming. One was Seattle Slew, who won it in 1977 - the first to sweep the Triple Crown unbeaten, and still named among the greatest American horses ever. The other was Affirmed, who swept it in 1978. Beaten only twice in 16 starts, both 2nds at 2, he was a giant with 8 G1 wins.\nThe two Triple Crown winners finally met in September's Marlboro Cup Invitational H. It was the first-ever clash of Triple Crown winners in American racing history and drew fierce attention from across the country and the world. Affirmed, riding high after his Triple Crown, was favorite, while Seattle Slew, who had won only two allowance races that year, was 2nd choice. But once the race began, Seattle Slew grabbed the lead, kept it all the way and held off Affirmed in 2nd to win by 3 lengths - the senior Triple Crown winner showing his class.\nThe next month they clashed again in the Jockey Club Gold C. This time Affirmed hung onto Seattle Slew early, but couldn't keep up and faded. Seattle Slew seemed set to dominate, but Exceller came from behind and the two fought a fierce duel, Exceller winning by a nose. Seattle Slew won their head-to-head 2-0, but Exceller, who beat them both, became famous overnight.\nMaruzensky, brought from America to Japan with off-the-charts power, was the same age as Seattle Slew. It was whispered in America at the time that had he debuted there, Seattle Slew's Triple Crown might have been in danger.\n\n* The Rival Who Threatened Affirmed's Triple Crown\nAffirmed became the 11th US Triple Crown winner, but the road was far from smooth, because his crop included the formidable Alydar. They clashed again and again from their debuts; in G1s at 2 they met four times, Affirmed winning three and Alydar one. In the 3yo Triple Crown races Alydar was 2nd every time, but by margins of a length and a half, a neck and a head - razor-thin - so Affirmed's Triple Crown was won through battle after battle. Affirmed's trainer Barrera said: "Affirmed is better than Secretariat (the 9th US Triple Crown winner in 1973), because he won the Triple Crown by beating a great rival in Alydar."\nAffirmed's jockey Steve Cauthen was just 18, the youngest jockey ever to win the Triple Crown, and was later inducted into the US Racing Hall of Fame.\nBoth Affirmed and Alydar made their mark as sires too, but Alydar's offspring, including two Kentucky Derby winners, far outperformed Affirmed's.\n\n* Alleged Wins Back-to-Back Arcs\nIn Europe Alleged became the 5th horse ever to win the Arc twice and was rated 140 lbs in the International Classification (later revised to 134). He retired after this year and became a leading sire of the Ribot line.\nThis was also the year the first crop of future great sire Mr. Prospector debuted. It included It's in the Air, who won the Eclipse Award for champion 2yo filly right away - a brilliant start that made him North America's leading first-crop sire.
40=* Affirmed, US Horse of the Year Two Years Running\nThe year before, Affirmed won the US Triple Crown and Horse of the Year, but in the second half he couldn't win, losing three straight. Starting this year in January, he still couldn't win, and his losing streak stretched to 5. Everyone feared he would never run like a Triple Crown winner again. But in the G1 Charles H. Strub S. he won by 10 lengths. Affirmed was fully back, and after an allowance race he won 5 more G1s in a row. With his win in the Hollywood Gold C. during that streak, his career earnings passed $2.03 million, making him America's first $2 million horse and breaking the world record then held by Kelso.\nHis farewell race was the Jockey Club Gold C., in which he had been 5th a year earlier. It was then the autumn championship for the best dirt horse, a race he badly wanted. Perhaps scared off, only 4 ran, but they included US double classic winner Spectacular Bid, a field worthy of a farewell. Affirmed led with Spectacular Bid chasing, but he held on to win, ending his career in style.\nAfter faltering once, Affirmed ran on with rare fighting spirit, winning 6 G1s that year, and was named Horse of the Year for the second year running.\n\n* Exceller, "Maker of Great Races," Retires\nExceller was a champion without a crown. With two Triple Crown winners, Seattle Slew and Affirmed, around at the same time, he was often overlooked, but his strength was real - as the 1978 Jockey Club Gold C. proved.\nIn the earlier Marlboro Cup, Seattle Slew had won the clash of Triple Crown winners, and the two met again in the Jockey Club Gold C.\nAs fans thrilled at the rematch, the race became a blistering match race between the two Triple Crown winners fighting for the lead. Exceller ran more than 20 lengths back. But when Affirmed faded, Exceller charged forward. Seattle Slew showed astonishing grit for a horse who had set such a fast pace, but Exceller won the fierce duel by a nose - the only horse ever to beat two Triple Crown winners in the same race.\nExceller retired in 1979 at 6. He went to stud at a famous American farm, where his high fee showed the hopes placed on him, but his offspring did poorly and he was sent to Sweden to continue as a sire. After his owner went bankrupt, Swedish law prevented him from even covering mares, and he was slaughtered. His death shocked racing people and fans, and led to groups protecting retired racehorses such as the "Exceller Fund."
