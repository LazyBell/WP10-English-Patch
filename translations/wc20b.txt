105=* "Bewitching Dark Bay" Mejiro Ramonu\nIn the year free "racing programs" began being handed out so every visitor could enjoy racing more, a new heroine was born: Mejiro Ramonu, a filly with a beautiful dark bay coat.\nShe won her debut by 20 lengths, showing her talent, but her true power emerged the next year when she teamed up with jockey Hiroshi Kawachi. In the 1986 Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (West) (now the Fillies' Revue), she dropped to last when the leaders faltered, then burst clear in the straight with an astonishing late charge. She went on to win the Oka Sho, the Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (East) (now the Flora S.) and the Oaks for an easy double. Kawachi compared her to Tesco Gaby, famed as the greatest filly ever: "I think she's surpassed her."\nIn autumn she won the Rose S. and went for the Triple Crown in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup. Hopes for the first fillies' Triple Crown made her 1.3. She moved from the third turn and drew level with the leaders. Used to winning from behind, her early move looked premature to crowd and commentator alike, but she held off the rest in the straight to win by half a length - the first fillies' Triple Crown in JRA history. Having also won every trial, she was hailed as a "perfect Triple Crown." Several fillies have since won the Triple Crown, but she alone did it perfectly.\nWith no graded wins against colts, some rate her only "the best filly," but the next year her Triple Crown earned her a Hall of Fame place alongside seven-crown winner Symboli Rudolf - the first female inducted.\n\n* Japan's First Night Racing\nSouthern Kanto local racing runs mainly on weekdays to avoid clashing with weekend JRA racing. At first it ran on weekday afternoons, which salaried workers couldn't attend. So in 1986 Oi Racecourse began "Twinkle Races," night racing that people could attend after work - the first night racing not just in horse racing but in all public betting sports, including keirin. Night racing proved popular and is now held at many local tracks.
108=* The Heisei Big Three\nThe year before, racing was thrilled by the grey duel. In this, the first year of Heisei, the epic battles of three horses sparked the second racing boom: Oguri Cap, Inari One and Super Creek, the "Heisei Big Three."\nInari One moved from local Oi. While Oguri Cap and previous Kikuka Sho winner Super Creek rested in the first half of the year, he won the Tenno Sho (Spring) and the Takarazuka Kinen, rising fast on the main path with fierce fighting spirit. In autumn he met Oguri Cap for the first time in the Mainichi Okan, a thriller decided by photo: Oguri Cap by a nose, showing the pride of "the idol horse of the age."\nIn the Tenno Sho (Autumn) Super Creek joined, and the future Heisei Big Three met for the first time. From the widest draw, thought a disadvantage, Super Creek raced forward with ease and held off Oguri Cap's charge by a neck.\nAll three met again in the Arima Kinen. Thanks to recent form, Oguri Cap and Super Creek got single brackets, and it was billed a "Big Two." As expected, the two raced forward and rounded the final turn together. But in the straight Inari One charged as if to prove he was one of the "Big Three," hitting the line level with Super Creek, and won by a nose after a photo.\nThe Heisei Big Three thrilled racing with neck-and-neck battles, and kept leading the boom with more fierce clashes the next year.\n\n* Causes of the Second Racing Boom\nThe bubble economy was of course a big factor. But unlike the first boom, driven by horses like Haiseiko, this one was also boosted by attention on jockeys. Most popular of all was Yutaka Take, who rode all three of the Heisei Big Three. At first he was known as "Kunihiko Take's son," after his father, a top jockey until a few years before. But breaking the rookie wins record and becoming the youngest classic winner ever, he quickly became the star of the boom. He is still riding at the top today as racing's "living legend."\nThis year also saw the birth of Turfy, the JRA mascot. Perhaps helped by such efforts, child fans who couldn't even bet - unheard of in the first boom - surged.\n\n* A First for Greys\nGreys were starring, but late bloomer Tamamo Cross and Oguri Cap, who wasn't nominated for the classics, never ran in the Japanese Derby.\nBut this year a new grey, Winner's Circle, emerged. 2nd in the Satsuki Sho, he became the first grey ever to win the Derby. It was also the first Derby win by a horse bred in Ibaraki, where few are foaled, which made big news.
109=* The Wonder Brought by "the Wind God"\nThe second racing boom, which began in the late 1980s, peaked this year.\nAfter wins like the Asahi Hai Sansai S. the year before, Ines Fujin was seen as the top classic contender. But he lost the Yayoi Sho to Mejiro Ryan and the Satsuki Sho to Hakutaisei. Some called for jockey Eiji Nakano, his rider since his debut, to be replaced, but trainer Shusuke Kato kept him on.\nThen came the Japanese Derby. In the middle of the boom, with fans excited by the clash of Mejiro Ryan, Hakutaisei and Ines Fujin, over 190,000 people packed Tokyo Racecourse. It far exceeded the first boom's record and still stands as the world record for racecourse attendance.\nAs the gates opened, Ines Fujin went to the front. He never slowed; Hakutaisei and others closed, but he pulled away again, kept his lead in the straight and won from the front. Having run a stunning time that broke the race record by a second, he could only walk slowly afterwards, returning to the stands alone, far behind the rest. Seeing the winner's calm return, the huge crowd chanted "Nakano! Nakano!" for the jockey's superb ride and the horse who answered it. Chanting a winner's name was unheard of then - a new culture was born. It may have been the moment racing was accepted as a sport.\nAfter the race Ines Fujin suffered a tendon injury. He tried to return but couldn't, leaving the turf as if he had burned out in the Derby.\n\n* Oguri Cap's Miracle Last Race\nOguri Cap led the second boom. In his first race this year, the Yasuda Kinen, he was ridden by Yutaka Take, another pillar of the boom. Two of racing's biggest stars together drew huge attention, and Oguri Cap won in course-record time.\nBut he was 2nd in the Takarazuka Kinen, then a poor 6th in the Tenno Sho (Autumn). Aiming to bounce back in the Japan Cup, he ran a dismal 11th, his first double-digit finish. People whispered "Oguri Cap is finished," and some fans, unable to bear seeing him lose, pleaded, "Please just let him retire."\nHis camp chose the Arima Kinen as his farewell. They asked Take to ride him again, the first time since the Yasuda Kinen, and say they'd have withdrawn him had he refused. Over 170,000 fans packed Nakayama to see Oguri Cap's last race, but he was only 4th choice, and few thought he could win.\nJust before the start, Take reportedly encouraged his partner: "Hey, you're Oguri Cap, remember." The pace was unexpectedly slow, and many horses fought their riders. But the experienced Oguri Cap and Take saved their energy. Moving up from the third turn, he took the lead in the straight and held off the rest on sheer guts. The miracle last race, defying all predictions, set the crowd roaring, and soon chants of "Oguri! Oguri!" rang out. The idol horse who sparked the boom left the stage with the perfect finale.
