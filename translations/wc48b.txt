691=Always in the top five, she rose through the classes, then won her first graded try, the Fukushima Himba S. at 4, from up front as 7th choice. A regular in fillies' graded races, her best after was 2nd in the Hakodate Kinen; she retired at the end of '07.
696=Debuting very late, in September at 4, he bloomed at once with 4 straight turf sprint wins. A last-to-first closer, he won the Silk Road S. at 6 and the Negishi S. on dirt that year. At 7 he won the Kakitsubata Kinen, Procyon S. and Sirius S., and at 8 the Garnet S. - a top sprinter on turf or dirt for years. In his farewell, his only race abroad, he was a fine 5th of 13 in the Dubai Golden Shaheen.
700=Stuck in the lower classes, she finally won her 3rd race in a September special. With no graded record and no late-improver form, 10th choice for the Shuka Sho was fair. But she broke clear from up front, beating the 2yo queen, Oka Sho winner and Oaks winner together - top of her crop. Called a fluke, she then ran 2nd twice in fillies' graded races and 3rd in the next Queen Elizabeth II Cup, silencing doubters.
711=Her dam Ski Paradise was a French star who won the Prix du Moulin. Air Thule won the Hanshin Himba S., was 3rd in a Singapore G3, then a fine 2nd in Europe's G1 Prix Maurice de Gheest. As a dam she excelled: first foal Ultima Thule won 2 graded races, and second foal Captain Thule the '08 Satsuki Sho.
714=At 2 she led to win the Fantasy S. by 3 lengths and was 5th in the Hanshin Sansai Himba S. At 4 she led all the way in the Centaur S. against Sprinters S. winner Daitaku Yamato. As a dam she produced A Shin Memphis (08: Medaglia d'Oro), who led all the way in the Aichi Hai as 7th choice.
716=A dogged front-runner. Hurt after debut, he rested 19 months, then won 2 JRA-local graded races.
723=Unlike his name, his legs weren't tough, and injuries often sidelined him. But he was talented, with 5 wins and five 2nds from a sharp late charge. At 4 he won the Kabutoyama Kinen in good time, closing from last in 33.0.
708=By NHK Mile-Derby double winner King Kamehameha out of queen Air Groove - one of the finest bluebloods in modern Japanese racing. He won his debut at the end of his 2yo year by 3 lengths as 1.5 favorite, already tipped for the classics. But in the Wakakoma S. (OP) he couldn't catch Hiruno d'Amour, who broke out on the inside, 2nd. In the Armeria Sho (5M) he was bumped badly but surged in the straight for his 2nd win. Boxed in on the inside in the Mainichi Hai, he was 5th, and lacking earnings missed the Satsuki Sho. He won the Principal S. by 4 lengths, then was 5th in the Derby. Hoof trouble followed, delaying his return to December's Naruo Kinen, which he won for his first graded title, then a good 6th behind Victoire Pisa in the Arima Kinen. At 4 he won the Nikkei Shinshun Hai by 2 lengths and went to the Dubai Sheema Classic, but fought his rider and was 6th. Back home, in the Kinko Sho he missed the break badly, yet with an astonishing kick that made others look still, won his 3rd graded race. 2nd choice for the Takarazuka Kinen, he couldn't close from behind, 5th. Leg trouble then sent him straight to the Arima Kinen; down to 11th choice, he closed fastest for 4th. At 5 he won the American JCC, was 3rd in the Nikkei Sho, then went to Hong Kong's QE2 Cup, racing forward and powering clear to win his long-sought first G1 abroad. Back home he developed a bad habit of missing the break badly in 4 straight G1s: 2nd in the Takarazuka Kinen, 3rd in the Tenno Sho (Autumn), Japan Cup and Arima Kinen. He went to stud that year, and his pedigree free of Sunday Silence blood made him instantly popular. His first crop gave Kikuka Sho winner Kiseki and Danburite; in 2019 Mer de Glace won the Australian G1 Caulfield C. on a 6-race streak; in 2022 Dolce More won the Asahi Hai FS; and Soul Rush won the 2024 Mile CS and 2025 Dubai Turf. He remains near the top of the sire rankings.
717=Good in allowance races but poor in graded ones, until the Hanshin Himba S. at the end of her 4yo year, where she swooped past Oaks winner Daiwa El Cielo and others. After a dip she was 2nd in the Queen S. the next summer, then won the Sapporo Kinen. In the Tenno Sho (Autumn) her astonishing finish caught Zenno Rob Roy and others - only the 3rd filly or mare to win the Tenno Sho, after Air Groove. Jockey Mikio Matsunaga bowed to the attending Emperor. She ran well against colts, 7th in the Japan Cup and 6th in the Arima Kinen, and retired at the end of 2005. As a dam she produced JBC Classic winner Awardee (10: Jungle Pocket), 5-time local dirt graded winner Amour Briller (11: Smart Strike) and UAE Derby winner Lani (13: Tapit).
