56=* First-Ever Unbeaten European Triple Crown Winner\nThis year an unprecedented horse appeared: just 4 starts and 4 wins in his life, three of them the European Triple Crown. Lammtarra, well known in Japan too. He debuted the year before, was off for nearly a year with illness, then won the Derby on his return. He went on to beat strong fields with ease in the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. and the Arc, taking Europe's top titles without fuss. He was the first European Triple Crown winner since Mill Reef in 1971, 24 years earlier, and the first ever to do it unbeaten. For his miraculous record and his name (Arabic for "the invisible"), he was called "the horse of God."\nYet Lammtarra was not named Cartier Horse of the Year, and was rated only 130 lbs in the International Classification, low for his record. Reasons given were the modest quality of the horses he beat and his unspectacular style, but even more important was Ridgewood Pearl, a star of the mile division.\nRidgewood Pearl, a filly of the same crop, won mile G1s in four countries: the Irish 1000 Guineas, Britain's Coronation S., France's Prix du Moulin de Longchamp and America's Breeders' Cup Mile. She ran impressively too, setting a record time in the Coronation S. In the end the Cartier Awards named her Horse of the Year, while Lammtarra took champion 3yo colt.\nThe decision was controversial, with claims that Lammtarra was held back for being owned by Dubai's powerful Maktoum family, while Ridgewood Pearl, raised by a small Irish breeding couple, was favored. Even so, Lammtarra was a great horse, and he was later imported to Japan as a leading heir to Nijinsky for 4.4 billion yen (at the time's exchange rate).\n\n* Cigar, America's Best of the 1990s\nAt the same time, America had a horse on an incredible winning run: Cigar, who set an unprecedented 16-race winning streak from 1994 to 1996. This year, at 5, he won all 10 starts, including 8 G1s (equaling the record then), and was named Eclipse Horse of the Year almost unanimously. Most stunning was the Breeders' Cup Classic, where he clocked 1:59.58 on a sloppy track, overwhelming everyone with his astonishing potential. He was rated 132 lbs in the International Classification this year and 135 the next, and is called America's greatest horse of the 1990s.\nThe 3yo crop had a star too: Thunder Gulch, winner of the Kentucky Derby and Belmont S., and his clash with Cigar in the Jockey Club Gold C. drew great attention. But Cigar won easily, and Thunder Gulch was a well-beaten 5th, over 10 lengths back. It was his last race.\nAnother standout that year was Inside Information, who won the Breeders' Cup Distaff by a record thirteen and a half lengths - a margin still unbeaten some 30 years later.
57=* A Victory Signal Rising From the Dust\nThe year before, Cigar won all 10 starts, including 8 G1s, and was Eclipse Horse of the Year. In 1996, at 6, he raced on, starting in the G1 Donn H., which he had won before. In a strong field including 8-time G1 winner Heavenly Prize, he was overwhelming favorite at 1.2. He won so easily he was eased down in the straight, winning the race for the second time. Next he was invited to the new Dubai World Cup, founded that year. He beat horses like Japan's best dirt horse of the time, Lively Mount, with ease to become its first champion.\nIn America at the time, racecourse after racecourse announced bigger purses only for races Cigar entered, trying to lure him - that is how big his impact on American racing was. Back home he won a listed race to stretch his streak, which began two years before, to 15. One more win would equal the American record of 16 straight set by Triple Crown winner Citation in 1950. For this historic attempt a special race was staged, the Citation Challenge Invitational S. Cigar won it, and the crowd applauded his 16th straight win for a full 20 minutes.\nHe lost his next race, ending the streak, but by retirement his earnings totaled $9,999,815. The world's leading money earner, Cigar was Eclipse Horse of the Year for the second straight year.\n\n* The Dubai World Cup Is Founded\nSince its first racecourse was built in 1969, racing culture spread quickly in Dubai. Sheikh Mohammed did most to drive it. He owned British fillies' Triple Crown winner Oh So Sharp and Arc winner Carnegie, and founded the Godolphin racing operation with a training base in Dubai - a tireless champion of racing.\nIn 1996 he founded the Dubai World Cup, an international race at Nad Al Sheba Racecourse. Its defining feature was being the world's richest race ($4 million in total). The first running being won by Cigar, hailed as America's best, may have helped cement its status.\nMore races were added to the same day, including the turf Dubai Turf Classic (now the Dubai Sheema Classic), and today Dubai World Cup Day is an event that draws top horses from around the world.
58=* A European-Trained Dubai World Cup Winner\nThe Japan Cup, made an international G1 in 1992, was booming. In 1996 stars came from around the world, including Helissio, fresh from a dominant Arc win, and Pentire, Europe's best older horse and King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. winner. But the race was snatched by Singspiel, a British horse with just one G1 win, 4th choice.\nIn 1997, at 5, Singspiel rose even higher. He opened in the second Dubai World Cup. Rare torrential rain delayed the meeting by 4 days, and it was his first race on dirt, but he shrugged it all off to become the first European-trained winner of the Dubai World Cup. For Japanese fans, though, it was a sad race: Japan's "Queen of the Sand" Hokuto Vega fell during the race and had to be put down. She was due to retire after it, so the grief of her camp and fans was all the greater.\nSingspiel then won two more G1s at home in Britain, the Coronation C. and the International S. He traveled again for the Breeders' Cup Turf, but fractured a leg in training the day before, was withdrawn and retired.\n\n* Young Genius Trainer A. O'Brien\nA. O'Brien, influenced by his father, a former jockey and trainer, became an assistant trainer and took over his trainer wife's stable in 1993. He first shone over jumps, champion jumps trainer in Ireland for five straight seasons from his first in 1993.\nSoon his talent shone on the flat too. He won his first G1 with Desert King in 1996, and an Irish double with him the next year. That year, at 26, he became the youngest-ever champion flat trainer in Ireland. He kept it up, developing greats like Galileo and Rock of Gibraltar, and was champion trainer 10 years running from 1997 to 2006. With 10 Derby wins and 16 Irish Derby wins by 2024, he is one of modern racing's leading trainers.
59=* Swain Defies the Odds\nSwain was a good horse who had won the Coronation C. and the Prix Foy. But the 1997 King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. was packed with stars like French Horse of the Year Helissio and US Horse of the Year Singspiel, and Swain was only 6th choice. The race was later called "The Race of the Decade." In heavy rain, Swain passed leader Helissio 2 furlongs out and held off the rest to win by a length.\nAs older stars like Helissio and Singspiel left the turf, Swain raced on at 6 in 1998. But wins dried up, and he came into his second King George on a 5-race losing streak. Despite being the previous winner, he was only 4th choice. In mid-straight he won a duel with High-Rise and went on to win by a length - only the second horse ever to win the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S. twice, and the first since Dahlia 24 years earlier.\n\n* The Charm of the Grey\nGreys often become idols in Japan, and it's the same abroad. Silver Charm was a grey who captivated American fans with his beautiful build and fighting spirit. In 1997, at 3, he ran in the Kentucky Derby and held off the late charge of favorite Captain Bodgit by a head. In the Preakness S. he just won a three-way photo with Free House as well, taking two legs of the Triple Crown. He narrowly lost the Belmont S., but was named Eclipse Horse of the Year.\nWinning two G2s early in 1998, Silver Charm headed to the Dubai World Cup on a roll. He faced the formidable Swain, but was favorite with British bookmakers (betting isn't offered in Dubai for religious reasons). Silver Charm led with 100m left, then Swain launched a fierce attack. They hit the line almost together, but Silver Charm won by a short head. Afterwards jockey G. Stevens praised his spirit: "He got passed once, but he put his head down and fought to get back in front." All three of his G1 wins to that point came by a head or less - proof of his extraordinary will to win.
60=* Montjeu Stands in Japan's Way\nMontjeu was an Irish-bred, French-trained colt. He won both starts at 2, and in his first race of 1999 took the G2 Prix Greffulhe for his first graded win, becoming a classic contender. He was narrowly 2nd in the G1 Prix Lupin, but crushed the field by 4 lengths in the big one, the French Derby. On a roll, he ran in the Irish Derby. Held up at the back, he moved up between the third and final turns and swept past the field in the straight to win by 5 lengths, confirming himself as Europe's best 3yo.\nIn autumn he started in the Prix Niel, the Arc prep, as overwhelming favorite at 1.1. Bienamado went for home early and almost held on, but Montjeu just got by him by a head. Then the Arc, on a 3-race streak since the French Derby. Montjeu was favorite, with Japan's El Condor Pasa, on a roll after winning the Grand Prix de Saint-Cloud and the Prix Foy, a close 2nd choice. The going was extraordinarily heavy, and the race was seen as a duel between the two mud-lovers. El Condor Pasa broke well and led alone. In the straight he showed no sign of tiring and tried to leave the field behind, but Montjeu alone closed in. With about 100m left Montjeu went by, and held off El Condor Pasa's fightback by half a length. Japanese fans had hoped for Japan's first Arc win, but Europe showed the true depth of its racing.\nThe next year Montjeu won 3 G1s and retired. At stud his powerful kick passed on to his offspring, and he sired four Derby winners.\n\n* Mr. Prospector, Working to the End\nAs a racehorse Mr. Prospector won 7 of 14 with no graded wins - hardly top class. His true worth showed at stud. He started in Florida, but his first crop produced 5-time G1 winner It's in the Air, his reputation soared, and he moved to Kentucky, the heart of American breeding. His son Conquistador Cielo became US Horse of the Year, and his standing as a sire kept rising.\nHis defining trait was toughness. In 1999 he was put down at 29 due to severe colic, yet he covered 47 mares that very year. He left many offspring, who succeeded hugely as racehorses and as sires, so his blood still has a major influence on world racing.\n\n* The WSRC Is Founded\nThis year, sponsored by a Dubai-based airline, the World Series Racing Championship was founded, awarding points for placings in the world's major G1s. The original races were the King George VI & Queen Elizabeth Diamond S., Irish Champion S., Cox Plate, Breeders' Cup Turf, Breeders' Cup Classic, Japan Cup, Canadian International S., Hong Kong Cup and Dubai World Cup. The Arc was added in 2001. The first champion was Daylami, who won 3 of the races for 38 points, still the series record. Created to develop racing internationally, it was renamed the World Racing Championship in 2005, but ended that year after 7 editions.
